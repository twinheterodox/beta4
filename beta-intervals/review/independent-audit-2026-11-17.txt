Audit of Two shorter intervals of even Dirichlet beta values
Manuscript dated 17 November 2026

Verdict

I found no mathematical error or unproved step affecting Theorem 1. Each of the
two lists, beta(6), beta(8), ..., beta(66) and beta(8), beta(10), ..., beta(98),
contains an irrational value. They have 31 and 46 entries. A single irrational
value in their overlap would satisfy both assertions. The earlier powered-kernel
paper gives endpoints 74 and 110 for those same starting indices.

Mathematical checks

1. Formation of the linear forms. Counting multiplicities gives gamma = 341/16,
G = 221/16 and degree -Gn-24. Reflection preserves R_n and pairs the order-i
principal parts with sign (-1)^i. Because n is even, this eliminates every
odd i in the aggregate beta coefficients. The prefactor removes one nominal
order at the central pole. The derivative of order 2q at every required
integer zero vanishes, since that zero has multiplicity 3q. Differentiating
and shifting the half-integer fractions gives the rising factorial, power
of two and alternating sign in (2.6)-(2.8). The even exponents range from
2q+2 through the largest even integer at most 33q.

2. Integer coefficients. For a local series with coefficient valuations at
least v-lambda*h in degree h, the qth power has valuations at least
qv-lambda*h. Applying the rule before extracting degree qB_a-i gives
(3.5) and (3.6), also at the central pole. At small odd primes the harmonic
denominator costs at most (i+2q)*lambda, leaving q times the original bound.
At 2, all harmonic denominators are odd. For a repeated large-prime class
the bound i-qF_p(a) loses at most i+2q powers at a bad denominator. For an
isolated class the change of variable ell=a-h rewrites the rational constant
as (3.7). The zero identity at -ell lets one take the side that omits the
sole bad address. This also handles primes larger than n. These calculations
give the very same D_n raised to q.

3. Growth and decay. I checked the Legendre estimates for the small primes
in the fifteen-value paper's Appendix A. After subtracting G*log(n!), the
small odd primes contribute o(n) to the upper bound and the prime 2 costs at
most 15*n*log(2)+o(n). For large odd primes I checked the real envelope,
including the restricted domain 0 <= y <= x-1 for 1 <= x < 2, the endpoint
convention, the prime-number-theorem integration, and the tail estimate.
My code generated the partition from affine-offset coincidences. It computed
each maximum directly at every eligible y-breakpoint and just to its right.
On 38,371 open intervals, the sum rounded upwards cell by cell reproduced

    body = 73794151590486879261 / 9223372036854775808
    tail upper bound = 34253 / 319200
    total upper bound = 8.10808684435616...

I checked the uniform Stirling estimate including t=rn+1, the fixed-order
derivative bound, and summability on t>3n. The critical polynomial has
degree 33 and one sign change. Exact rational bisection and outward interval
logarithms put its maximum point at x = 0.757334986669258889... and the
phase there at -18.835112366808193600.... For x >= 3 the elementary
derivative estimate gives x*Phi'(x) <= -383/64. Proposition 3's coefficient
per q is consequently at most -0.2327922916008203... using its stated
constants. Using the verified density and phase bounds directly gives the
stronger upper bound -0.3298 per q.

4. Prime dilation and the final inference. At n=96p the selected zeros are
the odd multiples of p up to 239p in absolute value, with the prefactor
accounted for separately. The zero address class has total pole count
2077 and the other classes have count 2046. Accounting for the prefactor
and 720 selected zero factors gives excess 1356 in the selected class.
The two extra endpoints give the unit -1. At an unselected pole the
corresponding deficit is at most 1326. These counts give the local
principal-part estimates of Lemma 4. After multiplying by p^(1358q)
every beta coefficient is divisible by p. The harmonic denominators cost
at most i+2q at an ith principal part, leaving at least 30q powers of p in
each unselected constant contribution. For a selected address the surviving
terms have h=pv+(p-1)/2, giving the sign chi_4(p). I also tested the actual
large kernel at n=96p for p=347 and p=349, checking the local counts, the
scalar reduction at all unit residue classes away from the selected poles
and zeros, and the normalized harmonic sums for all 97 selected addresses
and exponents through 99. These finite checks supplement the general proof.

If all the beta values in the indicated list were rational, one fixed T
would clear them. For all sufficiently large primes p avoiding the
numerators and denominators in the nonvanishing argument, the scaled
constant is a p-adic unit while every other surviving term is divisible
by p. This proves nonvanishing on n=96p. The real numbers T D_n^q L_{q,n}
are then nonzero integers on that subsequence and tend to zero, giving
the contradiction for each q separately.

5. Independent calculation of the finite constants. I constructed the global
numerator P and denominator Q of the trimmed seed over each finite field.
I translated these global polynomials to each of the 97 pole centers with
binomial coefficients, divided the resulting series after removing the
nominal pole, and computed the powered principal parts. I reconstructed
the full rational function at q*2077 distinct regular field points for
each q and each modulus. This establishes the full identity in each case,
since multiplying its difference by Q^q gives a polynomial of degree
strictly less than q*2077. I also checked reflection and odd-coefficient
cancellation and then evaluated the finite harmonic sums.

    1           3738             5333              938
    2           8279             5481             5660
    3           9978             6289             4500

The first two columns reproduce all six printed residues, covering 24,924
nominal principal-part coefficients. The last column uses an additional
prime. I wrote the verification programs separately from the supplied
checkers, and used the prime number theorem, Chebyshev's upper bound,
Stirling's formula, Legendre's formula, Wilson's theorem and Descartes'
rule of signs as standard inputs. I checked the earlier powered-kernel
result for the comparison endpoints. The proof here needs only q=2 and 3.

Exposition

On pages 7-8, introduce 1358q as 1356q+2q at its definition. A selected
coefficient of pole order i then carries i+2q powers of p, exactly the
amount the harmonic sum can consume. An unselected contribution has a
surplus of at least (1356-1326)q=30q. This explains why the normalization
preserves precisely the part of the constant the finite calculation detects.

On page 8, replace “Either nonzero residue in either of the last two rows”
with “Each of the last two rows has a nonzero entry, proving respectively
that k_2 and k_3 are nonzero.” One prime per row is sufficient. In the final
proof, it would also help to recall that D_n^q clears the form globally,
while the power of p tests nonvanishing on the selected subsequence.

Reproduction

From the beta-intervals project directory, run
  python3 -B checks/independent-audit/run_checks.py
The programs
require Python 3.9 or later, NumPy, mpmath and a C++17 compiler named g++.
Run Python with assertions enabled. The package includes the output of a
full run.
