Decay with the published normalization

In the fifteen-value paper the fifth numbered equation of Section 4,
(4.5), gives the certified upper bound

    A = 745969417189431202995707 / 92003136067626388684800.

The proof following (4.6) bounds the normalized large-prime contribution
by n(A+o(1)) for precisely the isolated-pole exponents used in the
revised manuscript. Rounding A upward gives 8.10809. After subtracting
G log(n!), the small odd primes contribute o(n) to the upper bound
and 2 contributes at most 15n log 2+o(n). The global clearer for the
powered form is D_n^q. Thus each of these terms enters the logarithm
with a factor of q.

Equation (4.9) bounds the global maximum of the same phase by
-18835112315041/10^12 = -18.835112315041. The revised -18.83511
rounds upward. The derivative estimate for x>=3 in the same source

the fixed polynomial from the Stirling error on t>3n. On the finite

The (n!)^(-qG) exactly cancels the factorial contribution from D_n^q.

Local scaling and nonvanishing

p^(1356q-i)c_(q,96p;pa,i). Multiplication of c by p^(1358q) therefore
leaves at least s powers of p in the beta contribution. At an unselected
address the valuation bound i-1326q gives at least i+32q=s+30q after
scaling. The rising factorial (i)_(2q) is integral in each case.
Since 2h+1<192p<p^2, a harmonic term can cost at most s powers.
Unselected terms still vanish modulo p. At a selected address the
surviving denominators are p(2v+1), 0<=v<a, with 2v+1<=191<p.

constant congruence with the selected-pole factor (-1)^q.
