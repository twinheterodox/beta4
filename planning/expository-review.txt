Equation-by-equation expository review, 31 October 2026

Scope and standard
This pass reviews the main paper and technical supplement for an expert
mathematical reader who does not already know these constructions. For
each displayed equation and argument, the review asks whether its objects
have been defined, its hypotheses are visible, its purpose is explained,
and the transition from the preceding step is reasonably recoverable.
Compressed derivations and proof organization are revised where needed.
This is work by the assembling model, not an independent mathematical
audit, human review, priority assessment or formal verification. Both
drafts remain local and unpublished.

The baseline is commit local-prepublication-revision.
planning/expository-baseline.json records its source and artifact hashes.
Unlike the preceding accessibility pass, this revision edits proofs.
All 21 theorem, lemma, proposition, corollary and definition environments
are preserved verbatim; their surrounding derivations and proof text
have been expanded or reorganized. The comparison does not certify the
mathematical truth of those statements.

Main-paper review
1. Introduction and constructions. Separate subsections introduce the
   two families. The first retains the preceding revision's derivation
   of the divided-difference entries, squared nodes and constants. The
   second now starts with the positive double integral, explains its
   Gram interpretation, and describes the middle-term contribution and
   cancellation of the cubic tails before presenting the coefficients.
   The two partial-sum conventions are related explicitly. The existing
   irrationality criterion, degree cost, common proof outline and growth
   table were checked against the results they describe.
2. Primitive normalization. Define coefficient extraction, valuations,
   p-integrality, p-units and reduction modulo p. Retain the concrete
   common-content example and the warning about increasing degrees.
3. Positive kernel. Explain the purpose of the two transforms; insert
   the nonnegative double integral and the divided-difference identity.
   Separate identifying the density from passing to determinant minors.
   Link the sign of the Cauchy factors to their explicit product formula.
   Explain the roles of the comparison constants and show the row/column
   factorization before invoking the rank-one determinant identity.
4. Consecutive arithmetic. Explain the selected prime interval, high
   nodes, and the principal-minor coefficient formula. Retain the worked
   prime-seven example. Introduce the competing real and arithmetic
   exponents before computing them, explain the two offset regimes,
   derive the Vandermonde factorial product, and identify the mn scale
   for offsets larger than the matrix. Check the final subsequence step.
5. Difference-variable moments. Show how parameter derivatives insert
   log-squared weights, then write the off-diagonal binomial sum before
   its reindexing. Give the half-square substitution and its Jacobian.
   Explain why monic basis changes preserve the determinant and why
   different orthogonal polynomial families serve the two real bounds.
   Review the arcsine leading-coefficient and Rodrigues norm arguments.
6. Exact primitive arithmetic. Explain the first possible residue index
   and derive the size and position of its supported block, including
   the empty-block case. Replace the compressed permutation argument
   by an explicit block-triangular reduction after scaling bottom rows
   by p^3. Explain how this attains the coefficient valuation. Review
   the leading-coefficient control and final prime summation.
7. Scope and appendix. Check that the statements distinguish full pencils,
   basis changes and different subspaces. Review the common-zero row
   operation, regularized density comparison, and its arithmetic factor
   two. The bibliography and attribution remain unchanged.

Supplement review
1. Conventions and positive cone. Define the arithmetic terminology;
   derive the measure normalization and formal pair by polynomial
   division, with examples. Explain the two-stage positive witness
   construction. Motivate the exterior Chebyshev point before choosing
   the test polynomial; explain the derivative-to-integral lower bound
   and the final degree asymptotic.
2. Euler sources and repeated quadratics. Derive the Euler identity by
   logarithmic substitution and integration by parts. Explain Taylor
   cancellation and the reversed polynomial term by term. Give the
   prime-cost/minimax proof plan and spell out why gcd removal can save
   at most the target coefficient. Review the moving-parameter bound.
3. Euler--Boole. Derive the correction coefficients from the tail integral.
   Divide the proof into real error/sign, reduced denominators and the
   remaining parameter regime. Explain the primes that corrections
   cannot cancel and the scale of optimal truncation. Review the Fourier
   partial fractions, uniformity quantifiers and product conclusion.
4. Continued fractions. Give initial approximants and distinguish the
   normalized coefficients from integer continuants. Derive the two
   generating-function differential equations and the beta integral
   behind the error asymptotic. Motivate the valuation-eight calculation
   before defining its auxiliary polynomials. Separate unit factors,
   denominator order and survival under differentiation; explain the
   characteristic-polynomial congruence and the exceptional-prime bound.
   Retain the fixed-endpoint/even-depth scope and complex-uniformity step.
5. Cyclotomic family. Motivate the trigonometric approximation, define
   the unit property used, and separate integrality, principal error,
   conjugates and full norm. Explain embedding indices and sine-product
   normalization. Clarify that the conjugates of magnitude greater than
   ten exclude both the principal conjugate and its negative.
6. Completed Mellin source. Derive the common weight through its geometric
   term integral, explain the factor m^4 and shared quadratic denominator,
   and motivate P=A+DV before imposing the two moment constraints. Give
   the sign-correction strategy before the theorem and organize its proof
   by root location, constrained moments and constants. Preserve the
   explicit external Broadhurst input and exact-certificate distinction.
7. Monodromy. Explain the telescoping choice, rational Taylor germ and
   failure of constant shifting to remove the branch. Spell out why
   differential operators commute with continuation and how a residue
   produces an additive constant. Explain the symmetry coordinate and
   the field over which the subsequent span has dimension five.
8. Moment variation. Begin with the Laplace identity motivating the
   parameter. Define the determinant before use and explain the rational
   basis, positive lower block and Schur complement. Work out the n=2
   example. Derive the constrained minimum and explain why stationarity
   removes the derivative of the minimizer. Separate positivity from
   the rate proof and explain the need for an interior evaluation bound
   rather than a direct density comparison. Preserve the unresolved
   denominator question and finite-data limits.
9. Method catalog. Review every displayed formula and every method's
   claimed scope. Supply missing definitions for the scalar ladder,
   bordered matrices, interleaved energy, quarter projection and Euler
   functional. Make the error and coefficient exponents in the paired
   quarter construction explicit. Explain exactly what a rational scalar
   removes in the dimensional rediscovery. Link record IDs to the source
   register; link its primary paths to files in the local checkout. Point
   Q04 to the corrected complete-deformation account. Summaries remain
   summaries: archived proofs are not presented as newly re-audited.
10. Evidence and references. Preserve the distinctions among selected
    complete proofs, archived claims, finite certificates and numerical
    diagnostics. No new source or attribution claim was introduced.

Verification

uses exact rational and Arb interval checks; the supplement replays the
Mellin, Euler--Boole and cyclotomic checks and reconstructs the existing
scalar, continuant and ordinary-moment examples. The scripts and their
exact structural results are unchanged. The cyclotomic mpmath diagnostics
remain numerical diagnostics, not interval certificates. These are finite
checks, not proofs of the asymptotic claims or an independent audit.

claim destinations, local source links and manuscript build hashes were

other links; final renders were compared with the inspected pages and the
changed pages inspected again.
