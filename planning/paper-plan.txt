ARITHMETIC OBSTRUCTIONS IN POSITIVE DETERMINANT APPROXIMATIONS TO beta(4)
Writing plan and proposed theorem statements — 31 October 2026

Decision
========

Prepare one focused mathematical paper, accompanied by a comprehensive
technical supplement. The main paper should prove the two determinant
obstructions C03 and C07, with their analytic and local-arithmetic lemmas.
It should not narrate eighteen research rounds or imply that beta(4)
irrationality itself has been ruled out as a research target.

The contribution worth testing with a mathematical reader is specific:
positive real determinants with very favorable decay can nevertheless have
provably growing primitive integer values, and in these two families the
loss is visible in exact coefficient valuations. A bad upper bound for a
chosen common denominator would not establish this conclusion.

The 57-entry inventory is a selection and reconciliation of the archive,
not 57 proposed new theorems. Twelve entries record archived primitive-value
obstructions of varying scope; only the selected determinant proofs have
received the focused source/proof review described in this writing pass.
General positivity, orthogonal-polynomial identities and content removal
must be credited as established tools. Priority of the specific new
statements remains provisional.

1. Conventions that the paper must establish first
================================================

Let theta=beta(4)=sum_{k>=0}(-1)^k/(2k+1)^4. If Q is a nonzero polynomial
in Q[X], its primitive representative is the unique positive rational
multiple Z=kappa Q with integral coefficients of gcd one, when Q has
positive leading coefficient. The primitive representative is independent
of multiplying Q by a positive rational scalar.

A sufficient denominator D, for which DQ is integral, need not equal
kappa. A lower bound for D is not automatically a lower bound for kappa.
Similarly, a lower bound for an integral coefficient-clearing multiple
can miss a rational primitive rescaling when the leading coefficient has
nontrivial numerator content. This distinction is the reason C03/C07 and
P06/Q05 have different statuses.

State the degree-sensitive rationality test correctly. If theta=a/b is
rational and deg Z_n<=n, then b^n Z_n(theta) is an integer. A sufficient
contradiction would be nonzero Z_n(theta) with

                 |Z_n(theta)|^(1/n) -> 0.

The much simpler linear-form criterion is a special case. Merely saying
that integer-polynomial values tend to zero while their degrees grow is
insufficient. No theorem from a recent manuscript is needed for this
elementary observation.

2. First family: cubic-tail kernel
=================================

For a positive integer a, set

 S_l(a)=sum_{j=0}^{a-1} [(4j+1)^(-l)-(4j+3)^(-l)].

For a,b positive integers define

 K(a^2,b^2)=[b S_3(b)-a S_3(a)]/[6(b^2-a^2)]       if a!=b,
 K(a^2,a^2)=theta-S_4(a)+S_3(a)/(12a).

Proposition A (positive representation; C01).
With A(s)=sech(2pi s) and

 omega(s)=pi^2 s^2 [1+A(s)-2A(s)^3]/96,

 K(x,y)=integral_0^infinity omega(s)/[(x+s^2)(y+s^2)] ds

at the specified squared nodes, and all its ordered square minors are
strictly positive. The zero-node extension has K(0,0)=2theta.

Proposition B (uniform comparison; C02).
For increasing positive integer row nodes a_1,...,a_n and column nodes
b_1,...,b_n, let C_ij=1/(a_i+b_j). Put

 c0=pi^3/192, M=1+2/(3sqrt(6)), tau=1/(sqrt(2)pi).

Then

 [c0/(1+tau)^2]^n det C <= det[K(a_i^2,b_j^2)] <= (M c0)^n det C.

The O(n) error in the logarithm is absolute and uniform in all nodes.
This strength is useful beyond the principal-family obstruction; it also
explains the real-size calculation for later nonprincipal experiments.

Proof structure for A and B.
Derive the representation directly. Apply the determinant integration
identity and the Cauchy product formula: the integrand has a fixed positive
sign. Compare densities, not individual matrix entries. Evaluate

 R_tau(a,b)=[ab+tau(a+b)]/[(a+b)(a+tau)(b+tau)]

by a rank-one update:

 det R_tau = det C
             product_i a_i/(a_i+tau) product_j b_j/(b_j+tau)
             [1+tau(sum_i 1/a_i+sum_j 1/b_j)].

Include its short interpolation proof. The common-zero version can be a
short lemma or appendix; it is not needed to understand the main theorem.

Theorem 1 (all consecutive offsets; C03).
Let Q_{n,m}(X) be the determinant obtained from K on the principal nodes
a_i=m+i, 1<=i<=n, replacing theta by X. Let Z_{n,m}=H_{n,m}Q_{n,m} be
primitive, with H_{n,m}>0. For every sequence of integers m_n>=0,

 liminf_{n->infinity} log Z_{n,m_n}(theta)/n^2 >= 2-2log 2 >0.

Proof outline.
Q is monic, so H is a positive integer. For a general principal node set
with maximum M0, a prime 2M0<p<4M0, p>=5, and

 r_p=#{i:4a_i>p},

the coefficient of X^(n-r_p) has valuation exactly -4r_p. Only one
principal minor survives the scaled reduction. This proves a lower bound
for the actual primitive multiplier, including any coefficient gcd.

For m/n->rho<infinity, the arithmetic lower exponent is

 A(rho)=2(1+rho)^2  for 0<=rho<=1;
 A(rho)=8rho       for rho>=1.

The negative Cauchy exponent is

 D(rho)=[(2rho+2)^2 log(2rho+2)-2(2rho+1)^2 log(2rho+1)
                                +(2rho)^2 log(2rho)]/2,

with 0^2 log 0=0. Since D'(rho)<=4log2<4<=A'(rho) on its pieces,
A-D is minimized at zero and equals 2-2log2 there. The prime number
theorem and the exact Cauchy product supply the two exponents.

Treat m/n->infinity separately: log H>=(8-o(1))mn, whereas the negative
real logarithm is O(n^2[1+log(1+m/n)])=o(mn). A subsequence argument
then proves the stated uniform liminf. Do not silently restrict m=O(n).

Scope.
This theorem concerns the entire determinant of this principal pencil.
Invertible rational row/column changes preserve its primitive polynomial.
Sparse nodes, nonprincipal frames, subspaces, weights, factorizations and
new combinations require their own arguments.

3. Second family: difference-variable Gram determinants
=======================================================

For a polynomial P define

 J(P)=integral_[0,1]^2 P(u,v)log^2(uv)/(sqrt(uv)(1+uv))du dv,
 mu_m=J((u-v)^(2m))/96,
 T_s(j)=sum_{k=0}^{j-1}(-1)^k/(2k+1)^s,
 C_m=binom(2m,m),
 B_m=-C_m T_4(m)+(1/6)sum_{0<=j<=2m,j!=m}binom(2m,j)T_3(j)/(j-m).

Proposition C (C04-C05).
mu_m=C_m theta+B_m. For

 Q_n(X)=det[C_{i+j}X+B_{i+j}]_{0<=i,j<n},

deg Q_n=n, its leading coefficient is 2^(n-1), and Q_n(theta)>0.
Moreover

 log Q_n(theta)=-(log 4)n^2+O(n log n).

Proof outline.
Integrate monomials and pair their off-diagonal terms to cancel beta(3).
Push forward by y=(u-v)^2 to get positive moments on [0,1]. The density
is bounded below by (1-y)/6144. Use explicit Jacobi norms for the lower
determinant bound and Chebyshev trial polynomials for the upper bound.
The central binomial coefficients are the arcsine moments on [0,4]; their
monic integral Chebyshev basis has norms 1,2,2,..., proving the leading
coefficient formula. No assumed period independence enters any step.

Theorem 2 (exact primitive prime exponents and growth; C06-C07).
Let Z_n=kappa_n Q_n be primitive with kappa_n>0 rational. For every prime
p>4n,

 v_p(kappa_n)=3 max(0,2n-2-floor(p/4)).

Consequently

 log Z_n(theta)>=(6-log 4)n^2+o(n^2).

Proof outline.
For p>4n the fourth-order diagonal tails are p-integral. The third-order
tails first acquire p at m0=floor(p/4)+1. Their first scaled residue is
nonzero, and the lower-right residue block is anti-triangular of rank
r=max(0,2n-1-m0). Entries outside that block are p-integral, not merely
zero after the first reduction. This stronger fact bounds every
coefficient valuation below by -3r.

Selecting the entire leading residue block leaves an upper-left pencil
whose leading coefficient is a power of two. Its p-unit coefficient
attains valuation -3r. That proves equality, not just a denominator
estimate. At other odd primes the leading coefficient prevents negative
v_p(kappa_n); at 2 it bounds the loss by n-1. Summing primes in (4n,8n)
gives 6n^2+o(n^2). Combine with Proposition C.

Scope.
This closes the defined principal Gram sequence, including all rational
rescalings of it. It does not close arbitrary weights, sparse frames,
projections or general determinant constructions.

4. Dependency map and proposed organization
===========================================

 Section 1. Introduction: the problem, prior determinant work, precise
            contributions and limits; state the two main theorems.
 Section 2. Primitive normalization and degree-sensitive smallness.
 Section 3. Positive cubic-tail kernel and uniform Cauchy comparison.
 Section 4. The consecutive principal obstruction, including all offsets.
 Section 5. Difference-variable Gram construction and real-size bounds.
 Section 6. Exact local valuations and its primitive-growth obstruction.
 Section 7. Scope: why the results leave other constructions open.
 Appendix. Optional zero-node comparison and finite check instructions.

 C01 -> C02 -> C03 (with exact local coefficient valuations and PNT).
 C04 -> C05; C04 -> C06; C05+C06+PNT -> C07.
 C08 is the elementary normalization convention shared by both proofs.

No computational search is needed as an input to these asymptotic proofs.
The exact scripts test identities and finite valuations, and interval
calculations check particular evaluations. They do not replace the proofs.

Standard mathematical inputs are the prime number theorem, Stirling's
formula, Cauchy determinants and elementary Gram/orthogonal-polynomial
identities. Supply short derivations of the identities actually used.
The note should not call itself elementary in the historical sense or
claim to prove all these background results from first principles.

5. Supplement: preserve breadth without diluting the main paper
==============================================================

Organize the supplement by method, following method-catalog.txt. Each
promoted item gets its definition, exact statement, proof, normalization,
reproducible calculation if applicable, attribution and scope paragraph.
Each unfinished route gets the actual established statements and the
missing lemma. It must not receive a theorem title implying impossibility.

The most substantial candidates beyond the main paper are the repeated
quadratic source obstruction (S03), Euler-Boole theorem (S04), fixed-endpoint
continued fraction theorem (S05), cyclotomic norm theorem (S06), constrained
Mellin positive-source theorem (S09), and monodromy statement (E04).
These need individual source/novelty/proof promotion before publication.
Their proofs are mathematically different enough that adding them all
to the main text would obscure its common mechanism.

Use S01, P06 and P07 to explain the boundary of the main conclusions.
In particular the positive-polynomial cone contains every positive rational
theta-form, and ordinary first variation retains unresolved arithmetic.
Neither can honestly be described as ruled out by the two main theorems.

Include unsuccessful estimates, exact equivalences, finite scans and
superseded claims in the catalog, with stable links to their original
records. Do not reproduce irrelevant chats, token counts or worker logs
as mathematical exposition. The 120 snapshots are a selected evidence
packet, not yet the complete publishable computational supplement.

6. Remaining work before a public manuscript
===========================================

The selection step is complete. The following work belongs to drafting
and checking the paper, not another attempt to prove irrationality:

 - Rewrite C01-C08 into one consistent notation, with every definition
   at first use and citations at the point of mathematical dependence.
 - Reconcile the cubic-tail representation against Carreira's different
   functionals explicitly; avoid claiming the first positive beta(4) kernel.
 - Audit the exact family definitions against any close prior variants.
   Search silence is not a priority certificate; scoped novelty remains
   provisional until that comparison and external scrutiny.
 - Have the assembled proofs checked as a single manuscript. Existing
   agent reviews are model cross-checks, not human or formal verification.
 - Convert the selected checks to a reproducible, version-pinned harness
   running away from the immutable source snapshots. The existing scripts
   write beside themselves; they must not overwrite historical results.
 - Audit each supplemental theorem separately before presenting it as
   an established new result; preserve the original evidence meanwhile.

The initial public version should distinguish the main
paper from its growing supplement and preserve tags/releases. There is no
reason to force this work into the tenth-discriminant version sequence.
