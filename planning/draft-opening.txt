Abstract

We study two explicit positive determinant constructions for the Dirichlet
beta value beta(4). Both produce small nonzero real determinants, but their
primitive integer polynomials have values that grow. For a cubic-tail kernel,
we prove a uniform two-sided comparison with a Cauchy determinant and show
that every sequence of consecutive principal offsets has primitive-value
lower exponent at least 2-2log 2 on the quadratic scale. For a difference-
variable Gram construction, we determine the exact primitive multiplier
valuation at every prime p>4n and obtain the lower exponent 6-log 4. The
arithmetic bounds apply after removal of all common coefficient factors.
They therefore rule out the specified determinant sequences as sources of
small primitive values. We state the restrictions on these conclusions and
separate them from estimates and finite experiments that do not establish
such an obstruction.

1. Introduction

Positive integral representations offer a way to construct nonzero small
quantities involving a mathematical constant. Their Gram determinants can
decay on a quadratic exponential scale, making determinant methods natural
tools in irrationality arguments. The arithmetic normalization is equally
important: a rational polynomial with a small value may cease to be small
when converted to a primitive polynomial with integer coefficients.

The determinant criterion and its relation to positive moments and capacity
are established features of irrationality methods; Zudilin [ZUDILIN2015]
gives a systematic account. Rational modifications of moment functionals
likewise belong to the classical orthogonal-polynomial framework, with an
explicit determinant treatment in Krattenthaler [KRATTENTHALER2021].
Prévost [PREVOST2017] develops positive-weight representations and Padé
approximants for Hurwitz zeta values. These precedents explain why real
smallness and positivity alone are not the contribution of the present note.

More directly, Carreira [CARREIRA2026] investigates determinant constructions
for beta(4), including primitive polynomial calculations and the cost of
their arithmetic normalization. Brown [BROWN2026, Section 7.4] discusses
denominator clearing, common divisors and congruences in integral
determinants. Thus neither the investigation of beta(4) determinants nor the
need to remove full coefficient content is new. Our proposed contribution
is an exact arithmetic analysis of two specified families, strong enough
to prove that their actual primitive values grow. No global irrationality
claim from these recent works is an assumption of our proofs.

The first construction comes from a positive kernel expressed by finite
cubic and quartic tails. We compare all of its ordered minors with explicit
Cauchy determinants, with an error of only O(n) in their logarithms. For
consecutive principal nodes, a unique nonzero minor in a primewise reduction
forces a denominator contribution that exceeds the real decay. The result
holds for every offset schedule, including offsets much larger than the
matrix size.

The second construction takes moments of the difference of two integration
variables. Its coefficient of beta(4) has full rank, and its real determinant
has quadratic decay exponent -log 4. A first nonzero residue followed by an
anti-triangular block determines the exact primitive multiplier at primes
p>4n. Summing these exponents supplies an arithmetic cost of at least 6n^2,
which proves the second obstruction.

These are statements about the families defined below. They do not rule out
other positive weights, nonprincipal designs, different subspaces or new
arithmetic cancellations. In particular a failed sufficient denominator
estimate is weaker than either theorem. The accompanying technical record
keeps such failures and finite observations separate from proved primitive
obstructions.
