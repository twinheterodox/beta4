Main-paper structural and explanatory revision
31 October 2026

Purpose

The reader should understand what Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 establish and why
one would try these constructions before encountering their full matrix
formulas. The intended starting background is rational arithmetic and
linear algebra. The detailed proofs still use calculus, elementary complex
integration, Stirling's formula and the prime number theorem; these inputs
are stated explicitly.

Reading order

The introduction now develops the rational-denominator contradiction,
extends it from linear forms to polynomials using a two-by-two determinant,
and explains primitive normalization with a numerical example. It then
describes each family, states its growth theorem, and interprets the
inequality and its scope in ordinary language. The common proof outline
and exponent table explain the comparison before the technical work.

The first matrix construction has moved from the introduction to Section
3.1, before its integral representation and real estimates. Section 4
treats its arithmetic obstruction. The second construction and moment
coefficients now appear together in Section 5.1; Section 6 treats its
arithmetic obstruction. The paired section titles make this organization
visible. The exact prime-valuation clause formerly in Theorem 1.2 is now
Proposition 6.1, beside its proof. Its content and formula are unchanged.

Section 2 proves why primitive normalization gives the least absolute
value among nonzero rational scalar rescalings with integer coefficients,
and explains how a coefficient valuation forces a prime power. Duplicate
examples and the repeated irrationality criterion were removed. The
matrix-rescaling observation now describes its matrices directly, before
the term matrix pencil is introduced, and explicitly assumes a nonzero
determinant polynomial. Standard terminology such as nodes is retained.
