Outcome
-------
The restricted-grid revision is suitable for the stated audience. Its
motivation, gain and remaining constraints are explained in reading order.
No substantial exposition revision is required. The local precision
suggestion sent during review is now incorporated: 1<=j<q appears in the
first support statement of Section 2. This makes the fraction range explicit
before the later sums introduce it formally. It does not affect any use
in the proof.

Abstract
--------
The abstract uses a liminf and names the beta and Riemann zeta functions. The abstract states the
counting problem, improved result, comparison with earlier work and fixed
initial-set consequence. It contains no construction details or proof
sketch. The ratio 1/sqrt(3) to the FLSZ leading constant matches the proof.
The decimal now uses an approximation sign, so it does not assert an
incorrect truncation. I did not independently rerun its numerical enclosure.

What the new explanation supplies
--------------------------------
The introduction now explains that only odd numerators have nonzero character
weight, that reducing them preserves divisibility of the denominator by four,
and why the resulting cost reduction permits a larger cutoff. This connects
the new numerator to the improved constant before the machinery is defined.
Section 2's q=12 example makes the distinction between contributing and
unused fractions concrete.

The new h_B and A1 discussion is sequential: grid count, partial summation,
uniform bound on denominators, removal of a negligible small-totient part,
then the matching asymptotic. The two inequalities are both visible. A reader
does not have to reconstruct why merely knowing the number of grids also
controls their weighted numerator cost.

Before defining the determinants, the manuscript explains the common
integer/small/nonzero contradiction and why two bases and a variable
determinant size are used. This addresses the requested purpose-before-
machinery improvement without putting a proof sketch into the abstract.

The arithmetic section now explicitly carries the exponent i in the local
valuation comparison. The basis-change paragraph separately identifies
the triangular basis determinant and the integer Gram determinant, making
the normalization readable without silently repairing an ambiguous sentence.

The signed-measure section explains why even reflected masses can coexist
with the opposite residues used in the contradiction. Its new distinction
between the reflection pair and the integer-translation pair is the exact
connection a reader otherwise has to supply. The proof visibly treats both
central-gap boundaries before invoking the abstract criterion.

The asymptotic section states what remains fixed while n grows. It explains
why the potentially large rationality-clearing factor and elimination weights
disappear from the rate, and only then sends s to infinity. The theorem's
non-explicit threshold and its distinction from a dimension bound are clear.
