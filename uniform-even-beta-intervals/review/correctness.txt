Mathematical review of the 18 November 2026 manuscript

The manuscript was compared with the four proof records of 17 November
for the bounded gaps, derivative estimates, explicit thresholds and
finite cover. The following points were checked in the assembled proof.

1. Both fixed families satisfy d < 3A. The same zeros remove the
   initial analytic terms and allow the complementary sum at each
   negative integer evaluation. The extra odd-prime denominator
   factor is H_n^(d-2A). Its rate is 2j in the (9j,20j) family,
   within the margin from nine copies of the original estimate.
2. The centered pole and derivative scales give P^(1356A+d). At a
   selected address the harmonic denominator can consume i+d powers.
   Other addresses retain at least 30A. The fixed rational constant
   includes the factorial d!, which is restored before excluding the
   finitely many exceptional dilation primes.
3. The change of summation order gives y_l-u_a = 2h+1 and the sign
   (-1)^l. Both families have positive zero order at y_l and at
   infinity. The root residues are bounded there. Algebraicity and
   nonpolynomiality give a fixed recurrence and prevent arbitrarily
   long runs of zeros. Only the normalized seeds enter the Frobenius
   congruence. The exponent prime and dilation prime are chosen at
   different stages of the argument.
4. The exceptional-prime bound, Dusart's stated hypothesis, both
   prime bands, the parity rounding and both overlaps were checked.
   The finite tables cover all of the initial range. The explicit
   endpoint 15m and the endpoint (299/20)m+C have different
   effectiveness statements in the abstract and the body.
5. All five certificate outputs reproduced using the bundled programs
   and matched the reference JSON. The companion Laurent routines used
   here agree with the original routines. The 89 seeds split 45 and 44
   between the two families. The 135 bridge residues are nonzero, and
   the intervals join. The second calculation uses 100003 for the
   initial seeds and redoes the bridge one prime at a time.
