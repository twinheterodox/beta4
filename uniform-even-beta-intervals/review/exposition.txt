Exposition review of the 18 November 2026 manuscript

The intended reader knows partial fractions, elementary valuations,
residues and basic facts about algebraic functions. The introduction
starts with a prescribed lower index. It gives the exact theorem,
then compares the asymptotic endpoint with the earlier published
bound in the same odd variable m. The ninth power at q=10 illustrates
why a successful exponent serves a requested interval.

The fixed-power argument separates the number of derivatives from the
kernel power. It explains the origin of the extra denominator cost
before comparing it with decay. The algebraic-function argument first
introduces a fixed residue integrand, then shows why the behavior at
infinity matters for the gaps. The prime argument explains why one
needs a bound on the total size of the exceptional primes. The final
two ranges and their endpoints can be checked directly against the
infinite range on a single line.

The abstract states the results and their significance. Code references
in the body identify the source directory. A short verification
paragraph identifies the version and scope of independent review.
The public bibliography links to primary versions and the project
repository. The paper distinguishes irrationality somewhere in an
interval from nonvanishing of an individual original seed.

The attribution review traced the intermediate beta papers to the
original arguments for reflection, alternating half-integer sums,
differentiation, and the arithmetic use of integer zeros. The revised
proof distinguishes Lai and Sprang's auxiliary-prime criterion from
the later dilation for this kernel. It also cites Stanley at the
passage from an algebraic generating function to a coefficient
recurrence. The precise sources and sections are in sources/inputs.txt.
