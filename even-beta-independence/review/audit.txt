Checks of the argument

1. Half-integer expansion. The continued functions Phi_j and H_j give
   H_j(-1)=0 at odd j and -2^(j+1) beta(j) at even j. The constant
   coordinate in the system is separate from the definition of H_j.
   The finite-tail correction has degree at most 2M-1. Its derivatives
   are removed before any denominator multiplier is applied.

2. Universal derivatives. The row recurrence represents derivatives for
   every solution of the system, not only the solution giving beta values.
   This is necessary for the arbitrary rational initial vector in the
   rank proof. Exact coefficient comparisons support the identities.

3. Local arithmetic. The distinct-product LCM is the positive-index form
   of Fischler's arithmetic device. Its prime valuations give the product
   formula and its asymptotic rate. In the gauged local solution, each
   integration contributes a strictly increasing cumulative degree. Thus
   there are at most d distinct integration denominators, while all series
   coefficients contribute a total power of 4 no larger than 4^k.
   Monotonicity controls products with the integral polynomial row.

4. Estimates. Cauchy's estimate is applied to the normalized fundamental
   solution on the disk of radius 1/2 about -1. The matrix norm is bounded
   independently of the dimension, apart from harmless fixed factors.
   Laurent-coefficient estimates give uniform tail bounds for positive
   and negative real arguments. Normalized monomial derivatives are
   bounded before summation. The gap kappa<omega makes the remaining
   tail summable uniformly in the derivative range. All parameters other
   than n are fixed at this stage.

5. Rank. Taylor corrections are made in z before passing to z=w^2, so the
   same polynomial row is used at i and -i. The enlarged system's binomial
   identities retain the diagonal -1/(2z) term, which the pullback removes.
   The residue descent applies to both kinds of link. It proves the
   independence of the high-order remainders within each local family.
   Homogeneous logarithmic solutions supply the other local families.
   Counting both evaluation points compensates for the doubled polynomial
   degree. The system and its finite pole set are fixed as n grows.
   Since 1<=b<=a, the finitely many possible systems give a uniform
   multiplicity constant when b depends on n.

6. Dimension. After the odd columns vanish, any rational kernel vector
   lifts to one for the unreduced rows with the same constant coordinate.
   The 2026 refined criterion therefore applies. Removing 1 loses at most
   one dimension. First n tends to infinity for fixed a, then a tends to
   infinity. The specified parameters give the limiting coefficient
   0.2174562348..., with strict slack over 0.21. Monotonicity extends the
   conclusion from multiples of 34 to every sufficiently large even s.

Supporting calculation for the periodic-polylogarithm comparison

Mistiaen's notation is L(f,j,z)=sum_(m>=1) f(m) z^m/m^j, initially
for |z|<1. Take the period-2 function f equal to 1 on odd integers and
0 on even integers. At z=i, the convergent series gives
L(f,j,i)=i beta(j) for j>=1. The point i is permitted by the theorem's
domain for period 2. Since beta(j) is real, a relation over Q(i) splits
into two relations over Q, so the two notions of linear independence
coincide for these values. Multiplication by the common factor i does
not change the dimension. This calculation supports the comparison in
the introduction; it is not part of the proof of the new theorem and
is retained here instead of in a paper footnote.
