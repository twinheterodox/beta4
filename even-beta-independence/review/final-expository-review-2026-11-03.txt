Decision

No further substantive exposition changes are recommended. I would end
this exposition pass and use the current draft for human mathematical
review. This judgment is about the manuscript's presentation for its
intended audience; it is not a certification of the theorem.

The intended reader knows linear algebra, power series, complex analysis
and basic linear differential systems, but has not studied Fischler's
construction. The three imported propositions are treated as stated
inputs. The paper remains technically demanding, especially in Section 6,
but now gives that reader the purposes, examples and connections needed
to follow its construction.

Findings

No unresolved substantive exposition findings were identified. The seven
findings in the first human-reader review and the two requested changes
from the second review have been addressed.

The result and its scope are clear at the beginning: the paper proves a
dimension bound for a growing family, does not identify an individual
even beta value as irrational, and does not assert an explicit threshold.
The introductory proof outline identifies the coefficient bound, value
bound and rank condition before the technical construction starts.

The roles of the parameters are now explained when they become useful.
The extra t-derivatives supply families for the rank proof and increase
the beta cutoff. The parameters omega and Omega control exact vanishing
and additional smallness separately. The z-derivatives provide rows and
remove the finite corrections. The matrix product rule makes the common
mechanism behind the small forms and the hypothetical kernel solution
explicit, including the factorial normalization.

The denominator section now gives a concrete reason for distinct factors
before introducing their least common multiple. Its comparison of an
exponent of order a*n with one of order n*log(a) explains why the saving
matters for the theorem. The order of limits is preserved.

The h=1 example now demonstrates both requirements of the construction:
the polynomial row stays fixed, and the new functions satisfy one common
differential system. The displayed derivative cancellation explains the
coefficient j and the preceding component Z_(j-1), including the first
link's source term. The general formulas therefore follow an example
that shows their essential mechanism.

The rank proof distinguishes independence of solution vectors from
independence of scalar remainders and states why the residue argument is
needed. Its six-point overview identifies each contribution, including
the reduction of the degree factor supplied by the infinity family.
The final table and count agree with that overview.

The revised Laplace passage now shows why Taylor coefficients of G at
zero correspond to Laurent coefficients of F_n at infinity. The finite
asymptotic expansion is justified with n and the truncation order fixed,
and the nonzero linear term of exp(-u)-1 explains the transfer of the
vanishing order to z=1. The offset from omega*n to omega*n-1 is consistent.

The square-root mechanism is explicit in the introduction and the final
parameter choice: the quadratic coefficient cost Omega^2/a is balanced
against log(a), giving Omega on the sqrt(a log(a)) scale; division by
the logarithmic coefficient-growth rate gives sqrt(a/log(a)). The beta
cutoff is proportional to a. The numerical choices follow the derivative
and rank constraints, with the exact rank slope 1251/1250 displayed.
The optional extra square-root display suggested in the second review
is not needed to complete the exposition pass.
