Mathematical audit of the powered-kernel result
11 November 2026

I found no substantive mathematical error or gap in the current proof.
Both assertions of Theorem 1 pass this audit:

  - At least one of beta(6), beta(8), ..., beta(74) is irrational.
  - At least one of beta(8), beta(10), ..., beta(110) is irrational.

The proof's general power criterion is also valid with its stated
nonzero-seed hypothesis. The manuscript verifies that hypothesis for
q = 1, 2, 3; it does not establish it for every q. The two asserted
irrational members need not be distinct.

This audit combines a check of the general arguments with independently
written exact calculations. In the preceding preliminary review I had
inspected and replayed the supplied checkers. The calculations for this
audit were written afresh and did not run or import those checkers.
This is therefore a fresh mathematical audit and computational

1. Construction and target beta values

The block endpoints are integral when n is a multiple of 480. The
numerator and denominator counts give

  3 #Lambda_n = 10n + 6,
  sum_j B_j(n) = (279/10)n + 35,
  deg R_n = -(179/10)n - 28.

The reflection identity is correct, including the sign contributed by
the central linear factor. Keeping the nominal central pole order and
allowing its top coefficient to vanish is legitimate.

Raising R_n to the qth power gives zeros of order 3q. Differentiating
2q times therefore preserves the integer zeros needed both to remove
the initial summands and to control the rational constant. The
partial-fraction expansion and alternating summation are valid; all
the differentiated series converge absolutely. Reflection cancels the
odd beta indices. The remaining indices run from 2q+2 to the largest
even integer not exceeding 37q. Thus q = 2 and q = 3 give precisely
the two claimed intervals, containing 35 and 52 values respectively.

2. Denominator transfer

The essential convolution argument is valid for the full local series:
if its degree-h coefficient has valuation at least nu-lambda*h, its
qth power has valuation at least q*nu-lambda*h. The slope does not
acquire an extra factor q. Combining this with the harmonic denominator
of exponent i+2q produces exactly q times the original valuation cost.

I checked all four prime ranges. For small odd primes, the required
whole-series estimate follows from the local-factor argument in
Krisman's Lemma 4.3, underlying Corollary 4.4. At 2, the harmonic
denominators are odd and the displayed power of 2 introduces no loss.
For odd primes greater than n, the grouped cancellation at the negative
integer zeros supplies the integrality of the rational constant: a
prime cannot divide denominators in both complementary groups because
their pole addresses differ by less than that prime.

Some E_p(n) can be negative. This causes no problem: the multiplier
D_n is a positive rational number, and the proof establishes the
integrality of the resulting coefficients, which is the property the
contradiction needs. It does not require D_n itself to be an integer.

3. Inherited arithmetic cost and real decay

I read and checked the relevant arguments in Sections 2--6 of the
pinned Krisman paper, including the passage from the prime-counting
certificate to the bound on log D_n. The source files used were:

  Krisman, Hexagon 2610.00285v2 (PDF)
  Text extracted from that PDF

The finite arithmetic partition accounts for the discontinuities of
the defining counts. The treatment of its endpoints and small primes
has sublinear logarithmic cost; the tail estimate and the
prime-number-theorem passage give the stated constant A.

The real product estimate is uniform over t >= rn+1, including the
unbounded tail. In particular, the residual logarithmic terms in
Krisman's Proposition 6.4 admit the stated uniform bound. The phase
and tail bounds used by the manuscript are valid. The logarithmic derivative
estimate introduces only a polynomial factor for each fixed q, and
the separate tail sum converges with the stated bound. All parameters
other than n are fixed when n tends to infinity.

Fresh exact arithmetic gives

  A = 12507029707866288048422972493354331
      / 790300921079786767495600904601600,

  Q = -744/25 + A + 20 log 2
    = -0.07140140819199213756... < -0.0714.

Consequently |D_n^q L_{q,n}| <= exp(qnQ + o(n)) for every fixed q.
Taking a power preserves the strict margin between denominator cost
and real decay.

4. Prime dilation and nonvanishing

The change of variables and its normalization are correct:

  R_n(t) = 2^(Gn+29) F_n(2t+n+1),
  L_{q,n} = 2^[q(Gn+29)+2q] ell_{q,n}.

At n = 480p, the selected numerator zeros reduce to the trimmed seed
with endpoints +/-1599, not the untrimmed endpoints +/-1601. The
selected-factor counts give A_0 = 8626. The unselected numerator and
denominator units give the sign -1 in the rational-function congruence.

The coefficient extraction has the necessary local-series
justification. After removing the selected pole and applying the
normalization, the series lies in Z_(p)[[z]]; its remaining denominator
factors have unit constant terms. Reduction therefore commutes with
coefficient extraction, giving the stated scaling p^(qA_0-i).

The bound for nonselected coefficients is also correct. Combined with
the harmonic denominators and tau_q = 8628q, it leaves positive
valuation q(S-1) in the nonselected constant contributions. At selected
poles, the surviving odd harmonic denominators supply precisely
chi_4(p). Thus the constant congruence has the stated factor
(-1)^q chi_4(p), while every scaled beta coefficient vanishes modulo p.

Under joint rationality, exclude the finitely many primes dividing the
fixed common denominator or the numerator or denominator of the
nonzero seed constant. The congruence then makes the forms nonzero for
every sufficiently large remaining prime. Together with integrality
and decay, this yields infinitely many nonzero integers tending to
zero. The argument does not require an irrationality claim for
Catalan's constant or Krisman's own nonvanishing argument.

5. Fresh finite calculations

For the actual n = 480 seed, I constructed its full numerator and
denominator polynomials over each finite field. Their degrees are
4801 and 13427. I Taylor-shifted these global polynomials at every
pole and recovered the local coefficients by series inversion. This
uses neither the reflection shortcut nor the supplied logarithmic-
derivative/factorial algorithm. Reflection was checked afterwards.

The independently reconstructed seed constants are:

  q                 modulo 10007       modulo 10009
  1 (control)              6332               9058
  2                        3182               5359
  3                        3335               6971

Both moduli were checked prime by trial division. All five residues
reported in the manuscript agree; q = 1 modulo 10009 is an additional
control calculation. All 191 even-beta coefficients in the five
supplied records also agree. There were 161,124 coefficient reflection
checks and 18 full rational-function reconstruction checks over these
fields. These residues prove the required rational seed constants
nonzero, because every inverted denominator is a unit at the chosen
prime.

For the arithmetic budget, a separate evaluator directly counted the
defining closed intervals and odd integers at every discontinuity in
the auxiliary variable and in every intervening open cell. This
differs from the supplied accumulated-jump algorithm. It independently
reconstructed all 62,132 event points and 62,131 interval maxima,
including the exact upward-rounded sum

  1243345293752876677540040193981

at scale 2^96. The resulting constant A agrees exactly. Rational
logarithm enclosures, using 70 terms of the elementary atanh series,
verified the curvature, tangent, monotonicity and tail inequalities
and the strict bound on Q.

For rational dilation tests, I used full integer polynomials, Hasse
derivatives, explicit rational long division and Cauchy products.
These tests covered q = 1, 2, 3 in four dilation geometries, including
uniform and nonuniform blocks and both odd residue classes modulo 4.
The additional seed n = 6, rn = 7 and prime 29 gives n = 174 and uses
the manuscript's ratio r = 7/6. Every test passed:

mathematical and computational review, not formal verification or a

the cited Lean project. The separate determinant pilot and other
