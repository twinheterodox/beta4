Verdict

I found no mathematical error or gap affecting Theorem 1.1, which asserts
that at least one of beta(4), beta(6), ..., beta(32) is irrational.
I checked the complete argument in Sections 1--5 and Appendix A and
independently reproduced the finite arithmetic, phase and nonzero-seed
inputs. The proof provides both decay after clearing coefficients and
nonvanishing along infinitely many prime-dilated indices under the joint
rationality hypothesis.

The conservative cleared-form upper exponent is

  41/5 + 15 log(2) - 18.83
    = -0.2327922916008203587415181781273514788...

Thus the quantitative comparison has a substantial strict margin.

1. General mathematical arguments checked

Construction and support

The nested blocks have gamma*n+31 denominator factors, with
gamma=341/16. The numerator has 15*n/2+6 zero factors including
multiplicity, and one additional linear factor. Consequently
G=221/16 and deg R_n=-G*n-24. Reflection gives R_n(-n-1-t)=R_n(t)
for every positive multiple of 96. It induces a_(i,n-a)=(-1)^i a_(i,a).
The alternating sum therefore retains only even i. Twice differentiating
leaves exactly the candidate set beta(4), ..., beta(32). I checked the
factors of two, i(i+1), finite harmonic corrections and absolute
convergence of the beta expansion.

The isolated-pole estimate

I checked the local-series estimate v_p(a_(i,a)) >= i-F_p(a), including
the zero constant term of the central prefactor. For p^2>7n every
nonzero divisible integer factor involved has valuation exactly one.
An isolated address has p-unit differences from every other pole, so
its entire principal part is p-integral for every odd p.

Regrouping the rational constant at the integer zeros gives the two
complementary sums in (3.2). For each evaluation only one address class
has p-divisible denominators. A singleton can be avoided by taking the
opposite side, and every remaining coefficient has the required bound.
For a repeated class, the valuation loss i+2 in evaluation cancels
the i in the local coefficient bound. This proves the stated exponent
max(0,M_p+2). The same argument gives integrality for p>n, including
evaluation denominators of higher valuation.

Small primes and dyadic cost

I checked the definitions of nu_p, the common Taylor-degree losses and
the exponents in Appendix A. The negative and positive odd numerator
products each start at 1, permitting their expression as (2A)!/(2^A A!).
The Legendre bounds give the displayed 128(lambda_p+1) small odd-prime
cost and 15n+36+107(lambda_2+1) dyadic cost after subtracting G v_p(n!).
These contribute o(n) and at most 15n log(2)+o(n), respectively.

Arithmetic envelope and passage from primes

I checked the closed-interval count N_j, the half-shift in C, and the
fact that C omits the two endpoint zeros and the prefactor. Those
omissions only increase the envelope. Repeated classes have y<=x-1
when 1<x<2. Every class is repeated for x>=2.

On each open interval between affine-offset collisions and integer
events, the residue-cell ordering and all floor counts are constant.
At a non-event x only one offset jumps at an interior cell boundary.
Its exact value agrees with a neighboring cell. At 0 and x-1 the
outer pole block has the indicated one-sided value. Thus the cell
maxima include the closed-boundary maxima. Event values of n/p
contribute O(log n) for the fixed finite partition.

The bound e(x)<=36+G*{x} follows from the interval lengths. The
positive-part tail integral is at most 36/X+G/(2(X-1)). PNT on each
finite partition, followed by the Chebyshev bound beyond a fixed M
and then M tending to infinity, proves the claimed asymptotic prime
sum. Negative body contributions are retained with their correct sign.

Real estimate

I checked the gamma product and the uniform Stirling estimate down to
t-rn=1. Its O(log(n+t)) error becomes only a fixed polynomial factor.
The logarithmic derivative and its derivative give the uniform
O((1+log n)^2) cost for R_n''. The critical polynomial has exactly
one positive root by its coefficient signs. The tail inequality
x Phi'(x)<=-383/64 for x>=3 suffices to sum the infinite tail while
absorbing the polynomial factor. These estimates give the printed
uniform bound for L_n, rather than a finite-index extrapolation.

Prime dilation and final contradiction

I checked R_n(t)=2^(Gn+25) F_n(2t+n+1) and the two further powers
of two from differentiation. Selecting p-divisible zero factors gives
the trimmed seed with odd roots through 239, rather than through 241.
There are 3L selected zero factors, W+31 selected denominator factors
and one selected prefactor. Thus A_0=1356 and tau=1358.

Wilson's theorem gives the stated combined unit -1. Coefficient
extraction after pole removal is legitimate in the local integral
power series. A nonzero address class contains W weighted poles and
at least L numerator roots before cubing. Its resulting bound is
v_p(c_(Np;a,i))>=-GN+i. After scaling, the beta coefficients vanish
modulo p. Nonselected rational-constant terms have valuation at least
30. The selected harmonic denominators give the character chi_4(p),
yielding p^tau q_(Np)=-chi_4(p)*q_tilde modulo p.

The seed residues below prove q_tilde is a nonzero rational number.
Under joint rationality of the fifteen beta values, excluding the
finite set of denominator and seed-numerator primes supplies infinitely
many nonzero L_(Np). Combining this with integrality and decay of
T D_(Np) L_(Np) gives the stated contradiction.

2. Independently reproduced finite inputs

Fresh programs in this scratch directory import none of the supplied
checkers. Supplied results are compared only after the calculations.

Arithmetic certificate

independent_arithmetic.py enumerates affine crossings by reducing their
numerators to arithmetic progressions. independent_floor.cpp evaluates
the closed boundary points and all open residue cells directly using
integer floor and ceiling formulas. This gives 38,372 event points and
38,371 intervals, agreeing with every supplied midpoint and maximum.
There are 23 cells with negative envelope value. Upward rounding at
96 bits gives the total bound

  3203914250644786841689526785574147 /
  395150460539893383747800452300800
    = 8.108086844356158... < 41/5.

The independently calculated bound agrees with the manuscript's
64-bit upward-rounded bound to the displayed precision.

Phase certificate

independent_phase.py constructs the derivative numerator directly from
the rational parameters. It checks degree 33, positive coefficients
in degrees 0,1,2 and negative coefficients in degrees 3 through 33.
It verifies the printed root bracket and bisects it further. 512-bit
Arb arithmetic gives

  max Phi = -18.8351123668081936003718677400690 +/- 7.31e-32,

which is strictly below the printed rational upper bound
-18835112315041/10^12 and the simpler bound -18.83.
The full independent arithmetic and phase enclosures together yield
a cleared exponent at most -0.3298178140528555490509126132735.
The manuscript uses the weaker conservative exponent above.

Seed certificate

independent_seed.py constructs the complete seed numerator and
denominator over each finite field. For each pole it uses a polynomial
Bezout inverse modulo the power of its pole factor, followed by
binomial translation of the polynomial residue. This provides a third
method alongside the supplied product and logarithmic-derivative
calculations. It reproduces all 2,077 coefficients at both primes,
all odd-index cancellations and the constant residues

  q_tilde = 3738 mod 10007,
  q_tilde = 5333 mod 10009.

Both moduli are checked prime by trial division.

Local arithmetic spot checks

independent_local_bounds.py also verifies the exact small-prime cost
bounds in 56 cases and the large-prime residue/envelope correspondence
in 222 cases at n=96,192,384,768. It checks 32,722 repeated residue
classes, including the half-shift and omitted endpoints in C.
These finite checks supplement the general proofs above.

Packaged checks

The complete supplied runner, including --with-product, passed in a
scratch copy. Its report is replay/checks/check-report.json. This replay
is additional to the independently written calculations.

3. Mathematical errors and exposition

No mathematical errors or gaps were found affecting the conclusion.

Two optional exposition improvements would make the prime-dilation and
small-prime counts quicker for a human reader to verify.

At Section 5.2, explicitly state that a block [p a,p b] contains b-a
representatives of every nonzero residue and b-a+1 representatives of
zero. Summing gives W and W+31. This exposes the source of the
selected/nonselected valuation separation.

In Appendix A, give the two odd-product lengths explicitly as
A_minus=(1+r)n-a+1 and A_plus=a+rn+1. This makes the double-factorial
estimate and its six logarithmic error terms immediately visible.

4. Accepted inputs and scope

Accepted classical inputs are partial fractions, elementary local
power-series arithmetic, Legendre's formula, Wilson's and Fermat's
theorems, Descartes' rule of signs, uniform real Stirling estimates,
the prime number theorem, the Chebyshev bound and ordinary convergence
estimates. Computational results use exact integer/finite-field
arithmetic and outward-rounded Arb interval arithmetic.

The quantitative lemmas needed for the theorem were checked as proved
in this manuscript. Krisman's cached v2 PDF was checked for the claimed
seventeen-value comparison and construction context. Full literature
dependency audits, priority claims, discovery-session independence and
external Lean formalizations are outside this audit's scope. The
verification method is mathematical reasoning with independently
implemented finite computations.
