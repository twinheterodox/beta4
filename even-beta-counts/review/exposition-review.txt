EXPOSITION REVIEW FOR HUMAN READERS
4 November 2026

Applied mathematical-exposition.txt before drafting and during the
reading-order review. Intended readers have standard undergraduate
algebra and analysis, including residues, elementary prime valuations
and asymptotic estimates. The arithmetic arguments are supplied in full.

1. Abstract and opening.
   The abstract states the bound, its range and the finite refinements.
   It contains no proof sketch, sequence of construction steps or
   intermediate technical formulas. The introduction defines beta and
   I(s) before the main theorem. The first examples explain how the
   floor gives counts two and three; the sharper table records genuinely
   stronger finite information. There is no deletion corollary.

2. The purpose of the sequences.
   The introduction states the contradiction assumption first. A fixed
   integer combination of the B forms at every index produces one
   sequence of rational numbers; clearing denominators produces integers
   tending to zero. The need for infinitely many nonzero terms is explicit.
   The three-form example shows how the same weights cancel the same
   two coefficients at every index.

3. Choices in the rational-function construction.
   The text explains the spacing four through the alternating signs on
   odd integers. It derives the zero-block length 2r+4 from reflection
   about the midpoint of the pole interval. The role of the numerator
   normalization is stated before its formula and proved in the
   progression lemma. The alternating character is introduced only when
   it shortens the actual sums.

4. Why the polynomial multipliers have this form.
   The target seed vector appears before its construction. A two-grid
   example gives the kernels, their common denominator and their integer
   numerators. The text explains that these numerators create products
   against a shared positive weight. It then explains the negative square
   in h_b(-(t+2)^2), the odd factor t+2 and the homogeneous lift to all n.
   The unique-pole argument gives a short reason for independence.

5. Arithmetic and decay.
   The progression lemma explains what is lost when a Taylor coefficient
   selects k variable terms. The constant-term proof isolates the only
   pole group that could have an exceptional denominator. A concrete
   identity at t=-7/3 shows why the group, rather than each term, is
   integral at 5. The decay section separates the bounded range from
   the infinite tail and explains why fixed polynomial degree leaves
   the exponential rate unchanged.

6. Prime dilation.
   The purpose is stated before the valuation calculation: the constant
   must survive while the beta coefficients vanish. The example modulo
   5 explains the exchange of the one-third and two-thirds classes.
   The local power-series statement precedes coefficient extraction.
   The change from exponent s to s-E is retained and explained.

7. From a count parameter to an endpoint bound.
   The finite family is introduced as an intermediate theorem. The
   inversion section explains where the square root comes from and
   why the logarithm of B^2 supplies the factor 2 in the constant.
   Right endpoints of the constant-count intervals are tested because
   the comparison function increases. The rounded test uses k^2,
   whereas the unrounded test uses (k-1)^2; the reason is explicit.

8. Presentation and scope.
   All subsections are numbered. There is no manual title line break.
   The title font was adjusted to keep the full descriptive title on
   one line. Appendix prose was shortened to keep the references
   together, without removing any proof step or finite comparison.
   The original three-through-364 manuscript remains separate and
   unchanged. The new manuscript does not inherit its independent audit.
