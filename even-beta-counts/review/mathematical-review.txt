MATHEMATICAL SELF-REVIEW OF THE NEW COUNT PAPER
4 November 2026

The manuscript was checked against the uniform-family proof and the
two explicit endpoint deductions. This is a developing-assistant
self-review, separate from the exact calculations and visual review.
The earlier independent audits cover the earlier papers only.

1. Construction and parity.
   The fraction classes are reduced and represented once, with the
   integer class represented by 1. The extra endpoint factor gives
   simple, reflection-symmetric roots. For odd n, the denominator is
   invariant and the base rational function is odd under reflection.
   The polynomial multiplier is also odd, so the resulting function
   is invariant and its odd beta coefficients vanish.

2. Uniform rank.
   The residue formula has the sign (-1)^(i-1) required by the shifted
   constant. The paired kernels give the stated g_b. Their integer
   numerators are independent by the unique smallest pole distance
   of the largest participating grid. N<=2D gives E=2N-1<s. The
   midpoint substitution and clockwise contour give the common sign
   sigma=(-1)^(DL/2+1). Kernel poles cancel against numerator zeros;
   the arc integral vanishes. The positive weight and polynomial
   independence prove strict definiteness for every B. Rational
   inversion then supplies fixed integer multiplier coefficients.

3. Denominators.
   The progression argument bounds every coefficient term at each
   prime-power level. Primes dividing the reduced denominator are
   covered separately by the extra normalization. The factor 4^(Sn)
   cancels exactly in the local expansion. The constant-term proof
   uses a numerator zero and at most one exceptional pole group at a
   prime; it does not assert termwise integrality in the finite sums.

4. Decay.
   The n log n and linear Stirling contributions cancel in the profile.
   Its derivative has the stated unique-zero behavior. A uniform
   estimate down to the first possible nonzero grid point controls
   the bounded range. The tail includes the polynomial degree and
   tends to zero uniformly as its cutoff grows. The two exact
   parameter inequalities imply decay after multiplication by delta^S.

5. Prime dilation.
   Selected numerator factors permute all reduced fraction classes.
   The three unit signs cancel for odd seed indices. Homogeneity
   changes the scaling exponent to tau=s-E>0. Selected-pole extraction
   is justified in the local power-series ring; nonselected poles
   satisfy the stronger valuation bound before scaling. Since finite
   denominators are below p^2, precisely the selected h=py,j=pk terms
   survive in the constant. The factor chi_4(p)(m+1)^(-S) is retained.

6. Contradiction.
   The generalized Vandermonde minor is nonzero at positive distinct
   points, so the last cancellation weight cannot vanish. The seed
   vector eta e_B therefore gives a nonzero seed constant for every
   possible exceptional set. Remaining rational beta values have one
   fixed common denominator. Infinitely many primes give nonzero
   terms in the integer sequence tending to zero.

7. Finite endpoints and inversion.
   The closed endpoint satisfies both criterion inequalities uniformly
   in B, including B=1. The ten smaller parameter choices match the
   exact construction output. The elementary totient bound includes
   both the floor error and the tail of the Mobius series. Ceiling
   errors are handled at s_k-2. The infinite-range comparison is
   reduced to one decreasing and one increasing function. The 230
   finite unrounded checks and the 23 rounded checks use different
   powers, (k-1)^2 and k^2, as required. Their intervals cover every
   even s>=160 with no missing transition endpoint.

8. Checks and source scope.
   Both standalone checkers passed after being assembled in the new
   paper directory. Construction checks include all ten seed matrices
   over Q and full small-index coefficient and constant congruences.
   Endpoint comparisons use explicit rational logarithm remainders.
   Decimals are not used as certificates. Source papers provide
   context and attribution; no external paper proposition is an input
   to the proof. The cited Kumar-Tosi result is identified as their
   stated asymptotic bound, not as a finite consequence used here.

No substantive mathematical change to the underlying proof or its
two finite deductions was needed during assembly. This review and
the finite computations are not formal verification or an independent
mathematical audit.
