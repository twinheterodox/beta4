ARCSINE CONTACT, THE ODD BOUNDARY, AND PRIME-LAYER OBSTRUCTIONS
28 October 2026. Local research record; not a publication.

Status
------
This investigation follows next-proof-avenues.txt. It proves an explicit
dilogarithm companion for the arcsine parameter family, a formula for its
odd boundary period, and two prime-layer congruences. It also constructs
five variants of exact beta(4)-only determinants. All 202 recorded finite
tests give primitive integer polynomials whose absolute values at beta(4)
are greater than one. None supplies an irrationality proof.

The odd-period formula was already observed numerically by Carreira [C,
Section 6]. The proof below supplies a derivation within this investigation;
no priority claim is made. The deformed measure specializes to Carreira's
weighted arcsine measure, and the full-parity polynomial rows are the
Chebyshev rows of the OpenAI Catalan construction [O]. Their contributions
are identified where used. The exact checks here are additional checks by
the same assistant, not independent mathematical or formal verification.

1. The parameter family and all boundary values
----------------------------------------------
Put G=beta(2), B=beta(4), Z=pi^2/4. For real |a|<1 set

 dmu_a(t) = |t|/sqrt(1-t^2)
             *cos(a arccos|t|)/cos(pi a/2) dt,   -1<t<1,
 m_v(a) = integral t^v dmu_a(t),
 M_a(i,j) = integral_{-1}^1 integral_0^1
                      t^i s^j/(1-ts) ds dmu_a(t).

All these integrals converge. Locally uniformly in a near zero, the
parameter factor and its derivatives are bounded; after integrating s,
the endpoint majorant is a constant times
(-log(1-|t|))/sqrt(1-t^2). This justifies the Taylor expansions used here.
Write M_a=M_0+a^2 M_1+O(a^4), and likewise for m_v.

With c_k=4^(-k) binom(2k,k), direct integration by parts gives

 m_(2k)(a) = 2(2k+1)! / product_{r=0}^k ((2r+1)^2-a^2),
 m_(2k+1)(a)=0,
 m_(2k),0 = 2/((2k+1)c_k),
 m_(2k),1 = m_(2k),0 sum_{r=0}^k 1/(2r+1)^2.                 (1)

For example, after t=cos u the moment integral is
2/cos(pi a/2) integral_0^(pi/2) cos^(2k+1)(u) cos(au) du.
Its initial value is 2/(1-a^2), and its recurrence is
((2k+1)^2-a^2)m_(2k)=2k(2k+1)m_(2k-2).

Let K_d^-(a)=M_a(d,0), K_d^+(a)=M_a(0,d), and
X(a)=sum_{v>=0}m_v(a)/(v+1)^2. Write X3=X(0). The boundary values are

 K_0^-=K_0^+=4 sum_{r>=1, r odd} chi_4(r)/(r^2-a^2),
 K_1^- = ((1+a^2)m_0(a)-a^2 K_0)/(1-a^2),
 K_1^+ = Z+a^2 Y+O(a^4),
 K_2^+ = 2+a^2 X(a).                                        (2)

To obtain K_0, set t=cos u and expand
log((1+cos u)/(1-cos u))=4 sum_{r odd}cos(ru)/r. The integral of
cos(au)cos(ru) on [0,pi/2] is
chi_4(r) cos(pi a/2) r/(r^2-a^2). Abel damping justifies the Fourier
calculation at the integrable logarithmic endpoint. For K_1^- use

 m_(v-2)(a)-m_v(a)
     = ((v+1-a^2)/(v(v+1)))m_v(a),  v even >=2,

and sum the telescoping differences. The formula for K_2^+ follows by
summing the moment recurrence after division by (v+1)^2, retaining the
initial term (1-a^2)m_0=2.

The full boundary recurrences, with the initial values (2), are

 (d^2-a^2)K_d^- = d(d-1)K_(d-2)^-
       +d(m_(d-2)+m_(d-1))
       -a^2(K_0-sum_{v<d}m_v/(v+1)),                  d>=2,

 (d-1)(d-2)K_d^+ = ((d-2)^2-a^2)K_(d-2)^+
       +a^2 K_0+2(d-2)/(d-1),                         d>=3. (3)

These follow by inserting the moment recurrence in the absolutely
convergent defining sums and partial fractions. For the first, add
a^2 sum_{v>=d}m_v/(v+1) to its left side after moving the K_(d-2)
term there. The coefficient of m_v simplifies to
d(v+1-a^2)/(v(v+1)); hence the sum telescopes to
d sum_{v>=d}(m_(v-2)-m_v)=d(m_(d-2)+m_(d-1)).
For the second put j=d-2 and sum the moment recurrence, including
its initial forcing 2, with multiplier j/((v+1)(v+j+1)). After the
shift by two, the coefficient is
j(j+1)/(v+j+3)-(j^2-a^2)/(v+j+1)-a^2/(v+1),
and the initial term is 2j/(j+1), as required.
The a^2 coefficient also agrees with the independent recurrences of
[C, Lemma nurec], under M_nu=-2M_1.

All entries reduce to a boundary value by

 M_a(i,j)=K_(i-j)^- - sum_{k=1}^j m_(i-j+k-1)(a)/k,   i>=j,
 M_a(i,j)=K_(j-i)^+ - sum_{k=1}^i m_(k-1)(a)/(j-i+k),  i<j. (4)

Consequently M_1(i,j) has an explicit expression in the rational span
of 1,G,B,Z,X3,Y. This is an expression with named coefficients; no
linear independence of these constants is assumed. For even i and j,
Z and Y do not occur. Discarding odd indices is the simplification used
in [C, Section 6], but the full list will be useful below.

The Taylor density is particularly simple. If theta=arcsin|t|, then

 dmu_1(t)=h(t)dmu_0(t),  h(t)=(pi theta-theta^2)/2,
                   0<=h(t)<=pi^2/8<sqrt(2).                 (5)

2. A universal dilogarithm companion
-----------------------------------
Let g_d denote the coefficient of 4G in K_d^-,1. The recurrences give

 g_0=0, g_1=-1,
 g_(2k)=(2k-1)/(2k)g_(2k-2)+(c_k-1)/(4k^2),
 g_d=(d-1)/d g_(d-2)-1/d^2,                       d odd >=3.

For f(z)=sqrt(1-z^2) and w(z)=z/(1+f(z)), their generating function is

                   sum_{d>=0} g_d z^d = -2 Li_2(w(z))/f(z). (6)

In particular its even part is -Li_2(w(z)^2)/(2f(z)). This identity
provides an explicit global companion rather than a new triangular
solve for every chosen column range.

Here is a derivation. Write theta_z=z d/dz. For the even generating
function g_e the recurrence gives

 [theta_z^2-z^2(theta_z+2)(theta_z+1)]g_e
                        =1/f-1/(1-z^2).

The operator factors as theta_z[(1-z^2)theta_z-z^2]. Integrating once,
using the zero constant term, and then changing to w gives
d(f g_e)=log(1-w^2) dw/w. Thus f g_e=-Li_2(w^2)/2.
For the odd part, the forcing is -z/(1-z^2); the first integral is
-atanh z. Since atanh z=2 atanh w, the second integral gives
f g_o=-(Li_2(w)-Li_2(-w)). The even and odd parts combine to (6).

For an even polynomial P(t) put

 Q(t) = [ P(t) Li_2(w(1/t)^2)/2 ]_(nonnegative powers of t). (7)

Then M_1(P,2j)+M_0(Q,2j) has no contribution from the lower-boundary
G coefficients. Indeed the G coefficient from P is the convolution
with 4g_(2k); the coefficient from Q is the convolution with 4c_k.
Equation (6) says exactly that these convolutions cancel.
If every exponent in P is at least every column index in use, there
are no forward G or X3 contributions, and each entry is in Q+Q B.

For unrestricted P, let x_k and u_k be the forward coefficients of
X3 and G in M_1(0,2k), respectively. Then

 x_1=1, x_k=(2k-2)/(2k-1)x_(k-1),
 u_1=0, u_k=(2k-2)/(2k-1)u_(k-1)+4/((2k-1)(2k-2)).

Equivalently x_(k+1)=m_(2k),0/2 and
u_k/x_k=2 sum_{l=1}^{k-1}c_l/l. These coefficients permit a simultaneous
contact construction: require both forward convolutions to vanish on
the chosen column band and then use (7) for the lower contribution.
The experiment solves these exact linear systems without using decimal
digits of B. No uniform normality or nonvanishing theorem is proved.

There is also an integral description of the two forward sequences.
For lambda_k=x_(k+1), delta_k=4 log(2)lambda_k-u_(k+1),

 lambda_k = integral_0^1 x^k/(2 sqrt(1-x)) dx,
 delta_k = integral_0^1 2x^k atanh(sqrt(1-x))/sqrt(1-x) dx.    (8)

After x=1-s^2 the second integral is
4 integral_0^1 (1-s^2)^k atanh(s) ds. Its initial value is 4log(2).
For k>=1, integrate the derivative of s(1-s^2)^k atanh(s) to get
delta_k=2k/(2k+1)delta_(k-1)-2/(k(2k+1)), which proves (8).
Thus this contact problem can be phrased using two explicit weights.
That observation alone does not prove an appropriate orthogonality or
arithmetic theorem.

3. The odd boundary is a quadratic expression in lower periods
--------------------------------------------------------------
The constant in (2) satisfies

                          Y=Z^2-4G^2=pi^4/16-4G^2.          (9)

[C, Section 6] already records the equivalent combination
G^2-pi^4/64 as a numerical observation. The following proof is useful
because it makes the extra constant in a full-parity construction
available without an additional unproved period identity.

Set b_n=1/(2n^2 c_n), O_n=sum_{r=1}^n(2r-1)^(-2), and define

 A(x)=(arcsin x)^2=sum_{n>=1}b_n x^(2n),
 V(x)=sum_{n>=1}b_n O_n x^(2n),
 H(x)=integral_0^x arcsin(t)/(t sqrt(1-t^2)) dt.

Equation (1) gives Y=V(1); also H(1)=2G. The last identity follows
from t=sin theta and integration by parts using
integral_0^(pi/4) log(tan u) du=-G.

The rational coefficient recurrence gives the differential identity

                       (1-x^2)V''-xV'=2H(x)/x.              (10)

For completeness, at x^(2n-2) its two sides are
2n(2n-1)b_n O_n-4(n-1)^2 b_(n-1)O_(n-1)
and 2n b_n/(2n-1). This follows from
O_n-O_(n-1)=(2n-1)^(-2) and the recurrence for b_n.

Now compose V and H with x=sin theta. Dots denote theta derivatives.
Equation (10) and the definition of H imply

 V-double-dot=2H/sin theta,   H-dot=theta/sin theta.

Both sides of d/dtheta(theta V-dot-V)=d/dtheta(H^2) agree and vanish
at theta=0, so

                          theta V-dot-V=H^2.               (11)

To justify the endpoint derivative, O_n-pi^2/8=O(1/n) and
b_n=O(n^(-3/2)) by the central-binomial estimate. Hence the derivative
in x of V-(pi^2/8)A has an absolutely convergent series at x=1.
Multiplication by cos theta makes this derivative tend to zero as
theta tends to pi/2. Therefore V-dot(pi/2)=pi^3/8. Substitution in
(11) gives (9). There is no numerical identification step in this proof.

4. Two exact large-prime layers
-------------------------------
In this section index i or j means the even actual index 2i or 2j.
Fix H>=1 and an odd prime p>H, with 0<=i,j<=H. Write R_e(i,j) for
the rational coefficient of M_e(2i,2j) in the expressions (3)--(4).
Let epsilon=(-1)^((p-1)/2). Then

 p^4 R_1(i,j) is p-integral, and modulo p it is

   -2 epsilon c_(i-j), if i>=j>p/2;
    0,                 otherwise.                         (12)

For the low columns 0<=j<=floor(p/2), a stronger comparison holds:

 p^3 R_1(i,j), p R_0(i,j) are p-integral, and
                   p^3 R_1(i,j) = p R_0(i,j) modulo p.      (13)

Proof. Since all relevant moment indices v are even and below 2p,
formula (1) gives v_p(m_v,0)>=-1, v_p(m_v,1)>=-3 and
p^3 m_v,1 = p m_v,0 modulo p. The only possible term 1/p^2 in its
odd harmonic sum occurs when v>=p-1. In particular

 p m_(p-1+2l),0 = 2 epsilon c_l modulo p

whenever p-1+2l is in range: start at p-1 using
binom(p-1,(p-1)/2)=epsilon modulo p, then use the moment recurrence.

For even lower boundary indices d<2p, all divisions by d in (3)
are p-units. The rational order-zero term has valuation at least -1,
and the order-one term at least -3. The rational order-one upper
boundary has valuation at least -2: its even recurrence can cross
d-1=p only once; the order-zero upper boundary has valuation at least
-2, and the subsequent order-one recurrence adds no new p denominator.
Thus no boundary contributes to (12).

In the finite tail (4), a fourth power can only arise from the divisor
k=p in the lower formula. It is present exactly when 2j>=p, and its
moment is m_(p+2(i-j)-1),1. This gives (12). In the upper formula,
if the divisor 2(j-i)+k is p, then k-1<p-1 and that moment has no
p denominator. No fourth power arises there.

For j<=floor(p/2), the lower finite-tail divisors k are units. The
lower boundary recurrence, after multiplication by p^3, reduces to
p times its order-zero recurrence: the remaining prefix terms have
valuation at least -2 and vanish modulo p after that multiplication.
The initial rational lower terms are both zero. Forward gaps in these
low columns are below p, so the upper-boundary and upper-tail terms
are p-integral. This proves (13).

These statements persist under the companion (7). If P has degree
at most 2H and p-integral coefficients, Q does too: the coefficients
of w are dyadic and the Li_2 denominators here are r^2 with r<=H<p.
The order-zero companion contributes at worst p^(-2), so it does not
affect (12), or (13) on low columns. If A_j and B_j are the beta(4)
and rational coefficients of M_1(P,2j)+M_0(Q,2j), respectively, then

           p^4 B_j = -(epsilon/2) A_j modulo p,  j>p/2.    (14)

For a square matrix consisting of such high columns, if its A matrix
is invertible modulo p, (14) implies v_p(det(B+X A))=-4n for every
p-integral rational X. Equivalently its primitive-polynomial clearing
factor has exactly 4n powers of p at this prime. Indeed p^4 B has
invertible reduction and p^4 X A vanishes there. The constant term
already has valuation -4n, while no coefficient has smaller valuation.

This is a conditional local obstruction for this family and range.
It does not cover every prime, a mixed low/high column pool, an
arbitrary beta(4) construction, or an assumed algebraic value of B.
In particular it is not an impossibility theorem for the whole method.

5. Exact constructions and measured outcomes
--------------------------------------------
For every square rational pencil B+X A, the programs interpolate
det(B+X A), clear denominators, and divide out the integer coefficient
gcd. Call the resulting primitive integer polynomial F(X). Arb then
encloses F(beta(4)), at precision determined by its coefficient height.
The interpolation is checked at an additional point and by the leading
coefficient det(A). This permits an exact arithmetic test, rather than
testing a possibly wasteful denominator multiplier.

Five sets of tests were performed. Parameter configurations can overlap;
the count is of recorded tests, not distinct polynomials.

 Construction                       Cases   Largest matrix   All |F(B)|>1
 High-support dilogarithm companion    88           12              yes
 Prime-constrained row lattice        40            8              yes
 Simultaneous forward contact         38           12              yes
 Full parity, even G auxiliary        15           12              yes
 Full parity, full G auxiliary        21           24              yes
 Total                               202

The first construction uses
P_r(t)=t^(2L)(1-t^2)^h T_(g+r)(2t^2-1), L=n+q,
and columns s^(2k)(1-s^2)^q. Equation (7) supplies Q_r. An invertible
change from monomials to the unshifted Chebyshev basis gives the same
primitive polynomial, as checked explicitly; this basis change by
itself cannot improve a determinant. At n=12 the best recorded
log|F(B)|/n^2 is 7.877652.

The second uses a larger pool of 2n or 3n such rows. Exact congruence
lattices make selected prime denominators disappear. LLL is applied to
a rational upper Gram matrix independent of the digits of B; rows are
selected subject to full beta-coefficient rank. At n=8 with 24 source
rows, the normalized logarithm improves from about 21.716 to 5.768,
but remains positive. All 40 primitive values exceed one. Local
negative Smith exponents are saved as diagnostics; they show that
large-prime layers persist even before imposing row congruences.

There is a proved rational real bound for this search. With the
polynomials stored in t, define

 R_rs = integral (4P_r P_s+2Q_r Q_s) dmu_0,
 G_jk = integral_0^1 s^(4b+2j+2k)(1-s^2)^q ds.

For q>=2, the integral operator C(s) -> integral C(s)(1-s^2)^q/(1-ts)ds
has squared norm at most 8 between the indicated weighted spaces.
This follows by Cauchy--Schwarz, integral dmu_0=2, and
integral_0^1(1-s^2)^q/(1-ts)^2 ds<=4 for -1<t<1.
Equation (5) gives the row Gram bound R. Consequently, for a rational
row matrix U and primitive clearing factor rho,

 |rho det(E)|^2 <= rho^2 8^n det(U R U^T) det(G).             (15)

The right side is rational and was also computed. It is greater than
one in every tested lattice case. The actual Arb values, not just this
upper bound, fail the desired size test.

The third construction removes both forward coefficients in Section 2
by solving 2(n+q) exact contact equations. It allows lower exponents in
P instead of enforcing high support. The tested contact matrices are
invertible, but the coefficients become costly. At n=12 the best
recorded log|F(B)|/n^2 is 21.735820. No uniform nonvanishing theorem
for this family was obtained.

6. Retaining both parities
-------------------------
The original Catalan proof [O, odd-primes.tex, leading-layer section]
uses pairs of raw columns whose indices differ by an odd prime p.
Restricting all raw indices to even numbers removes those particular
p-separated pairs. Pairs separated by 2p remain possible, and other
cancellation mechanisms are not excluded. This is a reason to examine
full parity, not a proof that even indices must fail.

Equation (9) permits an explicit full-parity construction. To explain
it, construct arrays

 C_G(i,j)=G delta_ij+B_G(i,j),
 C_Z(i,j)=Z delta_ij+B_Z(i,j),

where B_G,B_Z are rational. One choice of C_G is obtained by replacing
the even monomial row t^(2i) in M_0/4 by
sum_{k=0}^i s_k t^(2(i-k)), with
s_k=c_k/(1-2k)=[x^k]sqrt(1-x). The inverse convolution makes the G
coefficient delta_ij at even column 2j. There is no Z term.

C_Z is 3/2 times the ordinary Cauchy integral on [0,1]^2. Thus
B_Z(i,i)=-(3/2)sum_{r=1}^i r^(-2), and for i!=j
B_Z(i,j)=(3/2)(H_j-H_i)/(j-i).

A second, lower-degree G normalization uses the full monomial row
sum_{k=0}^{floor(i/2)}s_k t^(i-2k) in M_0/4. Its G coefficient is
again delta_ij; its forward Z coefficients are removed by C_Z on the
finite column range. Both choices have been implemented.

Use entrywise products, denoted by a circle, to define

 C_Y=C_Z circle C_Z -4 C_G circle C_G
             -2 B_Z circle C_Z +8 B_G circle C_G.

By direct expansion and (9),

 C_Y(i,j)=Y delta_ij+B_Y(i,j),
                    B_Y=4 B_G circle B_G-B_Z circle B_Z.    (16)

This is an exact quadratic integral realization of the unwanted odd
period, rather than treating it as a new independent constant. It
does not by itself give a favorable real or arithmetic bound.

An X3 array C_X=X3 I+B_X is constructed from the even logarithmic
kernel L_mu(i,j)=integral integral t^i s^j(-log s)/(1-ts) ds dmu_0.
Use the same inverse convolution to make its X3 coefficient diagonal,
then subtract its G coefficient matrix times C_G. The recurrences
for L_mu are those of [C, Lemma nurec], reproduced in the program.

Now take the actual full-parity Chebyshev rows of [O]: with
L=b+n+q, C=L+g, and 0<=r<n,

 P_r(t)=(1-t)^h t^(C-1) T_|r-g|(1/t),
 D_r(t)=sgn(r-g)(1-t)^h t^(C-1) U_(|r-g|-1)(1/t).

They obey tP_r/sqrt(1-t^2)-D_r=O(t^L). From (3), the Y coefficient
of M_1(P_r,j) is precisely [t^j]tP_r/sqrt(1-t^2), hence is D_r,j
for j<L. Subtract sum_i D_r,i C_Y(i,j), retaining ALL coefficients
of D, including those above the contact band. Remove the remaining
G,Z,X3 coefficients with C_G,C_Z,C_X, respectively. The resulting
matrix has entries in Q+Q B. Apply the usual binomial column filter.

Retaining the high coefficients of D matters: symmetric Chebyshev
indices can give identical P rows with opposite companions. Truncating
D would discard this source of rational row variation. Nevertheless
the tested beta coefficient rank can be less than n, and the polynomial
degree and n must not be silently identified.

The 15 tests with the even G auxiliary all fail; at n=12 the best
log|F(B)|/n^2 is 4.877869. The 21 tests with full G normalization all
fail too; its baseline grows from 5.071338 at n=12 to 5.685716 at n=24.
The smaller auxiliary degrees therefore do not repair the measured
balance. The saved per-degree-squared diagnostic differs from the
per-n-squared value when the beta coefficient rank drops.

7. Consequences for the next direction
--------------------------------------
The present investigation resolves three concrete algebraic questions:
the global even companion, the odd boundary identity, and the leading
prime layers. It has not resolved the analytic-arithmetic balance.
The 202 tests give no evidence that enlarging these specific families
will yield the required decay.

A further attempt should address cancellation inside a common integral
representation, so that the lower-period corrections retain the same
endpoint decay and prime-column relations as the main term. Equations
(9) and (16) make a two-factor integral construction possible at the
algebraic level. The finite canonical projections tested here do not
supply the required simultaneous control. A candidate must exhibit
both a real bound and its prime reductions before a broad optimization
scan is justified; positivity/nonvanishing is a separate requirement.

The distinction between a period identity and a useful approximation
construction is essential. Projecting formal words onto an
indecomposable part, or cancelling products algebraically, does not
automatically preserve a bound on their numerical integrals. Nor does
a single-valued construction automatically annihilate products: its
period map is multiplicative. These ideas cannot supply free arithmetic
or analytic savings.

As a possible source of a different integral design, the selected
primary-source reading [BD, introduction and Sections 3.6--3.7] describes
quadratic expressions in period integrals and their complex integral
realizations. Brown [Br, introduction and Section 8.2] constructs
single-valued hyperlogarithms using products of ordinary hyperlogarithms.
These are established constructions, not results of this project.
Their application to the present beta(4) approximation problem remains
speculative: no suitable integral family, denominator theorem, favorable
decay estimate, or nonvanishing result has been produced from them.
No blanket claim about other approaches follows from these failures.

8. Reproducibility and validation
---------------------------------
The following commands run from the beta4 directory:

 .venv/bin/python experiments/arcsine_contact.py
 .venv/bin/python experiments/arcsine_arithmetic_rows.py
 .venv/bin/python experiments/arcsine_pade_contact.py
 .venv/bin/python experiments/arcsine_full_parity.py
 .venv/bin/python experiments/arcsine_full_parity_reduced.py
 .venv/bin/python experiments/arcsine_contact_checks.py
 .venv/bin/python experiments/arcsine_integral_checks.py

Results have matching names under results/. Exact checks include:

 - 80 coefficient checks of the Y differential recurrence;
 - 161 coefficients of its first integral;
 - 161 full and 81 even dilogarithm companion coefficients;
 - 1,922 comparisons between full and even boundary implementations;
 - 59 checks against Carreira's independently arranged recurrence;
 - 780 formal evaluations of the quadratic auxiliary identity;
 - 148,827 matrix-entry fourth-layer reductions, including 113,927
   low-column third-layer comparisons and 34,900 high-column cases;
 - 2,439 moment congruences and 2,439 polynomial-companion congruences.

The polynomial evaluations in the five scans are Arb interval
computations. A separate 55-digit mpmath comparison of 22 mixed
integrals agrees to better than 10^(-48); it is explicitly diagnostic,
not interval-certified. The written proofs establish the
uniform identities; finite checks do not replace those proofs.
