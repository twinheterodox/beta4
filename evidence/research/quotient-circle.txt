Optimal rational lifts: a two-dimensional reformulation
28 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

Follow-up: quotient-investigation.txt carries out the proposed prefix and
filter investigation. It proves a qualitative limiting-metric theorem,
compares all 144 new directions with continued fractions, and records
finite failures of explicit filters. The original pilot below is retained.
half-pole-arithmetic.txt records the resulting uniform arithmetic gain.

This follow-up arose while deciding what to investigate after
block-arithmetic.txt. It supplies an exact reformulation and one finite
pilot, not a uniform decay theorem or an irrationality proof. All checking
was by the same agent.

1. The source weights may be rational

The analytic moment inequality in block-arithmetic.txt applies to arbitrary
real source weights. For an irrationality argument, the FINAL coefficients
of beta(4) and 1 must be integral and the coefficient of G must vanish.
There is no need to impose integrality on the source weights themselves.

Let V be the 2w by 3 matrix of normalized source coefficient rows, ordered
as (G,beta(4),1), and let H be the rational positive definite circle Gram
matrix from that note. A row x of source weights gives image xV and norm
squared x H x^T. V has rank three for the blocks under consideration.

For any prescribed image u, put

    W=H^(-1)V,
    J=(V^T H^(-1)V)^(-1),
    x_*=u J W^T.

Then x_* V=u, and

    min_{xV=u} x H x^T = u J u^T.                           (1)

Indeed x_* H=u J V^T. If yV=0, the cross term between x_* and y is
zero, so (x_*+y)H(x_*+y)^T=x_* H x_*^T+y H y^T. This proves optimality
and uniqueness. All the data are rational, so x_* is rational for every
rational u; no approximation or rounding of the minimizer is needed.

For u=(0,A,B), let K be the beta/constant principal 2 by 2 submatrix of J.
Then every integer pair A,B has a rational lift and the certificate

    |A beta(4)+B| <= 514 sqrt((A,B) K (A,B)^T).              (2)

The two best independent integer directions for this positive definite
binary quadratic form are found by exact Gauss reduction. This removes
the exact-identity directions before optimization. Optimality is relative
to this particular circle-norm certificate, not to every possible bound.

Relation to the preceding integer-source search: clear the denominators
of x_* by D. The integer source vector Dx_* has coefficient image
(0,DA,DB). For a primitive pair A,B its image gcd is exactly D. Dividing
by this gcd recovers x_*. Thus these rational lifts are compatible with
complete primitive reduction of the old family. The old search optimized
integer source rows first and only then divided selected images by their
gcd; it could miss the much better primitive directions obtained here.
This is an improvement in formulation and optimization, without a claim
of a new general approximation theorem.

2. Exact pilot

Use N=8,w=16, so m=8,...,23, with the same normalization, circle and
constant 514 as before. Exact Gauss reduction gives coefficient pairs

 A_1=21476276077376486606272803816149626853903585025611,
 B_1=-21238846218409313605479349262563260808351391652700,
 A_2=36989132945306620308855585700905004922458739227456,
 B_2=-36580201499888406982459260186607049363669001143709.

Their determinant A_1 B_2-B_1 A_2 is 1. The coefficient bit lengths
are 164 and 165. The certified bounds are, respectively,

    |A_1 beta(4)+B_1| < 2.867*10^(-45),
    |A_2 beta(4)+B_2| < 8.724*10^(-45).

Both are below 9*10^(-45), verified by exact rational squared inequalities.
For comparison, the preceding source-lattice search on this SAME block
had both errors bounded by 2.55*10^(-19), with 248-bit coefficients.
Its larger N=16,w=24 block had bounds below 3*10^(-34) and coefficient
sizes 426 and 432 bits.

Arb evaluations give actual errors around -3.311*10^(-51) and
4.086*10^(-50). Those values were not used in choosing the coefficient
pairs or in the norm proof. No claim of superiority to continued fractions
at comparable height is made. Gauss reduction of a binary form is itself
closely connected to rational approximation, so a comparison with numerical
continued fractions would not establish a distinct irrationality mechanism.

The generator saves the rational source weights, image metric, and
Lagrange multipliers certifying optimality. A separate Fraction checker
rebuilds the three coefficient coordinates, directly translates the
polynomials onto the circle, verifies Hx=V lambda, checks the determinant,
and verifies the Gauss reduction inequalities and absolute error bounds.

3. The next mathematical target

For growing blocks, the target is a uniform upper bound tending to zero
for the SECOND independent integer direction in K_(N,w). Control of the
shortest direction alone does not suffice: at a rational beta(4), one
direction can vanish identically. The existing pilot proves nothing about
the required limiting behavior.

One can write K=[[k11,k12],[k12,k22]] as

    (A,B)K(A,B)^T
       =k22 (B+alpha A)^2 + delta A^2,
    alpha=k12/k22,   delta=det(K)/k22>0.

In particular |beta(4)-alpha|<=514 sqrt(delta), by using the rational
image (0,1,-alpha). A small delta alone does not bound the second integer
direction: its behavior also depends on rational approximation to alpha.
This prevents a determinant or volume argument from silently assuming
the irrationality it is meant to prove.

The recommended next investigation is to use the now-proved recurrence
to understand the exact changing binary forms, their reduced coefficient
pairs, and possible explicit recurrences for good pairs. In parallel in
the mathematical sense, the positive moment model suggests constructing
source polynomials with the arithmetic conditions built in. The existing
filter [t(t-1)(t-2)]^n contracts on [0,256phi^(-10)], but has not been
made arithmetically admissible with a proved acceptable cost.

Integer polynomials small on an interval connect this latter direction
to the integer Chebyshev problem. Pritsker's paper below studies the
unconstrained extremal problem; it does not supply our auxiliary-period
cancellation or denominator theorem. Only its abstract was consulted for
this planning connection, not its full proof.

I. E. Pritsker, Small polynomials with integer coefficients,
https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0101166.
