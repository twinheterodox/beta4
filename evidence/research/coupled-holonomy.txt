Coupled generating functions: an arithmetic-holonomy feasibility test
27 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

Update, 28 October 2026: block-arithmetic.txt proves the mixed recurrence
by rational telescoping and thereby certifies the order-four recurrence
left conjectural in Section 4 below. Positive moment representations for
both normalized error sequences also prove the missing slit continuation
for S. The new note sharpens the proved residue denominator bounds and
gives exact powers-of-2 valuations. This closes those specific gaps in the
original investigation; the failed arithmetic-holonomy budgets, missing
functional-independence input, and absence of an irrationality proof remain.
The original dated account below is retained as a record of that stage.

Outcome
-------
The coupled-family approach avoids the extra coefficient denominator from
eliminating Catalan's constant separately at each index. However, the direct
functional cancellation tested here loses the small-error behavior. Standard
slit-domain holonomy templates also have a large negative arithmetic margin,
even if granted substantially stronger inputs than we have proved.

This is a negative feasibility result for the constructions and templates
specified below, not an impossibility theorem for arithmetic holonomy or
beta(4). In particular, the necessary functional independence and global
continuation for a successful holonomy application have NOT been proved.
There is no irrationality proof or independent verification claim.

1. The full parameter family
----------------------------
Write G=beta(2), B4=beta(4), and let n>=0 be even. Define

 P_n(t)=(2t+n+1) product_{ell=-3n/2}^{n/2-1}(t-ell),
 p_j=(2j+1)/4,  0<=j<=2n+1,
 R_n(t)=P_n(t)/product_j(t+p_j)^2,
 R_(n,e)(t)=P_n(t)/product_j((t+p_j)^2-e^2),
 K_n=(2n+1)!/2^(2n+2).

The numerator product is empty at n=0. Set S_n(t)=[e^2]R_(n,e)(t),
so S_n=R_n sum_j(t+p_j)^(-2). These are the classical-parameter
quarter-pole construction and deformation from hypergeometric-arithmetic.txt.

There is an exact one-period identity before taking the e expansion. Put

 T(e)=sum_{k>=0}[1/((k+1/4)^2-e^2)-1/((k+3/4)^2-e^2)]
     =16G+256 B4 e^2+O(e^4).

For rational functions a_n(e),b_n(e), even in e and regular near zero,

 K_n sum_{t>=0}R_(n,e)(t)=a_n(e)T(e)/16+b_n(e).       (1)

Proof. At x_j=-p_j, put

 H_j(e)=P_n(x_j+e)/product_{h!=j}[(p_h-p_j)(p_h-p_j+2e)].

The residues at x_j+e and x_j-e are respectively H_j(e)/(2e) and
-H_j(-e)/(2e). Reflection gives H_(2n+1-j)(e)=-H_j(-e).
The sum of all residues is zero. Hence h(e)=sum_{j even}H_j(e)
is even, and sum_{j odd}H_j(e)=-h(e). Grouping the shifted quarter
progressions gives (1), with a_n(e)=16 K_n h(e). All finite shifts
contribute a rational function b_n(e). The original summand is even in e,
so b_n(e) is even as well. This proves the identity for |e| sufficiently
small; the convergent sums justify coefficient extraction there.

Write a_n(e)=a_n+c_n e^2+O(e^4), b_n(e)=b_n+d_n e^2+O(e^4). Then

 r_n:=K_n sum R_n = a_n G+b_n,
 s_n:=K_n sum S_n =16 a_n B4+c_n G+d_n.                (2)

In particular, (a_0,b_0,c_0,d_0)=(8,0,0,0), r_0=8G, s_0=128B4.
The a_n and -b_n are eight times the classical Catalan recurrence
coefficients at even n. The normalization is checked against that
recurrence, not inferred from a numerical fit.

2. What can be proved about arithmetic and convergence
------------------------------------------------------
Let L_N=lcm(1,...,N), and L_0=1. A uniform, deliberately conservative
bound that follows directly from the residue products is

 16^n a_n is integral,
 16^(n+1) L_(6n+3)^2 b_n is integral,
 2*16^n L_(6n+3)^2 c_n is integral,
 96*16^n L_(6n+3)^4 d_n is integral.                   (3)

Here is an elementary justification retaining all differentiation costs.
Let alpha_j=16 K_n times the double-pole coefficient of R_n. With
m=2n+1 and
 Q_j=product_{ell=-3n/2}^{n/2-1}(-(2j+1)-4ell),

 alpha_j=8/4^n * Q_j/(2n)!
          * binom(m,j)[binom(m,j)-2 binom(m-1,j-1)].

Take the binomial with lower index -1 to be zero. For every odd prime,
Q_j has at least the valuation of (2n)!: it is a product of 2n consecutive
terms in a progression with step 4. Its power-of-2 denominator is cleared
by 16^n. Thus 16^n alpha_j is integral.

The next three Taylor coefficients of 16 K_n H_j(e) are

 alpha_j l_1,
 alpha_j(l_1^2+l_2)/2,
 alpha_j(l_1^3+3l_1 l_2+l_3)/6,

where l_i are logarithmic derivatives at zero. Inspection of their product
factors shows L_(6n+3)^i l_i is integral. The finite quarter-progression
tails of order s are cleared by L_(6n+3)^s. Combining these statements,
including the division by 16 in the normalized partial fractions, proves
(3). No differentiated factorial cost is omitted.

The substantially sharper inclusions with L_(2n)^2 for b_n,c_n and
L_(2n)^4 for d_n, all with multiplier 16^n, hold in all 360 computed
even indices n=0,...,718 (1440 exact checks). The a_n,b_n bounds are
classical. The sharper c_n,d_n inclusions are FINITE OBSERVATIONS here,
not uniform theorems.

There is also a useful rigorous analytic bound. At positive even n, the
integer zeros permit the sums defining r_n,s_n to start at t=n/2. Every
remaining summand of R_n is positive, and

 0 < S_n(t)/R_n(t)
   <= (2n+2)/(n/2+1/4)^2 < 8/n.

Consequently 0<s_n<(8/n)r_n. The classical Catalan moment representation
has peak epsilon=phi^(-5), phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2, and gives
0<r_n<=8G epsilon^n at even n. Therefore the generating functions

 R(z)=sum_{m>=0}r_(2m)z^m,
 S(z)=sum_{m>=0}s_(2m)z^m

are holomorphic for |z|<R_*:=phi^10=122.9918693812442... .
The classical moment integral continues R to C minus [R_*,infinity).
The corresponding slit continuation for S has NOT been proved here.

3. Functional cancellation and its direct test
----------------------------------------------
Define A(z),B(z),C(z),D(z) from a_(2m),b_(2m),c_(2m),d_(2m). Then

 R=GA+B,        S=16 B4 A+GC+D.

In particular, the following cancellation is exact:

 A S-C R=16 B4 A^2+A D-C B.                            (4)

If B4 were rational, (4) would be a power series with rational coefficients.
This is an appropriate candidate to test: it retains B4 and removes G,
without introducing the large individual denominator of c_n/a_n.
The common-denominator bound in (3) is stable under these Cauchy products:
at series index m, 96*256^m L_(12m+3)^4 clears the rational part of (4).
The experimentally stronger L_(4m)^4 also clears all available terms.

But multiplying the small functions R,S by A,C does not preserve their
large domain of analyticity. The actual coefficients demonstrate the
problem in the tested range. Let F_m=[z^m](A S-C R). We obtain

 m       F_m/a_(2m)      log of the primitive |A_int B4+B_int|
 16       79.83894                     400.3552
 64       78.73971                    1643.0901
256       78.46260                    6718.9998.

These are Arb enclosures of exact coefficient expressions, not upper
bounds inferred from absolute summands. The primitive integer coefficient
pairs were reduced by their complete gcd. All nine tested values exceed
one. The raw coefficients have the scale of the large Catalan coefficients
in this range, rather than the scale of r_n or s_n.

This supports retention of the nearest singularity. It is NOT a proof of
the radius of convergence of (4), an asymptotic theorem, or an obstruction
to every possible differential or nonlinear cancellation.

Rationality of B4 alone also does not make R or S rational-coefficient
series: their coefficients still contain G. One cannot put the two small
error functions directly into a rational arithmetic-holonomy theorem.

4. Differential equations: proved facts versus discovered candidates
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The classical recurrence, after eliminating odd source indices, gives a
proved order-two recurrence for a_(2m),b_(2m), with coefficient polynomials
of degree 14. Its leading characteristic polynomial is

  X^2-123X+1.

The polynomial coefficients are in results/coupled_guess_a.json. Despite
that discovery filename, this particular recurrence is now proved from
the classical one by exact symbolic elimination. Its generating-function
operator is explicitly

 L= sum_{j=0}^2 z^(2-j) q_j(theta-j),  theta=z d/dz,

where q_j are the stored recurrence polynomials. Its order in theta is 14,
and L A=0, while L B is a polynomial of degree one. No minimal-order claim
is made.

For c_(2m) and d_(2m), modular discovery followed by rational reconstruction
found the SAME order-four recurrence, with polynomial degree 52. It passes
all exact checks through m=359, including 60 coefficient terms beyond the
300-term discovery input. It also annihilates a_(2m),b_(2m) throughout the
tested range. Its leading characteristic polynomial is

  (X^2-123X+1)^2.

An exact calculation in the shift-operator algebra shows that this
candidate operator has the proved order-two operator as a RIGHT factor.
That factorization is a polynomial identity; the assertion that the
candidate annihilates c or d for every index remains UNPROVED. We did not
promote a guessed recurrence to a theorem based on long finite agreement.

If certified, it would yield an order-52 generating-function equation
with explicit polynomial boundary terms by the same recurrence-to-ODE
conversion. These data suggest an extension of the old system with the
same exponential scales. They do not establish independence over Q(z).
Constant-linear independence of finitely sampled sequences, recurrence
order, and Q(z)-linear independence of functions are different assertions.

5. Arithmetic-holonomy feasibility bounds
----------------------------------------
The relevant precise input is Calegari--Dimitrov--Tang [CDT], Theorem 2.5.1:
for Q(z)-independent holonomic rational series and a shared meromorphic
pullback under phi, one needs a positive denominator

 log|phi'(0)|-tau,
 tau=(1/M^2) sum_{i=1}^M (2i-1)sigma_i

for the ordered denominator profiles considered there. A positive value
is only an entry condition; the theorem's numerator and dimension bound
must still be checked. Functional independence and common continuation
are hypotheses, not consequences of having several displayed series.

Use the experimentally favorable denominator types, absorbing 256^m by
z ->256z. The error disk radius is then

 R_*/256=0.4804369897704852... .

Even grant a shared slit domain C minus [R_*/256,infinity), which is more
than has been proved for the deformation and the rational candidate (4).
The standard conformal slit map phi(w)=4(R_*/256)w/(1+w)^2 has

 log|phi'(0)|=0.6532351672363626... .

The following direct templates all fail the entry condition:

 denominator profiles       tau       log|phi'(0)|-tau
 [16]                        16           -15.34676
 [0,16]                      12           -11.34676
 [0,8,8,16]                  11           -10.34676.

The first two model a rational beta(4) candidate, with or without the
constant function. The last grants all four favorable arithmetic types
and a shared domain. These are diagnostic templates, not assertions that
their independence or continuation hypotheses hold. Proved bound (3)
would make their arithmetic cost larger still.

This computation does NOT optimize all uniformizations. Maps permitting
continuation around singularities, arithmetic changes of variable, other
coefficient combinations, and other denominator profiles require separate
arguments. In particular it is not a no-go theorem for arithmetic holonomy.
The needed new global continuation or divisibility mechanism is still absent.

6. Assessment and reproducibility
----------------------------------
The arithmetic improvement from keeping the coupled system is real: it
removes the division by a single large coefficient a_n. The direct
functional projection sacrifices the needed analytic advantage, however,
and the stated favorable slit-map templates remain far from the holonomy
threshold. Thus this pass does not justify a large investment in proving
the guessed order-52 equation as a route to beta(4) irrationality.

The precise outcomes are: the parameter identity (1); uniform bounds (3);
the positivity and error-disk bounds; an exact functional cancellation;
a proved base differential equation; and a carefully labeled conjectural
larger equation with finite arithmetic/interval diagnostics. Functional
independence and a successful holonomy bound remain unestablished.
