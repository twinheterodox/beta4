Following the quotient metric: approximation, positivity, and arithmetic
28 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

This continues the investigation proposed at the end of quotient-circle.
The exact prefix metrics are now computed through width 96. A better
interval norm improves finite certificates, but all 144 selected integer
directions are continued-fraction convergents or intermediate convergents.
A qualitative theorem explains why the analytic metric degenerates and
why its second integer minimum remains the essential arithmetic issue.

Two explicit contracting polynomial families and a separate positive
Catalan-cancellation construction were tested with full primitive reduction.
None of those tested families supplies shrinking integer forms. The useful
arithmetic result that emerged during this investigation is recorded in
half-pole-arithmetic.txt: an exact parameter identity and smaller proved
denominator bounds. No irrationality proof or independent audit is claimed.

1. Fixed conventions and exact metric updates

This note uses the BLOCK index m. Write

 A_m=a_(2m), B_m=b_(2m), C_m=c_(2m), D_m=d_(2m),
 R_m=A_m G+B_m,
 S_m=16A_m theta+C_m G+D_m,
 theta=beta(4), G=beta(2).

The positive moment representations in block-arithmetic give

 Rbar_m=R_m/(4m+1)^2=integral t^m dmu(t),
 Sbar_m=S_m/(4m+1)^2=integral t^m deta(t),

with positive infinite-support measures on [0,rho], rho=phi^(-10), and
masses <8 and <128. Here phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2. For the circle with center
h=1/256 and radius r=1/224, they give the uniform certificate

 |Rbar(P)+Sbar(Q)| <=514 sqrt(||P||_circle^2+||Q||_circle^2). (1)

The norm is normalized L2 on that circle. The underlying coefficient
matrix V has rows (A_m,0,B_m)/(4m+1)^2 and (C_m,16A_m,D_m)/(4m+1)^2,
in coordinate order (G,theta,1). For the prefix m=0,...,w-1, w>=2,
put J_w=(V^T H^(-1)V)^(-1), where H is the source norm Gram matrix.
The beta/constant principal submatrix K_w is the exact optimal metric:

 min_(xV=(0,A,B)) xHx^T=(A,B)K_w(A,B)^T.

The minimum permits rational source weights. Only A,B have to be integers.
The proof and explicit minimizer are in quotient-circle, Section 1.

The circle polynomials P_j(t)=((t-h)/r)^j=(224t-7/8)^j are orthonormal.
Let Z_j be the two coefficient-image rows of Rbar(P_j), Sbar(P_j).
Then M_w=J_w^(-1)=sum_(j<w) Z_j^T Z_j. Consequently

 J_(w+1)=J_w-J_w Z_w^T (I_2+Z_w J_w Z_w^T)^(-1) Z_w J_w.  (2)

This exact rank-two update was verified at every width through 96. It is
a useful computational identity; it is not a recurrence for the reduced
integer coefficient pairs and does not imply their required decay.

2. A stronger interval-norm certificate

The containing interval [0,b], b=1/122, has rational endpoint. On it use
the normalized uniform L2 norm and shifted Legendre polynomials

 P_j(t)=L_j(244t-1)
       =sum_(m=0)^j (-1)^(j-m)binom(j,m)binom(j+m,m)122^m t^m.

Their squared norms are 1/(2j+1). For any polynomial of degree <w,
the facts |L_j(x)|<=1 on [-1,1] and sum_(j<w)(2j+1)=w^2 give

                   ||P||_infinity <= w ||P||_L2.

The moment masses then imply

 |Rbar(P)+Sbar(Q)|
       <=129w sqrt(||P||_L2^2+||Q||_L2^2).                (3)

All metric calculations remain rational. In this basis
M_w=sum_(j<w)(2j+1)Z_j^T Z_j; the update (2) uses I_2/(2w+1)
in place of I_2. The dependence of the certificate constant on w is
retained. In particular, for this norm it is 129w times the second
minimum, not the second minimum alone, that must tend to zero.

Examples from EXACT Gauss reduction, with Arb enclosures of the resulting
norm bounds, are below. Each bound applies to both independent directions.
The coefficient size is the maximum bit length of their four integers.

  width    circle bound       bits     interval bound      bits
   16      6.67 * 10^(-30)     100      1.49 * 10^(-32)      107
   32      4.33 * 10^(-65)     215      3.89 * 10^(-70)      231
   64      8.24 * 10^(-136)    450      5.47 * 10^(-146)     483
   96      1.52 * 10^(-206)    685      7.92 * 10^(-222)     736

These are prefixes N=0. They should not be confused with the earlier
N=8,width=16 pilot. The new sample covers widths 2,...,32 and 40,48,64,
80,96 for both norms: 72 metrics and 144 integer pairs.

After constructing the metrics and choosing the pairs, the script computed
an Arb-certified continued-fraction prefix of theta. Of the 144 pairs,
114 are convergents and 30 are intermediate convergents. Every first
Gauss vector is a convergent. The remaining second vectors have the
exact form

 (q_(k-2)+j q_(k-1), -(p_(k-2)+j p_(k-1))),  1<=j<a_k.

The numerical value of theta played no role in constructing the metrics
or selecting the pairs. This is nonetheless approximation of the familiar
continued-fraction kind, not evidence of a new irrationality mechanism.
No all-width claim about this exact CF classification is made.

3. What the circle metrics converge to

There is a uniform explanation for the analytic degeneration. This
section concerns the fixed circle norm, whose constant in (1) is uniform.
Let lambda_2(K_w) be the least possible maximum of the K_w lengths of
two linearly independent integer pairs. Then

             lambda_2(K_w) -> 0  IF AND ONLY IF theta
             is irrational.                              (4)

This is a characterization, not a proof that either side holds. Its
point is to isolate the arithmetic content which an estimate must supply.

Proof. The new terminating formula in half-pole-arithmetic implies
A_m>=8 and 0<=C_m/A_m<8. Define the rational number

 alpha_m=(C_m B_m-A_m D_m)/(16 A_m^2).

The source combination

 ell_m=S_m/(16A_m)-C_m R_m/(16A_m^2)=theta-alpha_m          (5)

has image (0,1,-alpha_m) and a source lift whose circle norm is at most

 epsilon_m=(sqrt(65)/128)(4m+1)^2 (15/1792)^m ->0.         (6)

Indeed its two normalized-source polynomials are multiples of t^m,
with common factor (4m+1)^2/(16A_m), and |t|<=h+r=15/1792 on
the circle. Inequality (1) also shows |theta-alpha_m|<=514 epsilon_m.

There is a fixed rational lift of the constant 1:

        1=(25/B_1)Rbar_1-(A_1/(8B_1))Rbar_0,             (7)

where A_1=649/8, B_1=-10699/144. Denote its circle norm by C0.
For any fixed real A,B, combine A times (5) with (B+A alpha_m) times
(7). The image is (0,A,B), and the norm is at most

               |A|epsilon_m+C0|B+A alpha_m|.

For rational A,B this is a rational lift, as required by the arithmetic
construction. Allowing real A,B here simply describes the quadratic form.

Enlarging the prefix only adds possible source polynomials, so K_w
decreases in the positive-semidefinite order and has a limit K_infinity.
The preceding lift and (1) give, for every real A,B,

 |A theta+B|/514 <= sqrt((A,B)K_infinity(A,B)^T)
                         <= C0 |A theta+B|.

It follows that

       K_infinity=c [[theta^2,theta],[theta,1]],
       1/514^2<=c<=C0^2.                                 (8)

Thus the analytic limit has rank one regardless of whether theta is
rational. This observation by itself says nothing decisive about the
second INTEGER minimum.

If theta is irrational, there are two independent integer pairs with
|A theta+B| as small as desired, by elementary rational approximation.
Fix two such pairs, then let w grow; (8) makes their K_w norms as small
as desired. This proves the forward implication in (4).

Conversely, if theta=p/q in lowest terms, any two independent integer
pairs include one for which |A theta+B|>=1/q. By (1) its K_w norm is
at least 1/(514q), for every w. This proves the other implication.

Consequences. A small determinant, small transverse eigenvalue, or fast
convergence of the rational center to theta is expected even in the
rational case. A proof of a quantitative upper bound tending to zero
for lambda_2 would still prove irrationality; (4) does not rule out such
an arithmetic proof. It explains why analytic richness and finite
success alone do not supply that proof.

4. A direct arithmetic sufficient estimate, and its finite failure

For a rational positive definite binary form K, write K=s Z, where s>0
is rational and Z is a primitive integral matrix. A Gauss-reduced matrix
g has 0<g11<=g22 and |g12|<=g11/2. Hence

   det K>=3 g11 g22/4,       g11>=s,
   lambda_2(K)^2=g22<=4 det(K)/(3s).                       (9)

This is a genuine arithmetic upper bound, rather than an argument from
volume alone. But it is far too weak for the tested metrics. At width
96 the natural logarithm of the resulting ERROR upper bound is about
+3624 for the circle metric and +4016 for the interval metric. In
contrast the actual reduced-vector bounds are those in Section 2.
Every one of the 72 tested estimates (9) is large, not small.

The rational centers alpha=k12/k22 also have expensive denominators:
11,516 bits and 12,840 bits at width 96 for the two norms. Their excellent
real approximation does not automatically turn them into useful primitive
integer forms. These finite diagnostics do not exclude sharper arithmetic
estimates, congruences, or a successful different construction.

5. Integer polynomial filters: the full normalization matters

Put u=256t. On [0,256rho] two integral polynomials contract:

 f(u)=u(u-1)(u-2),            |f(u)|<=2/(3sqrt(3))<2/5,
 g(u)=u(u-2)(u-1)^2,          |g(u)|<=1/4.

For the second assertion put v=u-1,z=v^2. Then g=z(z-1), and
0<=z<=(67/61)^2 because 256rho<128/61. The endpoint value is less
than 1/4 and the interior minimum is -1/4. This proves the bound.

For an unnormalized source, multiplication of the m-th moment by
(4m+1)^2 is the differential operator (4u d/du+1)^2. For F=f^N or
g^N, with |f|<=tau, |f'|<=d1, |f''|<=d2, it gives the bound

 B_N=16U^2[N(N-1)tau^(N-2)d1^2+N tau^(N-1)d2]
        +24UN tau^(N-1)d1+tau^N,
 U=17/8,
 (tau,d1,d2)=(2/5,3,7) or (1/4,4,14).

The filtered R and S have absolute values at most 8B_N and 128B_N.
If their coefficient quadruple is (a,b,c,d), eliminating G gives

                 16a^2 theta+ad-cb=aS-cR.

The experiment clears its exact denominators, divides the FULL gcd of
the two final integer coefficients, and tests the resulting primitive
form. Both families were checked at N=1,...,24,32,48,64: 54 cases.
Every primitive absolute value is rigorously above 1. At N=64 their
logarithms are about 4945 and 6617, respectively. Thus real contraction
of these particular powers is overwhelmed by their arithmetic cost.

This is not a uniform impossibility theorem for all integer filters,
all powers, or all ways of combining source rows. It is a failed finite
test of the two explicit constructions just described.

6. A uniform positive form with exact Catalan cancellation

There is a separate construction that guarantees nonvanishing, without
checking numerical continued fractions. Define rational functionals
on polynomials by

 calA(t^m)=A_m/(4m+1)^2,
 calC(t^m)=C_m/(4m+1)^2,
 calD(t^m)=D_m/(4m+1)^2.

Then Sbar(P)=16 calA(P)theta+calC(P)G+calD(P)=integral P deta,
and calC(1)=0. For any rational polynomial F put

           V=calC(F^2)-2calC(F)F.

Expansion gives calC(V^2)=0 exactly. Consequently, whenever V is a
nonzero polynomial,

          16 calA(V^2)theta+calD(V^2)=integral V^2 deta>0.  (10)

Strict positivity follows from the positive measure's infinite support.
For F=t^N, N>=1, formula (3) in half-pole-arithmetic gives calC(F)>0,
so V is nonconstant and (10) is a uniformly nonzero beta(4)-only family.
The underlying operation is an elementary rational parametrization of
a quadratic equation with the known isotropic vector 1; no claim of
a new general parametrization method is made.

The missing property is arithmetic smallness. The exact experiment tried
F=t^N; F=f(256t)^N; F=L_N(2t/123-1)-(-1)^N; and seven polynomials
obtained by solving calC(t^i F)=calC(t^i) on i=1,...,N. The first three
families use N=1,...,16,24,32, and the last uses N=2,4,8,12,16,24,32.
All 61 primitive integer values are positive and rigorously above 1.

For the last construction, the required finite matrices were invertible
at the tested sizes. No general positivity or invertibility theorem for
those calC Hankel matrices is assumed. The positive measure in (10) is
eta, not a hypothetical positive measure representing calC.

The positive cancellation identity is valid even though these choices
fail the size test. In particular, this direction has supplied a uniform
nonvanishing construction but not the normalization estimate needed for
irrationality.

7. Record and remaining mathematical issue

The exact parameter identity in half-pole-arithmetic is the strongest
new arithmetic result from this pass. It supplies information genuinely
missing from the earlier residue estimates, while the metric and filter
tests identify which observed successes do not close the proof.

What remains is a family of nonzero INTEGER beta(4)-only forms whose
values tend to zero, or another equally decisive irrationality criterion.
A sharper real estimate by itself will not settle the tested failures.
The parameter-level rational coefficient b_n(e) is a concrete unresolved
arithmetic object; its undeformed version has a classical multiple-sum
formula, but the derivative's full arithmetic has not been transferred.

Sympy/Fraction implementation rebuilds eight full monomial Gram metrics
(both norms at widths 2,4,8,12), checks 100 exact Legendre inner products,
and classifies all 144 saved directions as convergents or intermediate
convergents. These are separate implementations by the same agent, not
independent mathematical verification or formal proof verification.

coefficients, primitive scaling factors, error intervals, and the
continued-fraction diagnostic prefix are retained. All work remains local.
