ROUND11: SINE NORMALIZATION AND A COMPLETE CYCLOTOMIC NORM GATE
29 October 2026. Local and unpublished. No irrationality proof.

Outcome and exact scope
-----------------------
The nonlinear norm is genuinely outside the round10 rational-trace
obstruction. The prescribed sine normalization also calibrates its
principal embedding to UNNORMALIZED theta=beta4. However, the complete
conjugate product grows like exp(4*(p-1)*log(p)+O(p)); primitive content
cannot remove this growth. This is a theorem about the one family below,
with p running over ODD PRIMES. It does not close nonlinear norms,
other normalizations, other weights, or other algebraic approximants.

There is no claim that every odd composite level obeys all assertions
below. Several identities extend, but the degree, sine product and
asymptotic statement are proved only in the prime-level setting used.
No novelty claim is made.

1. Exact candidate and the rationality criterion
-----------------------------------------------
For an odd prime p let h=pi/(2p), w=exp(2*pi*i/(4p)), and define

 alpha_p=sin(h)^4 sum_(1<=n<p,n odd)
              chi_(-4)(n)*cos(nh)^3/sin(nh)^4.                (1)

Let F_p=Q(w+w^(-1)), the real cyclotomic field of degree d=p-1.
The alpha_p belong to F_p. In fact Section3 proves their actual minimal
degree is d as well; the norm argument would be valid in the full
field without that extra fact.

Write U_p=8*alpha_p. Then the exact identity is

 U_p=sum_(n odd,0<n<p) chi(n)
       [(w-w^-1)/(w^n-w^-n)]^4*(w^n+w^-n)^3.                (2)

Every term in (2) is an algebraic integer. Indeed z=w^2 is a primitive
2p-th root; for odd0<n<p, z^n is also primitive. The polynomial
Phi_(2p)(x)=x^(p-1)-x^(p-2)+...-x+1 has Phi_(2p)(1)=1.
Thus1-z^n is a unit in Z[z], by the polynomial Bezout identity obtained
from Phi_(2p)(x)-Phi_(2p)(1). The difference w^n-w^-n is that unit
times a root of unity. This proves U_p is integral with NO untracked
odd-prime denominator. It does not assert8 is always the least integer
scale: the exact p7 primitive polynomial, for example, has content64.

Define the monic rational norm polynomial and an integral polynomial

 M_p(X)=Norm_(F_p/Q)(X-alpha_p),
 J_p(X)=Norm_(F_p/Q)(8X-U_p)=8^d*M_p(X) in Z[X].           (3)

Its positive content g_p divides its leading coefficient8^d. Put
 P_p=J_p/g_p, the actual primitive polynomial, with positive leading
coefficient. All products at the unknown real theta below mean
NORM-POLYNOMIAL EVALUATIONS. The quantity theta-alpha_p is not asserted
to belong to F_p unless theta is first assumed rational.

Under theta=u/v, v>0, the nonzero integer criterion would be
 v^d*P_p(u/v) in Z\{0}.
Equivalently the sufficient uncleared norm is
 Norm(8v*alpha_p-8u), whose magnitude is
 (8v)^d product_sigma |sigma(alpha_p)-theta|.
The factor v^d is essential. The next sections show that even P_p(theta)
itself grows for this family; the criterion cannot succeed.

2. Correct principal normalization and signed error
---------------------------------------------------
The round10 cotangent expression Sigma_4 at the prescribed half cusp
satisfies alpha_p=(p/2)*sin(h)^4*Sigma_4. Its proved two-term expansion
therefore gives

 alpha_p=theta - [pi^2*(4*theta+5*beta(2))/24]*p^(-2)
                      +O(p^(-4)).                            (4)

For completeness this can be derived directly, with no topological
theorem: cos(x)^3/sin(x)^4=x^-4-(5/6)x^-2+R(x), where R is smooth on
[0,pi/2]. Alternating samples of R have uniformly bounded sum by its
total variation. Alternating tails of n^-4 and n^-2 have bounds p^-4
and p^-2. Hence
 sum chi(n)*cos(nh)^3/sin(nh)^4
 =h^-4*theta -(5/6)h^-2*beta(2)+O(1).
Multiplication by sin(h)^4 and
 (sin(h)/h)^4=1-(2/3)h^2+O(h^4) proves (4).

The coefficient in brackets is strictly positive. Consequently the
principal error theta-alpha_p is nonzero and comparable to p^-2 for
all sufficiently large p. This is not inferred from numerical values.

3. All conjugates: a small pole dominates
----------------------------------------
The embeddings of F_p can be represented uniquely by

 k odd, 1<=k<2p, k!=p,

and their values are
 alpha_(p,k)=sin(kh)^4 sum_(n odd,0<n<p)
                   chi(n)*cos(knh)^3/sin(knh)^4.             (5)
There are d=p-1 of them, with exactly one representative for each pair
of complex embeddings k and -k modulo4p. In this convention
 alpha_(p,1)=alpha_p and alpha_(p,2p-1)=-alpha_p.
These are two DISTINCT real-field embeddings.

Let f(x)=cos(x)^3/sin(x)^4 on(0,pi/2), and put
 C=sum_(n odd,n>=1)n^-4=pi^4/96,
 delta=2-C>0.
The function x^4*f(x) is strictly decreasing. To check this, its
logarithmic derivative is4/x-3*tan(x)-4*cot(x). The desired sign is
equivalent to H(x)>0 for
 H(x)=x*(3+cos(x)^2)-4*sin(x)*cos(x).
Since H(0)=0 and H'(x)=7*sin(x)^2-2*x*sin(x)*cos(x)
 >=5*sin(x)^2>0 (use x*cos(x)<=sin(x)), the sign follows.
In particular f(mh)<=f(h)/m^4 for odd1<=m<p.

Multiplication by k permutes the pairs {n,2p-n} of odd nonzero residues
modulo2p. For every n in our half set, write kn=2p*l+epsilon*m with
epsilon=+/-1 and odd0<m<p. Then f(knh)=(-1)^l*f(mh).
Therefore the sum in (5) is sum_m eta_m*f(mh), with EVERY eta_m=+/-1
and EVERY odd m in the half set appearing exactly once. Regardless
of these signs, the m1 term outweighs the whole tail:

 delta*f(h) <= |sum_m eta_m*f(mh)| <= C*f(h).

It follows uniformly over all embeddings that

 delta*cos(h)^3*[sin(kh)/sin(h)]^4
   <= |alpha_(p,k)|
   <= C*cos(h)^3*[sin(kh)/sin(h)]^4.                        (6)

This explicitly counts every conjugate rather than estimating only
the physical value. It explains why a normalizer small at k1 is costly
elsewhere: sin(kh) is of order1 for most k.

For p>=5 and k notin{1,2p-1}, sin(kh)>=sin(3h) and
 sin(3h)/sin(h)=3-4*sin(h)^2>=2.
Using delta>0.98 and cos(h)^3>=3*sqrt(3)/8>0.64, (6) gives
 |alpha_(p,k)|>10.                                      (7)
The principal value is positive by the alternating decreasing sum,
and at most C<1.02. Thus it is the ONLY conjugate in(0,2).
No nontrivial embedding fixes alpha_p, proving [Q(alpha_p):Q]=d.
For p3, alpha_3=3*sqrt(3)/8 has degree2=d directly.

4. Complete product and actual primitive growth
-----------------------------------------------
The exact sine product over our embedding representatives is

 product_(k odd,1<=k<2p,k!=p) sin(kh)=2^(1-p)=2^-d.        (8)

For example, (8) follows by evaluating the factorization of x^p+1
at x1 and taking absolute values, then removing the k=p term (which
has sine1). Equivalently it is the usual product over odd sine angles.
Applying (6) and (8),

 [delta*cos(h)^3/(16*sin(h)^4)]^d
 <= product_k |alpha_(p,k)|
 <= [C*cos(h)^3/(16*sin(h)^4)]^d.                         (9)

Since theta lies in(80/81,1), for the d-2 nonprincipal embeddings (7)
implies
 0.9*|alpha_(p,k)| < |theta-alpha_(p,k)|
                         < 1.1*|alpha_(p,k)|.
For k1, use (4); for k=2p-1, theta+alpha_p stays bounded above and
below by positive constants. Also alpha_p itself stays bounded above
and below by positive constants. Replacing the two principal factors
in (9) therefore changes the logarithm only by -2*log(p)+O(1), and
all other replacements change it by O(d). As sin(h)~pi/(2p),

 log|M_p(theta)|=4*d*log(p)+O(d).                         (10)

In particular M_p(theta) is nonzero for all large p. The actual
primitive polynomial satisfies

 |M_p(theta)| <= |P_p(theta)| <= 8^d*|M_p(theta)|,

because1<=g_p<=8^d. Hence

 log|P_p(theta)|=4*(p-1)*log(p)+O(p) -> +infinity.         (11)

This is a statement about the actual primitive polynomial in (3),
not an inference from a sufficient denominator-clearing estimate.
The separate minimal-degree argument in Section3 also shows it is
the primitive minimal polynomial of alpha_p. No conclusion is being
transferred silently from an ambient polynomial to one of its factors.

5. Prescribed checks and boundaries
-----------------------------------
Run: python3 collaboration/round-11/norms/check_sine_norms.py
Output: checks.json.

For p3,5,7,11 the script constructs U_p in Z[w]/Phi_(4p), verifies
reality and the sign involution, computes the exact resultant, and
finds the expected square of a degree(p-1) monic polynomial. It then
clears the factor8, computes the COMPLETE integer content, and keeps
the primitive polynomial. The contents are1,1,64,1; no assumed uniform
gcd has been used. Their log absolute primitive evaluations at theta
are approximately3.57,14.32,25.43,67.93.

Separate100-digit diagnostics at p11,31,101 check every conjugate in
(6), the principal p^-2 error and the full product. These numerical
checks are not certified intervals, proofs of the asymptotics, or
formal verification. The all-prime argument is the proof above.

The source of (1) is the round10 even-power extension, itself inspired
by Marché--Masbaum's CUBIC integer-signature formula. None of their
theorems assert the present even-power integrality or norm estimate;
these have been derived explicitly. Their original source/read scope
is frozen in round10/fresh/source_scope.json. The present proof imports
only elementary root-of-unity and trigonometric identities, all supplied.

No larger scan is warranted by this family. A repair would have to
change the distribution of the conjugates (or introduce a different
arithmetic mechanism), not just sharpen the principal expansion.
