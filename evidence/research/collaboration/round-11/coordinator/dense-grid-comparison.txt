THE DENSE-QUARTER NUMERATOR REMOVES ITS APPARENT POTENTIAL GAIN
29 October 2026. Local and unpublished. Analytic comparison only.

Archive input: round10/coordinator/dense-quarter-gate.txt and the
independent closed-residue formula in round10/fresh/dense_quarter_review.txt.
This is a short exact consequence, not another parameter scan.

Write R_K=S_K/D_K, a_j=(2j+1)/4. The closed residues give

 R_K(t)=sum_(j=0)^(K-1)
       (-1)^j 4^K/[(K-1-j)!(K+j)!] *1/(t+a_j^2).

Its coefficient at1/t at infinity is

 c_K=4^K/[(2K-1)(K-1)!^2],

because sum_(j=0)^(K-1)(-1)^j binom(2K-1,K-1-j)
=binom(2K-2,K-1). The last identity follows immediately by Pascal
telescoping. Thus

 R_K(t)/c_K=sum_j (-1)^j b_j/(t+a_j^2),
 b_j=(2K-1)(K-1)!^2/[(K-1-j)!(K+j)!],
 b_0=2-1/K,
 b_(j+1)/b_j=(K-1-j)/(K+j+1)<1.

For t>=0 the positive summands b_j/(t+a_j^2) strictly decrease.
An alternating finite sum lies between its first-minus-second term
and its first term, for either parity of K>=2. Since
b_1/b_0=(K-1)/(K+1) and a_1>a_0,

 [2b_0/(K+1)]/(t+1/16) <= R_K(t)/c_K <= b_0/(t+1/16).  (1)

The inequalities are strict here but non-strict ones suffice. This is
uniform on the entire positive axis, not just at a scaling limit.

Consequently, for any r linearly independent real polynomial sources
p_1,...,p_r, let G_K be their Gram matrix for the positive density
(R_K/c_K)w and G_0 that for w/(t+1/16), where w is the round10
positive Hurwitz density and t=y^2. Then, as positive matrices,

 ell_K G_0 <= G_K <= u_K G_0,
 ell_K=2b_0/(K+1), u_K=b_0,

so

 ell_K^r det G_0 <= det G_K <= u_K^r det G_0.             (2)

Proof: conjugate by G_0^(-1/2), whose existence follows from strict
positive definiteness, then multiply the positive eigenvalue bounds.
An additional fixed or K-dependent nonnegative multiplier can be put
into both positive densities without changing the same comparison.

When r=O(K), the logarithmic comparison error in (2) is O(K log K),
which is o(K^2). Thus the cancellation numerator in THIS construction
removes the tempting K^2-scale benefit of the denser denominator.
The rational scalar c_K changes no primitive polynomial on its own.

Scope: (2) compares analytic Gram values. The fixed-pole matrix is not
being asserted to have the same period space, degree, primitive content
or coefficient height. No general primitive-growth obstruction follows.
This identifies why more pole-count optimization lacks a specific
analytic advantage; it does not rule out a different interpolation,
signed source, proper subspace or arithmetic gain.
