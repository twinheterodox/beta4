Setup
-----
Let n>=1, h=2n, K=8n+4, a_j=(2j+1)/4 for0<=j<K, and put

 D(x)=product_(j=0)^(K-1)(x+a_j),
 V(x)=product_(m=1)^n(x-m)^4.

Let Lambda be the symmetric imaginary-line functional of fresh's note:
Lambda(P)=integral_0^infinity [P(iy)+P(-iy)]w(y)dy/2,
w(y)=-y^4 f'''(y)/3, f(y)=1/(exp(2pi y)-1). Its pole value is

 Lambda(1/(x+a))=a^4 zeta(4,a)-1/2-a/3.

For ell=0,...,2h-2, set R_ell=V(x)x^ell/D(x), and
H_ij=Lambda(R_(i+j)). Its degree at infinity is at most-6.
Thus the partial fractions of x^4 R have total residue zero; the
zeta4 coefficient cancels, as do the rational boundary pieces above.
Writing theta=beta4, every entry is B_ell+A_ell theta, with

 r_(j,ell)=V(-a_j)(-a_j)^ell/D'(-a_j),
 T_j=sum_(0<=s<floor(j/2))(s+(1 if j even else3)/4)^(-4),
H_ij=sum_k r_(k,i+j) a_k^4 [128(-1)^k theta-T_k].        (1)

All finite denominators in T_j are odd integers after multiplication
by4; equivalently T_j=256 sum_(r<j,r=j mod2)(2r+1)^(-4).

Theorem
-------
Assume theta=c is rational. Let p=8n+5 be prime and p not divide the
denominator of c. Then

                     v_p(det H(c))=-4h=-8n.             (2)

Hence det H(theta) is nonzero for infinitely many n under the assumed
rationality, using primes p=5 mod8 outside the finitely many denominator
primes. This is sufficient nonvanishing for a possible irrationality
contradiction if suitable size estimates were later proved.

Proof
-----
Here K=p-1, so all factors in D'(-a_j) are p-units: the differences
are (k-j)/2 with0<|k-j|<p. Also c is p-integral.

Set j0=(p-1)/2=4n+2, which is even. Since all odd denominators in T_j
are below2p, the only possible p-divisible one is p itself. It occurs
exactly at

                j=j0+2r, 1<=r<=h.

At these nodes a_j=p/4+r, so a_j=r modp and
p^4 T_j=256 modp; at all other nodes p^4 T_j=0 modp.
Thus p^4 H(c) is integral and, modulo p, is the discrete Gram matrix

     sum_(r=1)^h w_r v_r v_r^t,
     v_r=(1,-r,(-r)^2,...,(-r)^(h-1))^t,
     w_r=-256 r^4 V(-r)/D'(-a_(j0+2r)).                  (3)

Each weight is nonzero modulo p. Indeed r and each r+m are between1
and3n<p, and the denominator was already a unit. There are exactly h
distinct nodes, hence (3) has determinant
product_r w_r * product_(r<s)(s-r)^2, a p-unit. This proves (2).

For an explicit residue check, the roots -a_j,0<=j<p-1, are all of
F_p except1/4. Therefore D(x)=(x^p-x)/(x-1/4) modp and

 1/D'(-a_(j0+2r))=r+1/4 modp,
 w_r=-64 r^4(4r+1) product_(m=1)^n(r+m)^4 modp.          (4)

All factors in (4) are nonzero, since4r+1<=8n+1<p.

Infinitely many admissible primes need no progression theorem. Given a
finite set of forbidden odd primes, choose an odd A divisible by 5 and
all of them. Every prime divisor of A^2+4 avoids that set and is 1 mod 4,
since -1 is a square modulo it. As A^2+4 is 5 mod 8, at least one such
prime is 5 mod 8. It is not 5, so it has n>=1. Include the odd denominator
primes of c in the forbidden set. This gives infinitely many admissible n.

Scope
-----
No irrationality of theta is assumed to infer this; the proof begins
with its hypothetical rationality. It does not prove unconditional
nonvanishing at the actual unknown theta for every n, nor any estimate
on the primitive integer polynomial's real value. The latter remains
the central failure of the tested source. No larger index scan is
justified merely by obtaining nonvanishing.
