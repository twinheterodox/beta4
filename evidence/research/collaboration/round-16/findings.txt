BETA(4): LANDSCAPE REVIEW AND ONE RECOMMENDED NEXT INVESTIGATION
30 October 2026. Local and unpublished. Review completed; no new campaign.

Verdict

Recommend a bounded arithmetic feasibility study of nonprincipal,
interleaved-border minors of the positive cubic-tail kernel from round 1.
The new ingredient to test is the border geometry and local pairing
mechanism used in Litsey's trigamma work [L], applied to the beta(4)-only
kernel whose positivity and real size we already understand.

This is a recommendation about the value of the next test, not a claim
of favorable arithmetic, novelty relative to all literature, or proximity
to an irrationality proof. No construction presently has a proved favorable
complete budget. I retain only ONE developed proposal. The alternative
ideas considered below do not presently justify separate campaigns.

1. What this review covered and what it changes

Read all fifteen integrated round reports, the handover and archive index,
the complete method map and next-proof-avenues record, and the arcsine
investigation. Re-read underlying proofs where they determine the proposed
next step. Source scopes and unchecked dependencies are in sources.txt and
literature/reading-round-16.json; broad coverage is in landscape-coverage.txt.
This is not a new audit of every proof or computation in the archive, nor
an exhaustive reading of all relevant mathematics.

The distinction that most changes the research decision is between:

  (a) a lower bound for the ACTUAL primitive value of a specified family;
  (b) a structural obstruction under stated hypotheses;
  (c) failure of available upper estimates or of one normalization;
  (d) finite unfavorable examples or an unproved required step.

archive-audit.txt assigns the previous results to these categories.
In particular, rounds 14-15 did not prove that all primitive forms in their
families fail. The principal positive kernel, in contrast, has an actual
primitive-value obstruction. That theorem explicitly excludes different
row and column sets, while its positivity and Cauchy comparison cover them.
This is a substantive gap in the obstruction's hypotheses, not merely
another choice of scalar normalization for the same polynomial.

The repeated losses have come from isolating the period before examining
its integral structure, multiplying growing coefficients to eliminate
other periods, using principal frames, and estimating prime valuations
entry by entry. A new identity, gauge or interpretation alone repairs none
of those losses. The proposed frame changes the determinant polynomial
and its finite-prime rank structure while preserving target support and
nonvanishing.

2. The proposed family, completely specified

Write theta=beta(4), and for positive integers a set

  S_l(a)=sum_(j=0)^(a-1) [(4j+1)^(-l)-(4j+3)^(-l)].

The round-1 construction [A1] gives

  K(a^2,b^2)=[b S_3(b)-a S_3(a)]/[6(b^2-a^2)]   (a != b),
  K(a^2,a^2)=theta-S_4(a)+S_3(a)/(12a).

It also gives a positive integral representation

  K(x,y)=integral_0^infinity omega(s)/[(x+s^2)(y+s^2)] ds,
  omega(s)=(pi^2/96)s^2[1+A(s)-2A(s)^3],
  A(s)=sech(2 pi s).

The bracket is (1-A)(2A^2+2A+1)>0 for s>0. Andreief's identity and the
Cauchy determinant formula show that EVERY ordered square minor is
strictly positive, including nonsymmetric minors. The proof uses the
positive product of the two Cauchy determinants, not entrywise comparison.

Fix 0<c<=1, let m=floor(cr/2), and choose

  C={1,...,r},
  R=C union {r+1,r+3,...,r+2m-1},
  Q=C union {r+2,r+4,...,r+2m}.

Rows and columns are in increasing order. The matrix has size N=r+m.
Replace theta in the r common diagonal entries by X and call the resulting
rational matrix Phi_r,c(X). Put P_r,c(X)=det Phi_r,c(X).

Three facts already follow from [A1], with no new nonvanishing assumption:

  * P_r,c lies in Q[X] and P_r,c(theta)>0 for every r.
  * Its degree is exactly r. Its X^r coefficient is the disjoint-border
    minor, a strictly positive rational number (or 1 when m=0).
  * Uniformly over positive integer node sets,
      log det K(a_i^2,b_j^2)=log det[1/(a_i+b_j)]+O(N).

For the last fact the explicit lower and upper factors are

  [c0/(1+tau0)^2]^N det Cauchy <= det K <= (M c0)^N det Cauchy,
  c0=pi^3/192, tau0=1/(sqrt(2)pi), M=1+2/(3sqrt(6)).

Thus the real estimate has no hidden order-r^2 loss to recover. The
prospective gain must come from the arithmetic of a genuinely new frame.

3. Exact real-size benchmark

The empirical row and column measures, with mass normalized by r, both
tend to the density rho_c which is 1 on [0,1] and 1/2 on [1,1+c].
Equivalently rho_c=(1_[0,1]+1_[0,1+c])/2. The Cauchy product gives

  log P_r,c(theta)=-E(c)r^2+o(r^2),
  E(c)=integral integral rho_c(x)rho_c(y)
                         log((x+y)/|x-y|) dx dy.

Justification: the two within-node Vandermonde sums contribute one copy
of the log|x-y| double integral in total; the cross denominator contributes
the log(x+y) integral. The log r terms leave only -N log r=o(r^2).
Log singularities are integrable. Truncating them within distance epsilon,
using the bounded spacing/density of these grids, and then letting epsilon
tend to zero justifies the Riemann-sum limit. The O(N) kernel comparison
does not affect it.

For a,b>0 set

  I(a,b)=[(a+b)^2 log(a+b)+(a-b)^2 log|a-b|
                        -2a^2 log a-2b^2 log b]/2,

where 0^2 log 0=0. Two elementary integrations show that I is the same
log-ratio integral over [0,a] x [0,b]. Therefore

  E(c)=[I(1,1)+2I(1,1+c)+I(1+c,1+c)]/4.

  c          N/r          degree/r          E(c)
  0          1            1                 1.386294361120
  1/5        1.1          1                 1.652307066308
  1/2        1.25         1                 2.058598491557
  1          1.5          1                 2.818451239783

E(0)=2 log 2; E(1)=(log 2)/2+(9 log 3)/4. benchmarks.py independently
checks the closed expression against one-dimensional quadrature after
integrating in the other variable. The decimals are checks, not interval
certificates. This evaluates an existing determinant formula; it is not a
new beta(4) determinant or parameter scan.

4. Decisive missing lemma and the exact quantitative target

Let D_r,c=den(P_r,c), the least positive INTEGER clearing its coefficients.
It would suffice to prove, for one fixed c>0 and some delta>0,

  log D_r,c <= [E(c)-delta]r^2+o(r^2).                         (T)

Indeed, theta=u/v would make v^r D_r,c P_r,c(theta) a positive integer
whose logarithm tends to minus infinity. The v^r cost is O(r), whereas
the strict margin is of order r^2. A rational primitive normalization might
do still better; (T) is a sufficient target, not a necessary condition for
all ways of using the frame.

A useful local form of the missing lemma is a collection of valid
primewise upper bounds

  e_p(r,c)=v_p(D_r,c) <= r w_(c,j)(p/r)+O(1), p= j mod 4,

with a finite piecewise-integrable envelope and all its ranges justified,
such that

  A(c)=(1/2)sum_(j=1,3) integral_0^infinity w_(c,j)(x) dx < E(c).

PNT in progressions would then sum these bounds. Here x=p/r; an x^-2
Jacobian belongs to the different variable r/p and must NOT be inserted.
No candidate w satisfying this inequality has yet been proved or observed.

All denominator primes are <=4(r+2m), apart from fixed constants already
in that range for large r. For p^2>4(r+2m), p>3, every entry has valuation
at least -4. If p divides both nodes, the two factors in b^2-a^2 cost p^2,
but the numerator also has a factor p. Other cases cost at most one extra
p beyond S_3. Small primes contribute o(r^2) by the crude O(N log r) bound
per prime and at most O(sqrt(r)) such primes. Fixed primes are included.

This identifies the needed theorem as a finite-prime determinant problem,
not a missing real-size or nonvanishing theorem.

5. What transfers from Litsey, and what definitely does not

Litsey [L, Sections 2-4] uses a common block and interleaved disjoint
borders for trigamma kernels. His finite-prime method combines assignment
bounds, rank deficiencies, and pairings of row and column kernel vectors
through successive prime layers. These are the source of the proposed
geometry and arithmetic test, not a beta(4) theorem.

For the Gauss valuation v on Q(X), if B=diag(p^u)Phi diag(p^v) is integral,
tau=sum u+sum v, then elementary determinant/Smith arguments give

  v(det Phi)>=-tau+corank(B mod p),
  e_p<=max(0,tau-corank(B mod p)).

The max with zero must be retained when using this abstract statement.
If n1 rows and n2 columns can be made divisible by p with their common
corner divisible by p^2, then v(det B)>=n1+n2. These elementary facts
do not depend on Litsey's final theorem or computational certificates.

There is an explicit first-order bridge for OUR kernel. For p>3 and
p^2>4(r+2m), set B=p^4 Phi and

  V(a)=a p^3 S_3(a) mod p.

When a^2 and b^2 are different modulo p, direct substitution gives

  B_ab = p [V(b)-V(a)]/[6(b^2-a^2)]  mod p^2.                (B)

Thus B mod p is block diagonal on the squared-node fibres. If p-integral
vectors y and z are supported in two distinct fibres with residues s and t,
respectively (with reductions over F_p(X)), then

  y^T B z = p/[6(t-s)] det[[sum y, sum y V],
                           [sum z, sum z V]]  mod p^2.

This congruence is an immediate algebraic consequence of the displayed
kernel, not a claim of a full content bound. It gives the same two-moment
pairing shape as [L, Lemma 4.3]. Proportional pairs kill this pairing.

The unresolved issue is obtaining suitable local null vectors, transporting
their moments across the border, and lifting enough cancellations through
ALL relevant layers. Fibres now merge a and -a modulo p; the zero fibre
can have two denominator factors; the partial sums are cubic; common
diagonal entries are quartic. Litsey's cut-free translation lemma, his
profile constants and his positivity/selection machinery CANNOT simply
be imported. Only the matrix design and elementary local ideas are used.

6. A modest first test, with an explicit stopping rule

For a subsequent bounded investigation, preselect c=1/5,1/2,1. Do not
begin with a broad search over kernels or nodes.

  (i) Derive the local fibre blocks and the next prime layers beyond (B),
      including the zero fibre and the a versus -a interaction. Determine
      whether border kernel vectors have transportable moment data and
      whether this yields a saving of order r for a nonzero interval of p/r.
      Without such a systematic mechanism there is no reason to run a
      large determinant scan.

 (ii) Use a small predetermined check, for example r=8,16,24 at those
      widths, to compare exact coefficient Gauss valuations with the
      proposed local rule, keeping all layers and recording any numerator
      content separately. Computations can falsify a rule; they do not
      establish an asymptotic saving. These checks have NOT been run here.

(iii) Before building an all-primes certificate, compare the most favorable
      justified/explicitly conjectural local profile with the E(c) values.
      Separate proved from conjectural pieces. Continue only if there is
      a plausible strict margin and a specific remaining lemma to certify.

If the transport fails, or even the mechanism's optimistic profile misses
the budget, stop this implementation. A failed sufficient denominator
estimate is not a theorem that primitive numerator content cannot help.
Conversely, finite gcd savings must not justify an indefinite new campaign.

7. Why this is preferable to the other ideas considered

The alternating half-grid common-denominator idea was initially a second
candidate. The archive check removed it. Round 12 already computes the
extra beta(2) coordinate introduced by naive alternating half-grid sampling;
round 14 retains a complete alternating Whipple family at -1, including
its polynomial Abel part, and prices its scalar projection. Dilating the
grid can just return the quarter construction. No new complete identity
or cheaper integral module was specified by the renewed suggestion.

A common denominator is a classical principle, not a new mechanism in
itself. Van Assche's 2024 lecture [V] explicitly constructs a shared
denominator at i using symmetry, yet its supplied sufficient LCM estimate
does not prove shrinking integer forms. This is a warning about a bound,
not an actual primitive-value impossibility theorem. It supports checking
the FULL numerator and projection budget before importing round-15 rates
into a hypothetical different construction.

The generic arcsine parameter-jet idea is also already developed in
arcsine-contact.txt, with boundary recurrences, prime layers and 202 finite
tests. Its remaining mixed-frame questions need a new design. The present
positive kernel avoids its extra-period projection issue.

Prime-depleted germs remain a genuinely different reserve possibility.
Round 4 [A4] proves log^+ primitive multiplier=o(n^2) for a growing sieve.
The missing uniform real decay/nonvanishing is serious: the moving germ
has many new singularities, and one explicit oversieved family loses its
apparently small factor under primitive reduction. No new moving-cut
analytic mechanism was found in this review. It is less ready for a short
decisive test than the frame whose complete real estimate is available.

Holonomy, modular and geometric methods remain conceptually open. The
archive closes particular functions, coordinates and modules; it does not
close those theories. But we do not have a new calibrated beta(4) module
whose rationality hypothesis improves continuation enough to beat its
arithmetic dimension cost, nor a new geometry meeting a usable criterion's
full hypotheses. Structural E-value and p-adic ideas likewise lack a proved
bridge to this real constant. They are research aspirations, not yet
quantitatively grounded alternatives to the recommended test.

8. Limits, provenance and stopping point

This review does not establish that the bordered frame succeeds, that it
is globally optimal, or that no better idea exists. It identifies a local
problem with more of its surrounding proof already in place. Reopening
some old obstruction's complement is not evidence of a favorable margin.

labelled separately from the elementary derivations and the source reading.
