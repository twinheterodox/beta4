Round 03: dyadic residue ranks for joint completed-ladder Pade forms
28 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

Status

The arithmetic gate has a partial positive answer: the joint determinant
does have provable dyadic cancellation, and preserving the common powers
of the three arguments improves its sufficient dyadic multiplier rate
from (5/2)n^2 to (1/2)n^2. A coefficient denominator statement alone would
not establish this: the exact leading residue ranks of the bordered
matrices are proved below.

The fourth odd-prime layer nevertheless survives. A coordinator-derived
recursion, independently checked here, determines its exact exponent
unless a smaller endpoint pencil has a modular defect. There is an actual
exception, so universal normality must not be assumed.

These are local arithmetic theorems and upper bounds for one prime's part
of the ACTUAL primitive multiplier. They are not an upper bound for the
whole multiplier, an asymptotic formula for primitive height, an
irrationality proof, or an actual-value no-go theorem. No larger generic
Pade search was conducted. The old round-02 files were preserved.

1. Families and the precise endpoint normalization

The classical completed identity and the constants below are inherited
from round-02/fresh/findings.txt, which was read together with its
simultaneous.txt companion. Their Broadhurst source identity is not being
claimed as a new result or independently reproved here.

Write

 z_2=i/2, z_3=(1+i)/4, z_5=(1-i)/8,
 (c_2,c_3,c_5)=(225/136,-25/34,9/68),
 alpha_m=m/2, theta=beta(4).

I_s(z t) denotes the rational-coefficient imaginary-part germ, obtained
by subtracting the conjugate-argument polylogarithm and dividing by 2i.

The old equal-speed germ is

 H_old(t)=sum_m c_m [I_4(z_m t)+alpha_m ell(t) I_3(z_m t)
                       +(alpha_m^2/3)ell(t)^2 I_2(z_m t)],
 ell(t)=-log(1-t/2).

The PRIMARY new joint germ, proposed by the analysis worker, preserves
the common-power relation. Put q=(1-i)/2; then z_m=-q^m for m=2,3,5.
Define

 H_new(t)=sum_m c_m [I_4(z_m t^m)+(m/2)L_0(t) I_3(z_m t^m)
                       +(m^2/12)L_0(t)^2 I_2(z_m t^m)],
 L_0(t)=-log(1-t^2/2).                                     (1.1)

Both have rational Taylor coefficients, vanish at zero, and have value
theta at t=1. The different powers 2,3,5 in (1.1) mean it is not a common
reindexing of H_old. Its singularities all have modulus sqrt(2); the
separate analytic packet treats its real approximation problem.

For either series H(t)=sum_(j>=1) h_j t^j, put h_0=0 and

 T_ij=h_(i+j)-h_(i+j-1),  b_i=h_i,  1<=i,j<=n,

 J_n(X)=det [[X,-b^T],[b,T]]
       =A_n X+C_n,
 A_n=det T,  C_n=b^T adj(T)b.                               (1.2)

For n=0 set J_0(X)=X. Up to the sign (-1)^n, (1.2) is the leading
Hankel determinant of c_j(X)=X-sum_(l=1)^j h_l. Thus it is exactly the
ordinary diagonal-Pade endpoint form q_n(1)X-p_n(1), up to a rational
factor, whenever the usual normalized system is nonsingular. The
coordinator checked that comparison through separate Pade equations.

For a nonzero J_n define

 M_n=lcm(den A_n,den C_n),
 g_n=gcd(M_n A_n,M_n C_n)>0,
 kappa_n=M_n/g_n,
 P_n=kappa_n J_n primitive integral.                       (1.3)

In particular

             v_p(kappa_n)=-min(v_p(A_n),v_p(C_n)).           (1.4)

The multiplier can be rational. Set P_n=0 separately when J_n is the
zero polynomial; no multiplier is assigned in that case. There is no
factor 6 in this round's normalization.

2. The local quadratic extension is only a valuation tool

Work temporarily in K=Q_2(i), with uniformizer pi=1-i. Its valuation
nu is normalized by nu(pi)=1, so nu(2)=2 and nu(x)=2v_2(x) for rational
x. The valuation ring has residue field F_2. Thus every unit reduces
to 1. These algebraic row and column scalings are used to analyze the
rational polynomial (1.2), not to substitute algebraic coefficients
for the required integer forms.

We use the elementary rank-divisibility fact: if an N-by-N integral
matrix reduces to rank R over F_2, its determinant is divisible by
pi^(N-R). It also holds coefficientwise for the bordered pencil here:
the target entry reduces to zero, and a unit R-by-R minor of the
constant block can be used to eliminate R rows and columns over the
valuation ring. Every remaining entry is divisible by pi.

In all scalings below the final polynomial scalar is a Gaussian unit
times an integral power of 2. Its coefficient valuations are therefore
even integers under nu. A lower bound nu>=d can consequently be rounded
up to nu>=2ceil(d/2). This parity point supplies the ceilings in the
primitive multiplier bounds.

3. Old equal-speed H: a rank-four leading residue, not a scalar inference

The prior exact coefficient theorem says v_2(h_j)=-E_j, where

 E_(4a)=10a+4 (a>=1), E_(4a+1)=10a+5,
 E_(4a+2)=10a+11,      E_(4a+3)=10a+12.

Let z=z_5=pi/8, so nu(z)=-5, and define

 f_j=-64 z^(-j)h_j=pi^12 z^(-j)h_j.

These coefficients are integral, with valuations respectively

 j mod4:        0   1   2   3
 nu(f_j):       4   7   0   3.                              (3.1)

Consequently

               sum_j f_j x^j = x^2/(1-x^4) mod pi.          (3.2)

The scaled T entry is

 -64 z^(-i-j)T_ij=f_(i+j)-z^(-1)f_(i+j-1).

Since nu(z^(-1))=5, it has the SAME leading residue as f_(i+j).
Multiplying the full bordered matrix by -64 and scaling its row and
column i by z^(-i), i=0,...,n, gives residue

                 1_(i+j=2 mod4),  0<=i,j<=n.               (3.3)

The target entry vanishes modulo pi and agrees with this pattern at
(0,0). For n>=3 all four residue classes of indices occur. Rows in
the same class agree, and the four different classes have disjoint
nonempty column supports. Thus the rank is EXACTLY FOUR.

The determinant scaling factor has dyadic valuation

 B_old(n)=5n(n+1)/2+6(n+1).

The rank theorem and the even-valuation observation give the proved
primitive-multiplier bound

 v_2(kappa_n) <= B_old(n)-ceil((n-3)/2),   n>=3, J_n!=0.      (3.4)

Hence limsup v_2(kappa_n)/n^2<=5/2 for this old joint family.
The rank-four result proves genuine determinant cancellation. Its first
layer supplies only the displayed linear saving; it does NOT establish
a further quadratic saving, an exact determinant valuation, or a
primitive-value obstruction. In particular the scalar truncation
failure theorem from round 02 does not transfer to these determinants.

4. The common-base germ has three integral depleted polylog blocks

Set x=q t, with q=pi/2 and nu(q)=-1. Then

 I_s(z_m t^m) becomes
 B_(m,s)(x)=[Li_s(-x^m)-Li_s(-i^m x^m)]/(2i),

 [x^(mr)]B_(m,s)=(-1)^r(1-i^(mr))/(2i r^s).                 (4.1)

For m=2 this coefficient vanishes when r is even; otherwise it is
2-adically integral. For m=3,5 it vanishes when 4 divides r. At odd r
its pi-valuation is -1. At r=2 mod4 its valuation is -2s. In particular
the polylogarithmic denominators are UNIFORMLY bounded at 2; they do not
cost s*log_2 N. This depletion is specific to the argument differences.

Define the normalized blocks

 C(x)=-64 sum_m c_m B_(m,4)(x),
 B(x)=-64 sum_m c_m (m/2) B_(m,3)(x),
 A(x)=-64 sum_m c_m (m^2/12) B_(m,2)(x).

Also put L(x)=-log(1-i x^2). The exact identity is

           -64 H_new(x/q)=C(x)+L(x)B(x)+L(x)^2 A(x).         (4.2)

All three blocks A,B,C lie in the valuation ring coefficientwise.
More precisely they all have the SAME reduction:

                 A(x)=B(x)=C(x)=x^10/(1-x^20) mod pi.       (4.3)

Proof of the constants and reduction. Before multiplication by -64,
the worst pi-valuations for the three sources are bounded as follows:

                m=2       m=3       m=5
 pure Li4         -6        -10       -12
 one-log block    -6        -10       -12
 two-log block    -6        -10       -12.

For m=3,5 the worst case is r=2 mod4. Thus only m=5,r=2 mod4 can
survive after multiplication by pi^12=-64; these degrees are 10 mod20.
Writing r=2u with u odd, the respective coefficients of C,B,A are

             9i/(17u^4), 45i/(17u^3), 75i/(17u^2),

all units with residue 1. All other nonzero coefficients have positive
pi-valuation. This proves (4.3), not merely a lower denominator bound.

5. The logarithm layer and exact bordered residue rank

The coefficients at x^(2s) are

 [x^(2s)]L=i^s/s,
 [x^(2s)]L^2=2 i^s H_(s-1)/s,  s>=2.                       (5.1)

The exact harmonic valuation is
v_2(H_j)=-floor(log_2 j): among 1,...,j there is a unique denominator
with maximal power of 2. Therefore

 v_2(2 H_(s-1)/s)=1-v_2(s)-floor(log_2(s-1)).                (5.2)

For n>=8 put

 r=floor(log_2 n), delta=2r-2, N=n+1.

Through x-degree 2n, 2^delta L^2 is integral. Its reduction has exactly
the two possible terms

       2^delta L^2 = x^(2^(r+1))+x^(3*2^r) mod pi,           (5.3)

truncated at degree 2n. Indeed equality in the worst denominator
exponent 2r-2 occurs only at s=2^r and s=3*2^(r-1). For s<2^r the
maximum exponent is at most 2r-4; for 2^r<s<2^(r+1), the only integer
with v_2(s)=r-1 is 3*2^(r-1). The two surviving coefficients are units.
Since delta-r=r-2>=1, both 2^delta and 2^delta L vanish modulo pi.

Combining (4.2)--(5.3), define

 lambda=-64*2^delta,
 f_j=lambda q^(-j)h_j.

Then f_j is integral through degree 2n and its exact residue series is

 sum_(j<=2n) f_j x^j
   = x^10 [x^(2^(r+1))+x^(3*2^r)]/(1-x^20) mod pi.         (5.4)

As before the scaled T entry is f_(i+j)-q^(-1)f_(i+j-1).
Now nu(q^(-1))=1, so its residue is still f_(i+j). The first nonzero
degree in (5.4) is

                         d=2^(r+1)+10>n.                  (5.5)

Thus the entire target row and column vanish modulo pi after scaling,
and the n-by-n lower block has exact rank

                     R=max(0,2n-d+1)
                      =max(0,2n-2^(r+1)-9).                (5.6)

For R>0 its last R rows and columns form an anti-triangular matrix with
unit anti-diagonal; all preceding rows and columns vanish. Later terms
of (5.4), including possible cancellations between the two progressions,
cannot change that determinant. If d>2n the residue matrix is zero.
This proves (5.6) for the bordered (n+1)-matrix as well as the lower
block. It is an exact residue-rank theorem at every n>=8.

6. What the rank proves for the ACTUAL primitive multiplier

The normalized bordered determinant is

 lambda^(n+1) q^(-n(n+1)) J_n(X)
   =det [[lambda X,-f^T],[f,W]],                            (6.1)

where W_ij=f_(i+j)-q^(-1)f_(i+j-1). Its scalar is a Gaussian unit times
2^B, with

               B=n(n+1)/2+(6+delta)(n+1).                  (6.2)

The exact rank R in (5.6) makes every coefficient on the right divisible
by pi^(n+1-R). As those coefficient valuations are even, (1.4) gives

 v_2(kappa_n) <= n(n+1)/2+(6+delta)(n+1)
                          -ceil((n+1-R)/2),                (6.3)

for all n>=8 with J_n nonzero. In particular

              limsup v_2(kappa_n)/n^2 <= 1/2.               (6.4)

This is a bound on the actual primitive multiplier, not merely a
statement that a particular entrywise denominator clears the matrix.
The last term in (6.3) is a proved common divisibility of the determinant
coefficients after the natural grading. It does not determine all higher
elementary divisors or assert equality in (6.3).

The main leading-rate improvement, from 5/2 to 1/2, comes from the new
common-power construction and graded row/column normalization. The
leading residue rank then gives the explicit additional saving in
(6.3). These sources of improvement should not be conflated. No
quadratic saving beyond the rate 1/2 has been established here.

Neither (6.3) nor (6.4) bounds the whole kappa_n: its odd-prime factors
can dominate. Multiplying the raw form by its dyadic part alone is not
in general an integer form. Conversely, a large local denominator
exponent does not automatically appear in the final primitive leading
coefficient; it can cancel that coefficient's raw denominator.

The universal content lemma from round 02 remains available with the
factor 6 removed. If D clears the rational coefficient prefix, put
T0=DT,u=Db, Delta=gcd(det T0, entries of adj(T0)u), and
g=gcd(D det T0,u^T adj(T0)u). Then

                   Delta | g | D Delta^2,
                   kappa_n=D^(n+1)/g.                      (6.5)

Thus unknown higher content is an exact determinantal-divisor problem.
Nothing in the scalar coefficient law or the one-layer rank calculation
proves its absence.

6.1 A real bound compatible with the dyadic part

During coordination the analysis worker supplied a constructive contour
bound which can be paired with (6.4). I checked its elementary geometry.
All reciprocal polylogarithm singularities have radius 1/sqrt(2) and
angles on the 120th-root rays: the three pole classes use orders dividing
8,24,40. The logarithm poles use the real rays, and the possible point
at zero is included. The full 120-ray star is therefore an admissible
cut for the theta-specific germ (theta-H_new(1/w))/(w-1). Its apparent
pole at w=1 is removable because H_new(1)=theta and H_new is analytic
near 1.

The star is the inverse image of [0,2^(-60)] under w -> w^120. Its
capacity is exactly 2^(-31/60). The same Pade/Hankel normalization and
Polya inequality used in round 02 consequently give

 log|J_n(theta)| <= -(31/60)log(2) n^2+o(n^2).

For nonzero J_n put d_n=2^max(0,v_2(kappa_n)); for a zero polynomial set
d_n=1. Combining with (6.4) yields the genuine dyadic-only success

 log|d_n J_n(theta)| <= -(log(2)/60)n^2+o(n^2),               (6.6)

with zero values allowed. This is an improvement in compatibility, not
an integer-form theorem: d_n J_n still has its odd-prime denominators.
The full arithmetic cost has not been bounded by the small margin in
(6.6). Sharper cut geometries, if established by the analytic worker,
are not needed for this particular conclusion.

7. Odd primes: the fourth layer survives with an exact recursion

The coordinator proposed this reduction; I checked its proof and
independently reproduced it using the coefficient builder in this
directory. Let p be prime with

        n/2<p<=n, p^2>2n, p not dividing 2*3*5*17.

Then h_j is p-integral for j<2p, and every h_j through j=2n has
valuation at least -4. At the first possible degree,

          p^4 h_(2p)=chi_4(p)*225/272 mod p.                 (7.1)

Proof. Every summand has a polylogarithm index r and zero, one, or two
logarithm indices s. Its degree is mr+2sum(s), and its total denominator
weight is four. Below degree 2p all these indices are less than p. At
degree 2p, only the pure m=2,r=p Li4 term contributes a fourth layer.
Its residue is (225/136)*chi_4(p)/2^p, which reduces to (7.1). Through
degree 2n all indices are below p^2, so the total denominator weight
gives the asserted four-layer bound. The excluded fixed primes account
for constants and for the unit numerator in (7.1).

Set

            R=2n-2p+1, m=2p-n-1, u=chi_4(p)*225/272.

Scale the last R rows of the bordered matrix by p^4. Cross-block sums
are at most m+n=2p-1, so those entries are integral before scaling.
The lower-right block reduces to an anti-triangular matrix with unit
anti-diagonal u, and the upper-left block is exactly J_m(X). Therefore

       p^(4R) J_n(X)=(-1)^[R(R-1)/2] u^R J_m(X) mod p.       (7.2)

This is coefficientwise. The smaller coefficient prefix is p-integral
because 2m<=2p-2. Other fourth-layer entries, for example at degree 3p,
are below the anti-diagonal and do not invalidate the determinant.
Consequently

       v_p(kappa_n)=4R iff J_m(X) is nonzero modulo p.       (7.3)

For the unconditional indices n=2p-1, one has m=0,R=n,J_0(X)=X and

                         v_p(kappa_(2p-1))=4(2p-1).         (7.4)

The possible exception in (7.3) is real: n=10,p=7 gives R=7,m=3,
but the actual exponent is 27 rather than 28. The smaller J_3 vanishes
modulo 7. This was found by the coordinator and independently verified
here. A uniform assertion of normality would be false even in these
small cases.

The fresh worker supplied a useful PRIME-POWER refinement during review.
Let S be p^4 times the lower-right R-by-R block of the unscaled T. Its
determinant is a p-unit. Both unscaled cross-blocks are p-integral, so
after row scaling the Schur complement of S changes the smaller pencil
by p^4 times an integral matrix. It follows coefficientwise that

                 p^(4R) J_n = det(S) J_m mod p^4.           (7.5)

Put mu_m=min(v_p(A_m),v_p(C_m))>=0, taking the minimum as infinity for
the zero polynomial. If mu_m<4, (7.5) gives the EXACT exponent

                       v_p(kappa_n)=4R-mu_m.               (7.6)

If mu_m>=4 it gives only v_p(kappa_n)<=4R-4. For n=10,p=7 the smaller
content valuation is mu_3=1, explaining precisely the exponent 27.
This refinement has been checked directly modulo p^4 in every local
case saved here, rather than inferred from a modulo-p residue.

Equations (7.2)--(7.4) expose an arithmetic cost surviving the new dyadic
parametrization. They are LOCAL statements. They do not prove a global
lower bound on kappa_n, which may have negative valuations elsewhere,
and they do not give a lower bound on J_n(theta). No actual-value
obstruction follows merely by pairing them with a real upper estimate.

8. A signed Rodrigues subspace with only one residual denominator layer

The analysis worker derived a compatible rational Mellin kernel

 U(x)=900x^2/[17(x^4+4)]-2025x^3/[17(x^6-4x^3+8)]
                         -5625x^5/[17(x^10-8x^5+32)].

With l=log2, r=-log x, W(r)=r^3/6+l*r^2/4+l^2*r/12, its source
integral is I(P)=integral_0^1 P(x)U(x)W(-log x) dx/x and I(1)=theta.
Ordinary monomial Mellin integration gives the moment denominators below.

Let D0 be the least common multiple of the three monic denominators:

 D0=(x^4+4)(x^6-4x^3+8)(x^10-8x^5+32)/(x^2+2x+2)^2.

It has integer coefficients, degree16, D0(0)=256 and D0(1)=25. Put
N0=17D0U, an integer polynomial of degree14, and N=N0/17. For the
analysis worker's source integral I(P), a polynomial P=A+D0V gives

 I(P)=A theta+sum_j b_j/j^4+(log2/2)sum_j b_j/j^3
                          +((log2)^2/12)sum_j b_j/j^2,
                    NV=sum_j b_j x^j.                       (8.1)

Here is a specified INTEGER polynomial subspace satisfying both unwanted
moment constraints. For any S in Z[x] and A in Z set

 F=x(1-x)^2 N0^4 S,
 E=x d/dx,
 V=17 E^3 F/N0,
 P=A+D0V.                                                  (8.2)

Then V and P are integer polynomials. Each application of the derivation
E removes at most one factor N0, so N0 divides E^3 F in Z[x]. Moreover

 NV=E^3 F,
 sum_j b_j/j^3=F(1)=0,
 sum_j b_j/j^2=(EF)(1)=0,
 sum_j b_j/j^4=sum_j f_j/j=integral_0^1 F(x) dx/x.            (8.3)

The double zero at1 and the zero at0 justify the identities directly.
Thus this signed source family has the exact target-only form

                    I(P)=A theta+sum_j f_j/j,               (8.4)

whose rational term has denominator dividing lcm(1,...,deg F). This
is a genuine reduction from four residual integration layers to ONE
for the specified source family. It uses no unproved numerator gcd.
The program checks D0,N0, the integer division, both vanishing moments,
and the one-layer denominator on the exact symbolic example S=1.
The derivation above proves the result for arbitrary integer S.

This is NOT yet an approximation family. A remains free, and S is
unrestricted. No prescribed simultaneous rule for (A_n,S_n), no useful
bound on I(P_n), and no nonvanishing theorem have been supplied. Choosing
these coefficients by first approximating theta would merely encode
the original problem. The coefficient height of F can also be large;
a one-layer denominator is not a complete height estimate. The fresh
reviewer independently confirmed these limitations and the algebra.

The analytic worker additionally notes that the moment constraints
prevent a triangle-inequality smallness argument even for signed P:
one of the fixed lower moments forces an absolute-source lower bound
proportional to |A|. Thus a useful signed choice would have to exploit
actual cancellation in the integral. The precise real lemma is in the
analytic/fresh packets; this arithmetic subspace does not bypass it.
