A positive-source obstruction for the common-base Mellin construction
28 October 2026. Local, unpublished cross-review finding.

This note leaves the completed fresh construction packet unchanged.
It reviews the single rational Mellin representation supplied by the
round-03 analysis agent. No irrationality or priority claim is made.

Theorem. In the constrained polynomial family defined below, every nonzero
polynomial P nonnegative on [0,1] gives a positive beta(4)-only form.
After any positive rational normalization making its beta(4) coefficient
a nonzero integer, that form is strictly greater than 1/2. In particular,
its primitive integer forms cannot tend to zero.

This theorem does not concern signed P, joint Pade determinants, arbitrary
linear combinations outside this source family, or all Mellin integrals.


1. The exact family being reviewed

Let L=log 2, r=-log x, and

  W(r)=r^3/6+L*r^2/4+L^2*r/12,

  U(x)= 900*x^2/[17*(x^4+4)]
       -2025*x^3/[17*(x^6-4*x^3+8)]
       -5625*x^5/[17*(x^10-8*x^5+32)].                     (1)

The analysis agent's completed ladder identity gives

  theta=beta(4)=integral_0^1 U(x)*W(-log x) dx/x.           (2)

The rational Mellin representation is independently checked as follows.
With m=2,3,5, respectively, put

  z_m=i/2,(1+i)/4,(1-i)/8,
  c_m=225/136,-25/34,9/68.

Then U(x)=sum c_m*m^4*Im[z_m*x^m/(1-z_m*x^m)]. Expanding in absolutely
convergent geometric series and integrating each logarithmic power gives
I4(z_m)+(m/2)L I3(z_m)+(m^2/12)L^2 I2(z_m), with I_s=Im Li_s.
Thus (1) matches the analysis agent's common-base germ and endpoint identity.
Equation (2) uses the previously established completed ladder identity;
no numerical identification of theta is being used as a proof of it.

Let D be the monic least common multiple of the three denominators in (1),
without their factor 17. It has degree 16. Put N=D*U in Q[x]. Consider

  P=A+D*V,      A in Q, V in Q[x],
  N*V=sum_(j>=1) a_j*x^j,

subject to the two rational linear conditions

             sum_j a_j/j^3=0,   sum_j a_j/j^2=0.          (3)

The indices begin at j>=2 in fact, since N has a zero of order two.
All integrals converge at both endpoints. Define

                 I(P)=integral_0^1 U(x)P(x)W(r) dx/x.

Termwise integration of the polynomial NV proves the formal pair identity

                    I(P)=A*theta+sum_j a_j/j^4.           (4)

The factors multiplying the canceled sums are respectively L/2 and L^2/12.
This checks the moment orders in (3).

Define two real moments

  M1=integral_0^1 U(x)*r dx/x,
  M2=integral_0^1 U(x)*r^2 dx/x.

Conditions (3) are equivalently

  integral U(x)P(x)*r dx/x = A*M1,
  integral U(x)P(x)*r^2 dx/x = A*M2.                       (5)

No positivity assumption has yet been made.


2. U has exactly one sign change, with an exact certificate

The denominator D has no zero on [0,1] and is positive there. For example,
each original denominator in (1) is a positive constant times
|1-z_m*x^m|^2, and |z_m|<1. Exact polynomial arithmetic gives

                 U(x)=225*x^2*f(x)/(17*D(x)),             (6)

where

  f(x)=4*x^12-25*x^11+68*x^10-145*x^9+220*x^8-232*x^7
       +260*x^6-464*x^5+880*x^4-1160*x^3+1088*x^2-800*x+256.

A rational Sturm sequence, recomputed by the standard-library certificate
script, has successive degrees 12,11,...,0 and these variation counts:

             x       0       3/5       5/8       1
          V(x)       7        7         6        6.

Also f(0)=256, f(1)=-50,

  f(3/5)=802361314/244140625 >0,
  f(5/8)=-31248983321/17179869184 <0.

Thus f has exactly one zero x0 in (0,1), with 3/5<x0<5/8.
Consequently U is positive on (0,x0) and negative on (x0,1).
Put r0=-log x0. Then U(x) has the same sign as r-r0.

The factorization and Sturm signs are exact, not inferred from a plot or
floating point roots. The certificate script independently reconstructs
D and verifies (6) by polynomial division and multiplication.


3. The preserved moments force A>0 for every nonnegative source

Suppose P is a nonzero polynomial nonnegative on [0,1] satisfying (3).
Since U and r-r0 have the same sign and r>0,

  M2-r0*M1=integral U(x)*r*(r-r0) dx/x >0.                 (7)

Likewise the same integral with an additional factor P is strictly
positive: a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on an open interval.
By (5), that integral is A*(M2-r0*M1). Equation (7) therefore forces

                                  A>0.                   (8)

This step does not need separate sign estimates for M1 or M2.
In particular, a nonzero nonnegative source in this family cannot be a
pure rational direction with A=0, or a negative-theta-coefficient direction.


4. A dual polynomial gives a fixed positive lower bound

The exact identity

  W(r)-(L/4+r0/6)*r^2-(L^2/12)*r
                         = r^2*(r-r0)/6                 (9)

has a right side with the same sign as U. Multiplying (9) by U(x)P(x)
and integrating therefore gives

  I(P) >= A*C,
  C=(L/4+r0/6)*M2+(L^2/12)*M1.                            (10)

The inequality is actually strict for a nonzero polynomial P, but the
weak form suffices. This establishes the structural lower bound without
an absolute-value estimate on the original signed kernel U.

For a completely rational lower certificate for C, Section 5 proves

                         M1>9/10,  M2>193/100.             (11)

Moreover L=log 2>2/3, and x0<5/8 gives

                  r0>log(8/5)>6/13.                      (12)

The elementary inequality log a>2(a-1)/(a+1), for a>1, proves both
logarithmic bounds. It follows, with every quantity positive, that

  C > (1/6+1/13)*(193/100)+(1/27)*(9/10)
    =1309/2600 >1/2.                                     (13)

If a positive rational multiplier kappa makes the two formal coefficients
in (4) integers, then kappa*A is a positive integer by (8), hence at least
one. Thus

                       kappa*I(P)>1/2.                  (14)

In particular (14) holds for primitive integer normalization. This proves
the theorem. It is stronger than failure of a particular localized source
sequence, but restricted to the explicitly constrained nonnegative family.


5. Rational tail certificates for the moment signs

The exact series formulas are

  M1=sum_(m=2,3,5) c_m*m^2*Im Li_2(z_m),
  M2=2*sum_(m=2,3,5) c_m*m*Im Li_3(z_m).                  (15)

Use rational majorants rho_m=1/2,3/8,3/16, which satisfy |z_m|<=rho_m<1.
For s=2,3, after the first K terms,

  |Im Li_s(z_m)-sum_(k=1)^K Im(z_m^k)/k^s|
       <= rho_m^(K+1)/[(K+1)^s*(1-rho_m)].                (16)

Every finite Gaussian power and every tail bound in (16) is rational.
The certificate uses K=32, sums signed finite terms exactly, and adds
absolute tail bounds after multiplying by the coefficients in (15).
Its exact rational lower endpoints prove (11). Their approximate ranges,
provided only for readability, are

  0.9006560116125 < M1 < 0.9006560116154,
  1.9321066776805 < M2 < 1.9321066776807.

The saved JSON contains the actual rational interval endpoints; the proof
uses those exact inequalities, not the decimal displays. An earlier
separate Arb evaluation agreed with these intervals but is not required
by the certificate.


6. Reproduction and review limits

Run from beta4/ using standard Python only:

  python3 collaboration/round-03/fresh/mellin_obstruction_checks.py

The output is mellin_obstruction_checks.json. The script checks the
rational kernel factorization, exact Sturm variations and root bracket,
the two rational series-tail enclosures, and the lower constant 1309/2600.
The Sturm implementation uses Fraction polynomial remainders with positive
rescaling, so all sign data are preserved. It is separate from the SymPy
root count used during discovery.

This note is an adversarial review result by the fresh investigator.
The input Mellin representation comes from the analysis agent. Root has
also reviewed the structural dual-polynomial argument. These are model
cross-checks, not an external independent audit or formal verification.

The conclusion is limited to P>=0 satisfying BOTH rational moment
constraints. A signed P can evade the sign argument. Common-base Pade
pencils are not asserted to have such nonnegative polynomial witnesses.
The star-capacity bound, the dyadic multiplier estimate, and the separate
odd-prime mechanism are not contradicted by this theorem.
