Rational Euler kernels: exact scalar reduction and a uniform pole-position obstruction
28 October 2026. Local, unpublished research note.

No irrationality proof has been found. This pass supplies an exact reduction
for a rational differential-kernel construction and excludes one complete
family, including moving pole positions of subexponential arithmetic size.
It does not exclude arbitrary rational kernels or arbitrary positive weights.

The principal theorem is: for even n, positive integers u_n,v_n satisfying
log(u_n+v_n)=o(n), the primitive positive integer form obtained from

  I_n = (1/6) integral_0^1
          [x^2(u_n-v_n*x^2)]^n (-log x)^3/(1+x^2) dx

satisfies

  liminf_(n even) log(primitive I_n)/n
       >= 8/3-log(5+4sqrt(2))
        = 0.3004633900257932778784442723... > 0.              (T)

Thus even an optimally chosen rational pole location cannot make this
specific construction yield shrinking primitive integer forms. The bound
uses actual primitive normalization, not just a sufficient denominator.

All 90 finite examples tested have primitive values above one. The theorem
is proved below; those finite examples are checks, not its proof. A separate
symbolic implementation checks the main identities. Both implementations
are by the same investigator, not an independent audit or formal verification.
No novelty or priority claim is made.


1. Independent starting point and the prescribed pivot

00_initial_idea.txt was saved before reading prior notes. It proposed
integer polynomial weights in the elementary scalar Mellin integral,
starting with P_n(y)=y^n(1-y)^n. Reading README.txt and the round-01 fresh
note then showed that the broad scalar construction had already been
analyzed: unrestricted nonnegative polynomial weights encode all positive
rational beta(4) forms. Positivity alone cannot provide missing arithmetic.
The cyclotomic-integrals and ladder-integrals notes also explain why their
particular positive polynomial and hyperbolic constructions fail.

The starting binomial family itself has an immediate large-prime obstruction.
Instead of tuning that family, the prescribed pivot was to rational kernels
with conjugate imaginary poles and an Euler differential operator. Applying
the fourth power of that operator makes every auxiliary period disappear
exactly. At first this seems to offer rational endpoint denominators supported
on a small set of primes. The construction is set up in Section 2.

The pivot led to two precise findings:

  * Local Taylor cancellation still forces the fourth odd-prime denominator
    layer before primitive normalization (Section 3).
  * At the endpoint, every such Taylor-osculating rational kernel reduces
    to an explicit scalar polynomial weight (Section 4). For a repeated
    quadratic pole, this gives the uniform obstruction (T), even after
    optimizing the pole position (Sections 5-6).

This is a reduction of the proposed rational family, not a claim that all
rational-integral proofs reduce to the same family.


2. Differential exactness builds period cancellation into the kernel

Put E=t*d/dt, g(t)=t/(1+t^2), and

  J(R)=(1/6) integral_0^1 (-log t)^3 R(t) dt/t.

Then J(g)=theta=beta(4). Let H be a rational function regular on [0,1],
with H(t)=O(t) at zero. Four integrations by parts give

                         J(E^4 H)=H(1).                    (1)

For a direct justification put h(z)=H(exp(-z)). Its derivatives decay
exponentially as z tends to infinity. Since E^4H(exp(-z))=h''''(z),

  integral_0^infinity z^3 h''''(z) dz=6h(0).

The endpoint terms at zero vanish during the first three integrations;
the fourth gives 6h(0). Thus any rational A and H give the exact form

          J(A*g+E^4 H)=A*theta+H(1).                       (2)

There are no Catalan or other auxiliary periods to eliminate afterward.
For example, if H has an integer numerator and denominator
(v+u*t^2)^n, then H(1) has denominator dividing (u+v)^n. This observation
alone says nothing about the size of the A needed to make the kernel small.

For the concrete construction define

  F(y)=sum_(k>=0) (-1)^k y^k/(2k+1)^4,
  F4(t)=t*F(t^2),                         E^4 F4=g.

Let Q(y) in Q[y] satisfy Q(0)!=0, Q(1)!=0, with no zero on [0,1].
For an integer m>=0 let C_m(y) be the Taylor polynomial of Q(y)F(y)
through degree m-1 (zero when m=0). Set

                 H(t)=-A*t*C_m(t^2)/Q(t^2).               (3)

Then H=-A*F4+O(t^(2m+1)), and A*g+E^4H=O(t^(2m+1)).
The resulting endpoint form is

                 A*(theta-C_m(1)/Q(1)).                  (4)

The experiment uses Q(y)=(v+u*y)^n and m=2n. The kernel has poles
at the conjugate points +/-i*sqrt(v/u), of orders increased by E^4.
The reciprocal polynomial of Q determines the scalar error below.


3. A local integrality lemma: poles do not automatically save primes

Lemma. Suppose A is an integer, H=P/Q with P,Q integer polynomials,
Q(0)!=0, and the first m odd Taylor coefficients of A*g+E^4H vanish.
For every odd prime p not dividing Q(0),

  v_p(A) >= 4 max_(0<=k<m) v_p(2k+1).                      (5)

Proof. H has p-integral Taylor coefficients because Q(0) is a p-unit.
The coefficient of t^(2k+1) in the cancellation equation is

  A*(-1)^k+(2k+1)^4*[t^(2k+1)]H=0.

Its second term is divisible by p^(4 v_p(2k+1)), proving (5).
No beta irrationality assumption is involved. QED.

In particular, if Q(0)=1 or -1, the fourth power of
lcm(1,3,...,2m-1) divides A. For the explicit numerator construction,
when Q(0)=1 this is also the exact common denominator of the first m
Taylor coefficients of QF: multiplication by Q is an integral triangular
transformation with an integral triangular inverse. There is no loss or
gain in their common denominator.

This lemma concerns the integral SOURCE kernel. It is not by itself an
obstruction for the PRIMITIVE endpoint form: the endpoint rational number
can have common factors with A. Sections 5-6 track surviving primes after
that common content is removed.


4. Every Taylor-osculating rational kernel has an exact polynomial error

Use the formal coefficient map

  I(P)=(1/6) integral_0^1 (-log x)^3 P(x^2)/(1+x^2) dx
      =P(-1)*theta+B(P),

  B(P)=sum_j q_j/(2j+1)^4,
  sum_j q_j*y^j=(P(y)-P(-1))/(1+y).                         (6)

This is an explicitly defined map into Q^2; no assertion of independence
of 1 and theta is needed to speak about its coefficient pair.

Write Q(y)=sum_(j=0)^d q_j*y^j. Define

  W_(Q,m)(y)=sum_(j=m)^d q_j
               +sum_(j=0)^min(m-1,d) q_j*(-y)^(m-j).      (7)

Then the exact identity of formal pairs is

          theta-C_m(1)/Q(1)=I(W_(Q,m))/Q(1).               (8)

Proof. Write F(y) as the integral of 1/(1+y*x^2) against
(-log x)^3 dx/6. For each monomial q_j*y^j, subtract the terms of
QF with total y-degree below m and then set y=1. If j>=m this leaves
q_j/(1+x^2). If j<m, the geometric remainder is
q_j*(-x^2)^(m-j)/(1+x^2). Summing proves (7)-(8). Alternatively,
W(-1)=Q(1), and polynomial division in (6) directly gives the rational
coefficient -C_m(1). This proves the identity formally as well. QED.

For m>=d, (7) is the ordinary reversed polynomial times (-y)^(m-d).
In the repeated-pole example Q=(v+u*y)^n, m=2n,

  W_(Q,2n)(y)=(-y)^n*(u-v*y)^n.                            (9)

Consequently, putting P_n=y^n(u-v*y)^n and A_n=(-1)^n(u+v)^n,

                 theta-C_(2n)(1)/Q(1)=I(P_n)/A_n.         (10)

Multiplying a rational coefficient pair by a nonzero rational number
does not change its primitive pair, up to sign. Thus the rational Euler
kernel and P_n have exactly the same primitive arithmetic. For even n,
P_n>=0 on [0,1], and I(P_n)>0. This gives nonvanishing for every even n,
without imposing a pointwise sign on the differential kernel itself.

Identity (8) does NOT imply a universal obstruction. General polynomial
weights already encode all positive rational forms, as the round-01 note
proves. It only prevents us from treating the extra denominator poles in
(3) as a new endpoint approximation mechanism without further arithmetic.


5. Surviving fourth-power primes after primitive normalization

Fix even n and positive integers u,v. Put P=y^n(u-v*y)^n and
A=P(-1)=(u+v)^n. Write

             I(P)=A*theta+N/D,   gcd(N,D)=1, D>0.

Let q(y)=(P(y)-A)/(1+y), of degree 2n-1. Because P has a zero of
order n at y=0, its first n quotient coefficients satisfy

                  q_j=(-1)^(j+1)*A,     0<=j<n.           (11)

Take an odd prime p in the strict interval

                       4n/3 < p < 2n,                    (12)

and suppose p does not divide u+v. Among the odd denominators
1,3,...,4n-1 in (6), the only multiple of p is p itself: 3p>4n.
Its index j=(p-1)/2 is less than n, and (11) is a p-unit. Therefore

                         v_p(N/D)=-4.                    (13)

No other summand can cancel that valuation. In particular p^4 exactly
divides D and p does not divide N.

The primitive integer pair is

  (A_prim,B_prim)=(D*A/g,N/g),   g=gcd(D*A,N).

Since gcd(D,N)=1, g divides A. Its positive evaluation is

          L_n=(D/g)*I(P) >= D*I(P)/A.                     (14)

At every prime in (12) not dividing u+v, the primitive leading
coefficient still has valuation exactly four. Thus (14) gives

  L_n >= product_(4n/3<p<2n, p not dividing u+v) p^4
                      * I(P)/A.                          (15)

This is a lower bound for the ACTUAL primitive form, not an upper
bound obtained by choosing an unnecessarily large clearing factor.


6. Optimizing the real decay cannot overcome those primes

Put r=v/(u+v), so 0<r<1, and f_r(y)=y*(1-r-r*y). For even n,

  I(P)/A = integral_0^1 |f_r(y)|^n dmu(y),
  dmu(y)=(-log y)^3/[96*sqrt(y)*(1+y)] dy.                 (16)

For fixed r, the elementary Laplace principle gives exponential rate
M(r)=max_(0<=y<=1)|f_r(y)|. The best possible M(r), even with r real,
is exactly

  M_* = min_(0<=r<=1) M(r) = 1/(5+4sqrt(2)).              (17)

Indeed, for r>=1/3 the positive interior maximum is
(1-r)^2/(4r). For r>=1/2 there is also the negative endpoint magnitude
2r-1. Their unique equality occurs at r_*=(1+2sqrt(2))/7, and gives
M_*=(4sqrt(2)-5)/7. For r<1/3, M(r)>=1/3, so it cannot improve this.
This is an exact minimax calculation, not a numerical optimization.

A uniform lower bound is needed when r depends on n. Since |f'_r|<=2,
choose an interval of length ell=M_*/(4n), lying within ell of a
maximizer. On it,

  |f_r| >= M(r)*(1-1/(2n)).

On the central half of that interval, 1-y>=ell/4. Also

                dmu(y) >= (1-y)^3/192 dy.

Using (1-1/(2n))^n>=1/2 therefore proves, uniformly in r,

  I(P)/A >= M_*^(n+4)/(3*2^22*n^4).                       (18)

For clarity, the constant follows from central-half measure at least
ell^4/24576 and ell^4=M_*^4/(256*n^4). Its exact value is immaterial
for the exponential comparison.

Let

  S_n=sum_(4n/3<p<2n, p divides u_n+v_n) log p.

The prime number theorem and (15),(18) give

  log L_n >= [8/3-log(5+4sqrt(2))]*n-4*S_n+o(n).           (19)

The o(n) comes only from the prime number theorem and polynomial factors;
the real estimate is uniform in u_n,v_n. Since S_n<=log(u_n+v_n),
condition log(u_n+v_n)=o(n) proves theorem (T). More generally S_n=o(n)
is enough, including some sequences with very large powers of fixed primes.

For the initial choice u=v=1, M=1/8, giving the stronger lower exponent
8/3-log 8 = 0.587225125... . The optimized general bound is weaker but
still positive, and rules out repairing this family by moving the poles.
The theorem makes no claim about odd n, different osculation lengths,
arbitrary denominator polynomials, or combinations of different kernels.


7. Exact checks and reproduction

From beta4/:

  .venv/bin/python collaboration/round-03/fresh/rational_kernel.py
  python3 collaboration/round-03/fresh/symbolic_checks.py

rational_kernel.py uses Python Fraction for coefficients, polynomial
operations, Taylor truncation, primitive normalization, and prime valuations.
Arb is used only for certified evaluations at theta and the displayed
positive constant. Precision exceeds the largest primitive coefficient's
bit length by 512 bits.

The saved checks comprise:

  * 96 exact formal reductions (8), including m below the degree of Q;
  * 90 forms, using nine pole ratios and even n=2,4,6,8,10,16,24,32,48,64;
  * 291 surviving prime-valuation checks, including primitive content;
  * 90 certified positive evaluations, all above one;
  * 15 separate SymPy rational differential-kernel cancellations;
  * 15 separate SymPy endpoint coefficient-pair identities;
  * 72 exact common-denominator checks for Q(0)=1;
  * the two exact equations giving the optimized constant in (17).

For example, (u,v,n)=(5,6,64), close to the optimal real pole ratio,
has a primitive value about 7.1255*10^415. Its log divided by n is
about 14.9615. The proved bound is deliberately much smaller: a single
interval of unavoidable primes already establishes failure.

Results are rational_kernel_results.json and symbolic_checks.json.
They contain the exact primitive integer pairs, so every finite example
can be recomputed without reconstructing the polynomial source.


8. Scope, sources, and what remains missing

The source-level differential identity and endpoint reduction are elementary.
The scalar moment map, the broader cone warning, and related positive-weight
obstructions overlap earlier local work. Files consulted after recording the
initial idea include README.txt, collaboration/round-01/fresh/findings.txt,
cyclotomic-integrals.txt, ladder-integrals.txt, structural-followup.txt,
and selected text searches in research-notes.txt, hypergeometric-arithmetic.txt,
and orthogonal-cancellation.txt.

A limited primary-source web check consulted the indexed opening of
T. Rivoal and W. Zudilin, "Diophantine properties of numbers related to
Catalan's constant": https://rivoal.perso.math.cnrs.fr/articles/betacatalan.pdf .
Its historical method context was checked, not its full proofs. No current
claim of Catalan irrationality, nor any alleged consequence for beta(4),
is assumed or used. No exhaustive priority search has been performed.

The useful negative conclusion is narrow and concrete: exact period
cancellation by E^4 plus repeated quadratic poles and 2n Taylor conditions
still leaves an arithmetic cost larger than its best real decay. Improving
the real estimate or optimizing these pole positions cannot fix that family.

The general reduction (8) leaves open constructions with denominator
polynomials whose reversed coefficients create genuinely different prime
cancellation. Establishing enough such cancellation AFTER primitive
normalization, together with nonvanishing and a shrinking bound, is still
missing. The present work provides no successful candidate satisfying those
conditions, and no irrationality proof.
