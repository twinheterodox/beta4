Round 03: a common-base completed ladder, its real bound, and source limits
28 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

Status

There is a substantive compatibility improvement for a genuinely joint
germ. The same exact endpoint determinants have infinitely many nonzero
values and obey

  limsup log|J_n(beta(4))|/n^2 <= -(31/60) log 2.

The arithmetic worker proves limsup v_2(kappa_n)/n^2 <= 1/2 for their
ACTUAL primitive multipliers. Consequently the dyadic-only normalized
values tend to zero with a strict margin (log 2)/60 at the n^2 scale.
This does not make them integer forms. Odd-prime costs and common content
remain uncontrolled at the required scale. No irrationality proof or
actual-value obstruction for the joint determinants is obtained.

The three completed polylogarithm blocks also collapse to one rational
Mellin kernel and one four-step Euler differential tower. The minimal
kernel denominator has degree 16, not the degree 20 of its unreduced
product. An explicit signed Rodrigues subspace lowers the rational
remainder's denominator from a fourth LCM power to one LCM. This is an
exact algebraic reduction, not yet an approximation family: no choice of
its free parameters has both proved small error and nonvanishing.

There are useful rigorous limits on this source approach. The fresh
worker proves every nonzero nonnegative source in the constrained family
has normalized value greater than 1/2. Even for signed sources, EVERY
absolute-value bound on the defining weighted integral is bounded below
by a positive constant after integer normalization with a nonzero target
coefficient. A successful signed construction must exploit cancellation
in that integral. A large explicit exact-zero subspace also prevents a
bare dimension or small-vector argument from supplying nonvanishing.

All writes by this worker remain in this directory. This is coordinated
model derivation and checking, not external verification or a priority
claim. The completed Broadhurst identity is an inherited classical input.

1. The new germ preserves the common powers of the arguments

Write theta=beta(4), and set

 q=(1-i)/2,  m=2,3,5,
 z_2=i/2, z_3=(1+i)/4, z_5=(1-i)/8,
 (c_2,c_3,c_5)=(225/136,-25/34,9/68).

Exactly z_m=-q^m. For an analytic variable t, use the notation

 I_s(z_m t^m)=[Li_s(z_m t^m)-Li_s(conjugate(z_m)t^m)]/(2i).

This is an analytic germ with rational Taylor coefficients; it is not
the operation of taking the imaginary part of a function at complex t.
Put L(t)=-log(1-t^2/2), with L(0)=0, and define

 H(t)=sum_(m=2,3,5) c_m [I_4(z_m t^m)
          +(m/2)L(t) I_3(z_m t^m)
          +(m^2/12)L(t)^2 I_2(z_m t^m)].                 (1.1)

Then H(0)=0 and H(1)=theta by the completed ladder identity already
established in round-02/fresh/findings.txt. Its coefficients h_j are
rational. The different powers 2,3,5 mean (1.1) is not a common change
of variable in the previous equal-speed germ. It retains the relation
z_m t^m=-(qt)^m throughout the germ, while using a logarithm with the
same boundary modulus as every polylogarithm block.

The old equal-speed family and the coordinator's secondary classical-Q
identity were tested separately. They are not silently substituted for
(1.1) in any theorem below. The common-base family is the primary one.

2. One Euler tower and one rational source

Let E=t*d/dt. Define

 U(t)=900t^2/[17(t^4+4)]
      -2025t^3/[17(t^6-4t^3+8)]
      -5625t^5/[17(t^10-8t^5+32)].                       (2.1)

It is exactly sum_m c_m m^4 I_0(z_m t^m), where Li_0(z)=z/(1-z).
Let Y_0=U, and choose Y_r by

             E Y_r=Y_(r-1),  Y_r(0)=0,  1<=r<=4.

Coefficientwise or by differentiation of polylogarithms,

 Y_r(t)=sum_m c_m m^(4-r) I_r(z_m t^m),
 H(t)=Y_4(t)+(L(t)/2)Y_3(t)+(L(t)^2/12)Y_2(t).            (2.2)

These relations give an explicit unipotent rational differential system.
For example a closed list of 12 coordinates is

 1,L,L^2,Y_1,LY_1,L^2Y_1,Y_2,LY_2,L^2Y_2,Y_3,LY_3,Y_4.

Use E L=2t^2/(2-t^2), E Y_r=Y_(r-1), and the product rule. For Y_1,
the right side is the rational coefficient U times the coordinate 1.
Thus every derivative is a rational linear combination of this list.
No minimal differential order is claimed. Its only possible finite
singularities away from the removable initial point 0 are the poles of
U and t=+sqrt(2),-sqrt(2).

For r>=1, repeated Euler integration gives

 Y_r(t)=1/(r-1)! int_0^1 U(tx)(-log x)^(r-1) dx/x.

Consequently, writing rho=-log x,

 H(t)=int_0^1 U(tx)[rho^3/6+L(t)rho^2/4
                                  +L(t)^2 rho/12] dx/x. (2.3)

The formula initially holds for |t|<sqrt(2), by absolutely convergent
series, and elsewhere on the radial cut domain of section 5 by analytic
continuation. Equivalently it is the coefficient of s^3 in

 [sL(t)/(1-exp(-sL(t)))] int_0^1 x^(-s-1) U(tx) dx.        (2.4)

Only the expansion 1+sL/2+s^2L^2/12+0*s^3 is used in (2.4).
This common completion is the specific compatibility among the blocks;
it is lost if their endpoint values are approximated independently.

3. The minimal denominator and the exact endpoint normalization

Put p_2=t^4+4, p_3=t^6-4t^3+8, p_5=t^10-8t^5+32, and
g=t^2+2t+2. Each pair of p's has gcd g. Their monic LCM is

                 D(t)=p_2 p_3 p_5/g^2.                  (3.1)

It is squarefree, has degree 16, D(0)=256, and D(1)=25. In particular
the degree-20 product is an unnecessarily large source denominator.
The reduced numerator is

 U(t)=225t^2 f(t)/(17D(t)),

 f(t)=4t^12-25t^11+68t^10-145t^9+220t^8-232t^7+260t^6
             -464t^5+880t^4-1160t^3+1088t^2-800t+256.      (3.2)

The numerator and D are coprime. Exact division also gives

                   (2-t^2)D(t) | t^120-2^60.              (3.3)

There are 16 distinct U poles and two additional logarithm points; all
lie on |t|=sqrt(2), on rays of angle 2*pi*j/120. Equation (3.3) is a
simple certificate for their complete containment, without root numerics.
Some possible points might have cancellation in H; no assertion that
all 18 are genuine is needed for the upper estimate.

Write H(t)=sum_(j>=1) h_j t^j, h_0=0. The exact raw endpoint form used
by the coordinator and arithmetic worker is

 b_i=h_i,  T_ij=h_(i+j)-h_(i+j-1), 1<=i,j<=n,
 J_n(X)=det [[X,-b^T],[b,T]]
       =X det T+b^T adj(T)b.                             (3.4)

For n=0 set J_0(X)=X. Define

 c_j(X)=X-sum_(l=1)^j h_l,
 C_X(t)=(X-H(t))/(1-t)=sum_(j>=0)c_j(X)t^j.

Adjacent row and column differences in [c_(i+j)] give
[[X,-b^T],[-b,-T]]. Thus

       det[c_(i+j)(X)]_(0<=i,j<=n)=(-1)^n J_n(X).          (3.5)

This holds without normality or invertibility. It is a polynomial of
degree at most one because X originally enters by a rank-one matrix.
When the ordinary diagonal Pade system is normal it is proportional to
q_n(1)X-p_n(1), with the exact primitive pair checked independently by
the coordinator. There is no factor 6 in (3.4).

For nonzero J_n=A_n X+B_n, let kappa_n>0 be the rational scalar making
kappa_n J_n primitive integral. Then

       v_p(kappa_n)=-min(v_p(A_n),v_p(B_n)).               (3.6)

The scalar need not be an integer. Define the normalized form to be
zero separately when J_n is the zero polynomial. All bounds below use
this actual primitive multiplier, not an arbitrary raw clearing LCM.

4. A genuine logarithmic branch gives exactly the needed nonvanishing

Approach t_+=sqrt(2) along the real interval from below. The three
polylogarithm arguments in (1.1) tend to

                   i, exp(i*pi/4), exp(-i*pi/4).

None is 1, so the polylogarithm factors are locally analytic on this
continuation. The coefficient of L(t)^2 at t_+ is

 A_+=(75/272)[2 beta(2)-3 Cl_2(pi/4)],                    (4.1)

where Cl_2(phi)=sum_(j>=1) sin(j phi)/j^2. It is strictly negative by
a rational certificate. The alternating series gives beta(2)<1. The
first three terms of Cl_2(pi/4), followed by an absolute tail bound,
give

 Cl_2(pi/4) > 5sqrt(2)/9+1/4-1/3 > 25/36,

using sqrt(2)>7/5 and sum_(j>=4)1/j^2<1/3. Therefore

                    A_+ < -25/1088 <0.                  (4.2)

It follows that H has a genuine log-squared branch at t_+. No constant
X and division by 1-t can remove it. Hence C_X is nonrational for every
fixed complex X, including X=theta, without assuming irrationality.

Kronecker's criterion in the leading-Hankel indexing says a series is
rational if and only if all sufficiently large leading Hankel
determinants vanish. Applying it to (3.5) yields

        J_n(X) != 0 for infinitely many n, for every fixed X.       (4.3)

In particular the actual theta evaluations and their primitive
normalizations are nonzero infinitely often. We have not proved
nonvanishing at every size or on an independently selected subsequence.
No positive measure or positive-definite matrix is needed for (4.3).

5. A fully explicit admissible cut and its capacity

Use reciprocal coordinate w=1/t, and set

 G_theta(w)=(theta-H(1/w))/(w-1)
           =sum_(j>=0)c_j(theta) w^(-j-1) near infinity.

Define the filled compact star

               K={w: w^120 in [0,2^(-60)]}.               (5.1)

It consists of 120 radial segments of length 1/sqrt(2), with common
endpoint 0. Its spherical complement is simply connected and contains
infinity. It has no bounded complementary components, so K is
polynomially convex as required by the one-variable Polya theorem.

For completeness the continuation can be constructed directly, without
assuming arbitrary branch cuts are admissible. Inverting the complement
gives the t-plane with the 120 closed radial rays

 {r exp(2*pi*i*j/120): r>=sqrt(2)}, j=0,...,119,

removed. This open domain contains 0 and is star-shaped with respect to
0. Every pole in (3.3) lies on its boundary. The radial integrals defining
Y_r, and the branch L(0)=0, consequently give single-valued holomorphic
functions throughout this domain. The apparent dt/t singularity at 0
is removable because U=O(t^2), and all Y_r vanish there. There is no
uncontrolled logarithm-sheet choice in this construction.

In the w-plane the possible singular behavior at w=0 belongs to K.
At infinity G_theta is holomorphic and O(1/w). At w=1 the numerator
vanishes, since H(1)=theta and H is analytic near 1. This apparent pole
is REMOVABLE. For a general X different from theta it is a genuine
pole; the same small-star estimate is not asserted for G_X.

The elementary polynomial-preimage capacity formula gives

 cap(K)=[cap([0,2^(-60)])]^(1/120)
       =[2^(-60)/4]^(1/120)=2^(-31/60).                   (5.2)

One proof is to compose the interval's Green function with w^120 and
divide by 120. Its logarithmic asymptotic at infinity gives (5.2).
No extremal-cut or hedgehog theorem is needed; this is one explicit
admissible cut and an upper bound on the optimal capacity, not a claim
that the star minimizes it.

Applying the one-variable Polya inequality in precisely the coefficient
normalization (3.5), with matrix size n+1, proves

      limsup_(n->infinity) |J_n(theta)|^(1/n^2)
                             <= 2^(-31/60),              (5.3)

or, with log 0=-infinity,

      log|J_n(theta)| <= -(31/60)log(2)n^2+o(n^2).         (5.4)

The shift from size n+1 to exponent n^2 changes no leading constant.
To see the normalization directly, coefficient extraction gives the
Hankel determinant as

  1/[(n+1)!(2*pi*i)^(n+1)] int_(Gamma^(n+1))
       product_j G_theta(w_j) product_(i<j)(w_j-w_i)^2 dw.

Taking absolute values, using the Vandermonde definition of transfinite
diameter, and shrinking contour neighborhoods of K yields (5.3).
There is one capacity power at the n^2 scale, not twice that power.

This is a real upper bound on exactly the raw determinant in (3.4).
It is not a determinant asymptotic equality, a lower bound, or a bound
on every alternative numerator construction. No estimate on a failed
contour is promoted to an actual-value obstruction.

6. Pairing with the proved dyadic primitive-multiplier bound

The arithmetic worker's findings.txt, sections 4--6, proves the following
for the SAME H and J_n. If n>=8, r=floor(log_2 n), delta=2r-2, and
R=max(0,2n-2^(r+1)-9), then every nonzero J_n satisfies

 v_2(kappa_n) <= n(n+1)/2+(6+delta)(n+1)
                                  -ceil((n+1-R)/2).       (6.1)

The proof uses integral depleted Gaussian-polylogarithm blocks and the
exact rank R of the scaled bordered residue matrix. In particular it
controls the actual multiplier's valuation and gives the uniform rate

             v_2(kappa_n) <= (1/2)n^2+O(n log n).          (6.2)

For nonzero J_n put d_n=2^max(0,v_2(kappa_n)); put d_n=1 if J_n is the
zero polynomial. Combining (5.4) and (6.2) gives the proved strict
compatibility statement

 log|d_n J_n(theta)| <= -(log 2)/60 * n^2+o(n^2),          (6.3)

so d_n J_n(theta) tends to zero, with infinitely many nonzero values by
(4.3). The coefficients of d_n J_n are 2-integral. They generally still
have odd-prime denominators. This is a DYADIC-ONLY shrinking theorem,
not a sequence of shrinking integer linear forms.

For example, sufficient additional arithmetic would be a bound on
log(kappa_n/d_n) with leading rate strictly less than (log 2)/60, or a
stronger real estimate paired with its correct full multiplier. None
is available here. The arithmetic packet proves that a fourth layer at
large odd primes survives except for explicitly characterized smaller
modular defects, including an actual exception. That does not by itself
give a global multiplier lower bound or a lower bound on J_n(theta).

The coordinator's prescribed finite tests give the following diagnostics

7. The exact shared Mellin numerator constraints

At the endpoint put L=log 2, rho=-log x, and

 W(rho)=rho^3/6+L rho^2/4+L^2 rho/12,
 I(P)=int_0^1 P(x)U(x)W(-log x) dx/x.

Let N=D U, a polynomial with rational denominator 17 and a zero of
order two at 0. Take a constant A in Q and a polynomial V in Q[x],

                  P=A+D V,  NV=sum_(j>=1)b_j x^j.

Termwise integration gives the exact formal identity

 I(P)=A theta+sum_j b_j/j^4
             +(L/2)sum_j b_j/j^3+(L^2/12)sum_j b_j/j^2.   (7.1)

The two rational conditions

                    sum b_j/j^3=sum b_j/j^2=0            (7.2)

remove the log 2 products, leaving a rational linear form in theta.
They are essential; simply imposing congruence P=A mod D does not give
a beta(4)-only form. The generic denominator of the rational remainder
in (7.1) divides a fourth power of an LCM, which is too costly for the
usual interval polynomial smallness estimate. A more structured
subspace improves this denominator, as the next section proves.

8. A concrete signed Rodrigues subspace and its exact-zero directions

This construction was supplied by the arithmetic worker during review.
Let N0=17N=17D U=225x^2 f(x) in Z[x]. For ANY S in Z[x] set

 F=x(1-x)^2 N0^4 S,
 V=17 E^3 F/N0,     E=x*d/dx,
 P=A+D V.                                                (8.1)

Then V is an integer polynomial. Indeed at most three differentiations
can remove at most three of the four N0 factors; the product rule for
E preserves integrality. Exactly NV=E^3F. If F=sum f_j x^j, it has no
constant term, and F(1)=E F(1)=0. Thus

 sum b_j/j^3=F(1)=0,
 sum b_j/j^2=E F(1)=0,
 sum b_j/j^4=sum f_j/j=int_0^1 F(x) dx/x.                 (8.2)

For integer A, the form is therefore

            I(P)=A theta+sum f_j/j,                      (8.3)

and multiplication by lcm(1,...,deg F) makes both coefficients
integers. This is a genuine reduction from four denominator layers to
one. It is not an estimate on the size or common content of the final
primitive pair, and it does not yet choose S or A.

There is also a large exact-zero subspace inside (8.1). For any nonzero
Z in Z[x], put

 R0=N0^5(1-x)^3 Z,
 F=E R0,
 S=[5N0'(1-x)-3N0]Z+N0(1-x)Z'.                           (8.4)

The F in (8.4) is exactly x(1-x)^2 N0^4 S. Since R0(0)=R0(1)=0,
the rational remainder in (8.3) is integral F/x=R0(1)-R0(0)=0.
Taking A=0 gives a nonzero source polynomial P with the exact pair
(A,B)=(0,0). It is nonzero because E^4 is injective on polynomials with
zero constant term, and NV=E^4R0. This supplies infinitely many exact
zero sources, with explicit integer formulas.

Consequently a dimension count or a short nonzero coefficient vector
does not certify a nonzero endpoint form. A Siegel-lemma or lattice
argument must work in the quotient by the exact (A,B) kernel, or supply
another nonvanishing certificate. Choosing the free A and S only after
finding a rational number close to theta would just restate the desired
approximation and is not a construction. No noncircular parameter
choice with a proved shrinking error has been found in this packet.

9. Rigorous limits on positive and signed source smallness

Define the preserved moments

 M1=int_0^1 U(x)rho dx/x,
 M2=int_0^1 U(x)rho^2 dx/x.

The fresh worker's separate exact certificate proves M1>9/10 and
M2>193/100. It also proves U has exactly one zero x0 in (0,1), with
3/5<x0<5/8, U positive below x0 and negative above it. These statements
use rational Sturm signs and rational polylogarithm tail bounds, not
decimal sampling. Their complete proof and reproduction are in
../fresh/mellin_positive_obstruction.txt and its checks.py/json files.

Conditions (7.2) imply, for every signed P in the family,

 int P U rho dx/x=A M1,
 int P U rho^2 dx/x=A M2.                                (9.1)

Two consequences must be distinguished.

(a) ACTUAL values for NONNEGATIVE P. Put rho0=-log x0. The fresh
worker observes that U and rho-rho0 have the same sign, so a nonzero
P>=0 forces A>0 by integrating PU rho(rho-rho0). Moreover

 W(rho)-(L/4+rho0/6)rho^2-(L^2/12)rho
                                  =rho^2(rho-rho0)/6.

It follows that I(P)>A C with

 C=(L/4+rho0/6)M2+(L^2/12)M1>1309/2600>1/2.

After any positive normalization making the target coefficient a
nonzero integer, the actual form is therefore greater than 1/2. This
excludes every nonzero nonnegative polynomial source in (7.2), not just
one localized or Rodrigues family. It is not asserted for signed P.

(b) ABSOLUTE estimates for ALL signed P. Let

             B_abs(P)=int_0^1 |P(x)U(x)|W(rho) dx/x.

Since W(rho)>=(L^2/12)rho, equation (9.1) gives

 B_abs(P)>=(L^2/12) int |PU|rho dx/x
         >=(L^2/12)|A|M1.                               (9.2)

If a positive normalization kappa makes kappa*A a nonzero integer,
then kappa B_abs(P)>1/30, using L>2/3 and M1>9/10.
Thus even a signed source cannot prove shrinking integer values by
bounding the defining integral with its absolute integrand. This is
an obstruction to THAT proof method, not to the signed actual integral:
cancellation can make |I(P)| much smaller than B_abs(P).

The related uniform-norm obstruction is

 ||P||_[0,1] >= |A|M1 / int_0^1 |U(x)|rho dx/x.            (9.3)

In particular ||P||/|A| cannot tend to zero. The integral in the
denominator is finite and positive. This rules out designs whose only
real estimate is uniform smallness of a congruent numerator. If A=0,
the endpoint form in (7.1) is rational; after integer normalization any
nonzero value has absolute value at least one. Its exact zeros do not
help an irrationality argument.

The first-LCM arithmetic of section 8 survives these observations, but
it is not by itself a real approximation mechanism. A future signed
family must specify its parameters without using theta, prove a
cancellation estimate for (8.3) or (7.1), and separately ensure a
nonzero formal pair and nonzero endpoint values. No such family is
claimed here. Further generic polynomial or LLL scans have been stopped
at this structural boundary.
