ROUND17: THE FOUR NEGATIVE PRIME LAYERS AND THE BORDER PAIRING
31 October 2026. Local derivation; not formal verification.

Notation is exactly round16: C=[1,r], m=floor(cr/2), M=r+2m,
N=r+m, interleaved border sets, Phi(X), P(X)=det Phi(X).
For p>3 with p^2>4M, put B=p^4 Phi. All valuations of polynomials
are Gauss valuations, v(sum a_j X^j)=min_j v_p(a_j).

1. Exact singular/regular decomposition, including the zero fibre

Define
 H_l(a;p)=sum_(positive odd t, pt<4a) chi4(t)/t^l,
 U_l(a;p)=sum_(positive odd n<4a, p not dividing n) chi4(n)/n^l.
Since p^2>4M, all t in H_l are p-units. Exactly,
 S_l(a)=chi4(p)p^(-l)H_l(a;p)+U_l(a;p).

The regular cubic sum satisfies
 U_3(a+p)=U_3(a) mod p,
 U_3(p-a)=-U_3(a) mod p,
 U_3(pk)=0 mod p.
For the first identity, in any block of 4p consecutive integers, each
p-unit residue occurs in two odd positions separated by 2p; the chi4
signs are opposite. For the second, reflect n to 4p-n in a complete
block. Both chi4 and the inverse cube change sign, so their product
does not. The third follows by complete blocks. Extend by periodicity.

Consequently the matrix R(X) with entries
 R_ab=[b U_3(b)-a U_3(a)]/[6(b^2-a^2)]      (a!=b),
 R_aa=X-U_4(a)+U_3(a)/(12a)                (a common),
is p-integral. If a==b mod p or a==-b mod p but the residue is nonzero,
the numerator cancels the single p in the denominator. If both are zero,
a and b each contain one p, and U_3(a),U_3(b) each contain another p,
cancelling the two denominator factors. On a zero diagonal U_3(a)
cancels the p in a. There are no larger node-factor valuations because
2M<p^2/2. This covers all exceptional fibres.

Thus Phi=Psi+R(X), with R p-integral and
 Psi_ab=chi4(p)[b H_3(b)-a H_3(a)]/[6p^3(b^2-a^2)]  (a!=b),
 Psi_aa=chi4(p)[-H_4(a)/p^4+H_3(a)/(12ap^3)].

In particular B=p^4 Psi mod p^4. The regular terms DO NOT enter the
first four layers. This sharpens the preliminary note, which had allowed
an order-p^3 contribution before the character cancellation was proved.

2. Reduction on squared-node fibres

For different squared residues B_ab=0 mod p. For a nonzero fibre choose
1<=t<p/2 and write a=epsilon*t+pk, b=eta*t+pl.

 * Same sign, distinct nodes:
     B_ab=chi4(p)[H_3(b)-H_3(a)]/[12(l-k)] mod p.
 * Opposite signs:
     B_ab=chi4(p)[H_3(b)+H_3(a)]/[12(l+k)] mod p.
 * Nonzero diagonal: B_aa=-chi4(p)H_4(a) mod p.
 * Zero fibre a=pk,b=pl: off-diagonal
     chi4(p)[l S_3(l)-k S_3(k)]/[6(l^2-k^2)],
   and diagonal chi4(p)[-S_4(k)+S_3(k)/(12k)].

The formulas follow by cancelling the explicit node factors in Psi.
They keep a and -a together; treating them as separate fibres is wrong.

For distinct fibres s=a^2 and t=b^2 modulo p, let
 V(a)=chi4(p)a H_3(a) mod p.
Then B_ab=p[V(b)-V(a)]/[6(t-s)] mod p^2. For integral kernel vectors
y,z on the two fibres their first pairing is
 p/[6(t-s)] det[[sum y,sum y V],[sum z,sum z V]] mod p^2.
The additional factor a in V is material to transport between fibres.

3. A systematic failure of proportional-moment transport

Take p>max(M,7) and common nodes t in
 T={1<=t<=r: p/4<t<p/2, r<p-t<=M}.
Each such fibre consists of common t and the single border node p-t.
Both lie between p/4 and 3p/4, so H_3=H_4=1. If the border node is
row-only, the local column is chi4(p)*(-1,1/6)^T, with left kernel
vector (1,6). For a column-only border the right kernel is (1,6).
The moment pair is (7,-5 chi4(p)t).

For a row-surplus fibre t and column-surplus fibre s, direct substitution
therefore gives
             y_t^T B z_s=-35 chi4(p)p/[6(t+s)] mod p^2.       (3.1)
Every such pairing has valuation exactly one. Moreover its matrix after
division by p is a nonzero scalar times the Cauchy matrix 1/(t+s).
All its square minors are units: the t's and s's are distinct, their
differences are nonzero modulo p and 0<t+s<p. Hence its rank is exactly
the smaller number of surplus fibres on the two sides.

These are actual kernel pairings in the first Schur layer. Eliminating
the invertible part of B mod p changes them only at order p^2. There is
no freedom to choose a different moment direction inside these one-
dimensional local kernels. Mixing them retains the stated maximal rank.

The phenomenon occupies intervals of positive scaled length for EACH
prescribed width. Up to O(1) endpoint effects from m=floor(cr/2), use
 p/r in [3/2,31/20] for c=1/5,
 p/r in [9/5,19/10] for c=1/2,
 p/r in [11/5,5/2] for c=1.
For the first two, |T|/r=3(p/r)/4-1; for the third it is 1-(p/r)/4.
Row-only and column-only borders alternate, so both counts are |T|/2+O(1).
Thus the nonzero first pairings have rank of order r, not an isolated
exception. This blocks the direct proportional-pair window mechanism in
these regimes. It does not exclude cancellations involving other layers
or an entirely different arithmetic construction.

4. Truncated Smith exponents give all four negative layers

Let mu_i=min(4, actual Smith exponent_i of B over the Gauss DVR).
They are determined by B mod p^4, hence by Psi alone and independently
of X. Unit-pivot elimination over Z/(p^4) computes them exactly. If an
entry of least valuation v<4 is used as pivot, all other entries are
divisible by p^v; subtracting an integral multiple of its row clears
the column. This is a unimodular operation and recursively produces
the Smith exponents, capped at four.

Put tau_p=sum_i(4-mu_i). Then
   v_G(P)>=-tau_p,       v_p(den P)<=tau_p.                   (4.1)
After these pivots the remaining block is divisible by p^4. Factoring
it and the active pivots gives more precisely
   P=p^(-tau_p) u_p(X) det T_p(X),                           (4.2)
where u_p has Gauss valuation zero and T_p is integral in that DVR.
Thus v_G(P)=-tau_p+t_p with t_p=v_G(det T_p)>=0. The four-layer
estimate does not control t_p. If no capped exponent equals four,
there is no residual block and t_p=0 automatically.

This distinction is essential: the truncated negative layers do not
prove an actual primitive-value obstruction by themselves.

5. All four layers are explicit for p>2M+4

Let h=max(0,r-floor(p/4)). If 2M+4<p<4r, every border node is high
(4a>p), there are h high common nodes, and l=r-h low common nodes.
All node sums, differences and nodes are p-units. Only the odd multiple
p occurs in each singular sum, so H_3=H_4=1_high and
   p^4 Psi=chi4(p)(D+pF),
where D is -1 on high common diagonals and zero elsewhere. The low-low
block of F is zero. Its low-high entries are
   b/[6(b^2-a^2)].
These are Cauchy entries times unit column factors. Every maximal minor
with distinct row/column nodes is a unit. The endpoint allowance ensures
l>=h+m.

First eliminate the h unit pivots of D. On the remaining low-common and
border nodes the first layer has block form
    [ 0  C1 ]
    [ C2 C3 ],
with C1 full column rank m and C2 full row rank m. Its rank is 2m, so
there are precisely 2m pivots of valuation one.

Their left null vectors can be chosen on the low common rows, annihilating
C1 exactly over Z_p; right null vectors similarly annihilate C2. At the
second layer the induced matrix, up to a unit sign, is
   (Y F_low,high-common)(F_high-common,low Z).
Each factor has rank h: adjoining the h high-common Cauchy columns to
the m border columns is a unit full-rank Cauchy matrix, and likewise for
rows. The first factor is injective on an h-dimensional space and the
second surjects onto it, so their product has rank h. Eliminating the
first layer changes this second-layer block only at order p^3.

Finally D+pF itself has rank exactly 2(h+m) over Q_p. Indeed, group all
high nodes together. Its low-low block is zero and both off-diagonal
blocks have full rank h+m. The kernel consists exactly of low vectors
annihilated by one of those blocks. The h+2m+h pivots already found
account for this whole rank. There is no additional valuation-three
pivot; all remaining truncated exponents are four.

Therefore the COMPLETE capped Smith list in this range is
   0 repeated h, 1 repeated 2m, 2 repeated h,
   4 repeated N-2h-2m.                                    (5.1)
In particular tau_p=6h+6m.

If 4r<p<4M, there are no high common nodes. Let H=M-floor(p/4), the
total number of high border nodes, counting rows and columns together.
The same low/high Cauchy-block argument gives rank H for F, and the
capped Smith list is
   1 repeated H, 4 repeated N-H; tau_p=3H.                  (5.2)
Primes in the bounded endpoint strip do not affect the leading r^2 rate.

6. Quantitative obstruction to using these four layers alone

With m=cr/2+O(1), the prime number theorem sums (5.1)-(5.2) to
 sum_(2M+4<p<4M) tau_p log p=(3+3c^2)r^2+o(r^2).
Indeed the two integrals, in x=p/r, are
 integral_(2+2c)^4 6(1+c/2-x/4) dx=3(1-c^2),
 integral_4^(4+4c) 3(1+c-x/4) dx=6c^2.

This is the contribution to the SUFFICIENT denominator bound from the
four-layer method, not a lower bound for the actual primitive multiplier.
It already exceeds E(c) for all three c. Even charging zero for every
other prime leaves no favorable comparison using this method.

In fact changing the width anywhere in 0<c<=1 cannot fix this comparison.
Since 0<=rho_c<=1 on [0,1+c] and the energy integrand is nonnegative,
 E(c)<=2(1+c)^2 log 2<=4(1+c^2)log 2<3(1+c^2).
The last inequality uses 4 log 2<3. This is an analytic corollary of the
same formula, not an enlarged parameter scan or a primitive obstruction.

For completeness, the crude bound e_p<=4N at sqrt(4M)<p<=2M+4,
combined with (5), gives an all-primes sufficient upper coefficient
  log den P <= (11+12c+7c^2)r^2+o(r^2).
For p<=sqrt(4M), each rational entry has denominator valuation
O(log r/log p), uniformly. Multiplying the determinant valuation by log p
costs O(N log r) per prime. All these primes together therefore cost
O(r^(3/2) log r)=o(r^2).
This includes 2 and 3 and their higher powers. PNT is only an ordinary
prime sum here; the chi4(p) factor is a unit and does not affect the lists.
This is a bound on NEGATIVE valuations, not on positive numerator content.
No all-index estimate for the latter, even at a fixed prime, follows from it.

Recovering a favorable bound would require a new uniform theorem about
t_p in (4.2), or sufficiently large primitive numerator content elsewhere.
The precise arithmetic burden has not vanished; it is now isolated in
the residual integral determinant.

7. A stronger, narrower integer-normalization obstruction at c=1/5

For 2M+4<p<4r, the coefficient of X^(r-h) in P has EXACT valuation
  -4h-3m.                                                   (7.1)
Expand it as the sum of minors using h common nodes and all border nodes.
A minor with only j<h high common nodes has valuation at least
-4j-3(h+m-j)>-4h-3m. For the unique minor using all h high common nodes,
only the permutation terms using all h diagonals can reach the minimum.
The remaining border determinant is, modulo p after multiplying by p^(3m),
a unit scalar times the Cauchy determinant 1/(a+b). Its determinant is a
unit because all border differences and sums are nonzero modulo p.
Hence there is no cancellation at that coefficient's claimed valuation.

Consequently
  log den P >= (2-c-c^2)r^2+o(r^2).
At c=1/5 this constant is 44/25=1.76, whereas E(1/5)<1.653.
Using the inherited LOWER real-size bound, every positive INTEGER
multiplier clearing the coefficients gives values growing exponentially
in r^2. The difference is >0.1076r^2 asymptotically.

This does NOT exclude division by a large common integer numerator
factor after clearing denominators. The primitive multiplier may be
rational. No all-index bound on that numerator factor is proved here.
