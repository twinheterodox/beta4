Round 08: exact discrete tests, with no irrationality proof
29 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

The irrationality objective remains open in this packet. Two discrete
mechanisms were tested after the archive comparison. Neither supplies a
credible missing theorem. The tests below close only the stated proposals;
they are not obstructions to arbitrary arithmetic or continued fractions.

1. Rational tails do not become finite-state merely by rationality

Write S_N=sum_(0<=k<N)(-1)^k/(2k+1)^4 and, for a starting value x,

  t_N(x)=(-1)^N(x-S_N).

Then

  t_(N+1)(x)+t_N(x)=1/(2N+1)^4.                         (1)

Every rational x gives rational states. The distinguished starting value
x=theta is characterized by the decaying boundary condition t_N -> 0;
indeed t_N(x)=(-1)^N(x-theta)+t_N(theta), and

  1/[2(2N+1)^4] < t_N(theta) < 1/(2N+1)^4.             (2)

For (2), use the elementary Laplace representation
  t_N(theta)=(1/6) integral_0^infinity
      t^3 exp(-(2N+1)t)/(1+exp(-2t)) dt.
The denominator factor lies strictly between 1 and 2.

No bounded denominator or finite-state assertion follows from rational x.
For example, let p be an odd prime with (2N-1)/3 < p <= 2N-1.
There is exactly one denominator in S_N divisible by p, namely p^4.
For p not dividing den(x),

  v_p(x-S_N)=-4.                                       (3)

The archived round-05/fresh/findings.txt Section 4 proves the stronger
fixed-band denominator result, including more than the single-multiple
band in (3). Repeating that proof is unnecessary. Its proof applies to a
fixed rational correction x as well: the finite prime support of den(x)
does not change the eventual bands. Thus rational starting data do not
provide a cheap normalization of (1).

Exact irrationality implication that was sought:
  Prove that no rational orbit of (1) satisfies the decaying boundary
  condition. This would prove theta irrational directly.
Missing ingredient:
  A rigidity theorem stronger than rationality of every state or local
  valuations. Those properties already hold for all rational starts.
First decisive test:
  Derive rational states and their reduced denominators before asserting
  finite state. Equations (1)--(3) fail that proposed implication.

2. Real Euler products do not identify a p-adic value

For a finite prime cutoff X let

  E_X=product_(3<=p<=X, p prime)(1-chi_4(p)/p^4).

The absolutely convergent real Euler product gives E_X -> 1/theta.
Its exact primitive pair is

  U_X=product_p (p^4-chi_4(p)), V_X=(product_p p)^4,
  E_X=(U_X/gcd(U_X,V_X))/(V_X/gcd(U_X,V_X)).             (4)

For a fixed prime ell,
  v_ell(E_X)=sum_(p<=X) v_ell(p^4-chi_4(p))
             -4*1_(ell odd and ell<=X).               (5)
In particular, each prime p=1 mod(4ell) contributes positively to the
sum. There are infinitely many such primes. An elementary cyclotomic
proof suffices: for a prescribed finite set of primes choose an integer
A divisible by those primes and 4ell; every prime divisor r of
Phi_(4ell)(A)>1 avoids the prescribed set and 4ell, and has order 4ell
for A modulo r. Hence r=1 mod(4ell). For ell=2, each factor numerator is
even, so its valuation tends to infinity without this argument.

Consequently E_X -> 0 in every fixed Q_ell, while E_X -> 1/theta>0 in R.
There is no contradiction if the real limit is rational: the elementary
sequence n!/(n!+1) tends to 1 in R and to 0 in every Q_ell. A claimed
identification of these limits is false, even for positive rational
sequences. Equation (5) is not an assertion about a p-adic L-value.

The local argument is self-contained. For the cyclotomic step, the usual
factorization X^m-1=product_(d|m)Phi_d(X) and its reduction modulo a prime
r not dividing m imply that roots of Phi_m have exact multiplicative
order m; separability of X^m-1 supplies the disjointness. Phi_m(0)=1 for
m>1 ensures avoidance of primes dividing A.

Exact irrationality implication that was sought:
  Deduce that a nonzero rational real limit must agree with one of these
  p-adic limits, or obtain a simultaneous product-formula inequality.
Missing ingredient:
  A common quantitative height estimate forcing that agreement. Ordinary
  real convergence does not do so. The factorial example is a decisive
  counterexample to the unqualified implication.
Scope:
  This closes the bare infinite-valuation argument only. It does not rule
  out a construction using an Euler product plus a new height theorem.

3. A discrete continued fraction and its primitive normalization

This was the distinct second test after the recurrence route failed.
It does not differentiate the archived continued fraction in a parameter.
Its proposed mechanism was Euclidean descent from positivity and integer
partial coefficients, rather than a new asymptotic approximation family.

For ANY strictly increasing positive integers a_0,a_1,... define

  C_N = 1/(a_0 + a_0^2/(a_1-a_0 + a_1^2/(
                    a_2-a_1 + ... + a_(N-2)^2/(a_(N-1)-a_(N-2))))).

For N=1 this means C_1=1/a_0. Then exactly

  C_N=sum_(0<=k<N)(-1)^k/a_k.                           (6)

Proof by continuants. Set P_0=0,Q_0=1,P_1=1,Q_1=a_0, and for N>=2

  P_N=(a_(N-1)-a_(N-2))P_(N-1)+a_(N-2)^2 P_(N-2),
  Q_N=(a_(N-1)-a_(N-2))Q_(N-1)+a_(N-2)^2 Q_(N-2).

Induction gives

  Q_N=product_(k<N) a_k,
  P_N=Q_N sum_(k<N)(-1)^k/a_k.                          (7)

This proves (6), including all signs and indices. In the target case
a_n=(2n+1)^4, the positive partial denominators are

  a_n-a_(n-1)=16n(4n^2+1), n>=1,

and the partial numerators are perfect eighth powers (2n-1)^8.
All coefficients are integers. However, the ACTUAL primitive pair is

  p_N=P_N/gcd(P_N,Q_N), q_N=Q_N/gcd(P_N,Q_N),            (8)

which is exactly the reduced pair of S_N. Its error is bounded by

  q_N/[2(2N+1)^4] < |q_N theta-p_N|
                         < q_N/(2N+1)^4.              (9)

Thus the fraction has not created a smaller integer remainder. In
particular the surviving prime layers (3), and the archived stronger
denominator result, still apply after the full primitive reduction.
Raw factorial-sized continuants must not be counted as an improvement.

The exact criterion a successful Euclidean descent would need is stronger
than (9): if theta=u/v, it would have to force the positive complete
quotients to terminate or force a bounded-denominator integer remainder
to decrease to zero. Neither is a consequence of the displayed formulas.

4. A rational control with the same broad discrete features

Set

  a_n=(2n+1)(2n+3)(2n+7)(2n+9),
  f_n=1/[(2n+1)(2n+3)(2n+5)(2n+7)].

Direct cancellation proves

  1/a_n=(f_n+f_(n+1))/2,
  sum_(n>=0)(-1)^n/a_n=f_0/2=1/210.                    (10)

The corresponding fraction (6) therefore has a rational positive limit.
All its partial denominators are positive integers; its numerators are
perfect squares; a_n has degree four with leading term 16n^4; the partial
denominators have degree three; and the partial numerators have degree
eight. These are exactly the broad degree and positivity features of the
target fraction. Hence those features alone cannot justify irrationality.

The first control has overlaps between adjacent factors. Even that feature
can be removed. Set d=105 and instead take

  a_n=product_(j=0)^3(dn+1+2dj),
  g_n=1/product_(j=0)^2(dn+1+2dj).

Then

  1/a_n=(g_n-g_(n+2))/(6d),
  sum_(n>=0)(-1)^n/a_n=(g_0-g_1)/(6d)>0,              (11)

a positive rational number. Moreover gcd(a_n,a_(n+1))=1 for every n.
Indeed, any common prime between one factor of each product divides
d*(1+2k-2j), where the odd multiplier has absolute value 1,3,5, or 7.
All such primes divide d, but each linear factor is 1 mod d. This is
impossible. The corresponding Euler fraction therefore also has coprime
adjacent numerators and coprime corresponding numerator/denominator pairs.

There is a uniform version: any prescribed finite coprimality window K
can be matched by a rational control. Choose an even L>K and let d be
the product of all absolute nonzero integers Lr+h with -3<=r<=3 and
1<=h<=K. Put a_n=product_(j=0)^3(dn+1+Ldj). For 1<=h<=K, every prime
dividing gcd(a_n,a_(n+h)) would divide d*(Lr+h), hence d, again impossible.
With g_n the reciprocal of the first three factors,

  1/a_n=(g_n-g_(n+L))/(3Ld),
  sum_(n>=0)(-1)^n/a_n
       =sum_(0<=j<L)(-1)^j g_j/(3Ld),                  (12)

which is positive rational because L is even. These examples have the
same degree pattern and the same normalized leading behavior
(a_n-a_(n-1))/sqrt(a_(n-1)^2) ~ 4/n as the target.

The real difference these controls do NOT erase is that target a_n is
the fourth power of one linear polynomial. No theorem exploiting this
exact repeated-factor structure to force descent was found. Mere absence
of a rational-function antidifference is also insufficient: rationality
of a single definite sum does not assert such an antidifference exists.

The latter absence for the target follows independently: if a rational
function R(x) satisfied R(x)+R(x+1)=1/(2x+1)^4, a finite pole orbit modulo
Z would have noncanceling extreme poles unless the right side had poles
at both ends of the orbit. It has only one. Equivalently, its nonzero
order-four alternating discrete residue cannot occur in a rational
antidifference. This is functional nonsummability, NOT a numerical
irrationality theorem.

5. Checks and review

discrete_checks.py rebuilds continuants and fractions independently,
checks the rational control telescoping, records the actual primitive
target denominators and errors without using theta to select anything,
and checks finite Euler products and their valuation formula.

The adversarial worker independently verified (10), including the value
1/210, and (11), including its adjacent coprimality. It confirmed the
restricted conclusion about those features. The generalization (12) is
proved directly above and has exact finite checks; it has not received a
separate agent review. No review established a perfect-power descent.

The tests make no novelty claim and import no specialized irrationality
theorem. The identities have self-contained proofs above. The stronger
prime-band denominator estimate is explicitly reused from the archived
round-05 proof, not represented as a new result of this packet.
