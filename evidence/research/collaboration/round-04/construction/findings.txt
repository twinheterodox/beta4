Round 04 construction: differentiated hypergeometric tails for beta(4)
29 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

Status and selected construction

The initial independent ideas are recorded in 00_initial_ideas.txt. The
selected direction is a rational hypergeometric kernel, differentiated
twice and summed on an alternating half-integer tail. This specifies
every coefficient without knowing an approximation to beta(4), and is
not the earlier free-parameter Rodrigues space or a Hankel determinant.

The main integer-pole family has a proved two-sided real asymptotic:

 S_n ~ (-1)^(n+1) 3 sqrt(pi) n^(-5/2) (log n)^2 (27/256)^n.       (A)

In particular it is eventually nonzero. Its target coefficients and
rational remainders are explicit factorial/harmonic sums, and its
dyadic denominator cost is only polynomial. The odd-prime fifth layer
survives: whenever p=4n-1>=7 is prime, the ACTUAL primitive multiplier
has v_p(kappa_n)=5.

An explicit integer clearing multiplier is given below. That particular
cleared sequence actually diverges, by (A) and the prime number theorem.
The primitive sequence has smaller multipliers, and its tested values
also fail except n=0 and the isolated n=1 form 1458X-1441. No uniform
primitive lower bound or general impossibility theorem is claimed.

The preceding half-integer-pole variant is also documented. It has a
proved real asymptotic, but its actual dyadic-only normalization diverges.
The integer-pole modification removes this specific obstruction while
leaving a severe odd-prime/content problem. No irrationality is proved.

1. Definition and the exact beta(4)-only form

Let theta=beta(4). For n>=0 define

 R_n(u)=n! (2u+n) (u-n)_n (u+n+1)_n / (u)_(n+1)^3,          (1.1)

where (a)_m=a(a+1)...(a+m-1), with (a)_0=1. Our prescribed tail is

             S_n=sum_(k=n)^infinity (-1)^k R_n''(k+1/2).   (1.2)

The rational function is O(u^(-n-2)), so this sum converges absolutely
for every fixed n. No limiting interchange involving an unknown theta
is used to choose the starting index n or the coefficients.

Write its partial fractions as

 R_n(u)=sum_(j=0)^n [c_(3,j)/(u+j)^3+c_(2,j)/(u+j)^2
                                           +c_(1,j)/(u+j)].

Set H_0=0 and H_m=sum_(a=1)^m 1/a. Explicitly put

 a_j=(-1)^(n+j) binom(n,j)^3 binom(n+j,n) binom(2n-j,n),
 L_j=n-2j,
 d_j=4H_j-4H_(n-j)-H_(n+j)+H_(2n-j),

 e_j= -sum_(l=0)^(n-1) [1/(-j-n+l)^2+1/(-j+n+1+l)^2]
                         +3 sum_(l=0,l!=j)^n 1/(l-j)^2.

Then

 c_(3,j)=L_j a_j,
 c_(2,j)=a_j(2+L_j d_j),
 c_(1,j)=a_j[2d_j+(L_j/2)(d_j^2+e_j)].                    (1.3)

To derive these, remove (u+j)^(-3), separate the linear factor 2u+n,
and take the first two Taylor coefficients. The remaining factor has
value a_j, logarithmic derivative d_j, and second logarithmic derivative
e_j at u=-j. Formula (1.3) remains valid at j=n/2, where L_j=0.

The exact reflection is

                 R_n(-u-n)=(-1)^n R_n(u).

It gives c_(s,n-j)=(-1)^(n+s)c_(s,j), hence

                  sum_j (-1)^j c_(s,j)=0 for s=1,3.      (1.4)

Differentiating twice raises the three pole orders to 3,4,5, with
multipliers s(s+1). Define the rational finite tails

 T_r(m)=sum_(l=0)^(m-1) (-1)^l/(2l+1)^r,  T_r(0)=0.

Substitution in (1.2), followed by (1.4), proves EXACTLY

 S_n=A_n theta+B_n,
 A_n=96 sum_(j=0)^n (-1)^j c_(2,j),
 B_n=-sum_(j=0)^n sum_(s=1)^3
           (-1)^j 2^(s+2) s(s+1)c_(s,j) T_(s+2)(n+j).    (1.5)

There is no beta(2), beta(3), beta(5), or power of pi left in (1.5).
The unwanted odd beta coefficients vanish algebraically, before any
evaluation. This cancellation is the reason for differentiating a
third-order-pole reflection-symmetric kernel exactly twice.

For nonzero (A_n,B_n), define

 D_n=lcm(den A_n,den B_n),
 g_n=gcd(D_n A_n,D_n B_n)>0,
 kappa_n=D_n/g_n>0,
 P_n(X)=kappa_n(A_n X+B_n),                               (1.6)

so P_n is the primitive integer pair. The scalar kappa_n can be
rational. The real nonvanishing proved below is for the actual S_n,
and therefore also for its primitive normalization.

2. An exact contour reduction with a uniform remainder bound

For n>=2 put

 w=n-1/2, c=n-1, K(u)=-pi sec(pi u).

K has residue (-1)^k at u=k+1/2. Closing a vertical line to the right
and using the decay of R_n gives

 S_n=-1/(2pi i) int_(Re u=n) R_n''(u)K(u) du
     =-1/(2pi i) int_(Re u=n) R_n(u)K''(u) du.             (2.1)

The second equality is integration by parts twice; the boundary terms
vanish exponentially on the vertical line. For fixed n, the right
closing line tends to zero because R_n and its derivatives decay
polynomially, while the horizontal edges vanish by the trigonometric
factor. The lines Re u=n and Re u=c avoid all poles of R_n and K.

Shift the line in (2.1) from n to c. Exactly one pole is crossed,
at w=n-1/2. The local expansion is

 K(u)=(-1)^(n-1)[1/(u-w)+O(u-w)],
 K''(u)=2(-1)^(n-1)/(u-w)^3+O(u-w).

The residue of R_n K'' is consequently (-1)^(n-1)R_n''(w).
With the clockwise closing orientation accounted for, this gives

 S_n=(-1)^n R_n''(w)+E_n,
 E_n=-1/(2pi i) int_(Re u=c) R_n(u)K''(u) du.             (2.2)

We now prove the explicit uniform estimate

                       |E_n|<10 |R_n'(c)|, n>=2.         (2.3)

At c=n-1, R_n has a simple zero. Write g=R_n'(c). Along c+iy, divide
the product (1.1) by the corresponding nonzero factors defining g.
The first numerator product, after removing its zero, has distances
1,...,n-2 and an extra distance 1. Euler's product therefore gives

 product_first <= sqrt(1+y^2)
                       sqrt[sinh(pi|y|)/(pi|y|)].

The linear factor contributes at most sqrt(1+y^2), since 3n-2>=4.
Each second numerator factor has distance 2n-1+a, 1<=a<=n. Pair it
with one denominator factor of distance n+a-2; the numerator distance
is larger, so its factor sqrt(1+y^2/distance^2) is smaller. The two
remaining copies of each denominator only improve the bound. Hence

 |R_n(c+iy)| <= |g| |y|(1+y^2)
                       sqrt[sinh(pi|y|)/(pi|y|)].        (2.4)

At integer c, an elementary differentiation gives

 |K''(c+iy)|=pi^3 sech(pi y)|2 sech(pi y)^2-1|
                                      <=pi^3 sech(pi y).

Combining this with (2.4), and using sinh(v)<=exp(v)/2 and
sech(v)<=2exp(-v) for v>=0, bounds |E_n|/|g| by

 pi^2 sqrt(2/pi) int_0^infinity y^(1/2)(1+y^2)e^(-pi y/2)dy
                     =2sqrt(pi)+30/pi^(3/2)<10.          (2.5)

The last inequality follows already from 3<pi<4 and sqrt(3)>5/3.
This proves (2.3). Thus the contour estimate is fully explicit and
does not depend on an unproved steepest-descent or saddle assumption.

3. Stirling analysis and eventual nonvanishing

The estimates in this section are consequences of exact gamma products
and the standard Stirling expansion at positive real arguments. They
are separate from the contour inequality just proved.

The rational kernel has the gamma-product expression

 R_n(u)=Gamma(n+1)(2u+n)
       Gamma(u)^4 Gamma(u+2n+1)/[Gamma(u-n)Gamma(u+n+1)^4].

At w=n-1/2,

 R_n(w)=Gamma(n+1)(3n-1)Gamma(n-1/2)^4 Gamma(3n+1/2)
                         /[Gamma(-1/2)Gamma(2n+1/2)^4]
       ~ -3sqrt(pi) n^(-5/2) rho^n,
 rho=27/256.                                             (3.1)

At the zero c=n-1, differentiation of 1/Gamma(u-n) gives

 R_n'(c)=-Gamma(n+1)(3n-2)Gamma(n-1)^4 Gamma(3n)/Gamma(2n)^4
        ~ -8sqrt(3)pi n^(-3) rho^n.                     (3.2)

For the second derivative at w use R_n''=R_n[(log R_n)'^2+
(log R_n)'']. Logarithmic derivatives here mean the rational logarithmic
derivative R_n'/R_n, independent of a choice of logarithm of the negative
value R_n(w). The digamma recurrences yield

 R_n'(w)/R_n(w)=log n+C+O(1/n),
 (R_n'/R_n)'(w)=-(pi^2/2+4)+O(1/n),
 C=gamma+log(3/4)-2.                                     (3.3)

For example psi(-1/2)=-gamma-2log2+2 and
psi_1(-1/2)=pi^2/2+4; all other arguments are positive multiples of n
plus constants. Thus

 R_n''(w)=R_n(w)[(log n+C)^2-(pi^2/2+4)+O(log n/n)].       (3.4)

Equations (3.1)--(3.4), with (2.2)--(2.3), prove

 S_n=(-1)^(n+1) 3sqrt(pi)n^(-5/2)rho^n
       [(log n+C)^2-(pi^2/2+4)+O(n^(-1/2))+O(log^2 n/n)]

and in particular the leading equivalence (A). Therefore S_n is
nonzero for all sufficiently large n, with eventual sign (-1)^(n+1).
No conjecture about Catalan's constant or beta(4) is used.

The logarithmic correction in (3.4) causes slow sign changes; the
leading log-squared term must not be used as a sign prediction at small
n. In exact/Arb checks, the direct residue approximation is close at
n=256 and (2.3) alone then certifies nonvanishing. The theorem itself
does not rely on fitting these finite data.

4. Explicit arithmetic, including the fifth odd-prime layer

Let L_m=lcm(1,...,m), O_m=lcm of the positive odd integers <=m,
and s=floor(log_2(2n)), for n>=1. Formula (1.3) gives

 a_j,c_(3,j) in Z,
 L_(2n)c_(2,j) in Z,
 2L_(2n)^2 c_(1,j) in Z.                                 (4.1)

This is immediate from the displayed harmonic sums; all their integer
denominators have magnitude at most 2n. The finite tails in (1.5) use
odd denominators at most 4n-1. The coefficient factors are respectively
16,96,384 for s=1,2,3 in (1.5). Since the odd part of L_(2n) divides
O_(4n-1), it follows that

 M_n=2^max(0,2s-3) O_(4n-1)^5

              satisfies M_n A_n,M_n B_n in Z.             (4.2)

This is a fully specified integer sequence. In particular its dyadic
cost is only O(log n): the actual primitive multiplier obeys

                 v_2(kappa_n)<=max(0,2s-3).              (4.3)

Together with (A), (4.3) proves that dyadic-only normalized values
2^max(0,v_2(kappa_n))S_n shrink and are eventually nonzero. It does
not make their odd denominators disappear. The exact tests have
v_2(kappa_n)=-5 for all tested positive indices, but no uniform claim
of equality is needed or proved here.

The fifth odd-prime layer in (4.2) is not just an artifact of this crude
clearing. Suppose p=4n-1 is prime, with p>=7. All partial-fraction
coefficients are p-integral because p>2n. Among the finite tails in
(1.5), the denominator p first occurs at l=2n-1, and only the term
j=n includes it. Contributions from pole orders 3 or 4 cannot survive
after multiplication by p^5. Therefore

 p^5 B_n =384(-1)^(n-1)n binom(2n,n) mod p,                (4.4)

which is nonzero: p>2n and p does not divide 384. Also A_n is
p-integral. Hence the exact local primitive exponent is

                         v_p(kappa_n)=5.                 (4.5)

The sign and constant in (4.4) come from c_(3,n)=-n binom(2n,n)
and the final character value (-1)^(2n-1)=-1. The checks reproduce
(4.4)--(4.5) for nine prescribed primes. This local result is not a
global lower bound on kappa_n or on the total common content.

The sequence A_n/192 agrees in every checked case with the Apéry
zeta(2) numbers sum_k binom(n,k)^2 binom(n+k,k). That recognition is
recorded as finite evidence, NOT used as an unproved identity in any
theorem here. The explicit formulas (1.3)--(1.5), arithmetic (4.1),
and real nonvanishing are sufficient for the present conclusions.

5. The actual budget and a concrete stopping criterion

The prime number theorem gives log O_(4n-1)=4n+o(n), so

                    log M_n=20n+o(n).

The two-sided asymptotic (A), not merely a real upper estimate, therefore
proves ACTUAL divergence of this particular explicit cleared sequence:

             lim log|M_n S_n|/n=20+log(27/256)>0.          (5.1)

This does not settle the primitive sequence. If

             G_n=gcd(M_n A_n,M_n B_n)>0,

then the actual multiplier is kappa_n=M_n/G_n. An exponential-scale
primitive success would require a common-content rate exceeding

                   20+log(27/256)=17.75065942... .        (5.2)

Conversely, a uniform limsup log G_n/n strictly below (5.2) would prove
actual primitive divergence by (A). No such uniform content theorem is
available. The fifth-layer result (4.5) alone does not supply it.

Finite exact primitive diagnostics for the main family include:

 n     log|S_n|       log|P_n(theta)|      v_2(kappa_n)
 1      -1.0552           -0.1265               -5
 2      -3.8888           13.7181               -5
 4     -10.0543           35.4031               -5
 8     -20.4982          106.9671               -5
16     -38.8193          217.5724               -5

The isolated n=1 pair is 1458X-1441. The n=0 pair is just X.
Neither is an asymptotic success. Larger prescribed checks at
32,64,128,256 were made to test the DERIVED contour estimate and sign
crossover, not to scan for a miraculous primitive reversal.

The current branch should not be continued as a generic parameter scan.
Its precise next gate would be a structural primitive-content theorem
or a new modification that removes the surviving fifth odd-prime layer
while retaining the explicit real cancellation. Without such a mechanism,
the known integer normalization fails by (5.1), and all tested primitive
values beyond the isolated n=1 case are large. This is a decision to
pivot, not a theorem excluding every hypergeometric construction.

6. The preceding half-integer-pole candidate and why it was replaced

For comparison, the first candidate was based directly on Zudilin's
Catalan kernel

 Q_n(t)=n!(2t+n+1)(t-n+1)_n(t+n+1)_n/(t+1/2)_(n+1)^3,
 U_n=sum_(k=n)^infinity (-1)^k Q_n''(k).                   (6.1)

It corresponds to alpha=1/2 in the check script's unified kernel

 R_(n,alpha)(u)=n!(2u+n)(u-n+alpha)_n(u+n+1-alpha)_n
                                                     /(u)_(n+1)^3.

The main candidate is alpha=0. Both have exactly the same reflection
parity and beta(4)-only extraction. In (6.1) the initial undifferentiated
zeros are at 0,...,n-1, but differentiation destroys their vanishing.
Starting the derivative sum at n is essential: one cannot simply
transfer the known Catalan error theorem to a differentiated full sum.

This first candidate also admits a self-contained contour estimate.
Use K(t)=pi csc(pi t), shift from Re t=n-1/2 to Re t=n-3/2, and
integrate by parts twice as before. Exactly the root at n-1 is crossed:

 U_n=(-1)^n Q_n''(n-1)+Etilde_n,
             |Etilde_n|<40 |Q_n(n-3/2)|, n>=2.            (6.2)

For the bound, the first numerator distances are the positive
half-integers, with an extra distance 1/2. Pair the second numerator
with one denominator block. Euler's product for cosh gives

 |Q_n(n-3/2+iy)| <=2|Q_n(n-3/2)|(1+y^2)sqrt(cosh(pi y)).

At that half-integer line, |K''|<=pi^3 sech(pi y). The resulting
integral constant is at most 4sqrt(2)pi+32sqrt(2)/pi<40.
This proves (6.2), including its orientation and residue sign.

Direct Stirling calculations give

 Q_n(n)~(3pi/4)sqrt(3/2)n^(-1)rho^n,
 Q_n'(n-1)~4pi sqrt(3/2)n^(-2)rho^n,
 Q_n''(n-1)~8pi sqrt(3/2)n^(-2)log n rho^n,
 Q_n(n-3/2)=O(n^(-5/2)rho^n).

Thus

 U_n~(-1)^n 8pi sqrt(3/2)n^(-2)log n rho^n, rho=27/256,    (6.3)

which proves eventual nonvanishing for this candidate too.

Its target coefficient is 192u_n, where u_n is the classical Catalan
coefficient in [Z], with u_0=1,u_1=7/4. This factor follows directly
by raising pole order two to four: the beta(4) coefficient is 24 times
the original beta(2) coefficient, and the original kernel sum has
coefficient 8u_n. The source recurrence gives an exact 2-adic theorem.
Write

 p(n)=20n^2-8n+1,
 q(n)=3520n^6+5632n^5+2064n^4-384n^3-156n^2+16n+7.

Both are odd at integer n. The recurrence is

 (2n+1)^2(2n+2)^2 p(n)u_(n+1)
       =q(n)u_n+(2n-1)^2(2n)^2 p(n+1)u_(n-1).

An induction shows

             v_2(u_n)=-4n+2s_2(n),                       (6.4)

where s_2(n) is the binary digit sum. Indeed the second term on the
right has valuation 4+4v_2(n) larger than the first, under this formula.
The denominator on the left has valuation 2+2v_2(n+1), giving the
inductive step; the stated initial values start it.

Consequently the ACTUAL primitive multiplier for (6.1) has
v_2(kappa_n)>=4n-2s_2(n)-6. Combining this with the TWO-SIDED (6.3)
proves that its dyadic-only normalized absolute values tend to infinity,
since 16*rho=27/16>1. This is an actual obstruction at that local
normalization, not a claim about the full primitive values: negative
odd valuations from common numerator content could in principle alter
the full multiplier. The integer-pole modification (1.1) removes this
proved exponential dyadic cost without claiming that it solves the odd
arithmetic problem.

7. Reproduction, provenance, and review scope

From beta4/ run:

 PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 .venv/bin/python \
     collaboration/round-04/construction/derivative_tail.py
 PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 .venv/bin/python \
     collaboration/round-04/construction/residue_checks.py

The first script computes the prescribed forms from exact rational
partial fractions, checks reflection and beta-value cancellation, and
normalizes each pair primitively before Arb evaluation. It covers both
alpha=1/2 and alpha=0. The second checks the rigorous contour bound at
eight prescribed indices through n=256, the explicit integer clearing,
the finite Apéry-coefficient recognition, and nine exact fifth-layer
congruences. Its n=256 residue alone exceeds the proved remainder bound,
giving a numerical certificate of nonvanishing without using theta.

Both scripts write only adjacent JSON files. Larger checks were selected
to challenge the derived asymptotic, including its sign crossover. They
do not constitute a proof of that asymptotic or a primitive-size law.

[Z] W. Zudilin, An Apéry-like Difference Equation for Catalan's Constant,
arXiv:math/0201024, especially equations (2)--(4), (7), (17)--(18), and
the coefficient recurrence in Lemma 4:
https://www.math.ru.nl/~wzudilin/PS/cf_catalan.pdf
Read those definitions and recurrence statements. The original Catalan
kernel and recurrence are classical inputs. Its error bound is NOT
assumed to persist under differentiation; sections 2,3,6 supply new
explicit estimates for the tails considered here.

Also inspected for context, without importing a theorem into this
construction: Zudilin, A few remarks on linear forms involving Catalan's
constant, arXiv:math/0210423, section 1; and P. Eskandari, Improved
rational approximations to Catalan's constant, arXiv:2609.26354, abstract
and introduction. No optimality or novelty claim is inferred from that
limited reading. The goal was to obtain a concrete prescribed family
with auditable cancellation and arithmetic, not merely a generic
suggestion that hypergeometric functions might help.

The construction is ready for independent review of its residue signs,
uniform vertical-line estimate, Stirling constants, and local arithmetic.
The unresolved part is the total primitive common content. The present
packet supplies no favorable bound for it and does not assert that
beta(4), or Catalan's constant, is irrational.
