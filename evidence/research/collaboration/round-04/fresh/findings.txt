Character-compatible Loewner determinants for beta(4)
=====================================================
29 October 2026. Local and unpublished research record.

No irrationality proof has been found.  This investigation produces a
full-parity beta(4)-only determinant, a quantitative nonvanishing mechanism
for selected principal minors, and exact large-prime denominator laws.  Its
tested primitive values grow.  Pole cancellation by an entire multiplier
does not remove the fourth prime-denominator layer.  A binomial projection
restores some real decay in finite tests but also increases the arithmetic
cost.  No asymptotic failure theorem for all these projections is claimed.

The coordinator separately proves an actual primitive-value obstruction
for a heavily shifted full-parity subfamily.  I reviewed that proof in
coordinator_review.txt; it is not duplicated here.

1. Initial choice and pivot
----------------------------
00_initial_ideas.txt was written before reading the archive.  The first
choice was the elementary Mellin moment sequence

  m_j=(1/6) integral_0^1 x^(2j)(-log x)^3/(1+x^2) dx
      =(-1)^j[theta-sum_(k<j) (-1)^k/(2k+1)^4],
  theta=beta(4).

Its Hankel theta coefficient is the rank-one matrix [(-1)^i(-1)^j].
Thus that first construction does not give growing-degree polynomials.
The single recorded pivot was to cubic-tail divided differences.  The
archive's ordinary integer-step Loewner pencil was only a tested prototype;
its positive squared-node transform had already been analyzed separately.
The present work concerns the ordinary pencil and a denser, character-
compatible grid, not the already excluded positive transformation.

2. A full-parity pencil with no auxiliary period
------------------------------------------------
For integers a>=0 put

  S_j(a)=sum_(0<=k<a) (-1)^k/(2k+1)^j,
  g_j(z)=sum_(k>=0) (-1)^k/(2k+1+2z)^j.

Initially these series are used in Re z>-1/2.  For j>=2 they have the
usual meromorphic continuation, with the displayed poles on negative
half-integers.  Splitting off a finite prefix gives

  (-1)^a g_j(a)=beta(j)-S_j(a).                         (2.1)

Define F(z)=cos(pi z)g_3(z).  Then at every nonnegative integer,

  F(a)=beta(3)-S_3(a),
  F'(a)=-6[theta-S_4(a)].                              (2.2)

The sine contribution to F' vanishes at integers, and g_3'=-6g_4.
Consequently the normalized divided-difference matrix is

  K_aa(X)=X-S_4(a),
  K_ab(X)=(S_3(a)-S_3(b))/(6(a-b)), a!=b.              (2.3)

At X=theta it equals -F[a,b]/6, using F' on the diagonal.  The constant
beta(3)=pi^3/32 cancels from every off-diagonal entry.  No assumption
about Catalan's constant is made or needed.  On n distinct common nodes,
det K(X) is monic of degree n, so the beta coefficient has full rank.

The old cubic-tail pencil is exactly the even-node restriction a=2b:
S_j(2b)=sum_(k<b)[(4k+1)^(-j)-(4k+3)^(-j)], and the factor 6(a-b)
becomes 12(b-c).  Thus this is a concrete enlargement of that construction,
using every odd denominator rather than only complete positive-negative
pairs as interpolation nodes.

3. Pole cancellation is exact, but is not an arithmetic saving
---------------------------------------------------------------
At z0=-k-1/2 the principal part of g_3 is

  (-1)^k/[8(z-z0)^3].

The first derivative of cos(pi z) at z0 is pi(-1)^k.  Therefore F has
the principal part pi/[8(z-z0)^2], with no simple-pole term.

There is an even stronger completion:

  E(z)=cos(pi z)^3 g_3(z).                              (3.1)

All its apparent poles are removable, and E(z0)=pi^3/8.  Thus E is
ENTIRE.  Nevertheless it gives exactly the same values and first
derivatives as F at every integer, hence exactly the same matrix (2.3).
The function E is not rational: E(a)=(-1)^a g_3(a) is nonzero, alternates
in sign, and tends to zero as a tends to infinity.  A rational entire
function is a polynomial and cannot have those values.

These facts do not supply a determinant estimate.  In particular the
cosine multiplier introduces substantial growth away from the real
interpolation grid.  The exact prime laws below show directly that
removing the analytic poles has not removed the arithmetic fourth layer.
It would be incorrect to infer an inexpensive primitive normalization
from the word 'entire'.

The standard polygamma recurrence and pole description are recorded in
NIST DLMF section 5.15, especially 5.15.1 and 5.15.5:
https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.15
The identities (2.1)--(3.1) above also follow directly from the displayed
series and local Taylor expansion, without relying on a literature claim.

4. Nonvanishing from alternating Loewner data
---------------------------------------------
The following elementary rank fact is useful and does not assume a fixed
sign for determinants.

LEMMA. Let x_0<...<x_(N-1) be real, let f_j be nonzero real numbers with
alternating signs, and let L be any real symmetric matrix whose off-diagonal
entries are (f_i-f_j)/(x_i-x_j).  Its diagonal may be arbitrary.  Then

                       rank L >= ceil((N-1)/2).          (4.1)

Proof. Suppose rank L=r<N.  Among its first r+1 rows choose a nonzero
dependence v=(v_0,...,v_r).  Set

  p(z)=product_(i=0)^r(z-x_i),
  B(z)=sum_(i=0)^r v_i p(z)/(z-x_i),
  A(z)=sum_(i=0)^r v_i f_i p(z)/(z-x_i).

Both have degree <=r.  Neither is identically zero, by evaluation at
the x_i and the facts v!=0 and f_i!=0.  At every column j>r, the row
dependence gives A(x_j)=f_j B(x_j).  At j<=r this identity holds
automatically; if v_j!=0 then B(x_j)!=0, while if v_j=0 both A and B
vanish there.

Let C=gcd(A,B) have degree c, and cancel it to obtain the real rational
function R=(A/C)/(B/C).  Except at at most c of the N nodes, R(x_j)=f_j.
At each retained node its denominator is nonzero, since the two reduced
polynomials are coprime.  Deleting at most c entries from an alternating
list leaves at least N-1-2c sign changes between consecutive retained
entries.  Every such sign change requires a real zero or pole of R in
the intervening open interval.  These intervals are disjoint, whereas
the total number of real zeros and poles is at most 2(r-c).  Therefore
N-1-2c<=2(r-c), which is (4.1).  QED.

Apply the lemma to f_j=F(j)=(-1)^j g_3(j).  The alternating-series test
gives g_3(j)>0.  Multiplying all entries by -6 does not change rank.
Hence the actual N-by-N full-parity K(theta) has rank at least
ceil((N-1)/2).

This gives selected principal minors with a controlled node range.  If
a real symmetric N-by-N matrix has rank r, the coefficient of t^(N-r)
in its characteristic polynomial is the nonzero product, up to sign,
of its r nonzero eigenvalues.  It is also, up to sign, the sum of all
principal r-by-r minors.  Thus some such principal minor is nonzero.
Combining this elementary identity with the specific Loewner rank lemma
proves:

  For every N, some principal minor of K(theta), using only nodes
  0,...,N-1, is nonzero and has order r>=ceil((N-1)/2).       (4.2)

Each chosen minor is a monic rational polynomial in theta of degree r.
This is not a claim that the leading consecutive determinant is nonzero,
nor that every size has a nonzero principal minor of exactly that size.
It does not select a minor with a favorable primitive value.  Accordingly
it supplies a nonvanishing mechanism, not an irrationality estimate.

For contrast, the old EVEN-grid leading determinant of order 8 is
negative at offset 0 and positive at offset 8, as certified independently
by direct Hurwitz-tail Arb determinants.  Continuity in the real offset
therefore gives an actual zero in between.  This excludes a universal
sign-regularity or all-real-offset nonvanishing claim for that old family.

5. Exact fourth layers for the unprojected full grid
----------------------------------------------------
Let P_N(X)=det K(X) on nodes 0,...,N-1.  Its primitive positive multiplier
Q_N is the least common denominator of its coefficients.  It is an integer,
and Q_N P_N has content one because P_N is monic.

Fix an odd prime p>N, p>=5, and put

  t=N-(p+1)/2,

assuming t>0.  Only the odd denominator p can occur in the prefixes.
Writing chi_4(p)=(-1)^((p-1)/2), direct reduction gives

  p^4 K_aa(0) = -chi_4(p) if a>=(p+1)/2, and 0 otherwise,
  p^4 K_ab(0) = 0 for a!=b,                         modulo p. (5.1)

Off-diagonal entries have valuation at least -3 because p does not divide
a-b.  The coefficient of X^(N-t) is a sum of principal t-minors.  The
unique term of valuation -4t uses all t high diagonal entries.  Thus

  v_p([X^(N-t)]P_N)=-4t,
  p^(4t)[X^(N-t)]P_N=(-chi_4(p))^t mod p.                 (5.2)

In particular v_p(Q_N)>=4t.  Summing just these large primes, the prime
number theorem gives

                       log Q_N >= N^2+o(N^2).           (5.3)

Indeed the main weighted-prime sum is the integral of 4N-2x over
N<x<2N.  This is a lower bound on the ACTUAL monic primitive multiplier,
not merely a sufficient coefficient clearing factor.  It does not by
itself lower-bound |Q_N P_N(theta)|.

For comparison, on the old even grid with n nodes 0,...,n-1, primes p>n,
p>=7 have a high diagonal residue

  -chi_4(p)[1_(4a>p)-1_(4a>3p)/81].

Every nonzero residue is a unit: its value is -chi_4(p) or
-80chi_4(p)/81.  If r is the number of a with 4a>p, the coefficient
of X^(n-r) has valuation exactly -4r.  The corresponding large-prime
lower bound for its primitive multiplier is (9/2)n^2+o(n^2).
The denser grid does reduce this particular arithmetic lower bound;
the real determinant cost must be analyzed at the same time.

6. Binomial projections and a second exact arithmetic law
---------------------------------------------------------
To suppress the real alternation, take n,m>=0 with n>=1, put N=n+m,
and define the n-by-N integer matrix

  U_(i,k)=binom(m,k-i), 0<=i<n, 0<=k<N,

with out-of-range binomials zero.  This is a row filter with source
polynomials z^i(1+z)^m.  Set

  A=UU^T, B=U K(0) U^T,
  R_(n,m)(X)=det(XA+B)/det A.                            (6.1)

A is positive definite, because the shifted source polynomials are
linearly independent.  Thus R is a well-defined monic degree-n rational
polynomial.  The normalization is essential: raw determinant scaling
must not be mistaken for an arithmetic improvement.

Vandermonde convolution and the classical binomial Toeplitz determinant
give

  A_(i,j)=binom(2m,m+i-j),
  D_(r,m):=det A_r
    =product_(j=0)^(r-1) j!(2m+j)!/(m+j)!^2.              (6.2)

This product is classical, not a novelty claim.  One primary exposition
is Darij Grinberg, A hyperfactorial divisibility, Theorem 8, specialized
to a=b=m,c=r:
https://darijgrinberg.gitlab.io/hyperfactorialBRIEF.pdf
Only that theorem/formula and its immediate context were consulted here.

Let p>max(N,2m,3), p<2N-1, and t=N-(p+1)/2.  Then 0<t<n.  The last t
columns W of U vanish in its first n-t rows; their bottom t-by-t block
is triangular with diagonal one.  Formula (5.1) gives

                    p^4 B = -chi_4(p) W W^T mod p.        (6.3)

For the coefficient of X^(n-t) in det(XA+B), multiply by p^(4t) and
reduce.  The supported lower-right block in (6.3) forces

  p^(4t)[X^(n-t)]det(XA+B)
       =(-chi_4(p))^t D_(n-t,m) mod p.                    (6.4)

This residue is nonzero.  Indeed n-t=(p+1)/2-m, so the largest factorial
argument in D_(n-t,m) is m+(p-1)/2<p; all its numerator and denominator
factorials are p-units.  Therefore (6.4) is an EXACT valuation -4t,
and the monic coefficient in (6.1) has exact valuation

                  -4t-v_p(D_(n,m)).                     (6.5)

If Q_(n,m) is the actual monic primitive multiplier, (6.5) implies
v_p(Q_(n,m))>=4t+v_p(D_(n,m))>=4t.  The possible content of the
unnormalized determinant is not ignored; it is handled by (6.1).

For any fixed rho>=0 with m/n->rho, summing this prime band gives

  liminf log Q_(n,m)/n^2 >= (1+rho)^2,  0<=rho<=1,
  liminf log Q_(n,m)/n^2 >= 4,          rho>=1.             (6.6)

The integral is (2N-max(N,2m))^2.  The bound uses only one large-prime
band and can be far below the actual multiplier.  It is not a theorem
that all binomial projections have growing primitive values.

7. Isolated primes also give conditional nonvanishing
------------------------------------------------------
On consecutive full-parity nodes h,...,h+n-1, suppose

                         h+n<p<2h                       (7.1)

for a prime p.  Every prefix contains p, and no other odd multiple of p.
Its contribution cancels from every off-diagonal difference, so those
entries are p-integral.  For every p-integral rational x,

              p^4 K(x)=-chi_4(p) I mod p,
              v_p(det K(x))=-4n.                        (7.2)

Thus, under a hypothetical equality theta=a/b, any such prime not dividing
b certifies nonvanishing of the evaluated determinant.  This is sufficient
nonvanishing for a rationality contradiction if a favorable real/primitive
bound were also available.  It does not assert irrationality by itself.

At x=0, (7.2) also proves that the monic primitive multiplier has valuation
exactly 4n at p.  The coordinator combines this with a real lower bound in
the overshift theorem; see ../coordinator/loewner_shift.txt and my review.

8. Exact finite results and what they show
------------------------------------------
All displayed logarithms are natural.  Full-parity examples at offset 0:

    n       log |P_n(theta)|       log Q_n       log |Q_n P_n(theta)|
    2            -3.251631          3.583519             0.331888
    8           -48.476904        215.524807           167.047902
   16          -133.332949       1047.147981           913.815033
   24          -231.428011       2545.443642          2314.015631
   32          -337.964211       4749.296964          4411.332752

For n=2 the primitive polynomial is exactly 36X^2-36X-1.  The raw
values decay, but their observed logarithms do not have the old even-grid
quadratic size.  This finite observation is not stated as an asymptotic
theorem.  The coordinator proves a separate, uniformly overshifted failure.

The binomial projections use the monic normalization (6.1).  Examples:

   n=m      log |R_(n,m)(theta)|    log Q_(n,m)    primitive log
     4             -23.619003        142.559856      118.940852
     8             -81.088224        704.509281      623.421057
    16            -246.838624       3111.225170     2864.386546

There are 24 full-parity tests (orders through 32, offsets 0 and n) and
33 binomial-projection tests (orders through 16, m through 3n).  All 55
of those tests of degree at least two have primitive absolute value >1.
The two degree-one cases are the trivial X and X-1.  The separate old
even-grid baseline has 22 cases, including two degree-one forms, and
all of its 20 higher-degree primitive values likewise exceed one.

No limiting sign or asymptotic failure assertion is inferred from these
finite counts.  The new selected-minor nonvanishing theorem (4.2) does
not make these failing primitive budgets favorable.

9. Verification, sources, and reproduction
-------------------------------------------
loewner_probe.py constructs exact rational characteristic polynomials,
clears to primitive integer coefficients, and uses Arb Hurwitz-zeta
enclosures for theta.  filter_probe.py carries out (6.1) exactly.  The
separate checks.py does not import either implementation.  It checks:

  * 8 small polynomials by a separate permutation expansion;
  * 193 exact large-prime valuation/congruence instances;
  * 111 isolated-prime evaluations at explicit rational arguments;
  * 6 direct Hurwitz-tail determinant comparisons/sign certificates.

The auxiliary weight-kernel sign probes in loewner_probe.json use mpmath
and are labeled diagnostics; they are not used to prove any theorem.
The coordinator cross-review additionally checks exact Cauchy inverses
and determinant/norm bounds for 24 orders, in a separate file.

The archive's Loewner starting point was attributed there to selected
definitions in a working repository by S. Litsey.  No claimed irrationality
result from that repository is used here.  The present elementary rank,
period, and prime calculations are proved above, not inferred from a claim.

Targeted literature searches for Dirichlet beta/Loewner/divided differences,
Catalan/Loewner, and cosine/polygamma entire interpolation did not locate an
identified exact match for (2.3) or (6.1).  This was a narrow search and
does not establish novelty.  The binomial determinant (6.2) is explicitly
classical.  There is no priority claim, external audit, or formal verification.

What remains missing is one family of nonzero selected polynomials whose
real decay beats its ACTUAL primitive multiplier.  Neither the entire
completion, the rank theorem, nor the coefficientwise prime laws alone
provide that joint estimate.
