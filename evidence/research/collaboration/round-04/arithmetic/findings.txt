ROUND 04 — EXACT EULER DELETION, A SPARSE PADE FAMILY, AND OVERSIEVING
29 October 2026. Arithmetic worker. Local, unpublished research packet.

Outcome and scope
=================

There is a genuine mechanism forcing away the repeated fourth odd-prime
layers: apply exact Euler depletion to the ORIGINAL cyclotomic beta(4)
germ before taking the endpoint determinant. Every selected prime then
has exponent at most FOUR in the ACTUAL primitive multiplier, independent
of determinant order. The scalar endpoint factor is included in this
claim. An exact example changes v_11(kappa) from 24 to 4 at order n=8.

Selecting every prime above a slowly growing cutoff gives a uniform
subquadratic upper bound log^+(kappa)=o(n^2). This is an arithmetic theorem,
not a small-integer-form theorem. The price is a changing germ with many
new singularities and possible exact rank defects.

A fully prescribed nonzero polynomial-pencil family is developed below.
Keep only powers of 3 in the relevant coefficient prefix and take
n=3^k-1. Its diagonal Pade approximation and endpoint determinant are
explicit. Primitive normalization removes the entire apparently tiny
determinant scalar. The remaining form is an Euler-product tail plus a
known term of size 3^(-4k). At the natural prime cutoff 2n, neither a
uniform lower bound nor a useful real upper bound is proved.

For a precisely specified larger cutoff, however, there IS an actual
failure theorem: k>=2 and prime cutoff p congruent 3 mod 4, p>=3^(4k),
give primitive absolute values >1. The proof combines a real LOWER bound
with an isolated prime in the ACTUAL multiplier. It does not infer a
no-go result by multiplying two upper bounds.

This round does not prove beta(4) irrational and does not rule out all
Euler-depleted, hypergeometric, or varying-cutoff constructions.

1. Choices made before discussion
=================================

The initial record 00_initial_choices.txt compares three distinct designs:

* Factorial coefficient multipliers, such as binom(j,n)^4, force local
  prime divisibility but do not automatically preserve a beta(4)-only
  endpoint. Recovering the endpoint through integration may restore the
  denominators. No completed endpoint construction was obtained here.
* Explicit Frobenius/Euler depletion changes the endpoint by a known
  rational scalar. On the original cyclotomic germ the deletion is exact.
  This is the selected construction, developed in this packet.
* Constrained Mellin numerators/Euler derivatives retain the previous
  one-LCM Rodrigues subspace, but its free coefficient and free polynomial
  do not supply an approximation rule. This round does not repackage
  arbitrary rational approximation as a new construction.

The coordinator independently suggested exact Euler deletion while this
initial record was being prepared. That input is explicitly acknowledged
there. Other workers' round-04 constructions were not read before the
choice was recorded. No external literature was needed or browsed for
the elementary arguments below.

2. Exact deletion and endpoint normalization
===========================================

Let chi=chi_4, theta=beta(4), and

  F(t) = sum_(j>=1) chi(j)t^j/j^4.

For an odd prime p and T_p f(t)=f(t^p), unique factorization gives the
EXACT identity

  F(t) - chi(p)F(t^p)/p^4
       = sum_(p not dividing j) chi(j)t^j/j^4.                 (2.1)

Thus for a finite set S of odd primes, P=product_(p in S) p,

  G_S(t) = product_(p in S)(1-chi(p)T_p/p^4) F(t)
         = sum_(gcd(j,P)=1) chi(j)t^j/j^4,
  alpha_S = product_(p in S)(1-chi(p)/p^4),
  G_S(1)=alpha_S theta.                                      (2.2)

The identities converge absolutely at 1. They are NOT asserted for the
completed Broadhurst common-base germ of round 03.

The change of germ has a concrete analytic cost. For nonempty S, every
primitive (4P)-th root of unity is a genuine singularity of G_S. In the
divisor expansion of (2.2), only the term with d=P can be singular at
such a root. Indeed, with E=t*d/dt, E^4 F(t^d)=d^4 t^d/(1+t^(2d));
at a primitive (4P)-th root its denominator vanishes only for d=P.
That term has a nonzero coefficient, so cancellation with the other
divisors is impossible. This fact prevents silently reusing the fixed
two-branch analytic bound of the original F. It is not, by itself, a
capacity or actual-value no-go theorem for varying S.

Write G_S(t)=sum h_j t^j. Use exactly the endpoint normalization

  b_i=h_i,
  T_ij=h_(i+j)-h_(i+j-1),       1<=i,j<=n,
  J_(n,S)(X)=det [[X,-b^T],[b,T]]
           =det(T) X+b^T adj(T)b.

The beta(4)-only form is J_(n,S)(alpha_S theta), not J_(n,S)(theta).
For a nonzero rational pencil aX+c, its positive primitive multiplier is

  kappa = lcm(den(a),den(c)) /
          gcd(lcm(den(a),den(c))*a, lcm(den(a),den(c))*c).

Its valuation is exactly -min(v_p(a),v_p(c)); kappa may be rational.
The zero pencil has no primitive multiplier.

3. Forced prime-layer saving
============================

THEOREM 3.1. For every p in S and every nonzero endpoint pencil
J_(n,S)(alpha_S X),

  v_p(kappa) <= 4.                                           (3.1)

Proof. All h_j are p-integral by exact deletion. Therefore det(T) and
b^T adj(T)b are p-integral. Writing alpha_S=E/P^4 with integral E gives
v_p(alpha_S)>=-4, including every possible cross-cancellation between
different Euler factors. Both endpoint coefficients have valuation >=-4.
This proves the statement about the actual primitive multiplier. It
does not require any normality or common-content conjecture. QED.

Now put N=2n, choose a real cutoff y>=2, and delete

  S_n={odd primes p: y<p<=N}.

Every surviving j<=N has only prime factors <=y. The exact common
denominator of the coefficient prefix divides (indeed equals, apart
from empty factors)

  D_(y,N)=product_(odd primes p<=min(y,N)) p^(4 floor(log_p N)).

Write T_0=D T, u=D b, d=det(T_0), Q=u^T adj(T_0)u. An explicit integral
pair for J_(n,S)(alpha_S X) is

  (E D d, P^4 Q),                                            (3.2)

with common scalar denominator P^4 D^(n+1). Consequently

  kappa <= P^4 D^(n+1),
  log(kappa) <= 4 log(P)+4(n+1) pi(y) log(2n).                 (3.3)

This is a sufficient upper bound on an actual primitive multiplier;
common numerator content can make it much smaller. The elementary
bound log(P)<=2n log(2n) already suffices to deduce

  log^+(kappa)=o(n^2) if pi(y_n) log n=o(n),                  (3.4)

where log^+(x)=max(0,log x). No matching lower bound is asserted.

For example y_n=sqrt(n) works using only pi(y)<=y. Fixed y gives
O(n log n). No prime number theorem is needed for these conclusions.

The saving is real in a small exact comparison. For the original F,
n=8 and p=11 give v_11(kappa)=24. Deleting just p=11, with its endpoint
alpha included, gives v_11(kappa)=4. Both were computed from exact
rational pairs and their full integer gcd, not entrywise estimates.

4. A prescribed family and its exact determinant collapse
=========================================================

Fix k>=1, M=3^k, n=M-1. Choose ANY integer B>=2n and delete

  S_B={odd primes p<=B: p != 3}.

Through degree 2n the surviving series is exactly

  g(t)=r(t)+c t^M,
  r(t)=sum_(0<=a<k) (-1)^a t^(3^a)/3^(4a),
  c=(-1)^k/M^4,
  deg r=M/3 < M/2.

Let r=r(1). The polynomials

  q(t)=r(t)/t-c t^(M-1),
  p(t)=r(t)^2/t

have degrees <=n and satisfy the EXACT identity

  q(t)g(t)-p(t)=-c^2 t^(2M-1).                               (4.1)

Since q(0)=1 and 2M-1=2n+1, these form a genuine ordinary [n/n] Pade
pair for G_(S_B), with no free approximation choice. Its endpoint is

  L_(k,B)(theta)=(r-c) alpha_(S_B) theta-r^2.                  (4.2)

THEOREM 4.1. With epsilon=(-1)^(n(n-1)/2+1),

  J_(n,S_B)(X)=epsilon c^(M-2) [(r-c)X-r^2].                 (4.3)

In particular the polynomial pencil is nonzero. Its primitive integer
pair agrees, up to an overall sign, with the primitive pair of (4.2).
The factor |c|^(M-2) disappears COMPLETELY in primitive normalization.

Proof of the determinant identity. The following argument applies to
any polynomial g=r(t)+ct^M with r(0)=0 and deg(r)<M/2.
Let R reverse the n columns, L=I-shift_down, and delta=det(R).
The matrix TR has entries h_(M+i-j)-h_(M+i-j-1). The contribution of
c is cL. Multiplication by L^(-1) makes the lower-degree contribution

  K_ij=r_(M+i-j)-r_(M-j)=N_ij-1_i v_j,
  v_j=r_(M-j).

N is supported in its upper-right corner, with row indices <=deg(r)
and column indices >=M-deg(r). Hence N^2=0 and v^T N=0. The determinant
lemma gives

  det(T)=delta c^n det(I+c^(-1)N-c^(-1)1 v^T)
        =delta c^(n-1)(c-r).

The (1,1) cofactor is delta c^(n-1): after reversing its columns the
minor is upper triangular with diagonal -c. Also T1=ce_1-b. The
adjugate identities, valid without an inversion assumption, imply

  b^T adj(T)b=c^2 adj(T)_(1,1)-(c+r)det(T)
             =delta c^(n-1)r^2.

Combining these two identities proves (4.3). QED.

This also explains a systematic rank hazard. If instead M=3^k<n<3M/2,
the prefix through 2n is a polynomial of degree M. Its cumulative
sequence is constant from index M onward, so the last two rows of
the (n+1)-square cumulative Hankel matrix coincide. The endpoint
pencil is IDENTICALLY ZERO. Depletion must not be accompanied by a
blanket claim of determinant normality.

5. Exact primitive normalization and the real target
====================================================

Put s=81/82 and sigma=(-1)^k. Then

  c=sigma/M^4,
  r=s(1-c),
  R_k=(M^4-sigma)/82 is an integer,
  r=R_k/81^(k-1),
  Q_k=81R_k-sigma,
  r-c=Q_k/81^k,
  gcd(R_k,Q_k)=gcd(R_k,3)=gcd(Q_k,3)=1.                      (5.1)

Write alpha=E/P^4 without assuming this fraction is reduced. For k>=2,
an exact integral pair for L is

  (Q_k E 81^(k-2), -R_k^2 P^4),                             (5.2)

with scalar denominator 81^(2k-2)P^4. The gcd of this displayed pair
gives the EXACT primitive multiplier; all cross-prime Euler-factor
cancellation is thereby included. Formula (5.2) is a normalization
identity, not an assumption that its gcd is small.

Absolute Euler-product convergence gives

  alpha_(S_B) theta
    =s product_(odd primes p>B)(1-chi(p)/p^4)^(-1).

For B>=3, an elementary bound is

  |alpha_(S_B) theta-s| <= 1/B^3.                            (5.3)

Indeed the absolute logarithm of the product is at most
sum_(j>B) 1/(j^4-1) <= 1/(2B^3), and exp(u)-1<=2u here.

The exact endpoint decomposition is

  L_(k,B)(theta)
   =(r-c)(alpha_(S_B)theta-s)+s(s-1)c-s^2c^2.                (5.4)

At the NATURAL cutoff B=2M-2, (5.3) supplies only O(M^(-3)) control.
It does not prove that the actual error has polynomial size, and does
not rule out cancellations with the explicit M^(-4) term. This packet
makes NO asymptotic no-go claim for that natural-cutoff family.

Exact finite diagnostics at the prescribed k=1,2,3 give:

  k  n   B    log |primitive L(theta)|
  1  2   4       -2.3702940450872444
  2  8  16       14.081186415438562
  3 26  52      121.29455964540273

For k=1 the primitive pair is 82*theta-81. These are finite diagnostics
of this specified family, not a larger parameter search or a proof of
eventual failure.

6. A genuine actual-value obstruction for an oversieved subfamily
================================================================

THEOREM 6.1. Let k>=2, M=3^k, and let p be ANY prime congruent 3 mod 4
with p>=M^4. Set B=p and S=S_B. Let kappa_L be the exact positive
primitive multiplier of L_(k,p). Then

  |L_(k,p)(theta)| >= 1/(200 M^4),
  kappa_L >= p^4/R_k^2,
  |kappa_L L_(k,p)(theta)|
       >= p^4/(200 M^4 R_k^2)
       > (6561/200) M^4 > 1.                                (6.1)

In particular these forms are nonzero and cannot prove irrationality
by tending to zero. No prime-distribution theorem is used: the result
holds for every prime satisfying the displayed hypothesis. There are
arbitrarily large primes 3 mod 4 by the elementary Euclid argument.

Proof of the real lower bound. For k>=2, |c|<=1/6561 and

  1-r=(1+81c)/82 >=80/(81*82),
  |(r-c)s-r^2|=s|c|(1-r)
               >=(20/1681)/M^4 >1/(100M^4).

Also 0<r-c<2, so (5.3) changes this value by at most 2/p^3.
Since p>=M^4 and M>=9, this is <=1/(200M^4). The error cannot cancel
the displayed main term. Its sign is -sign(c).

Proof of the multiplier lower bound. The largest selected prime p
cannot divide ANY Euler numerator q^4-chi(q), q<=p. For q=p the
numerator is 1 modulo p. For q<p with chi(q)=-1, q^4=-1 modulo p
would make -1 a square, impossible for p=3 mod 4. For chi(q)=1,
q^4=1 forces q=+/-1 modulo p; the only possibilities below p are
1 and the even integer p-1, neither an odd prime. Thus

  v_p(alpha_S)=-4.

By (5.1), 0<Q_k<M^4<=p and 0<R_k<p, so

  v_p(kappa_L)=4 EXACTLY.                                   (6.2)

At any other prime, a negative valuation of kappa_L can be no larger
in magnitude than the corresponding valuation of the numerator
R_k^2 of the constant coefficient. (At 3 that coefficient has a
denominator, so no negative valuation occurs.) Therefore the entire
negative part of kappa_L divides R_k^2, and kappa_L>=p^4/R_k^2.
Finally R_k<M^4/81 gives (6.1). QED.

The small checked instance k=2, M=9, p=6563 has the exact certified
lower bound

  |primitive L(theta)| >=1855280658474961/8398080000 >220000.

The script also computes its full rational alpha, full primitive pair,
and actual Arb value; the theorem itself does not need that computation.

7. What this construction contributes, and what remains
=======================================================

The useful transferable result is Theorem 3.1: exact prime deletion
forces the fourth layer to occur once per selected prime, with its
endpoint factor accounted for. It is not an accidental modular defect.
The local example v_11(kappa):24->4 verifies that the saving can survive
global primitive normalization.

The sparse family is a noncircular construction: its prime set, order,
Pade pair, and beta(4)-only endpoint are all prescribed. It also makes
the main risk precise. Keeping too few coefficient indices causes
exact zero pencils or collapse of the determinant to a low-complexity
Euler-product approximation. Increasing the deletion cutoff enough
to dominate the explicit term causes a provable primitive-value failure.

No argument here excludes a less sparse cutoff y_n tending to infinity,
a different Pade type, or a way to control weighted varying cuts. For
the natural y=3 cutoff, uniform nonvanishing of the evaluated form and
the actual error asymptotic remain open. The coefficient/primitive
arithmetic saving is therefore a construction tool, not yet an
irrationality argument.
