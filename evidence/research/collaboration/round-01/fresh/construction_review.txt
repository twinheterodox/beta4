Adversarial reading of construction/findings.txt, sections 3-4
28 October 2026. Local, unpublished.

Scope: a separate agent's mathematical reading of the displayed proofs,
not independent formal verification. I read the kernel/comparison setup
to identify dependencies, then checked the arithmetic and branch arguments
in sections 3-4 directly. I did not rerun the construction agent's large
finite checks. No files outside fresh/ were changed.

Conclusion: I found no error in the stated specified-family obstruction
or in the nonreal logarithmic-branch obstruction. Their restrictions on
scope are essential and are stated correctly. Details of the checks and
potential failure points follow.

1. Exact coefficient valuation (3.1)-(3.3)

For 2M0<p<4M0 and p>=5, every a_i, a_i-a_j, and a_i+a_j is a p-unit.
The differences are nonzero because the principal nodes are distinct.
All odd tail denominators are <4M0, whereas 3p>6M0; hence the only
multiple of p in a relevant tail is p itself. The S_4 term therefore
has residue chi_4(p), while all S_3 contributions disappear after
multiplication by p^4 and reduction modulo p. This validates the diagonal
rank-r_p reduction, including its sign.

For the coefficient of X^(n-r_p), multiplying each r_p-minor by p^(4r_p)
indeed leaves exactly one nonzero reduction: the minor selecting all
high nodes. This does not rely on a generic noncancellation hypothesis.
The case r_p=0 is harmless: the coefficient is the monic leading term.

Monicity of Q is what prevents negative prime valuations in its primitive
scaling factor H: H itself is an integer. For the common-zero principal
family, the leading coefficient is 2, and the unique surviving minor's
complement includes the zero index. Its additional factor is exactly 2,
a unit for these primes. Thus the only possible negative contribution
to log H is the stated -log 2. There is no missing normalization loss
at other primes in this lower bound.

2. Asymptotic lower bound and uniformity in the offset

The unshifted weighted-prime integral is

  4 integral_(2n)^(4n) (n-x/4) dx =2n^2.

Floors contribute O(theta(4n))=O(n), not a hidden n^2 term. Reversing
the sum for general shifted nodes gives, for each high node a,

  4[theta(4a)-theta(2M0)] =16(a-M0/2)+o(M0),

uniformly over the node interval. For m/n tending to a finite rho this
sums to the stated A(rho)n^2+o(n^2), with

  A(rho)=2(1+rho)^2 for rho<=1, and A(rho)=8rho for rho>=1.

I checked the Cauchy-product exponent separately: the numerator contributes
n^2 log n-(3/2)n^2+o(n^2), and the denominator contributes
n^2 log n+n^2 integral_[0,1]^2 log(2rho+x+y) dxdy+o(n^2).
This yields exactly the displayed D(rho). Its derivative at zero is
4 log 2, and is decreasing. Consequently A-D has its minimum at zero,
equal to 2-2 log 2>0.

When m/n tends to infinity the same prime-number-theorem error is
o(nM0)=o(nm). The exact reversed main term is 8mn+O(n). The elementary
Cauchy-product lower bound is -O(n^2[1+log(1+m/n)]), which is indeed
o(mn) in this regime. Thus the proof handles arbitrarily large offsets,
including superexponential ones, rather than assuming m=O(n).

The subsequence argument giving the universal liminf is valid: every
sequence m_n/n has a subsequence converging in the compactified interval
[0,infinity], and each possible limit is covered. Rational invertible
changes of basis multiply the full determinant by a rational scalar, so
primitive content removes the change exactly. This observation does not
cover deleting rows or choosing a different subspace, as the note says.

3. Local singular coefficient in section 4

At x0=i*pi/2, with epsilon=x-x0,

  sech(x)=-i/epsilon+O(epsilon),
  sech'(x)=i/epsilon^2+O(1),
  x^2 sech'(x)/8
       =-i*pi^2/(32 epsilon^2)-pi/(8 epsilon)+O(1).

Three local integrations therefore give

  h(x)=i*pi^2/32 epsilon log epsilon
       -pi/16 epsilon^2 log epsilon + analytic.

In particular the note's first-order logarithmic coefficient and its
sign are correct. Analytic continuation can add a quadratic polynomial
to a branch of h but cannot alter this local logarithmic coefficient.

For a root z0^k=-q^2 with k odd, changing the sign of a square root
changes t0^k's sign, so one can choose t0^k=iq. The globally holomorphic
principal v(t)=2atanh(t^k) on |t|<1 then takes v(t0)=i*pi/4. The
derivative (2v)'(t0)=4k t0^(k-1)/(1+q^2) is nonzero.

The strip exclusions are complete, not just numerical separation:

  - u has imaginary part in (-pi/2,pi/2), so h(u) has no pole argument.
  - u+v has imaginary part in (-pi/4,3pi/4), so the only possible sech
    pole is i*pi/2, forcing u=i*pi/4 and t0=iq. Its kth power has the
    wrong modulus q^k!=q.
  - u+2v has imaginary part in (0,pi), so the only possible pole is
    i*pi/2, forcing u=0 and t0=0, also impossible.

Thus only the -2h(2v) term is singular at the selected point and its
first-order logarithm cannot cancel. The map z=t^2 is locally invertible
there. Exactly one of the k roots z0 is real (negative), leaving k-1
nonreal branch points inside the unit disk.

4. Algebraic modifications and the moment-transform exclusion

Locally the singular part has analytic coefficient A(z) with a nonzero
first-order zero multiplying log(z-z0). An algebraic prefactor may have
a Puiseux zero or pole there, but after a finite number of loops its
branch returns. The additive monodromy of the product is then a nonzero
multiple of a(z)A(z). Algebraic addition cannot cancel it. This proves
the stated exclusion for every nonzero algebraic prefactor, including
ones vanishing at every selected branch point.

A finite signed or complex measure on compact real support gives a
transform integral dmu(s)/(1-zs) holomorphic at all nonreal z. Its
continuation from zero through the nonreal domain is single-valued and
regular at the exhibited nonreal points. Hence the branch obstruction
does rule out that class of moment representations.

The field in the note is explicitly C(z), so treating the constant
6 beta(4) as an algebraic constant when absorbing its multiple into b
is legitimate; no algebraicity of beta(4) over Q is being assumed.

The exclusion does not extend automatically to nonlinear changes of
the independent variable, nonalgebraic logarithm-cancelling operations,
noncompact/nonreal support, or matrix-valued representations in which
this scalar germ is absent. The note already preserves these limits.

5. Review limitations

The asymptotic arithmetic conclusion depends on the positive lower
determinant comparison in section 2, rather than on the upper bound
alone. I checked its use and the rank-one regularized Cauchy identity
while reading the setup, but this review's target was sections 3-4.
The finite computation files are corroboration, not the proofs of
uniformity or logarithmic monodromy. Agreement between agents does not
constitute independent mathematical verification.
