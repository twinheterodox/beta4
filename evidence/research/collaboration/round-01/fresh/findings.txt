Scalar positive-polynomial witnesses for beta(4): an exact cone and degree law
28 October 2026. Local, unpublished research note.

There is no irrationality proof here. The useful output is a structural
theorem, rather than another unsuccessful fixed family. For the scalar
positive-polynomial integral below, the entire bounded-degree search is
exactly a two-dimensional rational cone. Unrestricted positive polynomial
weights, including integer weights after primitive normalization, encode
every positive rational linear form in beta(4). Their positivity imposes
no extra Diophantine restriction.

The cone has explicit rational boundary polynomials, a self-contained
proof, and a sharp exponential degree law. If L=A beta(4)+B>0 and A!=0,
the least degree of a positive-polynomial witness satisfies

  degree = log(|A|/L)/log(3+2sqrt(2)) + O(log log(|A|/L))

as |A|/L tends to infinity. This describes the cost of encoding an already
known form; it does not produce the required small integer forms.

All finite arithmetic below is exact, with Arb enclosures for evaluations
at beta(4). A second Fraction implementation checks the endpoint formulas.
Both implementations and the mathematical arguments are by the same
investigator, not an independent audit or formal verification. No priority
claim is made for the classical ingredients or their applications here.


1. Independent starting point, and overlap with the archive

The first candidate was to eliminate all auxiliary periods at the outset:

  I(P) = 1/6 integral_0^1
           (-log x)^3 P(x^2)/(1+x^2) dx,

with P a rational polynomial, preferably nonnegative on [0,1]. This is a
pure beta(4) form without a separate Catalan cancellation. The basic
construction is already inside the archive's differentiated cyclotomic
and tail constructions; it is not presented as a new period identity.

The following files were consulted after forming the candidate:

  beta4/README.txt
  beta4/cyclotomic-integrals.txt
  beta4/literature/weighted-tails.txt
  beta4/structural-followup.txt
  beta4/orthogonal-cancellation.txt
  beta4/quotient-investigation.txt
  relevant positive-form and Padé experiment code found by text search.

The cyclotomic note already proves prime-denominator obstructions for
particular families. The structural and orthogonal notes use hyperbolic
and discrete moment systems; they do not contain the scalar bounded-degree
cone classification proved below. The quotient investigations already
warn that unrestricted arithmetic optimization can be equivalent to the
original irrationality problem. The present result gives an explicit
complete version of that warning for this scalar family, including
rational extremizers and the optimal exponential degree scale.

No recent claim about Catalan's irrationality is used. No literature
claim of novelty is inferred from the local text search.


2. Formal coefficient pairs: no irrationality assumption

Write theta=beta(4), and introduce the positive measure

  dnu(t) = (-log t)^3/(96 sqrt(t)) dt,       0<t<1.

Its moments are rational:

  nu_k = integral t^k dnu(t) = 1/(2k+1)^4.                  (1)

Thus nu_0=1, and

  I(P) = integral P(t)/(1+t) dnu(t),
  theta = I(1),             0<theta<1.                     (2)

Define a FORMAL coefficient map, whether or not theta is irrational:

  A(P)=P(-1),
  B(P)=integral [P(t)-P(-1)]/(1+t) dnu(t).                 (3)

The quotient in (3) is a polynomial, so B(P) is rational when P is.
Equation (2) gives the evaluation identity

                   I(P)=A(P) theta+B(P).                  (4)

All assertions below about a pair (A,B) refer to this explicitly defined
map. They never recover coefficients by assuming that 1 and theta are
linearly independent over Q.

If P>=0 on [0,1] and P is not the zero polynomial, then I(P)>0. The
measure has a strictly positive density throughout the open interval.
This is uniform nonvanishing, with no arithmetic conclusion yet.


3. The unrestricted cone is the whole positive half-plane

Theorem 1. The image under (3) of the rational polynomials nonnegative
on [0,1] is exactly

  {(0,0)} union {(A,B) in Q^2 : A theta+B>0}.               (5)

Proof. Necessity follows from positivity of the integral. Conversely let
L=A theta+B>0. If A=0, take P=B(1+t), since I(1+t)=nu_0=1.

For A>0 set

  P_n(t)=A ((1-t)/2)^(2n).

For A<0 set

  P_n(t)=(-A) t ((1-t)/2)^(2n).

In either case P_n>=0, A(P_n)=A, and

               0<I(P_n)<=|A| theta 4^(-n).                 (6)

Choose n with I(P_n)<L, and put

  delta=B-B(P_n)=L-I(P_n)>0,
  P=P_n+delta(1+t).                                       (7)

Although L involves theta, delta is rational by its first expression.
The polynomial in (7) is nonnegative and has the prescribed formal pair.
For example, the sufficient choice

  n=max(0,ceil(log(2|A|/L)/log 4))

gives I(P_n)<=L/2 because theta<1. Its degree is at most
max(1,2n+[A<0]). The zero pair is represented by P=0. QED.

Integer-polynomial consequence. If (A,B) is a primitive integer pair
with A theta+B>0, clear the rational coefficients of the polynomial in
(7) by a positive integer K. Its formal pair is (KA,KB), and primitive
normalization returns exactly (A,B). Thus restricting the source to
integer polynomials does not restrict the primitive image when their
coefficient heights are unrestricted.

The construction is deliberately conditional on a prescribed positive
target form. Choosing n using L, or first finding the target by continued
fractions, already supplies the approximation one wished to prove exists.
Consequently (5) is not an irrationality construction. In particular:

  existence of such primitive positive forms tending to zero
                  is equivalent to irrationality of theta.

For a rational theta=a/b every positive integer form is at least 1/b.
For an irrational theta the usual rational approximations provide forms
tending to zero, and (7) then encodes them. An impossibility theorem for
ALL positive polynomial weights after primitive normalization would
therefore amount to proving theta rational. Particular-family obstructions
must not be extended to that unrestricted class.


4. An essentially sharp degree bound, with an explicit integer lift

Put rho=3+2sqrt(2). Let T_n be the Chebyshev polynomial, and define

  q_n(t)=T_n(2t-1)/T_n(-3),
  T_n(3)=(rho^n+rho^(-n))/2.

Then q_n(-1)=1 and |q_n(t)|<=1/T_n(3) on [0,1]. Replace the base
polynomial in Theorem 1 by

  P_n=A q_n^2                  if A>0,
  P_n=(-A) t q_n^2            if A<0.                       (8)

Again A(P_n)=A, and

  I(P_n)<=|A| theta/T_n(3)^2 <=4|A| rho^(-2n).              (9)

For example,

  n=max(0,ceil(log(8|A|/L)/(2 log rho)))

makes the right side of (9) at most L/2. Formula (7) therefore gives
a rational witness of degree at most max(1,2n+[A<0]).

For a primitive integer target let H=max(|A|,|B|), let
m=max(1,2n+[A<0]), and set

  d_m=lcm(1,3,5,...,2m-1),
  K=T_n(3)^2 d_m^4.                                        (10)

Then KP has integer coefficients, its primitive formal pair is the
original (A,B), and its largest coefficient in absolute value is at most

                         4 H K.                           (11)

Indeed, the roots of T_n(2t-1) all lie in (0,1), so its coefficients
alternate in sign and their absolute sum is |T_n(-3)|. The coefficient
absolute sum of q_n is 1, and that of either base polynomial in (8) is
at most |A|. Its coefficients have denominator dividing T_n(3)^2.
Polynomial division in (3) introduces no new denominator. The remaining
moments have odd denominators at most 2m-1 to the fourth power, proving
(10). Also |B(P_n)|<=I(P_n)+|A|theta<=2|A|, so
|delta|<=|B|+2|A|. This proves (11).

These are exact bounds, not an assertion that K is minimal. If desired,
the prime number theorem gives log d_m=2m+o(m), so the integer witness
has logarithmic coefficient height O(log H+log(|A|/L)). Its dependence
on the already-small target L remains the essential circularity.

4.1 A matching exponential lower bound for EVERY positive polynomial

Theorem 2. Let P>=0 on [0,1], with degree D>=1, and let A=P(-1).
Then

     I(P) >= |A|/[3*2^26 D^12 rho^D].                      (12)

The harmless power D^12 is not optimized. The exponential factor rho^D
is optimal in view of (8)-(9).

Proof. Put M=max_[0,1] P. Expand

  P((1+y)/2)=sum_(k=0)^D a_k T_k(y).

The cosine-integral expressions for these coefficients give |a_0|<=M
and |a_k|<=2M for k>=1. Since |T'_k(y)|<=k^2 on [-1,1],

  max_[0,1] |P'(t)| <=4M sum_(k=1)^D k^2 <=4M D^3.         (13)

The derivative bound on T_k follows directly from
T_k(cos u)=cos(ku) and |sin(ku)|<=k|sin u|; the endpoints follow by
continuity. Thus no sharp Markov inequality is needed.

Let ell=1/(8D^3). There is an interval J of length ell, contained in
[0,1] and within distance ell of a point where P=M, on which P>=M/2.
On the central half of J both distances to the endpoints 0,1 are at
least ell/4. Furthermore

  dnu(t)/(1+t) >= (1-t)^3/192 dt,                          (14)

because -log t>=1-t and sqrt(t)(1+t)<=2. Integration on that central
half gives

  I(P)>=M ell^4/49152=M/[3*2^26 D^12].                    (15)

The Chebyshev exterior-point inequality gives

  |P(-1)|<=M T_D(3)<=M rho^D.                             (16)

For completeness, interpolate at the D+1 points corresponding to
cos(j*pi/D), j=0,...,D, after the affine change from [-1,1] to [0,1].
At the exterior point -1, the Lagrange coefficients alternate in sign.
Their absolute sum is T_D(3): interpolation of T_D attains the same
triangle inequality with equality because its values at those points
also alternate. This proves (16). Combining (15)-(16) proves (12).
The zero polynomial is immediate. QED.

Let d(A,B) be the least degree of a nonnegative rational witness for a
given formal rational pair with L=A theta+B>0 and A!=0. The upper
construction and (12) imply, uniformly as R=|A|/L tends to infinity,

  d(A,B)=log R/log rho+O(log log R).                        (17)

More explicitly any degree D>=1 witness satisfies

  D log rho+12 log D >=log R-log(3*2^26),

while (8)-(9) give the upper bound with only an absolute additive
constant. This degree law describes approximation quality; it does
not imply that integer target pairs of that quality exist.


5. Exact rational endpoints for the entire bounded-degree cone

Here is the complete finite-degree classification. It avoids searching
over large polynomial coefficient spaces.

For w in {1,t,1-t,t(1-t)}, put c=w(-1), which is nonzero. For n>=0,
minimize I(w p^2) over real polynomials p of degree at most n satisfying
p(-1)=1. Write p=1+(1+t)h, with deg h<=n-1. Define rational data

  b0 = nu((w-c)/(1+t)),
  v_i = nu(w t^i),
  H_ij = nu(w(1+t)t^(i+j)),            0<=i,j<n.            (18)

The matrix H is positive definite: its quadratic form integrates a
nonzero polynomial square against w(1+t)dnu, whose density is positive
on the open interval. Therefore

  p_*(t)=1-(1+t) sum_i (H^(-1)v)_i t^i,
  r_(w,n)=(v^T H^(-1)v-b0)/c,                             (19)

are rational, and completing the square gives the exact minimum

  min I(wp^2)=c theta+b0-v^T H^(-1)v
             =c(theta-r_(w,n)).                           (20)

For n=0, the vector and matrix are empty, the quadratic expression is
zero, and p_*=1. This convention is used below. Formula (20) involves
theta only in a fixed constant term, so rationality of the minimizer
does not assume anything about irrationality of theta.

For D>=1 define rational endpoints by this table:

   degree        lower l_D                 upper u_D
   ----------------------------------------------------------------
   D=2n, n>=1    r_(1,n)                   r_(t(1-t),n-1)
   D=2n+1        r_(1-t,n)                 r_(t,n)

For each lower entry divide w p_*^2 by c>0, obtaining P_D^+.
For each upper entry divide w p_*^2 by -c>0, obtaining P_D^-.
These are nonnegative rational polynomials of degree at most D, with

  (A(P_D^+),B(P_D^+))=(1,-l_D),
  (A(P_D^-),B(P_D^-))=(-1,u_D).                            (21)

Both are nonzero. Positivity proves, without any irrationality assumption,

                         l_D<theta<u_D.                   (22)

Theorem 3. For every D>=1, the image of the cone of real polynomials
nonnegative on [0,1] and of degree at most D is exactly

  C_D={lambda(1,-l_D)+eta(-1,u_D):lambda,eta>=0}
     ={(A,B):B+l_D A>=0 and B+u_D A>=0}.                   (23)

Its rational points are exactly the image of the rational such
polynomials. A rational pair in C_D has the explicit rational witness

  P=lambda P_D^+ +eta P_D^-,
  lambda=(B+u_D A)/(u_D-l_D),
  eta=(B+l_D A)/(u_D-l_D).                                 (24)

Proof. Use the following elementary interval sum-of-squares fact:

  deg P<=2n:     P=p^2+t(1-t)q^2,    deg p<=n, deg q<=n-1;
  deg P<=2n+1:   P=t p^2+(1-t)q^2,   deg p,deg q<=n.        (25)

The coefficients of p,q need only be real. One proof of (25) is included
below. For any component w q^2, (20) gives

  I(w q^2)>=q(-1)^2 c(theta-r_(w,n));

if q(-1)=0 the right side is zero and ordinary positivity suffices.

For even degree put a=p(-1)^2, b=2q(-1)^2. Then A=a-b and

  B>=-l_D a+u_D b.

For odd degree put a=2q(-1)^2, b=p(-1)^2. The same statements hold.
Since a,b>=0 and u_D>l_D, both inequalities in (23) follow. Conversely,
the coefficients in (24) are nonnegative precisely under those two
inequalities, and (21) verifies their formal coefficient pair. If A,B
are rational, so are lambda,eta and the resulting polynomial. QED.

5.1 The interval sum-of-squares fact used in the proof

For a polynomial P nonnegative on [0,1], the even trigonometric polynomial
P((1+cos u)/2) is nonnegative. Its elementary spectral factorization is

  P((1+cos u)/2)=|F(exp(iu))|^2,

where F has real coefficients and degree at most deg P. To see this
algebraically, multiply its Laurent expression by a power of z. Zeros
off the unit circle occur in conjugate reciprocal groups, and zeros on
the unit circle have even multiplicity by nonnegativity. Selecting one
reciprocal half, with conjugate symmetry and half the unit-circle zeros,
gives a real polynomial F; the positive scalar is absorbed into F.

For even bound 2n, center F by exp(-inu). Its real part is a polynomial
of degree at most n in cos u. Its imaginary part is sin u times a
polynomial of degree at most n-1 in cos u. Taking the absolute square,
then setting t=(1+cos u)/2, gives the first line of (25).

For odd bound 2n+1 center by exp(-i(n+1/2)u). The real part is cos(u/2)
times a polynomial of degree at most n in cos u; the imaginary part is
sin(u/2) times such a polynomial. Since cos^2(u/2)=t and
sin^2(u/2)=1-t, this gives the second line. Lower actual degree causes
no problem: the centered frequencies remain inside the same bounds.
The zero polynomial is immediate. This is the classical interval
sum-of-squares argument, not a claimed new factorization theorem.

5.2 Boundary cases and convergence

Degree zero is separate: its cone is {(A,0):A>=0}, represented by the
constant P=A. The even-degree table must NOT be used with n=0.

At degree one,

  l_1=1/2,  u_1=1,
  P_1^+=(1-t)/2,       P_1^-=t.

At degree two,

  l_2=81/82,           u_2=161/162,
  P_2^+=((1-81t)/82)^2,
  P_2^-=t(1-t)/2.                                          (26)

The inclusions C_D subset C_(D+1) show that l_D is nondecreasing and
u_D is nonincreasing. Using the Chebyshev trial polynomials in (19)
gives the explicit bounds

  theta-l_(2n) <=theta/T_n(3)^2,
  u_(2n)-theta <=theta/[8T_(n-1)(3)^2],        n>=1;
  theta-l_(2n+1) <=theta/[2T_n(3)^2],
  u_(2n+1)-theta <=theta/T_n(3)^2,             n>=0.        (27)

Applying (12) to the canonical polynomials in (21), whose |A|=1,
supplies a matching lower bound for EACH of these two gaps. Therefore

  log(theta-l_D)=-D log rho+O(log D),
  log(u_D-theta)=-D log rho+O(log D).                       (28)

No unproved strong asymptotic theorem about the logarithmic weight is
needed for this exponential rate. It is purely real approximation.


6. The exact degree/height criterion, and the missing arithmetic

Theorem 3 is a complete membership test for a proposed integer form.
For a height bound H>=1 and D>=1, the least positive value represented
by a formal integer pair with max(|A|,|B|)<=H is exactly

  E_D(H)=min(1,
       min_(1<=q<=H) [q theta-floor(q l_D)],
       min_(1<=q<=H) [ceil(q u_D)-q theta]).                (29)

For A=q>0 the first cone inequality requires B>=-q l_D, so the smallest
admissible integer B is -floor(q l_D). For A=-q the second requires
B>=q u_D, giving ceil(q u_D). Since 0<l_D<u_D<=1, these choices also
satisfy |B|<=H. The case A=0 contributes 1. If a candidate is not
primitive, division by its content gives a smaller admissible integer
pair, so the minimum automatically includes full primitive normalization.

All candidate coefficients in (29) are determined by rational data.
The formula reduces arbitrary positive polynomial searches of bounded
degree and final coefficient height to a two-dimensional integer problem.

Rapid convergence in (28) does NOT force E_D(H_D)->0 for a usable H_D.
The rational approximants must lie at or beyond the endpoints l_D,u_D, and their
denominators can be large. Under theta=a/b rational, every term in (29)
is positive and at least 1/b, despite the same exponential convergence
of the endpoints. This is an immediate diagnostic against drawing an
irrationality conclusion from the real decay alone.

Nor can large primitive values on both canonical rays exclude good
interior integer directions. Rational combinations in (24) may have
large source denominators but a small primitive final pair. The selected
checks below explicitly illustrate that phenomenon.

For every fixed positive integer form there is eventually a D for which
it belongs to C_D, by (22), (27), or Theorem 1. Accordingly,

  inf_(D,H) E_D(H)=0  if and only if theta is irrational.   (30)

Equation (30) is a reformulation, not progress on the needed Diophantine
existence. A successful use of this family must therefore supply extra
arithmetic information selecting small integer directions as D grows.
Positivity, endpoint convergence, a large polynomial basis, and generic
clearing denominators alone do not supply that information.

cone_checks.py uses exact FLINT rationals for moments, matrices,
polynomials, primitive normalization, and cone tests. Arb encloses theta
using [zeta(4,1/4)-zeta(4,3/4)]/256 with precision exceeding the final
coefficient bit length by at least 512 bits.

Recorded checks:

  - 18 canonical endpoint forms at D=1,2,3,4,5,6,8,12,16;
  - 240 exact SOS/cone and rational reconstruction checks;
  - exact nesting checks through D=12;
  - 13 positive target forms lifted to integer polynomial witnesses,
    including A=0, both signs of A, and ordinary continued-fraction
    examples chosen only to test the encoding theorem;
  - exact minimum witness degrees for those 13 targets via (23);
  - 18 endpoint values reproduced by a separate Fraction implementation
    using bordered determinants instead of FLINT linear solves.

8. What this does and does not narrow

Proved: the scalar positive-polynomial method has no hidden auxiliary
period, an exact rational two-ray cone at each degree, explicit rational
extremizers, a complete integer-height membership test, and the optimal
exponential degree scale for encoding prescribed positive forms.

Not proved: an arithmetic selection theorem forcing E_D(H_D)->0, an
asymptotic obstruction even for the endpoint primitive forms, or any
irrationality/transcendence statement about beta(4).

Concrete research consequence: an unrestricted search over nonnegative
scalar polynomials with this kernel need not be repeated in a larger
basis. Formula (29) already solves its formal optimization. A materially
different construction must impose and exploit additional arithmetic
structure, or change the kernel/period realization; otherwise it merely
re-encodes rational approximation to theta. This conclusion is specific
to the scalar kernel (2), not to all positive multivariable periods,
determinant methods, or possible irrationality arguments.
