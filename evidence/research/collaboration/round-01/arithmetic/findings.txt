Round 01 arithmetic investigation: regular-word denominator savings
28 October 2026. Local, unpublished research record.

Scope and conclusion

This is an independent derivation of the denominator statements and an
independent rational-kernel coefficient implementation. It is not a human
or formal verification, a priority claim, or an irrationality proof.
No source outside this round's arithmetic directory was changed.

The denominator theorem in dilations-and-positive-kernels.txt is valid,
but its baseline 8 L_N^4 is loose for these regular iterated integrals.
There is a stronger explicit bound, and a further Frobenius refinement.
For fixed odd k>=3, if Q_(N,k) is the least common denominator of all
coefficients of r_k through t-degree N, the proved results imply

  3/2+1/(k+2) <= liminf_N log Q_(N,k)/N
               <= limsup_N log Q_(N,k)/N
               <= 3/2+1/k+1/(2k+4).                         (A)

These inequalities do not assert that a limit exists. They improve the
previous upper rate 2+2/k and show that a cost approaching 3N/2 persists
as k grows. There are also explicit first occurrences of the p and p^2
layers, and an exact residue-rank lemma for endpoint Pade determinants.
The latter gives a surviving raw determinant multiplier upper bound.
It is not a primitive height estimate or a nonvanishing theorem.

The exact implementation passed 54,532 coefficient/prime checks of the
strongest finite bound, 12,558 Frobenius coefficient checks, 18 surviving
fourth-layer checks, 25 determinant residue-rank checks, and six exact
comparisons of the new endpoint determinant with the old Pade solver.
The proof, rather than these finite checks, establishes the uniform claims.

1. Independent audit of the original bound

Write u=log((1+t)/(1-t)), v=log((1+t^k)/(1-t^k)), k odd, and

  r_k=2h(u)+4h(v)-4h(u+v)-2h(2v)+2h(u+2v),
  h'''(x)=x^2 sech'(x)/8,
  h(0)=h'(0)=h''(0)=0.

For x=a u+b v with the nonnegative integers used here, put
A(t)=(1+t)^a(1+t^k)^b. Then x' is an integer power series, and

  sigma(t)=sech(x(t))
    =A(t)A(-t)/[(A(t)^2+A(-t)^2)/2]

is an integer power series: its denominator is an integer polynomial
with constant coefficient 1. Thus both letters

  alpha=dx,  omega=sech(x) dx

are regular at zero and have integer coefficients. With I_j=int x^j omega,

  h=(3x I_2-x^2 I_1-2I_3)/8.                                (1)

Successive integrations and products give the stated crude bound
8 L_N^4. For p>N/k, the expansion

  r_k=2 sum_(j>=2) (2^j-2)v^j/j! * [h^(j)(u)-h^(j)(0)]       (2)

has j<p and p-integral v-coefficients through degree N. Moreover

  h''(u)=u^2 sech(u)/8 - (1/4) int_0^u s sech(s) ds,          (3)

and for j>=3,

  h^(j)(u)=(1/8)[u^2 sigma^(j-2)(u)
             +2(j-3)u sigma^(j-3)(u)
             +(j-3)(j-4)sigma^(j-4)(u)],                   (4)

where nonexistent negative derivatives are omitted. Derivatives here
are with respect to the logarithmic argument. Since sech(u) and tanh(u)
are integer series in t, all sigma^(m)(u) are integer series. The only
odd-prime denominator cost in (3)-(4) is two integrations or two logs.
This proves the original two-layer claim. If p=2 could be in the large
prime range, N<2k and r_k is zero through N; otherwise p=2 belongs to the
four-layer range. Thus the original D_(N,k) is sound, including p=2.

2. A stronger regular-word denominator lemma

Let L_0=L_1=1, and for r>=1 define

  W_r(N)=product_(j=1)^r L_floor(N/j),
  E_(r,p)(N)=v_p(W_r(N))
     =sum_(a>=1) min(r,floor(N/p^a)).                        (5)

Lemma. A length-r iterated integral based at zero whose letters are
regular integer power-series differentials has all its coefficients
through degree N in W_r(N)^(-1) Z. The same holds for integer linear
combinations of such words. A product of words of total length r has
this bound, by the integer shuffle identity.

Proof. For a coefficient of degree at most N, the integration indices
satisfy 1<=n_1<...<n_r<=N, and a summand denominator is n_1...n_r.
For every a, at most min(r,floor(N/p^a)) of these indices are divisible
by p^a. This proves the exponent in (5). Equality for the maximum over
sets of indices follows by selecting the r integers having largest
p-valuations (when N>=r; for N<r the word coefficients are all zero).
Equivalently, summing the exponents in the product of
LCMs gives exactly the right side of (5). The shuffle identity has
integer coefficients, so multiplication introduces no new denominator.
Regularity of the letters is essential; a dt/t letter would invalidate
the strict-index argument.

Formula (1) is a sum of length-four words and their shuffle products.
In fact, with words ordered from innermost to outermost integration,
there is the useful exact identity

  h=(I[alpha,alpha,omega,alpha]
             -I[alpha,omega,alpha,alpha])/4.               (6)

For example I_3=6 I[alpha,alpha,alpha,omega], and shuffling x I_2
and x^2 I_1 in (1) cancels that word and gives (6). Consequently

             8 W_4(N) clears every coefficient of r_k.    (7)

This is already an improvement to the general baseline:
log W_4(N)=(1+1/2+1/3+1/4)N+o(N)=(25/12)N+o(N).

For p>N/k, formulas (3)-(4) improve further: u^2 is twice a regular
length-two word; the integral in (3) is a length-two word; and u is a
length-one word. Multiplication by integer series or by the p-integral
v^j/j! in (2) preserves the W_2(N) bound. Constants h^(j)(0) have no odd
prime denominators. This proves the exact finite bound

  Q_(N,k) divides D^word_(N,k),
  D^word_(N,k)=8 product_p p^e_p,
  e_p=E_(4,p)(N) if p<=N/k,
      E_(2,p)(N) if p>N/k.                                  (8)

The factor 8 handles the prime 2; when the nonzero coefficients occur,
2 is in the first case. This deliberately unoptimized factor is harmless.
An equivalent expression is

  8 L_N L_floor(N/2)
    * product_(p<=N/k) p^[v_p(L_floor(N/3))+v_p(L_floor(N/4))].

For every fixed odd k,

 log D^word_(N,k)
   =[3/2+1/max(k,3)+1/max(k,4)]N+o(N).                      (9)

For k=3 this is 25/12: the slow-variable cutoff adds no leading-rate
improvement to the sharpened generic bound. For odd k>=5 it is 3/2+2/k.
Thus (9) improves the old bound for every k, but the origin of the gain
must not be misidentified at k=3.

3. A further finite Frobenius cap

The regular-word argument also audits the stated Frobenius congruence.
For a p-integral unit f, the Cartier identity for dlog f says that its
coefficient at t^(mp-1), reduced modulo p, is the p-th power of the
coefficient at t^(m-1). For alpha=dx the Frobenius letter is alpha.
For omega, use

  omega=-i dlog[(1+i exp x)/(1-i exp x)].

Its Frobenius letter is chi_4(p) omega. In a length-four word of degree
below p^2, multiplying by p^4 and reducing modulo p leaves just the
summands whose four integration indices are divisible by p. Formula
(6), followed by the fixed integer filter, therefore gives

  p^4 r_k(t)=chi_4(p) r_k(t^p) mod p,    through degree <p^2. (10)

All reduced coefficient expressions are p-integral in that range.
Let d=2k+4 for k>=3 (d=6 for k=1). Since r_k starts in degree d, (10)
shows that whenever

                 p>=5,  N<p^2,  N<dp,                     (11)

every coefficient through N has p-valuation at least -3.

An explicit bound stronger than (8) is therefore obtained by replacing
its prime exponent, including the separate factor 8 at p=2, by its
minimum with 3 whenever (11) holds. Call this bound D^F_(N,k). This is
the strongest finite bound implemented in the accompanying script.

For fixed odd k>=3, primes p<=sqrt(N) contribute only O(sqrt(N)log N).
For the larger primes the four possible layers have respective cutoffs

                 N,  N/2,  N/k,  N/(2k+4).

The prime number theorem now proves

  log D^F_(N,k)=[3/2+1/k+1/(2k+4)]N+o(N).                  (12)

The finite bound itself does not use the prime number theorem.

4. Exact first occurrences of the first two prime layers

These lemmas explain why the 3/2 base in (9) and (12) is not merely an
artifact of a bound. Throughout this section k>=3 is odd and p>=5 prime.

First layer. Every coefficient of degree <p+2k+3 is p-integral, and

  p [t^(p+2k+3)]r_k = -32/3 mod p.                          (13)

In particular this coefficient has valuation exactly -1.

Proof. The claimed degree N_1=p+2k+3 satisfies N_1<kp for all the stated
k,p, so (2) has p-integral v and j! factors. A convenient identity is

 h''(u(t))=-(1/2) int_0^t s u(s)^2/(1+s^2)^2 ds.             (14)

The first p-denominator in u^2 is at degree p+1, with p-times coefficient
8. The even series h'' cannot acquire an earlier denominator from its
outer integration: the first even positive multiple of p is 2p>p+3.
Thus its first denominator is at p+3, with residue -4/3 by (14).
The j=2 term of (2) is 2v^2 h''(u), giving residue 2*4*(-4/3)=-32/3.
The j=3 term has its first possible nonintegrality at fast degree p+2,
then has the factor v^3. For j>=4, (4) gives a lower fast degree p,
and v^j has degree at least 4k. These are strictly later than N_1.
This proves both the preceding integrality and (13).

Second layer. Suppose additionally

                       2p+2k+2<kp.                         (15)

Define delta_p=0 for p=3 mod4 and delta_p=1 for p=1 mod4. Every
coefficient of degree <2p+2k+2delta_p has valuation at least -1, and

 p^2[t^(2p+2k)]r_k=8 mod p          if p=3 mod4,
 p^2[t^(2p+2k+2)]r_k=-8 mod p       if p=1 mod4.              (16)

These coefficients have valuation exactly -2. The sufficient condition
(15) excludes only finitely many primes for each fixed k.

Proof. Put U(t)=u(t^p) and

  W(t)=int_0^t u(s)*2/(1+s^2) ds.

The two regular letters in W are du and sech(u)du. Their Frobenius
multipliers are 1 and chi_4(p), respectively. For the fast degrees
needed here (<p^2),

  p u(t)=U(t) mod p,
  p^2 W(t)=chi_4(p) W(t^p) mod p.

Formula (3) therefore gives

 p^2 h''(u)=U^2 sech(u)/8 - chi_4(p)W(t^p)/4 mod p.           (17)

For j>=3, formula (4) leaves only U^2 sigma^(j-2)(u)/8 after multiplying
by p^2 and reducing. With their v^j factors these terms begin strictly
later than 2p+2k+2. The relevant fast degree is at most 2p+2<p^2;
the full degree need not itself be below p^2. Condition (15) makes
v and the j! factors p-integral. Finally

  v^2=4t^(2k)+O(t^(6k)),  U^2=4t^(2p)+O(t^(4p)),
  sech(u)=1-2t^2+O(t^4),  W(t^p)=2t^(2p)+O(t^(4p)).

Multiplying (17) by 2v^2 gives the two initial residues

       4(1-chi_4(p)) t^(2p+2k)-8t^(2p+2k+2).

This proves (16) and the asserted lack of an earlier second layer.

Together with (10), which for p>d gives

   v_p([t^(dp)]r_k)=-4,
   p^4[t^(dp)]r_k=-4chi_4(p) mod p,                         (18)

these onset lemmas prove the lower inequality in (A): the first layer
is present for every sufficiently large p<=N-O_k(1), the second for
every sufficiently large p<=N/2-O_k(1), and two additional layers for
p<=N/d. Summing log p yields 3/2+2/d=3/2+1/(k+2).
The upper inequality follows from (12).

5. What survives in endpoint Pade determinants

Set a_j=[t^(2j)]r_k and a_0=0. Define the n-vector b and n-by-n matrix H by

  b_i=a_i,
  H_ij=a_(i+j)-a_(i+j-1),       1<=i,j<=n.

The endpoint determinant is the linear polynomial

  L_n(X)=det [6X  b^T; b  H]
        =A_n X+C_n,
  A_n=6det H,
  C_n=-b^T adj(H)b.                                        (19)

When the relevant Pade system is nonsingular, this produces exactly the
same primitive [n/n] endpoint form as the original solver. To see this,
let c_0=6X, c_j=a_j for j>0, and S_j=sum_(i=0)^j c_i. In the determinant
with first row (S_0,...,S_n) and subsequent rows
(a_l,...,a_(l+n)), l=1,...,n, the bottom n rows encode the reversed
Pade denominator vector. Subtract each column's predecessor (performing
these subtractions from right to left) to obtain precisely (19).
This also explains why there is only one target-period row.

The coefficient bound does survive a general, unoptimized raw clearing:
with D=D^F_(4n,k),

                    D^(n+1) L_n(X) is integral.             (20)

The corresponding fixed-k upper rate for the clearing logarithm is
[6+4/k+4/(2k+4)]n^2+o(n^2), instead of the old entrywise rate
[8+8/k]n^2+o(n^2). This is a valid upper bound, not a sharp multiplier
asymptotic, and no adequate real determinant bound is proved here.

A more informative local surviving statement follows from (16).
Suppose p>=5, p>n,

  4n<kp,  4n-2k<p^2,  2p+2k+2<kp,
  n<d_p:=p+k+delta_p<=2n.                                  (21)

The second displayed inequality is redundant in most of this range but
is retained for clarity. Then p^2 H is p-integral and

               rank_Fp(p^2 H mod p)=R:=2n-d_p+1.             (22)

Indeed, all a_j before j=d_p vanish after multiplication by p^2 and
reduction modulo p. At j=d_p the residue is a unit by (16). Thus rows
with i<d_p-n vanish. The bottom-right R-by-R block is anti-triangular
with a nonzero anti-diagonal: along i+j=d_p, H has the residue of a_d_p.
This proves both the upper and lower rank bounds exactly.

The first row and column of p^2[6X,b^T;b,H] vanish modulo p, so its rank
is also R. Elementary integral row/column elimination now gives

  v_p(A_n)>=-n-R,
  v_p(C_n)>=-n-R-1.                                        (23)

Thus the local exponent needed to clear the raw coefficients is at most
n+R+1, instead of the naive 2(n+1). No lower bound on that exponent,
no full p-adic Smith form, and no primitive size bound follows just
from the residue rank.

6. Raw clearing, primitive multiplier, and primitive height

These are three different quantities. For nonzero L_n, let

  M_n=lcm(denominator(A_n),denominator(C_n)),
  g_n=gcd(M_n A_n,M_n C_n),
  kappa_n=M_n/g_n,
  P_n(X)=kappa_n L_n(X) primitive integral, up to sign.

At every prime,

  v_p(kappa_n)=-min(v_p(A_n),v_p(C_n)).                       (24)

The multiplier kappa_n can be rational; M_n is an integer. Primitive
height is max(|kappa_n A_n|,|kappa_n C_n|), not either M_n or kappa_n.
The actual irrationality estimate would use

              |P_n(beta(4))|=kappa_n |L_n(beta(4))|.         (25)

A prime can occur to a large exponent in kappa_n and yet divide neither
primitive coefficient. This does NOT remove that prime's cost from (25).
For example, k=3,n=30,p=41 has

  v_41(A_n)=v_41(C_n)=-36,
  v_41(kappa_n)=36,
  v_41(primitive beta coefficient)=0.

The raw denominator cost remains directly relevant through kappa_n.
Conversely, residue rank alone does not lower-bound primitive height;
primitive content and the real sizes of the raw coefficients matter too.

For each x=a u+b v it builds x' directly from rational functions,
integrates to x, builds sech(x) from the integer polynomial A, and
uses (1). It does not use exponential/log series routines or the earlier
Euler-number expansion. Only the final six Pade cross-checks import the
old solver; this does not enter coefficient generation or any bound check.

Exploratory third-layer onsets, which are saved but NOT used as a theorem,
suggest (k+4)p+k for fixed k and sufficiently large p. There are exceptions

proving that onset would sharpen the coefficient interval, but would
still not by itself settle the determinant multiplier problem.
