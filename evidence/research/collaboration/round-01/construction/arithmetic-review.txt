The coordinator requested a proof review of the regular four-word bound,
the two-word large-prime improvement, and the asymptotic comparison.
I reconstructed these before reading the completed findings, then read
the complete writeup. I found no mathematical gap in those statements.
This is same-model cross-review, not independent human or formal
verification. No additional coefficient scan was used as evidence.

1. Regular-word denominators

The decisive hypothesis is that all differentials are REGULAR at the
basepoint. Writing a length-r coefficient explicitly gives a sum with
denominators n_1...n_r, where

                    1<=n_1<...<n_r<=N.

For each p^a, at most min(r,floor(N/p^a)) of the distinct indices can
be divisible by p^a. Summing these incidence bounds gives

 v_p(n_1...n_r)<=sum_{a>=1}min(r,floor(N/p^a))
              =sum_{j=1}^r v_p(L_floor(N/j)).

The last equality follows by exchanging the a,j sums; it is exact,
including higher prime powers. Thus W_r(N)=product_{j=1}^r L_floor(N/j)
is a valid common denominator through degree N. This is not the invalid
claim that arbitrary repeated logarithmic integrations always have
distinct denominator indices: the regularity condition is essential.

For h, both alpha=dx and omega=sech(x)dx have integer regular Taylor
series. The integer shuffle identity applies to the displayed products.
I also checked the more economical word identity explicitly. With
innermost-to-outermost ordering, write A=I[alpha,alpha,alpha,omega],
B=I[alpha,alpha,omega,alpha], C=I[alpha,omega,alpha,alpha]. Then

 I_3=6A,  x I_2=6A+2B,  x^2 I_1=6A+4B+2C.

Substitution into (3x I_2-x^2 I_1-2I_3)/8 gives h=(B-C)/4.
Therefore the stated 8 W_4(N) bound is safe. Its constant factor can be
improved because both letters have even integer coefficients, but this
does not alter any exponent or asymptotic rate being claimed.

2. Why two words suffice at p>N/k

In the expansion in v, only j<=floor(N/k)<p occurs, and all coefficients
of v through degree N have p-integral denominators. Thus v^j/j! and
the integer multipliers introduce no p denominator.

The expression for h''(u) is a sum of u^2 times an integer series and
one regular length-two integral. For j>=3, the formula for h^(j)(u)
is a polynomial of degree at most two in u, with integer-series
coefficients apart from the fixed factor 1/8. Derivatives of sech at
u(t) are integer series because they are integer polynomials in
sech(u)=(1-t^2)/(1+t^2) and tanh(u)=2t/(1+t^2).

Products of two logs are shuffle sums of length-two words. Multiplying
by a p-integral power series preserves the degree-N denominator bound:
every coefficient used has degree <=N, and W_2(M) divides W_2(N) for
M<=N. This checks an otherwise easy-to-miss convolution issue.

The constant terms h^(j)(0) have no odd-prime denominators. If p=2 is
in the large-prime range then N<2k, before the slow filter's first
nonzero coefficient, so no 2-adic exception is missed. Accordingly
the exact piecewise W_4/W_2 finite bound is valid.

3. Rates, and the Frobenius refinement

The regular-word baseline is

                log W_4(N)=(25/12)N+o(N).

The two-word base contributes (1+1/2)N. Each extra j=3,4 layer is
restricted both to p<=N/j and to p<=N/k. Higher prime powers contribute
o(N) for fixed k. This gives exactly

 3/2 + 1/max(k,3) + 1/max(k,4)

as the coefficient of N. In particular k=3 has no improvement over
the sharpened generic baseline at THIS stage. The arithmetic findings
state this qualification correctly.

I checked the additional Frobenius step as well. For alpha, the Cartier
letter is alpha. For omega=-i dlog[(1+i exp x)/(1-i exp x)], it is
chi_4(p)omega: the logarithmic derivative has Gaussian coefficients,
and its scalar -i changes by the factor chi_4(p). Every length-four
word for h has one omega and three alpha letters. Below degree p^2,
p^4 kills every summand except those with all four integration indices
divisible by p. Dividing those indices by p proves the claimed
Frobenius congruence, with the indicated chi_4(p) factor.

Since the first nonzero coefficient has degree d=2k+4, this really
caps the denominator exponent at 3 when N<dp and N<p^2. For primes
above sqrt(N), the final four cutoffs are consequently

                     N, N/2, N/k, N/d.

For k=3 the uncapped fourth-layer cutoff had been N/4; for k>=5 it
had been N/k. The cap gives N/d in both cases. Small primes contribute
only o(N). The final upper coefficient rate is therefore correctly

                     3/2+1/k+1/(2k+4),

for each fixed odd k>=3.

4. Check of the lower-rate comparison

Although the primary request concerned upper bounds, I read the onset
proofs needed for the displayed two-sided interval as well.

For the first layer, u^2 first has a p denominator at degree p+1 with
residue 8. In h''=-1/2 int t u(t)^2/(1+t^2)^2 dt, this gives degree
p+3 and residue -4/3. No earlier denominator can arise from the outer
integration, because the first even multiple of p is 2p>p+3. The j=2
term multiplies by 2v^2, giving degree p+2k+3 and residue -32/3. The
j>=3 terms start too late, as asserted.

For the second layer, below the relevant fast cutoff p^2 one has
p u=U and p^2 W=chi_4(p)W(t^p) modulo p, with U=u(t^p). Therefore

 p^2 h''=U^2 sech(u)/8-chi_4(p)W(t^p)/4.

After multiplication by 2v^2 the first two candidate residues are
4(1-chi_4(p)) at degree 2p+2k and -8 at degree 2p+2k+2. The condition
2p+2k+2<kp makes the slow factors and factorials p-integral. The
remaining fast degrees are at most 2p+2<p^2, even if the TOTAL degree
is larger than p^2. This distinction is handled correctly in the proof.
The j>=3 terms are again later. The stated exact second-layer onsets
therefore follow under their hypotheses.

For fixed k, the omitted primes in these onset hypotheses form a finite
set. The fourth-layer coefficient at degree dp is nonzero modulo p
for p>d, since the original degree-d coefficient is -4. Consequently
the common denominator has at least one layer through N-O_k(1), a
second through N/2-O_k(1), and two extra layers through N/d, up to
finitely many primes. The lower rate 3/2+2/d=3/2+1/(k+2) follows.

Thus the displayed inequality for liminf and limsup is supported by
the stated proofs. It neither asserts a limit nor identifies the
unresolved third-layer onset.

5. Scope that must remain explicit

All rates above use the original t-degree N. Reindexing the even series
by z=t^2 doubles a rate per coefficient index. The endpoint n-by-n
matrix needs original degree 4n; the arithmetic writeup uses this
cutoff consistently.

These are common-denominator bounds for coefficients and consequent
raw clearing upper bounds. They do not by themselves bound the
primitive determinant multiplier sharply, prove nonvanishing, or give
a small real determinant. The cross-review therefore supports the
arithmetic improvement, while supplying no inference that it resolves
the compatibility problem from the research task.
