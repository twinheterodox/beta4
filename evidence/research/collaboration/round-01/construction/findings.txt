Beta(4), coordinated round 01: construction investigator
28 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

Status and scope

This is a derivation and a separate implementation of checks by the same
construction agent. It is not independent mathematical verification,
formal verification, an irrationality proof, or a priority claim. B=beta(4).
The prior starting point is ../../../dilations-and-positive-kernels.txt
relative to this file. No prior files have been changed.

The main outcomes are negative but uniform:

  1. The positive cubic-tail kernel's Cauchy upper determinant bound is
     sharp up to exp(O(n)) for EVERY positive integer row/column node
     design. This also holds after a common zero node is included, with
     the positive-node Cauchy determinant as comparison. Thus a better
     real estimate cannot supply a missing n^2-scale gain in a fixed
     node design.

  2. For the principal matrices on ANY shifted consecutive positive
     integer nodes m+1,...,m+n, primitive integer determinant values
     grow at least exp((2-2 log 2-o(1)) n^2), uniformly over sequences
     of nonnegative integer offsets m=m_n. The same lower exponent
     holds for principal nodes 0,...,n-1. This rules out these complete
     families, not only the previously tested small examples.

  3. For every odd slow-filter parameter k>=3, the rational germ in
     z=t^2 has explicit nonreal logarithmic branch points. They survive
     removal of its initial zero coefficients and even multiplication
     by a nonzero algebraic function plus algebraic addition. Consequently
     this entire class of same-variable repairs cannot turn the germ
     into a Cauchy/moment transform of a real compactly supported measure.
     In particular, an arcsine-type algebraic factor alone cannot supply
     the desired positive real moment representation.

These results do NOT rule out nonprincipal node designs, compression to
a genuinely different row/column space, a bivariate kernel, nonlinear
reparametrization, a different period representation, or a non-moment
nonvanishing mechanism. The slow filter and positive kernel have not
been combined.

1. Independent reconstruction of the positive kernel

Let

 f(z)=sum_{j>=0}[(4j+1+4z)^(-3)-(4j+3+4z)^(-3)].

Expanding sech(u)=2 sum_{j>=0}(-1)^j exp(-(2j+1)u), or summing the
two geometric progressions, gives

 f(z)=(1/4) integral_0^infinity u^2 sech(u) exp(-4zu) du,
 f(0)=pi^3/32,                 f'(0)=-12B.

For nonnegative integers a let

 S_l(a)=sum_{j=0}^{a-1}[(4j+1)^(-l)-(4j+3)^(-l)].

Then f(a)=f(0)-S_3(a), f'(a)=-12[B-S_4(a)]. Define

 g(x)=sqrt(x)[f(0)-f(sqrt(x))]/6,
 K(x,y)=(g(y)-g(x))/(y-x), with its diagonal derivative.

The cosine integral

 integral_0^infinity x[1-cos(us)]/(x+s^2) ds
       = (pi sqrt(x)/2)[1-exp(-u sqrt(x))]

and Tonelli's theorem yield

 K(x,y)=integral_0^infinity omega(s)/[(x+s^2)(y+s^2)] ds,
 omega(s)=(pi^2/96)s^2 q(s),
 q(s)=1+A(s)-2A(s)^3,
 A(s)=sech(2pi s).

Here the Fourier calculation is

 Re f(is)=(pi^3/32) A(s)[2A(s)^2-1].

It follows by differentiating twice the cosine transform
integral_0^infinity sech(u)cos(tu)du=(pi/2)sech(pi t/2).
The density is strictly positive for s>0 because

 q=(1-A)(2A^2+2A+1)>0.

It is O(s^4) at zero after multiplication by s^2, and O(s^2) at
infinity. The integral is finite even at x=y=0. Direct differentiation
and the finite-sum identities reproduce

 K(a^2,b^2)=[b S_3(b)-a S_3(a)]/[6(b^2-a^2)]       (a!=b),
 K(a^2,a^2)=B-S_4(a)+S_3(a)/(12a)                  (a>0),
 K(0,0)=2B.                                                    (1.1)

For increasing distinct nonnegative x_i and y_j, Andreief's expansion
of the determinant is a multiple integral of

 product_l omega(s_l)
 det[1/(x_i+s_l^2)] det[1/(y_j+s_l^2)] / n!.

On ordered positive s_l both Cauchy determinants have the same strict
sign. Absolute integrability follows term by term from the finite
entries. Thus every ordered square minor is strictly positive. This
also justifies density comparisons for nonsymmetric minors: the product
of determinants has a fixed positive sign. An entrywise comparison
alone would NOT be sufficient for a determinant comparison.

2. A two-sided determinant comparison

Put

 c0=pi^3/192,  M=1+2/(3 sqrt(6)),  tau0=1/(sqrt(2) pi).

The upper density bound q<=M is attained at A=1/sqrt(6). For the lower
bound, cosh(v)>=1+v^2/2 implies

 q(s)>=1-A(s)>=s^2/(s^2+tau0^2).                              (2.1)

For tau>0 the elementary rational integral is

 integral_0^infinity s^4/[(s^2+tau^2)(s^2+a^2)(s^2+b^2)] ds
   =(pi/2) R_tau(a,b),

 R_tau(a,b)=[ab+tau(a+b)]/[(a+b)(a+tau)(b+tau)].                (2.2)

This extends continuously to a=0 or b=0, with R_tau(0,0)=1/tau.
Let C(a,b) denote the n-by-n Cauchy matrix C_ij=1/(a_i+b_j).
For strictly positive distinct nodes, density comparison gives

 c0^n det R_tau0(a,b) <= det K(a_i^2,b_j^2)
                         <= (M c0)^n det C(a,b).              (2.3)

The determinant of the regularized matrix has an exact formula:

 det R_tau(a,b)=det C(a,b)
   * product_i a_i/(a_i+tau) * product_j b_j/(b_j+tau)
   * [1+tau(sum_i 1/a_i + sum_j 1/b_j)].                       (2.4)

Proof of (2.4), including the interpolation identity.
Write u_i=1/a_i, v_j=1/b_j. Factoring rows and columns gives

 R_tau = diag(a_i/(a_i+tau)) [C+tau u v^T]
                                  diag(b_j/(b_j+tau)).

It remains to show v^T C^(-1)u=sum_i 1/a_i+sum_j 1/b_j. Let
w=C^(-1)u and F(x)=sum_j w_j/(x+b_j). Then F(a_i)=1/a_i.
The rational function E(x)=1/x-F(x) consequently has the form

 E(x)=c product_i(x-a_i)/[x product_j(x+b_j)],

because its numerator has degree at most n and vanishes at all a_i.
The residue at zero fixes c. Its expansion at zero is therefore

 E(x)=(1/x) product_i(1-x/a_i)/product_j(1+x/b_j)
     =1/x-sum_i1/a_i-sum_j1/b_j+O(x).

On the other hand E(x)=1/x-F(0)+O(x). Hence
v^T C^(-1)u=F(0) has the required value. The determinant lemma proves
(2.4). This proof requires no symmetry between the row and column sets.

For positive INTEGER nodes, (2.3)-(2.4) imply the uniform bounds

 [c0/(1+tau0)^2]^n det C <= det K <= (M c0)^n det C.           (2.5)

In particular

                  log det K = log det C + O(n),              (2.6)

where the implied constants are absolute and independent of the node
positions, gaps, intersections, and maximum node. The Cauchy determinant
is explicit:

 det C = product_{i<j}(a_j-a_i)(b_j-b_i)
                                 / product_{i,j}(a_i+b_j).

2.1 A common zero node

Suppose a_0=b_0=0 and all remaining nodes are positive. Then

 det R_tau(a,b)=tau^(-1)
   * product_{i>0} a_i/(a_i+tau)
   * product_{j>0} b_j/(b_j+tau) * det C(a_+,b_+).             (2.7)

For a direct proof, multiply row i by a_i+tau and column j by b_j+tau.
The matrix becomes tau+ab/(a+b), with continuous value tau in its zero
row and zero column. Subtract the zero row from every other row. The
remaining nonzero block is a_i b_j/(a_i+b_j). Expanding at the first
column and undoing the scalings proves (2.7), including n=1.

The lower comparison is still c0^n det R_tau0. To get an upper bound
finite at the zero node, note

 q(s)<=5[1-sech(2pi s)]<=10pi^2 s^2,
 q(s)<=(M+10pi^2 tau^2)s^2/(s^2+tau^2)     for every tau>0.

The second inequality follows by multiplying by s^2+tau^2 and using
both separate bounds q<=M and q<=10pi^2s^2. Consequently

 c0^n det R_tau0 <= det K
       <= [c0(M+10pi^2 tau^2)]^n det R_tau.                   (2.8)

Choose any fixed tau, for example 1/8. For integer positive remaining
nodes, each product in (2.7) lies between (1+tau)^(-2(n-1)) and one.
Thus

              log det K = log det C(a_+,b_+) + O(n),          (2.9)

uniformly over these frames. This completes the zero-node case that
the unregularized Cauchy comparison cannot handle.

3. An arithmetic obstruction for every consecutive principal family

Let nodes a_i=m+i, 1<=i<=n, where m>=0 is any integer, and let

 Q_{n,m}(X)=det(X I+R),
 R_ii=-S_4(a_i)+S_3(a_i)/(12a_i),
 R_ij=[a_j S_3(a_j)-a_i S_3(a_i)]/[6(a_j^2-a_i^2)].

At X=B this is the strictly positive kernel determinant. Let
P_{n,m}=H_{n,m} Q_{n,m} have primitive integer coefficients, with H>0.
Since Q is monic, H is a positive integer. This is the primitive
normalization, not an arbitrary sufficient common denominator.

3.1 Exact coefficient valuations

More generally take ANY principal positive integer node set with maximum
M0, and let p be a prime with 2M0<p<4M0, p>=5. Set

        r_p=#{i:4a_i>p}.

Among the odd denominators below 4a_i, the only possible multiple of p
is p itself; 3p>4M0. Every a_i, a_i-a_j, a_i+a_j is a p-adic unit.
Hence all entries p^4 R_ij are p-integral, and their reductions are

 p^4 R_ij == -chi_4(p) if i=j and 4a_i>p,
              0       otherwise,                         mod p. (3.1)

The coefficient of X^(n-r_p) in det(XI+R) is the sum of all principal
r_p-by-r_p minors of R. Multiplying by p^(4r_p), every such minor
vanishes modulo p except the unique minor using ALL r_p high nodes.
That minor reduces to (-chi_4(p))^(r_p), a unit. Therefore

 v_p([X^(n-r_p)] Q)=-4r_p,
                         v_p(H)>=4r_p.                      (3.2)

This is an exact valuation statement, with a unique nonzero term after
reduction; no generic rank or absence-of-cancellation assumption is used.
It yields

 log H >= 4 sum_{2M0<p<4M0} r_p log p.                        (3.3)

For principal nodes 0,...,n-1 the beta coefficient matrix has A_00=2
and all other diagonal entries one. The coefficient in (3.2) acquires
the unit factor 2 modulo these odd primes, since the high nodes never
include zero. Its valuation is unchanged. The leading coefficient is
2, so 2H is an integer and (3.3) holds with an additional -log 2.

3.2 The unshifted asymptotic inequality

For a_i=i, M0=n and r_p=n-floor(p/4). The prime number theorem gives

 4 sum_{2n<p<4n} [n-floor(p/4)] log p
        = 4 integral_{2n}^{4n} (n-x/4) dx + o(n^2)
        = 2n^2+o(n^2).                                      (3.4)

Floors contribute O(n), and partial summation justifies replacing
the weighted prime sum by the integral.

The exact Cauchy product is

 det[1/(i+j)]_{i,j=1}^n
       = product_{j=1}^{n-1}(j!)^2 / product_{i,j=1}^n(i+j),

and standard sum-to-integral estimates, or Stirling's formula, give

                   log det C=-2 log(2) n^2+o(n^2).           (3.5)

Combining the LOWER real bound (2.5), primitive arithmetic (3.4), and
strict positivity proves

 log P_{n,0}(B)>=(2-2log 2)n^2+o(n^2).                       (3.6)

The coefficient 2-2log 2 is approximately 0.6137056389, strictly
positive. These primitive values grow, so this family cannot prove
irrationality through shrinking integer polynomial values. For nodes
0,...,n-1 use (2.9), M0=n-1, and the final observation of section 3.1;
the same leading lower exponent follows.

3.3 All nonnegative consecutive offsets

Suppose first m/n->rho, 0<=rho<infinity. Reversing the order of the sum
in (3.3), and using theta(x)=x+o(x) uniformly on [2M0,4M0], gives

 4 sum_p r_p log p
   =16 sum_{a_i>M0/2}(a_i-M0/2)+o(n^2)
   =A(rho)n^2+o(n^2),

 A(rho)=2(1+rho)^2 for 0<=rho<=1,
         8rho         for rho>=1.                            (3.7)

The numerator Vandermonde product is unchanged by the shift. Thus

 log det C=-D(rho)n^2+o(n^2),
 D(rho)=3/2 + integral_0^1 integral_0^1 log(2rho+x+y) dxdy
       =(1/2)[(2rho+2)^2 log(2rho+2)
              -2(2rho+1)^2 log(2rho+1)
              +(2rho)^2 log(2rho)],                         (3.8)

where 0^2 log 0 means zero. The double integral is finite at rho=0.
Its derivative is decreasing and

 D'(rho)=2 integral_0^1 integral_0^1 1/(2rho+x+y) dxdy
        <=D'(0)=4log 2<4.

On both pieces A'(rho)>=4. Therefore A-D is strictly increasing and

                 A(rho)-D(rho)>=2-2log 2>0.                 (3.9)

For every finite proportional offset the primitive values satisfy

 log P_{n,m}(B)>=[A(rho)-D(rho)]n^2+o(n^2).                  (3.10)

If m/n->infinity, all nodes are high for the relevant initial prime
intervals. The same reversed sum and the prime number theorem give

 log H >= 8mn+o(mn).

The exact Cauchy product and Stirling give the sufficient bound

 log det C >= -O(n^2[1+log(1+m/n)]) = -o(mn).

The real comparison still costs only O(n). Hence

                  log P_{n,m}(B)>=(8-o(1))mn.                (3.11)

Every sequence of offsets has subsequences for which m/n tends to a
finite rho or to infinity. Combining (3.9)-(3.11) consequently proves
the uniform statement

 for EVERY sequence m_n>=0 of integer offsets,

 liminf_{n->infinity} log P_{n,m_n}(B)/n^2 >= 2-2log 2.       (3.12)

This is the specified-family impossibility theorem. It does not address
arbitrary sparse node sets or rectangular row/column configurations.

3.4 Changes of basis do not evade this result

Multiplying a full pencil on the left and right by invertible rational
matrices changes its determinant by a nonzero rational scalar. Its
primitive integer polynomial is unchanged up to sign. Therefore rational
row operations, column operations, rational congruences, diagonal
rescaling, and preconditioning of these FULL principal pencils cannot
evade (3.12). Selecting a different subspace is a different construction
and is not excluded by this observation.

4. Nonreal branch points of the slow filter

Use the analytic germ h defined by

 h'''(x)=x^2 sech'(x)/8,  h(0)=h'(0)=h''(0)=0.

For odd k>=3 let

 u(t)=2 atanh(t),       v(t)=2 atanh(t^k),
 r_k(t)=2h(u)+4h(v)-4h(u+v)-2h(2v)+2h(u+2v).

The slow filter is J_k(t)=6B+r_k(t). Its germ r_k is even and
r_k(t)=-4t^(2k+4)+O(t^(2k+6)). Set z=t^2 and R_k(z)=r_k(sqrt(z)).
Let q=sqrt(2)-1=tan(pi/8).

Claim. Every point z0 satisfying

                         z0^k=-q^2                          (4.1)

is a genuine logarithmic branch point of analytic continuation of
R_k. In particular k-1 of these k points are nonreal, and all are
inside |z|<1.

Proof. Since k is odd, choose the square root t0 of z0 for which
t0^k=iq. It lies in |t|<1 and is nonzero. Then

             v(t0)=i pi/4,          2v(t0)=i pi/2=:x0.

Near x0, writing epsilon=x-x0, the Laurent expansion is

 h'''(x)=-i pi^2/(32 epsilon^2)+O(1/epsilon).

Integrating three times locally shows

 h(x)=(i pi^2/32) epsilon log epsilon
                         +O(epsilon^2 log epsilon)+analytic. (4.2)

The coefficient displayed is nonzero. Also

 (2v)'(t0)=4k t0^(k-1)/(1+q^2) !=0.

Thus the -2h(2v) summand has a nonzero first-order logarithmic branch
at t0. The other summands cannot cancel it. To check this precisely,
u maps |t|<1 into the strip |Im u|<pi/2, and the poles of sech(x)
are x=i pi/2+i pi Z.

  * h(u) is locally analytic because u lies in that open strip.
  * h(v) is regular at v=i pi/4.
  * h(u+v) could be singular here only if u=i pi/4, hence t0=iq.
    But |(iq)^k|=q^k !=q for k>=3, contradicting t0^k=iq.
  * h(u+2v) could be singular here only if u=0, hence t0=0,
    another contradiction.

One can continue from the original germ along paths avoiding the
isolated preimages of the poles; at a regular argument every branch
of h is locally analytic. No choice of continuation changes the
nonzero logarithmic coefficient in (4.2). Finally z=t^2 has nonzero
derivative at t0, so the branch is also genuine at z0. This proves
the claim.

4.1 Why removing the initial zeros or inserting an algebraic weight fails

The normalized nonconstant germ R_k(z)/z^(k+2) has the same nonreal
branch points, since none is zero. More generally, let a(z) be any
nonzero algebraic germ and b(z) any algebraic germ, and consider

                         a(z) R_k(z)+b(z).                   (4.3)

This includes nonzero rational prefactors, rational additions, removing
the initial zeros, and arcsine-type algebraic factors. It also applies
to a(z)J_k(sqrt(z))+b(z), since 6B a(z) is an algebraic function of z
over C(z) and can be absorbed in b.

Locally at a point (4.1), R_k has the form A(z)log(z-z0)+B0(z),
where A,B0 are analytic and A has a nonzero first-order term. Algebraic
functions have finite local monodromy (equivalently Puiseux expansions).
After a common positive number d of small turns around z0, a and b
return to their starting branches, whereas R_k changes by 2pi i d A.
Thus (4.3) changes by 2pi i d a A, not identically zero. A zero or pole
of finite algebraic order in a does not remove this monodromy. The
nonreal singularity survives.

For a finite real measure supported on a compact real set, the function

                       integral dmu(s)/(1-zs)                (4.4)

is holomorphic away from the reciprocal real support and has no
nonreal singularity. The same analytic conclusion holds for finite
signed or complex measures on a compact real support. Consequently
none of the germs (4.3), when nontrivial, can equal such a transform.
This rules out the usual same-variable real-support moment/Hankel
route even after the elementary contact-normalization repair.

The statement does not rule out transformations of the INDEPENDENT
VARIABLE, higher-dimensional integral representations, non-algebraic
operations that remove the logarithmic monodromy, complex contours,
or a matrix kernel that does not have (4.3) as a scalar moment germ.
It is not a universal obstruction to combining arithmetic and
nonvanishing in some other construction.

4.2 A simpler direct Loewner warning

For completeness, if F(z)=J_k(sqrt(z))=c-4z^d+O(z^(d+1)), with
c=6B>0 and d=k+2>=5, then the ordinary divided-difference kernel of F
has a zero (0,0) entry and nonzero (0,z) entries, so its two-by-two
principal minor is negative. Multiplying by z to retain the beta
constant in the divided difference does not fix this: for g(z)=zF(z),

 det[[g'(0),g(z)/z],[g(z)/z,g'(z)]]
       = -4c(d-1)z^d+O(z^(d+1)) <0

for sufficiently small positive z. These direct Loewner realizations
therefore fail positivity before arithmetic is considered. Section 4.1
is stronger because it also excludes first factoring out the long
zero prefix and then seeking a standard real moment representation.
