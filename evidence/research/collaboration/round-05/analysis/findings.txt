Outcome and scope
=================

The balanced quadratic prime filter found independently by the arithmetic
worker and this worker does preserve a theta-only Fricke endpoint. The
arithmetic packet gives its exact normalization. This note supplies two
analytic developments and two precisely scoped obstructions.

1. A rational F-only cusp construction retains exact physical prime
   deletion without the higher-level inverse-coordinate problem. Its error
   has a uniform two-sided exponential estimate and an eventual nonzero
   sign, including an explicit dependence on the selected-prime product.
   These are analytic q evaluations; no rational approximants with
   controlled height have been constructed.
2. For every fixed finite set of selected primes, that q germ has the unit
   circle as a natural boundary. Its best admissible-cut capacity is one.
   This rules out a negative n^2 constant from the ordinary unweighted
   absolute-contour determinant estimate; it is NOT a lower bound for the
   actual determinants, and it is NOT a varying-prime-set no-go theorem.
3. A rational Mobius coordinate can make that capacity smaller, but its
   cumulative Hankel pencil is an exact rational multiple of the old one.
   Its primitive endpoint pair is identical. This obstruction is exact.
4. A different Fricke-invariant integral coordinate, built from the product
   and sum of the old parameter, avoids root-extraction denominators but
   creates a genuine principal-branch singularity at a negative parameter
   value of size 1/(2 e m). The new theta remainder does not cancel it.
   This is a coefficient-growth obstruction for that coordinate, not a
   theorem about the primitive values after all content cancellations.

No shrinking primitive integer family or irrationality theorem is proved.

1. Inputs and normalization
===========================

Write theta=beta(4), chi=chi_4, q=exp(2 pi i tau), and D=q d/dq. The
archived modular-detour.txt supplies the following classical input:

 F(tau)=sum_(d odd>=1) chi(d) q^d/[d^4(1+q^d)],
 G(tau)=sum_(d odd>=1) q^d/[d^4(1+q^(2d))],

 tau^3 F(-1/(8tau))-i G(tau)/64
   =theta tau^3/2-i pi^4(tau^2/512+1/12288).             (1.1)

Both D^4 F and D^4 G are holomorphic weight-five modular forms on a
finite-index congruence subgroup. The precise level can be enlarged when
taking finitely many dilations. The archived eta/theta identities give

 t(q)=q u(q), u(q)=1-2q+O(q^2) in Z[[q]],
 t(W_32 tau)=t(tau), J(W_32 tau)=-i sqrt(32)tau J(tau),
 J(q)=theta_3(2tau)^2+2sqrt(2)theta_3(16tau)^2,
 J(0)=1+2sqrt(2).                                      (1.2)

Here W_N tau=-1/(N tau), and t is the exact eta product

 t=q product_(d in {1,2,4,8,16,32}) product_(j>=1)
       (1-q^(dj))^e_d,
 (e_1,e_2,e_4,e_8,e_16,e_32)=(2,-3,1,1,-3,2).          (1.3)

This round inherits (1.1), modularity, and (1.2), rather than claiming a
new independent audit of their classical provenance. New assertions below
are proved from those explicit inputs. No theorem about a fixed-level
Taylor radius is imported into a higher-level construction.

Set a_1=16,a_2=-10,a_4=1 and

 A=16F-10V_2 F+V_4 F,
 B=(sqrt(2)/2)(4G-5V_2 G+V_4 G), H=A+B.                (1.4)

The sums of a_r r and a_r r^3 are zero; sum a_r=7. Thus H has theta-only
Fricke target 7theta/2 at level32. For an odd prime p, put e=chi(p) and

 C_p(V)=(1-e p^(-4)V)(1-pV),
 C_p^*(V)=e(1-p^(-4)V)(1-e pV).                        (1.5)

The quadratic filtered H_(p,2)=C_p(V_p)A+C_p^*(V_p)B satisfies

 H_(p,2)(W_(32p^2)tau)-c_(p,2)theta
   =i/[(32p^2)^(3/2)tau^3](H_(p,2)(tau)-c_(p,2)theta),
 c_(p,2)=(7/2)(1-p)(1-e/p^4).                         (1.6)

This follows from cubic weighted coefficient reversal; products over

For the quartic comparison define

 D_p(V)=p^3+(p^4-p^3-1)V+V^2.

It is self-dual for cubic reversal and D_p(1)=p^4. We use the arithmetic

 c_(p,4)=p c_(p,2), level32p^4.                        (1.7)

An unnormalized integral D_p would rescale both H and c by p^3 and change

2. A rational cusp construction with exact prime deletion
========================================================

Let S be any finite set of odd primes, P=product_(p in S)p (P=1 if S is
empty), and alpha_S=product_(p in S)(1-chi(p)/p^4). Define

 A_S(tau)=product_(p in S)(1-chi(p)p^(-4)V_p)A(tau)
   =sum_(r in {1,2,4},d|P) a_r mu(d)chi(d)d^(-4) F(rd tau).
                                                                  (2.1)

Here mu(d)=(-1)^(number of prime factors of d), since d|P. This is the
minimal physical deletion of the F outer divisor, without the additional
Fricke balancing factor1-pV_p. Directly from the Lambert series,

 (1-chi(p)p^(-4)V_p)F
  =sum_(d odd,p not dividing d) chi(d)q^d/[d^4(1+q^d)].  (2.2)

Thus all its Fourier coefficients are p-integral. The operation does NOT
delete every Fourier index divisible by p. In fact, if p does not divide m,

 F_(p^a m)=F_m sum_(j=0)^a (chi(p)/p^4)^j,

so after (2.2) the coefficient at p^a m equals F_m for every a>=0.
The same statement holds for A, since its dilations are powers of two.
This distinguishes the Lambert construction from the sparse original
polylogarithm deletion in round04.

Dominated convergence along q increasing to1 gives

 A_S(1^-)=c_S theta, c_S=(7/2)alpha_S.                 (2.3)

For direct comparison with primitive endpoint pencils, set h_S=2A_S.
Its rational coefficients satisfy h_1=32 and endpoint h_S(1^-)=7alpha_S
theta. The precise order-n endpoint pencil is

 b_i=(h_S)_i, T_ij=(h_S)_(i+j)-(h_S)_(i+j-1),
 J_(h_S)(X)=det[[X,-b^T],[b,T]], evaluated at X=7alpha_S theta.
                                                                  (2.4)

This is (-1)^n the leading Hankel determinant of
c_j(X)=X-sum_(l=1)^j(h_S)_l, j>=0. If its rational coefficient pair is
nonzero, clear and divide by its FULL gcd. The minimal F-only deletion
has the same selected-prime determinant bound v_p(kappa)<=4: the entries
are p-integral and only the theta endpoint carries p^-4. No evaluated
nonvanishing assertion for (2.4) follows from the cusp sign theorem below.

3. Exact transformed cusp error, including a uniform sign theorem
================================================================

Solve (1.1) for F(rd tau) by replacing its tau with -1/(8rd tau).
The theta term remains theta/2, the G term is -8i(rd tau)^3
G(-1/(8rd tau)), and the remaining polynomial consists of terms
proportional to rd tau and (rd tau)^3. Summing with (2.1) kills both
polynomial terms because sum a_r r=sum a_r r^3=0. Consequently

 A_S(tau)-c_S theta
  =-8i tau^3 sum_(r in {1,2,4},d|P)
      a_r r^3 mu(d)chi(d)/d G(-1/(8rd tau)).             (3.1)

This is an EXACT identity, not a small-q asymptotic or a numerical fit.
In particular, for tau=iy, y>0,

 A_S(iy)-c_S theta
  =-8y^3 sum_(r,d) a_r r^3 mu(d)chi(d)/d
                  G(i/(8rd y)).                       (3.2)

Set x=pi/(16Py), z=exp(-x), and
sigma= sum_(d|P)1/d=product_(p in S)(1+1/p).
The unique largest dilation is rd=4P. Its coefficient inside the sum
is64mu(P)chi(P)/P, and its G argument has q=z. Every other rd is at
most2P. For0<z<=1/2, the elementary Lambert bounds are

 |G(z)-z|<=2z^3,   0<G(z)<=2z.                         (3.3)

For clarity, in (3.3) G(z) denotes the q series, not the tau variable.
The first term z/(1+z^2) differs from z by at most z^3; the sum over
odd d>=3 is at most z^3/(1-z^2)<=4z^3/3. A one-sided estimate of the
two contributions gives the stated bound2z^3. All other transformed
G arguments are powers z^(4P/(rd)), with integer exponent at least2.
Since sum |a_r|r^3=16+80+64=160, it follows that

 A_S(iy)-c_S theta
  =-512 mu(P)chi(P)y^3 z/P (1+epsilon),
 |epsilon|<=2z^2+5P sigma z.                           (3.4)

Indeed the nonleading terms have absolute sum at most320sigma z^2;
division by64z/P gives5P sigma z. Including the leading term's error
gives (3.4). The bound deliberately overcounts the omitted leading term.

COROLLARY 3.1. If x>=log(20P sigma), the relative error is less than1/2.
The cusp error is nonzero with sign -mu(P)chi(P), and

 256 y^3 e^(-x)/P
  < |A_S(iy)-c_S theta| <768 y^3 e^(-x)/P.              (3.5)

This estimate is uniform as S varies. The sufficient approach scale is
y<=pi/[16P log(20P sigma)], explicitly displaying the cost of growing
the selected-prime product. For fixed S it also gives the sharp first
term -512mu(P)chi(P)y^3 e^(-pi/(16Py))/P.

These are analytic values at q=exp(-2pi y). They do not by themselves
give rational approximants with controlled height, coefficient forms, or a proof
of nonvanishing for their endpoint determinants. In particular, choosing
such a small y cannot be treated as an arithmetic-free substitution.
No transcendence assertion about these q values is needed or established.

4. Natural boundary and the exact scope of the capacity-one barrier
==================================================================

LEMMA 4.1. A nonzero positive-integer-weight holomorphic modular form
with period1, on a finite-index subgroup and holomorphic at its cusps,
cannot be meromorphic through any finite rational cusp in the tau plane.

Proof. Let gamma=(a b;c d) in SL_2(Z), c!=0, represent that cusp. Increase
the cusp width if necessary to kill its finite character. Then f|_k gamma
has a Fourier expansion a_0+O(exp(-eta Im z)), eta>0, at infinity. If f
were meromorphic at a/c in the ordinary tau coordinate, f|_k gamma would
be meromorphic in1/z near infinity. Its difference from a_0 would be
exponentially flat on the imaginary axis, hence identically zero: a
nonzero convergent Laurent series has a first nonzero power and cannot
be flat. It follows that f(tau)=a_0(a-c tau)^(-k) on the upper half-plane.
If a_0=0 this contradicts f!=0; otherwise it contradicts period1. QED.

D^4 A_S is a finite linear combination of dilations of the inherited
holomorphic modular form D^4 F, hence is such a modular form after
passing to a common finite-index subgroup. It is nonzero: its q^1
coefficient is16. Every rational tau cusp therefore gives a nonremovable
singularity at its corresponding root of unity in q. If A_S extended
holomorphically there, its fourth D derivative would also extend.
The roots of unity are dense, so the unit circle is a natural boundary
of A_S, for every fixed finite S.

It follows also that the evaluated cumulative germ (X-h_S(q))/(1-q)
is nonrational for EVERY fixed complex X. Kronecker's criterion, in the
exact leading-Hankel indexing of (2.4), therefore gives

 J_(h_S),n(7alpha_S theta)!=0 for infinitely many n, for each FIXED S.

The criterion and proof were re-read in Aitor Iribar Lopez, The
Carlson-Polya theorem on rational functions, Theorem1, PDF pages2-3:
https://people.math.ethz.ch/~airibar/Polya_Carlson.pdf . This is the
same application recorded in kronecker-nonvanishing.txt, with the
present cumulative coefficients. It needs no rationality hypothesis on
theta and gives neither a density of nonzero indices nor nonvanishing
when S varies with n. Primitive normalization preserves these nonzero
values. A uniform primitive size estimate for the SAME fixed S is absent.

The same conclusion applies to every nonzero fixed finite oldform
filter of F and G whose fourth derivative is a holomorphic modular form;
it is not specific to the minimal filter. Constant subtraction and
division by1-q cannot remove a natural boundary. Thus for every X,

 C_X(q)=(X-h_S(q))/(1-q),
 mathcal G_X(w)=C_X(1/w)/w                              (4.1)

has the reciprocal unit circle as its natural boundary from infinity.
If a filled compact set K has connected complement containing infinity
and supports single-valued holomorphic continuation of this germ there,
K must contain the closed unit disk. Otherwise a path in the complement
from infinity to a missing point of the circle or disk would first cross
the natural boundary. Conversely the closed disk is an admissible K.
Therefore the infimum of the capacities of these admissible K is exactly1.

This gives a scoped obstruction to the usual unweighted absolute
Cauchy/Vandermonde or Polya determinant estimate: its n^2 rate supplied
by such a compact is log cap(K)>=0. It supplies no negative n^2 error
constant to pair with arithmetic. This is NOT a lower asymptotic for
actual Hankel determinants, NOT a proof that cancellations cannot make
them small, and NOT a uniform theorem when S grows with n. No determinant
converse, Stahl theorem, or approximation lower bound is being invoked.

5. Mobius coordinate improvement cancels exactly in primitive forms
==================================================================

Let a>0 be rational, b=(a-1)/a, c=1/a, and substitute

 q=c t/(1-bt)=t/[a-(a-1)t].

It fixes the endpoint1. The reciprocal image of the unit-circle boundary
has center b and radius c, so its capacity is c. However the cumulative
generating function transforms EXACTLY as

 C_t(t)=(X-h_S(c t/(1-bt)))/(1-t)
       =(1-bt)^(-1) C_q(c t/(1-bt)).                  (5.1)

Thus c'_j=sum_(k=0)^j binom(j,k)b^(j-k)c^k c_k. For the leading
Hankel matrices of size n+1, Vandermonde convolution gives

 H'=B H B^T,
 B_ik=binom(i,k)b^(i-k)c^k, 0<=k<=i<=n.

Their determinants satisfy

 det H'=c^(n(n+1)) det H=a^(-n(n+1)) det H.            (5.2)

The entire endpoint pencil is multiplied by one nonzero rational scalar;
its primitive integer pair is therefore unchanged up to an overall sign.
This is an exact normalization identity, even if a and S vary with n.
The apparent capacity gain is exactly offset by primitive normalization.
It is stronger than a failure of a sufficient estimate, but is restricted
to this rational Mobius family.

6. An integral Fricke coordinate with a new accessible singularity
=================================================================

The tempting product (t(q)t(q^m))^(1/(m+1)) has leading term q but
introduces root-extraction denominators. To avoid them, consider instead
the explicit rational product/sum coordinate, for an odd square m>1,

 T_m(q)=t(q)t(q^m)/(t(q)+t(q^m))^m.                    (6.1)

It is invariant under W_(32m), which exchanges its two t factors. Since
t(q)=q u(q), it has the useful exact formal factorization

 T_m(q)=q u(q)^(1-m)u(q^m)
        [1+q^(m-1)u(q^m)/u(q)]^(-m).                 (6.2)

Consequently T_m belongs to q+q^2 Z[[q]] and its formal compositional
inverse is integral. This is a genuinely different arithmetic-compatible
coordinate; no root extraction or fractional power was silently used.

Nevertheless, uniformly on compact z sets, with all complex derivatives,

 m T_m(z/m) -> z exp(2z).                              (6.3)

To prove it, use log u(z/m)=-2z/m+O(m^-2), while u((z/m)^m) tends
to1 and the last factor in (6.2) tends to1 faster than any fixed inverse
power of m. Uniform convergence on slightly larger compact sets justifies
the derivative assertion by Cauchy's formula. Expanding one order further
also gives an O(m^-1) remainder locally in (6.3).

The derivative of z exp(2z) is exp(2z)(1+2z); it has a simple zero at
z=-1/2. The analytic implicit function/Rouche argument gives a real simple
critical point and value

 q_m=-1/(2m)+O(m^-2),
 T_m(q_m)=-1/(2 e m)+O(m^-2).                          (6.4)

Reality follows from uniqueness near-1/2 and conjugation. This is on the
PRINCIPAL inverse branch: for any fixed small delta>0, uniform derivative
convergence shows strict monotonicity on the real segment from0 to
(-1/2+delta)/m; the unique simple zero accounts for the remaining short
segment to q_m. The denominator in (6.2) is nonzero throughout this small
neighborhood. Thus no earlier pole or critical point blocks continuation
along the negative real interval. Its image is the interval from0 down
to T_m(q_m). This addresses sheet accessibility, not just criticality.

Put

 J_m(q)=J(q)+sqrt(m)J(q^m),
 Y_m=D log T_m, mathcal A_m=J_m^5/Y_m.                 (6.5)

For m a square, J_m has coefficients in Z[sqrt(2)], and Y_m is an
integral unit series, so the prefactor and its expression in T_m retain
formal integer arithmetic. The Fricke weights match the new H filter.
At q_m, J_m=(1+sqrt(m))(1+2sqrt(2))+O(1/m) is nonzero, while Y_m
has a simple zero. Hence mathcal A_m has a simple pole in q-q_m.

For the quadratic single-prime construction use m=p^2. Its first
coefficient is16+2sqrt(2)chi(p), and all nontrivial p dilations are
exponentially tiny at q=O(p^-2). Thus

 H_(p,2)(q_m)=O(p^-2), c_(p,2)~-(7/2)p,
 H_(p,2)(q_m)/c_(p,2) ->0.                            (6.6)

For the CANONICAL quartic construction use m=p^4 and (1.7). Its
constant dilation coefficient is still1, so

 H_(p,4)(q_m)=O(p^-4), c_(p,4)~-(7/2)p^2,
 H_(p,4)(q_m)/c_(p,4) ->0.                            (6.7)

The polynomially growing other filter coefficients multiply q^(p^j),
so cannot alter either bound. Since theta>80/81, the target factor
H_(p,r)(q_m)-c_(p,r)theta is nonzero for all sufficiently large p.

The completed remainder

 R_(p,r)(T)=mathcal A_m(q(T))
              [H_(p,r)(q(T))-c_(p,r)theta]             (6.8)

therefore has a nonzero inverse-square-root singularity at the negative
point in (6.4). If its Taylor radius were larger than |T_m(q_m)|, its
Taylor germ would be analytic all along the preceding negative interval,
contradicting this singularity. In particular, for each sufficiently large p,

 limsup_(j->infinity) |[T^j]R_(p,r)|^(1/j)
     >=1/|T_m(q_m)|=2 e m(1+O(1/m)).                  (6.9)

This is an actual lower growth statement for the COMPLETED REAL remainder
in this proposed coordinate, not merely an upper-estimate failure. It is
not an assertion about each Q(sqrt(2)) component separately. Nor does it
control full rational content of the integer linear forms or prove that
every primitive subsequence fails. The proved conclusion is that this
specific integral coordinate does not retain a decaying raw coefficient
remainder by the intended first-branch cancellation mechanism.
