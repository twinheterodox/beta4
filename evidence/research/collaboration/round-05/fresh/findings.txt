Euler--Boole tail approximants: a uniform primitive-value obstruction
===================================================================
29 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

No irrationality proof is obtained. The result below excludes one entire
prescribed family, with every integer tail endpoint and every truncation
order, by a lower bound on its ACTUAL primitive integer values. It is not
an inference from an unsuccessful sufficient denominator bound.

Let theta=beta(4), N>=1 and m>=0. Define

 S_N = sum_(0<=k<N) (-1)^k/(2k+1)^4,
 T_(N,m) = (1/12) sum_(0<=j<m)
            E_(2j)(2j+1)(2j+2)(2j+3)/(2N)^(2j+4),
 A_(N,m) = S_N + (-1)^N T_(N,m),                       (1)

where sech(t)=sum E_(2j)t^(2j)/(2j)! and an empty sum is zero.
Write A_(N,m)=a_(N,m)/q_(N,m) in lowest terms, with q_(N,m)>0.
Thus

                P_(N,m)(X)=q_(N,m)X-a_(N,m)           (2)

is the uniquely normalized primitive integer linear polynomial.

THEOREM. Every P_(N,m)(theta) is nonzero. Uniformly over all m>=1,

 |theta-A_(N,m)| >= pi^2 exp(-pi*N)/(144N),             (3)

and, uniformly over all m>=0,

 liminf_(N->infinity) inf_(m>=0) log(q_(N,m))/N >= 8.   (4)

Consequently

 liminf_(N->infinity) inf_(m>=0)
      log |P_(N,m)(theta)| / N >= 8-pi > 0.            (5)

Every sequence with N+m tending to infinity therefore has
|P_(N,m)(theta)| tending to infinity. Multiplying these primitive forms,
even with degrees tending to infinity, cannot give values whose
degree-normalized absolute values tend to zero (Section 6).

The real exponential constant pi in (3) is sharp: if
m=pi*N/2+O(1), then

 |theta-A_(N,m)| ~ pi^3/(48*sqrt(2N)) * exp(-pi*N).    (6)

The arithmetic rate 8 is a proved lower bound, not a claim to the exact
denominator rate for every growing truncation schedule. Actual denominators
can be much larger. No assertion is made about arbitrary rational linear
combinations, Padé approximants, or determinants of these forms.

2. Exact tail identity and integral remainder
---------------------------------------------

The Euler numbers used here are integers directly from

 E_0=1,  E_(2r)=-sum_(0<=j<r) binom(2r,2j)E_(2j), r>=1,

which follows by multiplying the formal power series of cosh and sech.
The Laplace representation 1/u^4=(1/6) integral_0^infinity t^3 exp(-ut)dt
and the geometric series give, for N>=1,

 theta-S_N = (-1)^N R_N,
 R_N = (1/12) integral_0^infinity t^3 exp(-2Nt)sech(t)dt.     (7)

The exchanges can also be justified first on a finite alternating sum;
the remainder is dominated by an integrable exponential. Integrating the
finite Taylor polynomial of sech(t) gives exactly T_(N,m), because
integral t^(2j+3) exp(-2Nt)dt=(2j+3)!/(2N)^(2j+4).
There are no auxiliary beta-values, powers of pi, logarithms, or other
periods in A_(N,m).

Put a=pi/2 and a_k=(2k+1)a. The classical partial fractions identity is

 sech(t)=2 sum_(k>=0) (-1)^k a_k/(a_k^2+t^2), t>=0.     (8)

Here is a short derivation, to make the remainder argument self-contained.
Expand f(x)=cosh(tx)/cosh(t), 0<=x<=1, in the mixed-boundary cosine basis
cos(a_k x). Twice integrating f''=t^2 f by parts gives

 integral_0^1 f(x)cos(a_k x)dx = a_k(-1)^k/(t^2+a_k^2).

The cosine series at x=0 equals f(0) by ordinary Fourier convergence
(the even extension is smooth near zero). Its coefficients are twice
these integrals. This proves (8), including the conditionally convergent
t=0 value. The remainder below is absolutely convergent.

The finite geometric identity applied to each term of (8) gives, for
every integer m>=1 and every t>=0,

 sech(t)-sum_(j<m) E_(2j)t^(2j)/(2j)!
   =(-1)^m t^(2m) C_m(t),

 C_m(t)=2 sum_(k>=0) (-1)^k a_k^(1-2m)/(t^2+a_k^2).    (9)

The coefficient identification follows by Taylor expansion near zero;
the finite geometric identity then establishes (9) for all real t>=0.
No convergent Taylor expansion outside |t|<pi/2 is being assumed.

Set f_k(t)=2a_k^(1-2m)/(t^2+a_k^2). For m>=1 these are strictly
decreasing positive terms, and

 f_1(t)/f_0(t)
   =3^(1-2m)(t^2+a^2)/(t^2+9a^2) <= 3^(1-2m) <= 1/3.

Therefore the elementary alternating-series bound gives

 (1-3^(1-2m))*2a^(1-2m)/(t^2+a^2)
       <= C_m(t) <= 2a^(1-2m)/(t^2+a^2).               (10)

Both inequalities are strict for t>0. In particular C_m(t)>0.
Equations (7)--(10) prove

 theta-A_(N,m)=(-1)^(N+m) epsilon_(N,m),
 epsilon_(N,m)=(1/12) integral_0^infinity
                   t^(2m+3)exp(-2Nt) C_m(t)dt >0.     (11)

This nonvanishing is unconditional and holds even under the hypothesis
that theta is rational.


3. A uniform lower bound, and the sharp raw exponential rate
-----------------------------------------------------------

The lower half of (10) implies

 epsilon_(N,m) >= (pi/18) integral_0^infinity
           (t/a)^(2m) [t^3/(t^2+a^2)] exp(-2Nt)dt.

For t>=a, (t/a)^(2m)>=1 and t^3/(t^2+a^2)>=a/2. Restricting the
integral to t>=a gives

 epsilon_(N,m) >= (pi/18)*(a/2)*exp(-2Na)/(2N)
                =pi^2 exp(-pi*N)/(144N),

which proves (3) uniformly in the completely unrestricted integer m>=1.

For m=0 the separate estimate sech(t)>=exp(-t) in (7) gives

                 R_N >= 1/[2(2N+1)^4].                (12)

Thus m=0 is also nonzero and is larger on the exponential scale.

For completeness, the exponential lower bound is not an artifact of a
coarse estimate. Let m=aN+delta_N, with delta_N bounded. Equations (10)
and (11) reduce epsilon to the first-pole integral with relative error
O(3^(1-2m)):

 epsilon_(N,m) = (1+O(3^(1-2m)))*(pi/12)
   integral_0^infinity exp(N phi(t))
       [t^3/(t^2+a^2)] (t/a)^(2delta_N) dt,
 phi(t)=2a log(t/a)-2t.

The unique maximum is at t=a, with phi(a)=-2a=-pi and
phi''(a)=-2/a. The remaining factor at a is a/2, independently of
delta_N. Laplace's method, uniform for bounded delta_N, gives

 epsilon_(N,m) ~ (pi/12)*(a/2)*sqrt(pi*a/N)*exp(-pi*N),

which is precisely (6). Standard localization proves this directly:
outside a fixed neighborhood of a the exponent has a strict gap, and
inside it the second-order Taylor term gives a Gaussian after scaling
by sqrt(N). The factors (t/a)^(2delta_N) and their local derivatives
are uniformly bounded for bounded delta_N.


4. Large primes in the ACTUAL reduced denominator
-------------------------------------------------

Let p be an odd prime with p^2>2N and p<2N. Define J=J(N,p) as the
number of positive odd integers r for which pr<2N. Multiplying S_N by
p^4 and reducing modulo p leaves exactly the denominators divisible by p:

                 p^4 S_N = chi_4(p) S_J mod p,         (13)

provided reduction of S_J is defined. Indeed every such denominator is
pr with r odd and r<p, and chi_4(pr)=chi_4(p)chi_4(r). All remaining
summands vanish after multiplication by p^4.

If p does not divide 6N then T_(N,m) is p-integral for EVERY m>=0:
its only rational denominators in (1) divide 12 times a power of 2N.
The integer Euler coefficients cause no additional denominator primes.
Consequently, if S_J is a nonzero element of F_p, then

       v_p(A_(N,m))=-4 and v_p(q_(N,m))=4              (14)

for every m>=0. This is an exact valuation of the reduced fraction,
not a factor asserted in a chosen clearing multiplier. Addition of a
p-integral correction cannot cancel the p^(-4) principal part.

Fix an integer K>=1. For all sufficiently large N, every prime in

                      2N/(2K+1) < p < 2N              (15)

has p^2>2N and 1<=J(N,p)<=K. Each S_j is a strictly positive rational
number: it is an initial alternating sum of strictly decreasing positive
terms, starting at 1. The finite list S_1,...,S_K therefore has finitely
many numerator and denominator prime divisors. For sufficiently large N
all primes in (15) avoid that finite exceptional set. Thus (14) holds for
every prime in (15) other than the prime divisors of 6N.

Let vartheta(x)=sum_(p<=x) log p. Since the sum of log p over the distinct
prime divisors of 6N is at most log(6N), we obtain, uniformly in m,

 log q_(N,m) >= 4[vartheta(2N)-vartheta(2N/(2K+1))-log(6N)]
                     - O_K(1).                        (16)

Endpoint conventions affect at most O(log N), and 2N is not an odd prime
for N>=2. The prime number theorem vartheta(x)=x+o(x) yields

 liminf_(N->infinity) inf_m log q_(N,m)/N
               >= 8[1-1/(2K+1)].                      (17)

K is arbitrary. Taking K to infinity AFTER the liminf proves (4).
There is no unproved uniformity in K: every fixed K has its own finite
threshold, which is all that the limiting argument needs.

The obstruction already follows from K=1 alone: for primes
2N/3<p<2N there is exactly one odd denominator divisible by p, so
S_J=S_1=1 and no numerator exceptions occur. That gives denominator
rate 16/3 and primitive-value rate 16/3-pi>0. The limiting argument
sharpens the proved rate to 8-pi; it is not needed merely for divergence.

In fact the proof of (4) applies to S_N+C_N for ANY rational correction
C_N whose denominator primes are confined to those dividing 6N. It does
not give a lower bound on the error of an arbitrary such correction;
the signed integral in Section 3 is essential for the theorem about (1).


5. Primitive values and all escaping parameter sequences
---------------------------------------------------------

For m>=1 combine (3) and (4). For m=0 combine (12) and (4). The errors
in these bounds are uniform in m, so they prove (5) for an arbitrary
choice m=m(N), with no size restriction on that choice.

If N is bounded and m tends to infinity, (10)--(11), restricted to any
fixed interval [b,b+1] with b>a, give

 epsilon_(N,m) >= c_(N,b)*(b/a)^(2m),  c_(N,b)>0.

This diverges uniformly over any fixed finite set of N. Since q>=1,
the primitive values also diverge. Combining this observation with (5)
proves divergence whenever N+m tends to infinity, even when N itself
does not tend to infinity.

The theorem is compatible with finitely many small primitive values.
For example N=1,m=0 gives X-1; N=2,m=0 gives 81X-80; and N=1,m=1
gives 32X-31. Their values at theta are nonzero and smaller than one.
They cannot be extrapolated to a shrinking sequence in this family.


6. A limited growing-degree corollary
-------------------------------------

All the factors (2) are primitive and nonzero at theta, and their
absolute values tend to infinity off finite subsets of the parameter
lattice. It follows that

              c = inf_(N>=1,m>=0) |P_(N,m)(theta)| > 0.    (18)

For any finite product of d such factors, repeated factors allowed,
Gauss's lemma says that the product is already a primitive integer
polynomial of degree d. Its value satisfies

                  |product P_(N,m)(theta)|^(1/d) >= c.    (19)

Thus merely multiplying these prescribed forms does not repair the
problem by increasing the degree. To see the relevance, if theta=u/v
were rational, every nonzero integer polynomial F of degree d would
satisfy |F(theta)|>=v^(-d). A sufficient polynomial irrationality
sequence would have |F(theta)|^(1/d) tending to zero. Products in (19)
cannot do this; for all v>=1/c the bound (19) alone is compatible with
the rational lower bound. This does not exclude sums, resultants, or
general determinant combinations, which need not factor in this manner.


7. Extension and limitations
-----------------------------

The same proof works for beta(s), for every fixed integer s>=2. Replace
t^3/12 by t^(s-1)/[2(s-1)!] in (7), and replace the correction coefficient
by E_(2j)(s+2j-1)!/[2(s-1)!(2j)!]. The large-prime reduced denominator
rate becomes at least 2s. Restricting the positive remainder integral to
[pi/2,pi/2+1/N] gives a uniform lower bound C_s exp(-pi*N)/N. Thus the
primitive-value lower rate is at least 2s-pi>0. This generalization is
included as a proof consequence; numerical checks below focus on s=4.

What is established:
  * exact beta(4)-only rational approximants;
  * unconditional remainder sign and nonvanishing;
  * a uniform lower bound, with sharp optimal raw exponential rate;
  * exact surviving prime valuations in primitive normalization;
  * failure of every endpoint/truncation schedule, and of products of
    its primitive factors under the degree-normalized criterion.

What is not established:
  * irrationality or rationality of beta(4);
  * an obstruction to arbitrary Euler-type transformations;
  * an obstruction to linear combinations of (1), Padé reconstruction,
    nonlinear cancellation, or determinants that do not factor;
  * the exact denominator rate for a general growing m;
  * novelty, priority, human audit, or formal verification.

-------------------------------------

  python3 collaboration/round-05/fresh/exact_checks.py

The first script uses exact rational arithmetic and independent formal
series inversion. The second constructs the correction from factorial
Laplace moments, computes theta and the tail separately with Arb Hurwitz
zeta, checks signs and bounds, and records actual reduced coefficients.
These are separate implementations by the same worker, not independent
human verification. Their precise finite coverage is in their JSON outputs.

Completed checks: 160 Euler coefficients by three exact implementations;
13 direct symbolic sech coefficients; eight finite geometric identities;
652 exact prime valuations; 43 primitive rational forms and 43 direct
Hurwitz-tail comparisons; 16 signed remainder inequalities at points
inside and outside the Taylor disk; four primitive product checks.
All checks passed. Of the 43 primitive values, exactly the three small
examples listed in Section 5 are below one. Four asymptotic ratios are
also recorded as diagnostics; they are not the proof of (6).
