ROUND 05 — FRICKE-COMPATIBLE EXACT PRIME DELETION
29 October 2026. Local and unpublished arithmetic research packet.

Outcome and exact scope
=======================

Exact prime deletion DOES transfer to the two Lambert/Eichler germs,
but a single dilation factor is incompatible with their Fricke pairing.
A quadratic factor repairs this. It is the unique minimal-degree
simultaneous deletion filter, up to a scalar. It preserves the two
period-killing moments and a beta(4)-only endpoint, at level multiplied
by p^2. Products over primes preserve the same statements.

The arithmetic gain depends on the precise target:

* For an order-n endpoint determinant, selected fourth powers are paid
  once in the endpoint scalar, while the coefficient entries are integral
  at those primes. This gives a substantive saving. An exact modular
  F-part example has v_11(kappa):24->4 at n=8.
* For a single modular coefficient form, the endpoint scalar can restore
  the same fourth-power clearing that was removed from its coefficient.
  There is no automatic improvement in that scalar-form budget.

A quartic self-dual extension is also explicit. With the SAME canonical
constant dilation coefficient 1, its scalar selected-prime bound is 3
rather than 4, but the endpoint grows by p and the level grows by another
p^2. Its determinant bound is worse, because entry denominators return.
In the same n=8,p=11 example its actual exponent is 21. No invariant
primitive-value advantage is established for the quartic extension.

An integral local modular-coordinate/prefactor construction is supplied
to make the coefficient arithmetic concrete. Its Fricke symmetry is
exact, but its new fixed point need not lie on the first inverse sheet.
No real decay, evaluated nonvanishing, or irrationality theorem is claimed.
The analytic worker and coordinator separately investigate that obstruction.

1. Input, choices, and notation
===============================

The initial choices are recorded in 00_initial_choices.txt, before
outgoing discussion with the analytic worker. Three mechanisms were
compared: outer Lambert-index deletion, full Fourier-index deletion,
and a quadratic Fricke-compatible repair. The last is the primary choice.
The coordinator independently derived the linear incompatibility and
subsequently the minimal-degree classification; the analytic worker
independently found the same quadratic repair. This is coordinated
elementary research, not a novelty claim.

Use theta=beta(4), chi=chi_4, q=exp(2pi i tau), and V_p h(tau)=h(p tau).
The inherited modular-detour.txt defines

  F(tau)=sum_(d odd>=1) chi(d) q^d/[d^4(1+q^d)],
  G(tau)=sum_(d odd>=1) q^d/[d^4(1+q^(2d))].              (1.1)

Its established period-filter statement is the input used here. If
a_r, r|M, satisfy sum a_r r=sum a_r r^3=0, put

  S=sum a_r, K=sqrt(M^3/8), b_s=K a_(M/s)/s^3,
  H=sum a_r F(r tau)+sum b_s G(s tau).

Then, with N=8M and W_N tau=-1/(N tau),

  H(W_N tau)-S theta/2
      =i/[N^(3/2)tau^3] (H(tau)-S theta/2).              (1.2)

Thus H(i/sqrt(N))=S theta/2. The starting weights are
a_1=16,a_2=-10,a_4=1, M=4, S=7, giving

  H_32=16F-10V_2F+V_4F
        +sqrt(2)[2G-(5/2)V_2G+(1/2)V_4G].               (1.3)

The arithmetic arguments below are independent of a global modular
coordinate. We use (1.2) as the previously recorded classical period
identity, not as a theorem newly independently re-proved in this round.
The word level refers to the displayed Fricke level and the underlying
modular forms; H itself is an Eichler combination. A single Fricke law
does not assert vanishing of its periods for the whole congruence group.

2. Exact physical deletion and Fourier operators
================================================

For an odd prime p, let lambda=chi(p)/p^4 and mu=1/p^4. Splitting the
outer index d according to p|d gives EXACTLY

  F^[p]=(1-lambda V_p)F
       =sum_(d odd,p not dividing d) chi(d)q^d/[d^4(1+q^d)],
  G^[p]=(1-mu V_p)G
       =sum_(d odd,p not dividing d) q^d/[d^4(1+q^(2d))]. (2.1)

Both resulting Fourier series have p-integral coefficients. Products
over different primes commute. This is an exact Lambert-index identity,
not an assumption that cyclotomic deletion commutes with an arbitrary
completed modular coordinate.

For clarity, the Fourier coefficient-selection operator U_p is different:
U_p(sum h_n q^n)=sum h_(pn)q^n. The divisor sums in (1.1) give

  U_p F=(1+lambda)F-lambda V_pF,
  U_p G=(chi(p)+mu)G-chi(p)mu V_pG.                       (2.2)

Indeed F_(p^k)=1+lambda+...+lambda^k, whereas
G_(p^k)=chi(p)^k F_(p^k); the same local recurrence holds with any
prime-to-p index attached. In particular full Fourier-index deletion is

  (1-V_pU_p)F=(1-V_p)(1-lambda V_p)F,
  (1-V_pU_p)G=(1-chi(p)V_p)(1-mu V_p)G.                  (2.3)

The F polynomial in (2.3) has value zero at V=1. In the period-filtered
construction it therefore kills the theta endpoint scalar too. This
specific full-deletion route does not retain a nonzero theta target.

3. The local Fricke classification and quadratic repair
========================================================

Take a polynomial C(V)=sum_(j=0)^r c_j V^j and enlarge M to Mp^r.
The paired G polynomial dictated by (1.2), including the change in K, is

  C^*(V)=p^(-3r/2) V^r C(p^3/V).                        (3.1)

For even r this remains over the old coefficient field Q(sqrt(2)).

THEOREM 3.1. A fixed polynomial C gives uniformly bounded p-denominators
for CF only if C(chi(p)p^4)=0, and this condition is sufficient for a
uniform bound. The analogous condition for C^*G is C(p^(-1))=0.
Consequently the minimal simultaneous degree is two, with unique
polynomial up to scalar

  C_p(V)=(1-chi(p)p^(-4)V)(1-pV).                        (3.2)

Proof. For k>=r, the coefficient at q^(p^k) in CF is

  C(1)/(1-lambda)-lambda^(k+1)C(lambda^(-1))/(1-lambda).

If the second coefficient is nonzero, its valuation tends to minus
infinity and eventually cannot cancel the constant first term. Conversely
the root condition factors out exact physical deletion (2.1), leaving
a fixed polynomial and hence a uniform denominator bound. For G the
growing local root is mu, so its condition is C^*(p^4)=0. By (3.1),
this is C(p^(-1))=0. The two roots chi(p)p^4 and p^(-1) are distinct.
This proves minimality and (3.2). QED.

With the canonical normalization C_p(0)=1, its partner is

  C_p^*(V)=chi(p)(1-p^(-4)V)(1-chi(p)pV).                (3.3)

Both CF and C^*G are p-INTEGRAL, not merely uniformly bounded: after
the respective physical deletions their remaining factors are integral.
The endpoint factor is nonzero:

  a_p=C_p(1)=(1-p)(1-chi(p)/p^4).                       (3.4)

This supersedes the two-dilation incompatibility. The latter was a
correct restriction at degree one, not a general obstruction.

For a finite nonempty prime set S and P=product_(p in S)p, apply the
product of C_p to the F part of (1.3) and the product of C_p^* to the
G part. Denote the result by H_2. Its level is

  N_2=32P^2,
  S_2=7 product_(p in S)(1-p)(1-chi(p)/p^4).              (3.5)

The original two moments remain zero, because multiplying divisor
weights multiplies each moment by the corresponding C_p(p) or C_p(p^3).
Thus (1.2) still holds with S=S_2 and N=N_2. The precise fixed-point
target is H_2(i/sqrt(N_2))=S_2 theta/2.

The same theta-only endpoint also exists at the real q cusp. Dominated
convergence gives F(q)->theta/2 as q->1 from below. The G constant
term there is independent of its dilation, and sum b_s=0 by the cubic
moment. Consequently H_2(q)->S_2 theta/2 at q=1. In particular its
RATIONAL F component alone, doubled, has endpoint S_2 theta.

4. A concrete integral formal coordinate and prefactor
=======================================================

Let t_0 and J_+ be the level-32 eta/theta series in modular-detour.txt:
t_0=q+integer higher coefficients, t_0(W_32 tau)=t_0(tau), and
J_+(W_32 tau)=-i sqrt(32)tau J_+(tau). For any integer L>1 put

  t_L=t_0(tau)+t_0(L^2 tau),
  J_L=J_+(tau)+L J_+(L^2 tau),
  Y_L=D log t_L,  A_L=J_L^5/Y_L.                         (4.1)

At N=32L^2 these satisfy

  t_L(W_N tau)=t_L(tau),
  J_L(W_N tau)=-i sqrt(N)tau J_L(tau),
  A_L(W_N tau)=-i N^(3/2)tau^3 A_L(tau).                 (4.2)

For example W_N exchanges t_0(tau) and t_0(L^2 tau); the factor L
in J_L is exactly the weight-one matching factor. Y_L transforms by
N tau^2. These statements follow directly from the old Fricke laws.

Formally t_L=q+..., with integral coefficients. Its compositional
inverse q(t_L) is integral. Also Y_L is an integral unit series, and
J_L has coefficients in Z[sqrt(2)]. Thus A_L, expressed in t_L, has
both rational components integral. Take L=P for H_2, and put B_L=A_LH_2.
The meromorphic combination B_L-(S_2 theta/2)A_L is Fricke invariant.

This is NOT a claim that t_L is a global coordinate or that its new
fixed point is the dominant branch on the Taylor germ's sheet. Nor
does mere invariance prove holomorphy at a higher-order critical point.
The new fixed point has t_L=2t_0(iL/sqrt(32)), exponentially small
in L, but the principal inverse encounters other geometry first.
The coordinator and analytic worker investigate that issue separately.

5. Exact primitive arithmetic: scalar forms versus determinants
===============================================================

For a prefix of degree N, define

  d_(N,S)=lcm{d: 1<=d<=N, d odd, gcd(d,P)=1}.              (5.1)

All Fourier coefficients of the two deleted Lambert series through N
are cleared by d_(N,S)^4. The remaining quadratic factors are integral.
The old G coefficient 1/2 is the only extra rational factor in H_2.
Therefore, after the integral coordinate and prefactor in section 4,

  a_(n,j) in Z,
  2 d_(N,S)^4 b_(n,j) in Z,       n<=N, j=0,1,            (5.2)

where A_L=sum(a_(n,0)+sqrt(2)a_(n,1))t_L^n and similarly for B_L.
There are no new denominator primes from the level or coordinate in
this explicit FORMAL construction. This says nothing about coefficient
height or analytic continuation.

The exact scalar-form target is

  L_(n,j)=S_2 a_(n,j) theta-2 b_(n,j).                    (5.3)

If it is a nonzero rational pencil, define its primitive multiplier
kappa by clearing the pair and dividing by the full integer gcd. Then

  kappa <= P^4 d_(N,S)^4,
  v_p(kappa)<=4 for every p in S.                        (5.4)

These are bounds for the actual primitive multiplier under the stated
canonical normalization, not assertions of equality or small content.
Cross-prime factors in the endpoint numerator are included automatically
when the exact pair is normalized.

For a scalar coefficient form, (5.4) need not improve the old budget.
If all selected primes lie in (sqrt(N),N], then

  P^4 d_(N,S)^4 = O_N^4,

where O_N is the odd least common multiple through N. The deleted
fourth powers have simply moved to the endpoint scalar. At n=1, for
a single selected p, the actual exponent is 4 outside explicit small
exceptional divisors of the prefactor; the script checks both character
signs p=3 and p=5. No small-denominator claim is inferred from p-integral
Fourier coefficients alone.

For a DETERMINANT the budget is different. Let h(q) be twice the rational
F part of H_2, so h(1)=S_2 theta and h_1=32 for every selected prime set.
For order n, use its prefix through N=2n and define

  b_i=h_i, T_ij=h_(i+j)-h_(i+j-1),
  J_h(X)=det[[X,-b^T],[b,T]].

The exact endpoint form is J_h(S_2 theta). Its nonzero pencil has

  kappa <= P^4 d_(2n,S)^(4(n+1)),
  v_p(kappa)<=4 for every selected p.                    (5.5)

Proof: D=d_(2n,S)^4 clears h, so the constant determinant coefficient
is cleared by D^(n+1), and the theta coefficient by P^4D^n. Multiplying
by P^4D^(n+1) clears both. Selected p's do not divide D. QED.

If S is exactly the set of odd primes y<p<=2n, the elementary bounds
log d<=pi(y)log(2n) and log P<=2n log(2n) imply

  log^+(kappa)=o(n^2) whenever pi(y_n)log n=o(n).          (5.6)

This is the substantial determinant saving: P^4 is paid ONCE rather
than n+1 times. It does not imply real decay or uniform nonvanishing.
The analytic cusp route encounters a different barrier, treated by
the analytic worker; it is not resolved by (5.6).

An exact comparison at n=8,p=11 uses this SAME h_1=32 normalization:

  unfiltered F-part determinant:          v_11(kappa)=24,
  quadratic simultaneous deletion:        v_11(kappa)= 4.

This is computed from the complete rational pairs and their gcds.
It is not an entrywise-only comparison or a pure global rescaling.

6. Quartic self-dual extension: valid, but no established advantage
==================================================================

Define

  D_p(V)=p^3+(p^4-p^3-1)V+V^2.

It satisfies p^(-3)V^2D_p(p^3/V)=D_p(V) and D_p(1)=p^4.
Thus C_pD_p and C_p^*D_p remain a Fricke-paired deletion pair, now at
level multiplied by p^4. Their endpoint is integral at p. But reporting
only this fact would be misleading: multiplying the quadratic form by
p^4 would also make its local multiplier integral without changing
its primitive pair.

To compare actual constructions, KEEP the constant F dilation coefficient
equal to 1. Use

  C_(p,4)=C_p D_p/p^3,
  C_(p,4)^*=C_p^* D_p/p^3.                               (6.1)

The resulting H_4 has

  N_4=32P^4, S_4=P S_2,
  selected-prime coefficient valuations >=-3.             (6.2)

Use L=P^2 in (4.1). The corresponding scalar coefficient forms obey

  kappa <= P^3 d_(N,S)^4, v_p(kappa)<=3.                  (6.3)

However the endpoint has grown by P, and the cleared theta scalar is
IDENTICAL before the prefactor is included:

  P^3 S_4=P^4 S_2
     =7 product_(p in S)(1-p)(p^4-chi(p)).                 (6.4)

Moreover D_p/p^4 has nonnegative coefficients summing to 1. The
endpoint-normalized germs satisfy the exact convex-dilation identity

  H_4/S_4 = product_(p in S)[D_p(V_p)/p^4] (H_2/S_2).     (6.5)

Thus the one-layer sufficient denominator reduction is accompanied
by a changed source and level, not free primitive cancellation.

For an order-n determinant, selected coefficient denominators return.
The sufficient quartic bound is

  kappa <= [P^3 d_(2n,S)^4]^(n+1),                       (6.6)

which can be much worse than (5.5). In the SAME n=8,p=11,h_1=32
comparison, the quartic actual exponent is 21, versus 4 for the
quadratic construction. This is why the degree-two repair remains
the primary candidate in this packet.

Full primitive scalar-pair checks show no automatic advantage either.
At coefficient n=1, the component-0 primitive log absolute values are

  selected primes      quadratic          quartic
  {3}                    13.30396          11.41393
  {5}                    14.43998          12.79279
  {3,5}                  17.61473          17.66996

All are large. These prescribed checks illustrate scale/content effects;
they are not an asymptotic comparison or a parameter search.

7. Normalization safeguards and concrete first-coefficient formulas
===================================================================

Multiplying a whole rational form by a nonzero rational changes kappa
inversely but leaves the primitive pair unchanged up to sign. Likewise
for endpoint determinants,

  J_(h/a)(X)=a^(-(n+1))J_h(aX).                           (7.1)

Consequently one must not count new primes from an endpoint-normalizing
scalar separately while forgetting the cancelling determinant scalar.
All finite values in this packet use full primitive normalization.

The formal first coefficients make the chosen source height explicit.
Let L=P for degree two and L=P^2 for degree four, and let e=chi(P).
For both constructions the first Lambert coefficient is unchanged
apart from the same G sign e. Direct expansion of (4.1) gives

  a_(1,0)=(1+L)^4(3062+802L),
  a_(1,1)=(1+L)^4(2036+596L),
  b_(1,0)=(1+L)^5(6416+1192e),
  b_(1,1)=(1+L)^5(4768+802e).                             (7.2)

The factor (1+L)^4 is removed by primitive normalization. The remaining
polynomials, endpoint scalar, and exact gcd must still be retained.
In particular a coefficient-integrality theorem does not control
integer coefficient height. Formulas (7.2) are checked independently
against formal inversion and composition in the script.
