ARITHMETIC TRANSFER AND AN OPTIMISTIC CERTIFICATE CEILING
30 October2026. Local and unpublished.

Scope first

The result below obstructs the DIRECT TRANSFER OF THE SOURCE'S SPECIFIED
FORWARD-TERM / DUAL-ROW VALUATION ESTIMATES, used with the established
error exponent. It is NOT an upper bound for actual common numerator
content, NOT a lower bound for actual primitive height, and NOT a proof
that every normalization of this family fails. Cancellation inside sums
or minors, a different arithmetic identity, or an additional exponential
error cancellation is outside this ceiling.

1. A uniform integer normalization

Set E_n=60n+3 v2((10n)!), ell_n=floor(log2(16n)), and O_m the odd part
of lcm(1,...,m). For n>=1,

 D_n=6*2^(E_n+ell_n)*O_(76n)^4
       clears (Q_n,P3_n,P4_n) at BOTH quarters.                    (A1)

Each variable-upper binomial has denominator supported only at2. For an
odd prime power p^r, the arithmetic progression4t+c contains at least
floor(m/p^r) multiples in any m consecutive terms, proving odd integrality
after division by m!. At2 its valuation is exactly -2m-v2(m!). Three
degree10n factors are thus cleared by2^E_n; the other binomials are
integers. The binomial derivative identity

 d/dx binom(x,m)=sum_(j=1)^m (-1)^(j-1) binom(x,m-j)/j

and the source's fixed-upper/tail factorial argument show that
2^E_n*lcm(1,...,16n) clears every Gamma-interpolation derivative B'.
This includes both simple-zero tails. The shorter-degree binomials in
the derivative identity need no larger dyadic power.

H_m^r(c/4)=4^r sum_(j<m)(4j+c)^-r has odd denominator dividing O_(4m)^r.
Here m<=19n. Combine the derivative's one LCM layer with the three
harmonic layers, and include1/2,1/3. This proves(A1). Consequently
256 D_n^2 clears the two rational coefficients of W_n. This is only a
sufficient normalization; full primitive reduction is done separately.

2. Complementary, primewise lower bounds

Fix p>3 with p^2>120n. Every relevant factorial/difference numerator is
below120n, so each reciprocal differentiation factor costs at most one
power of p. All u_n entries are p-integral, and1/256 is a p-adic unit.

Forward: let h_m indicate whether some4j+c, 0<=j<m, is divisible by p.
For interior terms put v_k=v_p(B(n,k)). Then

 F_q=min_(0<=k<=14n) v_k,
 F_4=min_k { v_k-4h_(4n+k), v'_k-3h_(4n+k) },                    (A2)

where v'_k=v_k-1 in the interior, and the exact factorial valuation
of the simple-zero derivative is used in the tails. Absent B terms
have infinite valuation. These are valid lower bounds for v_p(Q),v_p(P4).

Dual: at pole s=-alpha-j, let m_j<=3 be its order and nu_j the valuation
of its leading Laurent coefficient. Expanding the regular factors gives

 v_p(c_(j,i)) >= nu_j-(m_j-i).

The polynomial(-s)^k is p-integral. Thus valid row bounds are

 m_A=min_(m_j=3)nu_j,
 m_B=min{ min_(m_j=3)(nu_j-1), min_(m_j=2)nu_j },
 m_D=min_(j,1<=i<=m_j)[nu_j-(m_j-i)-(i+1)h_(2n+j)].

Using the EXACT closed Lambda from complete-deformation.txt gives

 G_q=v_p(Lambda)+m_A+m_B,
 G_4=v_p(Lambda)+m_B+m_D.                                        (A3)

Take max(F_q,G_q) and max(F_4,G_4) separately. For W the q bound is the
sum of the two quarter q bounds; its rational-constant bound is the
minimum of the two P4-times-Q bounds. These formulas pay for Lambda,
the two different Q sequences, and the projection. No savings are added
twice. primewise_transfer.py verifies the inequalities against exact
coefficients at modest indices and records where the dual bound improves
the forward bound. The formulas, rather than those finite diagnostics,
are the all-index proof of the bounds.

For an explicit integer representative, start with M_n=256D_n^2 from(A1).
For each p>3 with p^2>120n and p<=120n, let w_p be the smaller of the
two derived W-coefficient bounds. Then
 product_p p^max(0,v_p(M_n)+w_p)
divides both integer coefficients of M_n W_n. This constructs a uniform
common divisor from the estimates, without inspecting the actual gcd.
The four finite improvements survive the final projection: at
(n,p)=(2,19),(3,23),(4,31),(4,37), the raw W-coefficient minimum improves
from-4 to-3. See cofactor-prime-improvements.json. This is an arithmetic
gain for the beta4 forms themselves, although far too small to settle
the quantitative balance.

3. An optimistic upper ceiling for what(A2)-(A3) can certify

Discard EVERY cost associated with P4 and retain only the q coefficients.
This can only help the normalization certified by(A2)-(A3). We now upper
bound the odd-prime saving its q bounds permit.

Let x=n/p. For a=c/4 and p odd, choose l in{1,3} with lp=c mod4 and
write t=l/4. Away from the finitely many limiting walls, a shifted
Pochhammer block contributes floors with phase t. The two quarters swap
t=1/4,3/4 as p changes residue class, so their SUM is independent of
p mod4. No prime-number theorem in progressions is required below.

At k=rn, r=0,...,14, the forward-term valuation has limiting value

 f_t(x,r)=sum_(A=12,13,14)
 [floor((r+A)x-t)-floor((r+A-10)x-t)-floor(10x)]
 +floor(15x)-floor((1+r)x)-floor((14-r)x)
 +floor(15x)-floor(rx)-floor((15-r)x).

The full minimum F_q is at most min_(r=0,...,14) f_t(x,r). Selecting
these terms supplies an UPPER ceiling for the minimum; no claim is made
that this sparse list finds its actual minimum.

At the triple poles j=rn, r=3,...,9, the leading valuation is

 nu_t(x,r)=floor((16+r)x-t)-floor((2+r)x-t)
          +floor((17+r)x-t)-floor((1+r)x-t)
          -sum_(A=r,11-r,r-1,12-r,r-2,10-r) floor(Ax).

From Lambda, put
 phi_t(x)=sum_A e_A floor(Ax)+sum_A f_A floor(Ax-t),
 e={8:1,9:2,11:2,12:1,14:-1,16:-1},
 f={11:1,12:2,13:2,14:1,16:-1,17:-2,18:-2,19:-1}.

The shifted multiplicities sum to zero and all weighted multiplicities
sum to zero. There is no missing phase constant or linear term.

Because m_A<=min_r nu_t and m_B<=min_r nu_t-1, the combined q bound
from(A2)-(A3) is at most

 U_t(x)=max{ min_(r=0,...,14) f_t(x,r),
              phi_t(x)+2 min_(r=3,...,9)nu_t(x,r)-1 }.              (A4)

This intentionally ignores P4, omits many summands/poles from the minima,
and permits separate per-prime choices of representation. Every choice
makes it an optimistic ceiling for the specified certificate.

4. Exact floor integration and passage to asymptotic densities

Every function in(A4) is periodic of period1: expanding x->x+1 cancels
the integer slope shifts. Its only breakpoints in(0,1) are among
(4m+l)/(4d), d=1,...,28, l=0,1,3. The explicit union has854 open cells.
On each cell every floor is constant, so one rational midpoint determines
the whole cell exactly. Additional one-third/period tests are diagnostics.

optimistic_arithmetic_gate.py integrates U_t(x)/x^2 through40 using exact
rationals. Both functions vanish near0. Their periodic essential maxima
are9 and11, so the remaining limiting integrals cost at most9/40,11/40.
It proves

 integral U_(1/4)(x)/x^2 dx <53.694,
 integral U_(3/4)(x)/x^2 dx <34.514,
 combined upper ceiling =88.20752949730584... .                    (A5)

To justify this as a ceiling for the FINITE estimates, first restrict
x to a fixed bounded interval away from the rational walls. The O(1/p)
offsets then preserve every selected-term floor, uniformly as n grows.
The PNT gives the integrals on those intervals. Neighborhoods of the
finite set of walls have arbitrarily small limiting contribution because
the finite one-level exponents are uniformly bounded. One may use32
as a bound for each quarter: the selected forward binomial valuation is
at most5; the selected triple-pole leading valuation is at most8, and
the balanced Lambda valuation is at most12. Their dual sum is at most27.
Thus the omitted tail p<n/X has upper contribution at most64n/X+o(n).
The primes below sqrt(120n) contribute o(n): each balanced odd-prime
valuation has O(log n) layers, with uniformly bounded contribution per
layer. These bounds also handle thin shifted walls in the tail.
Taking n->infinity, shrinking fixed wall neighborhoods, then X->infinity
proves the limsup ceiling by the full limiting integral. The last step
allows the sharper periodic tail bound in(A5). No finite-prime sample or
unverified wall-offset enumeration is used as an asymptotic proof.

5. The dyadic cost and quantitative shortfall

For the specified forward q bound, each summand has valuation

 -60n-3v2((10n)!)+O(log n)=-90n+O(log n),

uniformly in k; the two ordinary binomials cost only O(log n) carries.
For the dual q bound, a selected triple leading coefficient has valuation
-90n+O(log n), Lambda has valuation+90n+O(log n), and the derivative,
monomial and endpoint losses are O(log n). Thus the direct dual-row
estimate has the SAME dyadic exponent. Taking the better of the two
specified bounds does not change it. For the product coefficient, the
certified dyadic normalization therefore costs180 log2 per index.

The established relative error exponent is g=112.807539... and the q
growth exponent is sigma=65.451536.... Even after all P4 costs have
been forgiven, success using these estimates would require odd content
density greater than

 2sigma+180log2-g=142.8620... .                                   (A6)

But(A5) is below88.208. deformed_rates.py verifies the inequality with
Arb intervals: the shortfall is greater than54.65 per index. Equivalently,
these q estimates cannot certify a product height exponent below about
167.46, whereas the available relative error exponent is112.81.

This is NOT a claim that the errors grow at that rate, nor that the
actual primitive height has that lower bound. It limits what this
particular collection of lower valuation estimates can certify.
In particular, higher divisibility of the SUM Q, common content exposed
by a new identity, a cheaper character projection, or faster projected
decay could evade it. None is supplied by the completed transfer.

6. Primitive diagnostics and stopping decision

primitive_gate.py clears the EXACT two coefficients of W and divides
their full gcd. It certifies nonzero errors with Arb, without using beta4
to choose the family. The prescribed n=1,2,3,4,6 have log absolute
primitive errors approximately326.81,710.46,1081.43,1472.72,2233.45.
These finite values are unfavorable evidence, not an asymptotic theorem.

The complete paired identities genuinely repair round12's equality gap.
The transferred arithmetic mechanism, however, misses even the generous
q-only test by a large margin. No large parameter scan or complete
P4-density optimization is warranted by this construction. The bounded
phase should end after independent checking of this conclusion, with the
new identities retained and the above limitations explicit.
