Conclusion

I found no correction needed in the completed deformed certificate
definitions, boundary transfer, shifted valuation phases, selected-term
inequality directions, dyadic exponent, breakpoint grid, or PNT/tail
argument. The numerical ceiling and shortfall reproduce exactly.

The negative conclusion is valid in its stated restricted sense: the
specified forward-term/dual-row lower valuation estimates, combined with
the established upper error bound, do not certify a useful integer-form
estimate. This is not a bound on the actual primitive height or gcd, and
does not prove asymptotic growth or nonvanishing of the projected errors.
The current complete-deformation.txt, arithmetic-gate.txt, and findings.txt
preserve these distinctions. No numerical period independence is used.

solver to assert its own answer. All five residuals are zero at each

The forward recurrence conversion uses the correct C_(n+1)/C_n, including
the shifted factors 12,13,14 in its numerator and 2,3,4 in its denominator,
and the unshifted 15,10,1,14 factorial factors. The descending-shift
coefficient convention matches the companion's last column. The dual
recurrence has no forward scalar normalization. Both independent rebuilt
recurrences equal the saved ones exactly.

The forward Gamma summand has positive shifted factors at the finite
summation boundaries. Dividing it by the displayed K(n)D(n,k) gives
h(k+1)/h(k)=A(k)/B_-(k+1), exactly with the alpha shifts in fproblem.
At k=0 the B_- factor vanishes and h is finite. At k=14n+43 the
reciprocal Gamma factor with argument zero vanishes; the remaining
factors are finite. This sums the scalar recurrence at every n>=0.

For the dual, an explicit continuation consistent with the rebuilt
equations is, with a=1+alpha,

 T_alpha(n,k)=exp(i*pi*k) Gamma(16n+k+a) Gamma(17n+k+a)
  /[Gamma(2n+k+a) Gamma(n+k+a) Gamma(k+1)
    Gamma(11n-k+1) Gamma(k-n+1) Gamma(12n-k+1)

It obeys

 T_alpha(n,-s-alpha)
  =exp(-i*pi*(s+alpha)) sin(pi*(s+alpha))^3 R_n^alpha(s)/pi^3.

The multiplier is 1-periodic. The row weights must therefore be
(k+alpha)^j, and power(j,alpha) implements those weights exactly.
The dproblem and dshift products reproduce this Gamma ratio and its
index shifts. There is no extra n-dependent reflection sign.

2. Transfer boundaries: different substitutions must be retained

For the forward H kernel the reflection multiplier is pi^2/sin^2(pi*s),
so the certificate uses k=1-s. The sampling boundary is s=12n+alpha,
which explains the code's k=1-12n-alpha. These are compatible statements;
one must not replace the forward substitution by k=1-s-alpha.

For n>=2, all four shifted-index forward derivative tails start at
m=12n on the grid s=m+alpha. Their omitted terms lie in the common
triple-zero ranges. The certificate contains H_n(s-1), which has a
triple zero at this lower boundary. Its denominator is nonzero there.
The degree is at most zero, so its expansion at infinity is a constant
plus O(1/s), whose first and second derivatives vanish at infinity.
Local uniform convergence of the differentiated rational tails is enough
to pass derivatives through the sums.

For the dual the substitution is instead k=1-s-alpha. The potentially
extra positive integer poles from D_1 are at n+2, 2n+2, and 2n+3.
For n>=1, R_n(s-1) has the required zeros at all three points, and these
factors cancel. Every remaining finite pole lies in -alpha+Z.
Summing Laurent coefficients of each pole order is therefore invariant
under translation s -> s+1. This proves the A and B row recurrences
separately, without using independence of numerical zeta values.

The two dual differentiated tails can start at integer m=16n for n>=1.
All omitted terms are in the common double-zero blocks. The certificate
contains R_n(s-1), which has a double zero at s=16n, and its denominator
does not vanish there. Its degree is at most zero, so its derivative
vanishes at infinity. Subtracting the already proved A and B recurrences
then gives the D recurrence. The initial simple pole is retained.

I independently rechecked the lower-denominator nonvanishing tests and
the degree bounds from the saved polynomials. Using the separate

All pass at both quarters. These complete the initial-data and boundary
requirements for the all-index identifications in this construction.

3. Exact shifted valuation phase and the directions of the bounds

For p>3, c in {1,3}, let l in {1,3} satisfy lp=c mod 4, and put t=l/4.
The unique residue in [0,p-1] for which 4j+c is divisible by p is
j0=(lp-c)/4. Hence the exact count for a block of length L is

 #{0<=j<L : p divides 4j+c}
   =floor(L/p-t+(c/4-1)/p)+1.                         (F1)

The formula includes L=0. In the one-level range this is precisely
v_p((c/4)_L); at higher levels the same count is applied to p^r with
the appropriate residue phase. The independent script checks 2,208
literal finite counts as diagnostics of the algebraic formula.

Applying (F1) to interval products gives the stated f_t and nu_t with
the MINUS phase. For a selected triple pole j=rn, the six denominator
factorial lengths are

 rn, (11-r)n, (r-1)n, (12-r)n, (r-2)n, (10-r)n+1.

Thus the limiting formula drops only the last +1 offset. All finite
offsets remain O(1/p) and are treated separately at walls. The two
quarter phases exchange as p changes between 1 and 3 mod 4.

For Lambda, sum(SE)=0, sum_A A*IE_A=30, and sum_A A*SE_A=-30.
The +1 constants in (F1) cancel, as do all linear slopes. This proves
the stated phi_t with no omitted phase constant or linear term.

The inequality directions are correct:
 - The minimum over all forward terms is at most the minimum over
   k=rn, r=0,...,14.
 - m_A is at most the selected triple-pole minimum, and m_B is at most
   that minimum minus 1. Therefore their sum is at most twice that
   minimum minus 1.
 - Taking the maximum of these two upper bounds still bounds above
   the maximum of the two specified lower valuation estimates.

This yields exactly U_t. It is a ceiling on the ESTIMATES, not on the
valuation of Q itself. Discarding the P4 constraints can only make that
ceiling more optimistic. The explicit divisor paragraph added after
(A3) is also valid: integrality supplies the zero valuation lower bound,
so max(0,v_p(M_n)+w_p) is a legitimate common-divisor exponent.

4. Complete grid, exact integral, and the PNT limit

Every selected expression is a sum of floors d*x-t, with positive
integer d<=28 and t in {0,1/4,3/4}; zero slopes are harmless constants.
The jumps lie at (m+t)/d. The 854-cell grid is therefore a complete
refinement. I independently generated the grid from the slopes actually
present, obtaining 720 cells. It is contained in the coordinator's grid
and gives EXACTLY the same rational integrals and upper bounds.

Period one follows separately for each term from cancellation of its
integer slopes, not merely from sample tests. Both U functions vanish
near zero, are nonnegative, and have essential maxima 9 and 11. The
integrals through 40 plus (9+11)/40 give

 combined upper ceiling = 88.207529497305839689734595508644931... .

The finite-estimate passage is sound. On a fixed compact x interval
away from the finite rational wall set, all O(1/p) offsets in (F1) and
the factorial arguments preserve their floors for sufficiently large n.
Adding the two quarters removes the dependence on p mod 4. Ordinary
PNT for the weighted prime sum then gives the integral against dx/x^2;
PNT in arithmetic progressions is unnecessary.

Fixed wall neighborhoods are handled before shrinking them. The finite
selected forward valuation is at most 5. The selected triple-pole
leading valuation and balanced Lambda valuation have uniformly bounded
one-level contributions; the stated bounds 8 and 12 are conservative.
Thus 32 per quarter dominates the finite ceilings, including offsets
at walls. The neighborhoods' weighted contribution can be made
arbitrarily small by PNT. For p<n/X above the one-level cutoff, the
combined bound is at most 64*theta(n/X), giving 64n/X+o(n).
The primes below sqrt(120n) contribute o(n), because the selected
balanced valuations have only O(log n) bounded layers per prime.

The order of limits is therefore important and correct: first n tends
to infinity on fixed intervals, then wall neighborhoods shrink, then
X tends to infinity. This proves the limsup bound by the full limiting
integral. Only AFTER that passage is it legitimate to bound the remaining
limiting integral by the sharper periodic maxima 9/40 and 11/40.
Possible finite-prime wall exceptions are not silently bounded by those
essential maxima.

5. Dyadic exponent and analytic input

For every forward interior summand, exactly

 v2(B(n,k))=-90n+3*s2(10n)
             +v2(binom(15n,n+k))+v2(binom(15n,k)),

where s2 is binary digit sum. The two binomial carry counts are uniformly
O(log n), so the forward minimum is -90n+O(log n).

At a selected triple pole j=rn, its leading Laurent coefficient has

 v2(leading)=-90n-1+sum s2(L),

with the six factorial lengths L listed in Section 3. The numerator has
30n odd-quarter factors, of total valuation -60n. Also exactly

 v2(Lambda_n)=90n-1-sum_A IE_A*s2(An).

Derivative and coordinate losses are O(log n); the endpoint's dyadic
denominators have bounded order. Thus the specified dual-row q bound
has the same -90n+O(log n) exponent. Taking the better bound leaves the
product certificate's dyadic cost at 180 log 2 per index. This says
nothing about extra dyadic cancellation in the actual Q sums.

6. Interpretation, remaining scope, and reproduction

These inequalities compare a ceiling on what the specified arithmetic
estimates certify with an UPPER bound on relative error. They establish
that this combination of estimates is quantitatively inadequate. They
do not turn the upper error estimate into a matching lower estimate.
No optimal error exponent or eventual nonvanishing of the character
projection is used. The reported absence of such a nonvanishing theorem
is appropriate. Extra cancellation in the projected error, or stronger
content of the actual sums/minors, remains outside this assessment.

This audit did not re-evaluate the five primitive Arb diagnostics or all
442 prime diagnostics, and it does not independently certify the entire
source paper, source archive, or formal developments. Their values are
not needed for the certificate ceiling. The mathematical constructions,
all-index identities audited here, and finite tests are distinguished
from those other claims.
