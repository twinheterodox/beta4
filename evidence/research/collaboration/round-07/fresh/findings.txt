Third derivatives of continued-fraction convergents: exact eighth prime layers
============================================================================
29 October 2026. Local and unpublished. Bounded closing packet for round 07.

STATUS AND SCOPE

No irrationality proof of theta=beta(4) is obtained. This packet constructs
rational approximants from third parameter derivatives of a continued
fraction, proves an exact local law for their ACTUAL reduced denominators,
and excludes every fixed-tail-endpoint subfamily at even depth by an eventual
lower bound on actual primitive values. It does not exclude growing endpoints
N=N(n), other combinations of the convergents, or other continued fractions.

The result is not inferred from a large sufficient clearing multiplier.
For each admissible prime below, the exact exponent in the reduced rational
approximant is proved. The real error has a proved nonzero asymptotic; it is
not replaced by an absolute contour upper bound. No formal verification,
human mathematical audit, or claim of priority is made. The requested
bounded model cross-review is separate from this author's proof.

For each fixed integer N>=1, let alpha_(n,N) be defined in (5) below, and
write it uniquely as b_(n,N)/d_(n,N) with gcd(b,d)=1 and d>0. Then

 theta-alpha_(n,N)
   ~ (-1)^(N+n) K_N n^(-2N)(log n)^3,

 K_N=2^(-2N) Gamma(N+1/2)^2/12
    =pi (2N)!^2/[12*64^N*(N!)^2] >0.                    (T1)

Along even n=2m, its actual primitive polynomial is

 P_(2m,N)(X)=d_(2m,N)X-b_(2m,N),

and

 liminf_(m->infinity) log|P_(2m,N)(theta)|/(2m) >= 4.   (T2)

In particular every fixed-N even-depth family has eventually nonzero,
exponentially growing primitive values. The rational approximants themselves
still converge to theta. The theorem does not assert that all finite-depth
errors have the asymptotic sign, or give an explicit eventual-sign threshold.

2. Exact recurrence and an elementary error identity

For Re(a)>0 put

 S(a)=integral_0^infinity exp(-a t)sech(t) dt.

Define normalized polynomial continuants q_n(a),r_n(a) by

 q_0=1, q_1=a, r_0=0, r_1=1,
 (n+1)q_(n+1)=a q_n+n q_(n-1),
 (n+1)r_(n+1)=a r_n+n r_(n-1),  n>=1.                 (1)

Thus Q_n=n!q_n and R_n=n!r_n are integer polynomials satisfying

 Q_(n+1)=a Q_n+n^2 Q_(n-1),
 R_(n+1)=a R_n+n^2 R_(n-1).

Their quotient r_n/q_n=R_n/Q_n is the n-th convergent of
1/(a+1^2/(a+2^2/(a+3^2/(...)))). The construction does not rely on a
separate continued-fraction convergence theorem. All coefficients of Q_n
are nonnegative, and Q_n(a)>0 for real a>0.

The exact identity is

 q_n(a)S(a)-r_n(a)=(-1)^n I_n(a),
 I_n(a)=integral_0^infinity exp(-a t)sech(t)tanh(t)^n dt. (2)

Proof. The recurrence gives the ordinary generating functions

 Q(a,z)=sum_(n>=0)q_n(a)z^n
       =(1-z^2)^(-1/2) exp(a atanh z)
       =(1+z)^((a-1)/2)(1-z)^(-(a+1)/2),

 V(a,z)=sum_(n>=0)r_n(a)z^n
       =Q(a,z) integral_0^z [dt/((1-t^2)Q(a,t))].

For real 0<=z<1, substitution t=tanh(u) gives

 S(a)=integral_0^1 [dt/((1-t^2)Q(a,t))].

Writing z=tanh(u), translating the remaining integral by u, and simplifying
cosh(u)/cosh(u+v) gives

 Q(a,z)S(a)-V(a,z)
   =integral_0^infinity exp(-a v)/[cosh(v)+z sinh(v)] dv.

Expansion in z, justified on compact subdisks |z|<1, gives (2). These
identities extend holomorphically in a to Re(a)>0. QED.

Let

 T_N=sum_(k=0)^(N-1) (-1)^k/(2k+1)^4.

The Laplace representation of the alternating tail gives

 theta=T_N+(-1)^(N+1) S'''(2N)/12.                     (3)

Indeed -S'''(2N)/12 is the positive integral
(1/12) integral t^3 exp(-2Nt)sech(t)dt. Differentiating (2) three times
is legitimate on a complex neighborhood of each positive a. Therefore

 theta-alpha_(n,N)
   =(-1)^(N+n+1)/12 * (d/da)^3[I_n(a)/q_n(a)]_(a=2N), (4)

where the rational approximant is

 alpha_(n,N)=T_N+(-1)^(N+1)/12
                       * (d/da)^3[r_n(a)/q_n(a)]_(a=2N). (5)

No Catalan constant, powers of pi, or other auxiliary periods occur in (5).
The complete rational quotient is differentiated, rather than differentiating
only its numerator or treating q_n as constant.


3. Fixed-N actual error, constant, and sign

Uniformly on a sufficiently small fixed complex disk about a=2N, ordinary
endpoint asymptotics give

 q_n(a)=2^((a-1)/2) n^((a-1)/2)/Gamma((a+1)/2)
                * [1+O_N(1/n)],

 I_n(a)=2^(-(a+1)/2) Gamma((a+1)/2) n^(-(a+1)/2)
                * [1+O_N(1/n)].                       (6)

Here the disk can be chosen with Re(a)>1. For q_n, expand its displayed
generating function at z=1. The next local term has relative order 1/n;
the contribution at z=-1 has relative size O(n^(-Re(a))) and is smaller.
The standard coefficient formula for (1-z)^(-b), equivalently the gamma
ratio formula, establishes the leading constant. The resulting error is
holomorphic and uniformly O(1/n) on a slightly larger disk.

For I_n, put y=tanh(t) to obtain the real beta-type integral

 I_n(a)=integral_0^1 y^n(1-y)^((a-1)/2)
                           (1+y)^(-(a+1)/2) dy.

Expand the last factor about y=1. Its first omitted term contributes an
extra 1/n relative to the leading beta integral, uniformly on the same
complex disk. Gamma-ratio asymptotics yield the second formula in (6).
This also supplies a holomorphic uniform remainder. The leading q_n factor
is nonzero on the disk, so its uniform asymptotic makes q_n zero-free there
for all sufficiently large n. These standard local arguments justify
differentiating the asymptotics by Cauchy's formula;
pointwise real asymptotics alone would not suffice. The standard gamma-ratio
expansions are recorded in NIST DLMF 5.11(iii), equations 5.11.12--13:
https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.11#iii . The compact complex-disk argument above
supplies the local uniformity used here; no growing-N uniformity is claimed.

Consequently, with C(a)=2^(-a)Gamma((a+1)/2)^2,

 I_n(a)/q_n(a)=C(a)n^(-a)[1+O_N(1/n)],

 (d/da)^3[I_n(a)/q_n(a)]_(a=2N)
   =-C(2N)n^(-2N)(log n)^3[1+O_N(1/log n)].             (7)

Substitution in (4) proves (T1), including the factor 1/12 and sign
(-1)^(N+n). This proves nonvanishing for all sufficiently large n for
each fixed N. No uniformity for growing N is asserted.


4. Two explicit integer polynomials

For an integer N>=1 define, using empty products equal to one,

 A_N(X)=sum_(j=0)^N binom(2N,2j) 2^j X_(falling j)
                          product_(ell=0)^(N-j-1)(2X+2ell+1),

 B_N(X)=sum_(j=0)^(N-1) binom(2N,2j+1) 2^j X_(falling j)
                          product_(ell=0)^(N-j-1)(2X+2ell+1),

 C_N(m)=B_N(m-1/2)
       =sum_(j=0)^(N-1) binom(2N,2j+1)
          product_(s=0)^(j-1)(2m-1-2s)
          product_(ell=0)^(N-j-1)(2m+2ell).              (8)

A_N and B_N are integer polynomials. The second expression proves that
C_N(m) is an integer for integer m despite its half-integer argument.
They have degree N. For fixed N and m>=N, C_N(m)>0 and
log C_N(m)=O_N(log m).

Writing c_m=binom(2m,m)/4^m and h_N=(2N-1)!!, one has

 q_(2m)(2N)=c_m A_N(m)/h_N,
 q_(2r+1)(2N)=c_r B_N(r)/h_N.                           (9)

Proof. At a=2N the generating function becomes

 Q(2N,z)=(1+z)^(2N)(1-z^2)^(-N-1/2).

Separate the even and odd coefficients and use

 [(N+1/2)_(m-j)/(m-j)!]/c_m
       =m_(falling j)(m+1/2)_(N-j)/(1/2)_N.

Terms with m<j vanish by the falling factorial. This yields (9). QED.

For example A_1(m)=4m+1, C_1(m)=4m, and
A_2(m)=(32m^2+16m+3), C_2(m)=8m(4m+1).
The factor h_2=3 remains outside A_2 in (9).


5. Exact denominator theorem

THEOREM. Let m,N>=1 and let p be an odd prime satisfying

 m<p<2m,   2N<p,   p does not divide C_N(m).             (10)

For alpha_(2m,N)=b/d reduced as above,

 v_p(d)=8+4 v_p(A_N(m)).                                (11)

This is an equality for the actual reduced denominator. Primes excluded
by C_N(m) are not claimed to have this exponent. No assertion is made for
p outside (10), including p dividing h_N or a finite-tail denominator.

Proof step 1: the continuant polynomial modulo p.
For every odd prime p,

 Q_p(a)=a^p-a modulo p.                                (12)

An elementary proof uses the operator

 D=(1-z^2)d/dz+(p-1)z

on polynomials of degree at most p-1 over F_p. Its tridiagonal matrix has
adjacent off-diagonal products j(p-j). Leading principal characteristic
determinants therefore obey the recurrence with +j^2 modulo p, so its
characteristic polynomial is Q_p(a). The p polynomials

 (1+z)^j(1-z)^(p-1-j),  0<=j<p,

are eigenvectors with distinct eigenvalues 2j-(p-1), which run through F_p.
Thus its characteristic polynomial is product_(u in F_p)(a-u)=a^p-a.
This proves (12) without guessing a finite sequence of congruences.

For 0<=j<p, the same recurrence then gives

 Q_(p+j)(a)=(a^p-a)Q_j(a) modulo p.                     (13)

At a=2N, differentiation yields

 Q'_(p+j)(2N)=-Q_j(2N) modulo p.                       (14)

Proof step 2: units in the numerator and derivative.
The exact continuant Wronskian is

 Q_k R_(k+1)-Q_(k+1)R_k=(-1)^k(k!)^2.                 (15)

At k=p-1 and a=2N, (12) and Wilson's theorem give
Q_(p-1)(2N) R_p(2N)=1 modulo p. Thus R_p(2N) is a unit.
The recurrence after index p gives

 R_(p+j)(2N)=R_p(2N)Q_j(2N) modulo p.                   (16)

Now j=2m-p is odd and 0<j<p. Put r=(j-1)/2. By (9),
Q_j(2N) is a p-unit precisely when B_N(r) is, because j!, c_r, and h_N
are all p-units under (10). Since r=m-(p+1)/2,

 B_N(r)=B_N(m-1/2)=C_N(m) modulo p.

Therefore (10), (14), and (16) imply

 v_p(Q'_(2m)(2N))=v_p(R_(2m)(2N))=0.                  (17)

Proof step 3: the exact zero depth of the denominator.
In (9), v_p((2m)!)=1 and v_p(c_m)=1 for m<p<2m. Also h_N is a unit.
Consequently

 e:=v_p(Q_(2m)(2N))=2+v_p(A_N(m)).                     (18)

In particular e>=2; A_N(m)>0 in the range (10).

Proof step 4: third quotient derivatives and primitive normalization.
Expand the INTEGER polynomials at a=2N as

 Q_(2m)(2N+x)=u_0+u_1 x+u_2 x^2+u_3 x^3+...,
 R_(2m)(2N+x)=v_0+v_1 x+v_2 x^2+v_3 x^3+....

The coefficient of x^3 in R/Q is

 [v_3 u_0^3-v_2 u_1 u_0^2
   +v_1(u_1^2 u_0-u_2 u_0^2)
   +v_0(-u_1^3+2u_1u_2u_0-u_3u_0^2)]/u_0^4.           (19)

By (17)-(18), the unique p-unit term of its numerator is -v_0 u_1^3.
Thus this Taylor coefficient has valuation exactly -4e. Formula (5)
multiplies the coefficient by (-1)^(N+1)/2, a p-unit, since its third
derivative is 6 times its third Taylor coefficient. The finite sum T_N
is p-integral because p>2N. It cannot cancel a negative valuation.
Therefore v_p(alpha_(2m,N))=-4e. Reducing the rational alpha gives
v_p(d)=4e, proving (11). QED.

This calculation tracks true content: any common factors of a displayed
source numerator and denominator have already been removed by the unique
valuation of (19). It does not merely show that Q^4 clears a denominator.


6. Actual primitive growth at every fixed endpoint

Fix N. For sufficiently large m, p>m implies p>2N. The product of all
primes in (m,2m) that divide the nonzero integer C_N(m) divides C_N(m).
Its total logarithm is therefore at most log C_N(m)=O_N(log m).
By (11),

 log d_(2m,N)
   >=8 sum_(m<p<2m, p not dividing C_N(m)) log p
   >=8[theta_cheb(2m)-theta_cheb(m)]-O_N(log m)
   =8m+o(m),                                           (20)

using the prime number theorem in its Chebyshev-theta form. A primary
author source is D. Zagier, "Newman's short proof of the prime number
theorem" (1997), p.707, step VI, which proves theta_cheb(x)~x:
https://people.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/zagier/files/doi/10.2307/2975232/fulltext.pdf .
The omission of endpoints affects only O(log m). Additional factors A_N(m) in (11)
can only increase this lower bound.

Equation (T1) gives log|theta-alpha_(2m,N)|=O_N(log m), with a nonzero
error for all sufficiently large m. Since P=dX-b is primitive,

 log|P_(2m,N)(theta)|
   =log d_(2m,N)+log|theta-alpha_(2m,N)|
   >=8m+o(m).

This proves (T2). It is an obstruction to the specified fixed-N even-depth
families. It uses neither an assumed irrationality of theta nor a merely
sufficient arithmetic multiplier.


7. A cancellation repair that collapses to an archived scalar family

A tempting repair is to avoid differentiating a quotient by combining
third derivatives of (2) at the shifts a=2(L+j), 0<=j<=n, with positive
binomial weights binom(n,j). The shift identity

 S(a+2N)=(-1)^N S(a)
          +2 sum_(k=0)^(N-1)(-1)^(N-1-k)/(a+2k+1)

reduces the period coefficients to finite differences of q_n and its
first three derivatives. Because q_n has degree n and leading coefficient
1/n!, the n-th difference kills every lower derivative and leaves the
nonzero theta coefficient. However the error integral simplifies exactly:

 tanh(t)^n exp(-2Lt)(1+exp(-2t))^n
       =exp(-2Lt)(1-exp(-2t))^n.

After x=exp(-t), this is the familiar scalar Mellin polynomial weight
x^(2L)(1-x^2)^n. Thus this repair supplies no distinct construction; it is
recorded to prevent counting a rational rescaling of old forms as progress.
