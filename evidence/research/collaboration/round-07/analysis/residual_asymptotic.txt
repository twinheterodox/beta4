ACTUAL SIGNED ERROR OF THE SYMMETRIC CIRCULAR HERMITE SOURCE
2026-10-29. Round07 analytic work. Local and unpublished.

Theorem. For all sufficiently large n, take the unique circular Hermite
source of rank_asymptotic.txt and normalize its target coefficient to
A=1. Thus G=R/(1+x^2)^n, G(0)=0, deg R<=2n, and

  Q=1/(1+x^2)+E^4 G/x,   E=x d/dx,

has zeros of order at least n at 0 and 1. Put

  epsilon_n=beta(4)+G(1),   rho=(sqrt(2)-1)/2,
  h=log(1+sqrt(2)),        kappa=2+sqrt(2),
  C=pi*sqrt(pi)/48 * kappa^(7/2) * (sqrt(2)-1) * h^3.

Then C>0, numerically C=2.4193871616202196119..., and

  epsilon_n ~ (-1)^n C n^(5/2) (log n)^2 rho^n.          (T)

In particular the actual errors are nonzero and have alternating sign
for all sufficiently large n. This is a two-sided asymptotic for the
actual signed error, with all lower periods eliminated exactly. It is
not an estimate of a positive majorant. It does not assert positivity
at every index, or control the primitive integer target coefficient.

If a_n X+b_n is the primitive integer endpoint pair with a_n>0, then

  log|a_n beta(4)+b_n|
    =log a_n+n log rho+(5/2)log n+2loglog n+log C+o(1).   (U)

Thus the exact remaining arithmetic threshold is log(1/rho)
=log(2(1+sqrt(2))) per n. A limsup of log a_n/n strictly below that
threshold would prove shrinking integer forms; a liminf strictly above
it would prove growth for this family. Neither height conclusion is
assumed or established in this analytic note.

1. A determinant for the actual error, without auxiliary periods

Write

  F(x)=sum_(j>=1 odd) chi_4(j)x^j/j^4
      =x/6 integral_0^1 log^3(1/u)/(1+x^2u^2) du,
  D(x)=1+x^2,
  R_low(x)=-trunc_(degree<=n)[D(x)^n F(x)],
  S_n(x)=2^(-n)x^(-n)[D(x)^n F(x)+R_low(x)].             (1)

The series defines F near 0 and the integral continues it near [0,1].
In particular F(1)=beta(4), and E^4 F=x/D(x). The origin endpoint
condition says exactly that R-R_low is in the span of
x^(n+1),...,x^(2n).

Let W(t)=G(exp t)+F(exp t). The endpoint at x=1 says

  W(t)=sum_(k=0)^3 p_k t^k/k!+O(t^(n+4)),
  p_0=epsilon_n.                                      (2)

Since cosh(t)^n=2^(-n)exp(-nt)D(exp t)^n,

  cosh(t)^n W(t)-S_n(exp t)
       is in span{exp(t),...,exp(nt)}.

Every operator L_(n+r)(d/dt), r=0,...,3, kills this last span.
Its order is n+r<=n+3, so (2) suffices to evaluate its value at 0.
Using exactly the row replacement and factorial normalization from
rank_asymptotic.txt, this gives

  C_n p=e_n,
  e_n(r)=[z^(n+r)] (1+z)^(-1) S_n(1+z).               (3)

Here C_n is precisely the contour matrix in that note, not an
independently rescaled matrix. Therefore

  epsilon_n=det C_n[column0 replaced by e_n]/det C_n.  (4)

Equations (1)-(4) establish the actual residual identity directly.
The other p_k can contain beta(3), beta(2), beta(1), but Cramer's rule
for p_0 eliminates them identically. No linear independence assumption
on any period is used. Eventual invertibility is supplied by the
separate proved rank theorem.

2. Exact exterior-residue integral for the residual column

For 0<u<=1 put f_u(x)=x/(1+u^2x^2) and

  T_u(x)=D(x)^n f_u(x)-trunc_(degree<=n)[D(x)^n f_u(x)].

For fixed u, the function x^(-n-1) T_u(x) is analytic at x=0.
The coefficient in (3) is consequently computed by a contour about
x=1, and this contour can be enlarged past x=0. Its only other poles
are x_+=i/u and x_-=-i/u. Its coefficient integrand is

  2^(-n) T_u(x)/[x^(n+1)(x-1)^(n+r+1)].               (5)

It is O(x^(-r-3)) at infinity, so the coefficient is minus the sum of
the two exterior residues. At x=x_+ or x_-,

  Res f_u(x) dx=1/(2u^2),
  Phi(x-1)=D(x)/[2x(x-1)].

The conjugate residue sum gives the exact formula

  e_n(r)=-2^(-n)/6 integral_0^1 log^3(1/u) u^r(1-u^2)^n
       Im[i^(-r-1)(1+iu)^(-n-r-1)] du,
                                       r=0,1,2,3.     (6)

Finite Taylor coefficient extraction commutes with the u integral,
because its derivatives near x=1 are bounded by a constant times
log^3(1/u), which is integrable. The residue formula extends continuously
to u=0 after summing the conjugate residues; no contour at u=0 is used.
In particular no unproved polylogarithm continuation or logarithm-sheet
choice occurs in (6).

3. Turning the oscillatory endpoint into an ordinary Laplace integral

Define q(u)=(1-u^2)/(1+iu). Formula (6) is the negative imaginary part
of an integral with analytic factor

  i^(-r-1) u^r (1+iu)^(-r-1) q(u)^n log^3(1/u).

Fix delta=1/10. Deform only u in [0,delta] to the segment
u=-iv, 0<=v<=delta, followed by the quarter-circle from -i delta to
delta in the fourth quadrant. The logarithm is continued there with
arg(u) in [-pi/2,0]. The small circle about 0 has vanishing integral,
since u^r log^3(u) is integrable for every r>=0. The pole u=i lies
outside this deformation.

The connector and the remaining real interval contribute O(2^(-n) eta^n)
for some fixed eta<1. Here is a fully explicit modulus check. On the
quarter-circle write u=a-ib, a,b>=0, a^2+b^2=delta^2. Then

  |1+iu|^2-|1-u^2|^2
    =2b+3delta^2-4b^2-delta^4>0.

The quadratic is concave in b, so positivity follows from its positive
values 3delta^2-delta^4 and 2delta-delta^2-delta^4 at the two endpoints.
Compactness gives a strict uniform modulus bound |q|<1. On the real
interval [delta,1], |q|^2=(1-u^2)^2/(1+u^2)<1 as well.

On the vertical segment,

  phi(v)=q(-iv)=(1+v^2)/(1+v),
  phi(0)=1, phi'(0)=-1, 0<phi(v)<1 for 0<v<=delta,
  log(1/u)=log(1/v)+i*pi/2.

Tracking u^r du and i^(-r-1) gives exactly

  e_n(r)=(-1)^r 2^(-n) {
      pi/4 integral_0^delta v^r phi(v)^n/(1+v)^(r+1)
                                    log^2(1/v) dv
     -pi^3/48 integral_0^delta v^r phi(v)^n/(1+v)^(r+1) dv
                         } + O(2^(-n) eta^n).          (7)

This equality with an exponentially small remainder accounts for the
real cancellation in the original oscillatory integral. In particular
the cubic logarithm loses its leading cube and leaves a square.

Ordinary endpoint Laplace asymptotics now give

  e_n(r) ~ (-1)^r pi*r!/4 * 2^(-n)n^(-r-1)(log n)^2.   (8)

One elementary justification is to set v=w/n, use
phi(w/n)^n -> exp(-w), and dominate by exp(-c w) on the fixed interval;
split w near 0 and infinity to dominate the logarithmic moments.
The factors log(1/v)=log n-log w yield the displayed leading square.
For a more explicit check, the same Laplace expansion gives, for fixed r,

 e_n(r)=(-1)^r pi*r!/4 * 2^(-n)n^(-r-1)
   { (log n+gamma-H_r)^2 + pi^2/12-H_r^(2)
                                      +O((log n)^2/n) },       (9)

where H_0=H_0^(2)=0. This follows from the first two logarithmic
moments of w^r exp(-w) and the first-order analytic Laplace remainder.
Only the leading term (8), not the refinement (9), is needed below.

4. The relevant three-by-three saddle minor

Use s=sqrt(2)-1, R=2-sqrt(2), z0=-R, kappa=2+sqrt(2),
and h=-log s. Let M_00 denote the minor of C_n formed by deleting
row 0 and column 0. Its row functions are y,y^2,y^3 for y=1/z,
and its column functions are ell,ell^2/2!,ell^3/3! for ell=log(1+z).
The determinant integration identity yields the two Vandermondes,
multiplied by product_j y_j ell_j and by 1/(1!2!3!).

The same strictly dominant saddle and dominated-convergence argument
as in rank_asymptotic.txt applies with three variables. Since

  y0=-1/R, ell0=-h, y0*ell0=h/R>0,
  y'(0)ell'(0)=1/s,

and the three-variable Gaussian Vandermonde integral is
(2*pi)^(3/2)*(1!*2!*3!)=12(2*pi)^(3/2), it gives

  M_00 ~ (-1)^n (1+sqrt(2))^(3n)
     * s^(-6)(h/R)^3
       / [6(2*pi)^(3/2) kappa^(9/2)n^(9/2)].           (10)

The sign (-1)^n is the cube of the sign of Phi(z0)^n. The other
three column-0 minors are O((1+sqrt(2))^(3n)n^(-9/2)). To see this
without assuming their nonzero leading constants, their row determinants
are a Vandermonde in y times a bounded polynomial, because they select
three of the four monomials 1,y,y^2,y^3. The same Gaussian majorant
then proves this bound.

Expansion of the numerator of (4) in column 0, together with (8),
therefore shows that its row-0 term e_n(0) M_00 is the actual leading
term. The three other terms are smaller by at least a factor O(1/n).
No unknown phase or potential cancellation remains in this step.

5. Combining the constants and the arithmetic scope

The rank theorem gave

  det C_n ~ (1+sqrt(2))^(4n) s^(-10)
               / [(2*pi)^2 kappa^8 n^8].

Combine this with (8) for r=0 and (10) in (4). Before simplifying,
the leading positive constant is

  pi*sqrt(2*pi)/24 * kappa^(7/2) * s^4 * (h/R)^3.

Since R=sqrt(2)*s, this is exactly C in (T). The exponential quotient
is 2^(-n)(1+sqrt(2))^(-n)=rho^n, and the power of n is
8-9/2-1=5/2. This proves (T).

The eventual alternating sign is an actual nonvanishing certificate,
and remains true under any positive rational or integer normalization.
The primitive pair has value a_n epsilon_n, which proves (U).
Source-vector denominators, cleared determinants, and common content
cannot be substituted for a_n without an arithmetic argument.
