EVENTUAL FULL RANK OF THE CIRCULAR HERMITE SOURCE
2026-10-29. Round07 analytic work. Local and unpublished.

Theorem. Put L_m(x)=product_(j=1)^m(x-j), let a=n-2B with
B~Binomial(n,1/2), and define the 4-by-4 rational matrix

  K_n(r,k)=[t^k] E[(a+t)^r L_n(a+t)],  0<=r,k<=3.

As n tends to infinity,

  det K_n ~ product_(r=0)^3 (n+r)! * (1+sqrt(2))^(4n+10)
             / [(2*pi)^2 (2+sqrt(2))^8 n^8].             (T)

In particular det K_n is positive and nonzero for all sufficiently
large n. By the exact nullity theorem in round06/fresh/
hermite_analysis.txt, the symmetric circular Hermite source space is
then one-dimensional and every nonzero source has A!=0. This establishes
eventual uniqueness with a genuine target coefficient. It does NOT by
itself establish nonvanishing or smallness of A*beta(4)+G(1).

The determinant is not always positive: n=3 has negative determinant.
The asymptotic conclusion is deliberately eventual. No numerical rank
threshold is needed for (T).

1. Exact generating function and normalization

Replace the row polynomial x^r L_n(x) by L_(n+r)(x). Since
L_(n+r)/L_n is monic of degree r, this is a unit triangular row change.
It therefore preserves the determinant. The falling-factorial identity

  L_m(x)/m! = [z^m] (1+z)^(x-1)

and the binomial theorem give, for every m>=0,

  E[L_m(a+t)]/m!
    =[z^m](1+z)^(t-n-1)(1+z+z^2/2)^n.                  (1)

It follows that det K_n=product_(r=0)^3(n+r)! det C_n, where

  C_n(r,k)=1/(2*pi*i*k!) integral
        z^(-r-1) (1+z)^(-1) log(1+z)^k Phi(z)^n dz,

  Phi(z)=(1+z+z^2/2)/(z(1+z)).                          (2)

The contour in (2) is any counterclockwise circle about 0 of radius
less than 1. The logarithm is its Taylor branch, so there is no
continuation or logarithm-sheet ambiguity.

Arithmetic's uncentered matrix with D(x)^n=(1+x^2)^n is 2^n times a
unit triangular row transform of K_n. Its determinant is consequently
2^(4n) det K_n. Formula (T) is for the centered expectation matrix,
not for that integer matrix.

2. One strictly dominant saddle on an admissible circle

Write s=sqrt(2)-1, r0=2-sqrt(2), z0=-r0, and
kappa=2+sqrt(2). Direct differentiation gives

  1+z0=s,  Phi(z0)=-(1+sqrt(2))=-1/s,  Phi'(z0)=0,
  z0^2 Phi''(z0)/Phi(z0)=kappa.                          (3)

Parametrize the contour by z(theta)=z0 exp(i theta), -pi<=theta<=pi.
This parametrization is counterclockwise. There is a logarithm of
Phi(z(theta))/Phi(z0) near 0 with value 0, and

  log(Phi(z(theta))/Phi(z0))=-kappa theta^2/2+O(theta^3). (4)

The point theta=0 is the unique maximum of |Phi| on this whole circle.
Here is a direct global verification. For z=r exp(i psi), u=cos psi,

  |Phi(z)|^2 =
   [2r^2u^2+(2r+r^3)u+1+r^4/4]/[r^2(1+r^2+2ru)].      (5)

The second derivative of (5) with respect to u is

  2(2-2r^2+r^4)/(1+r^2+2ru)^3 > 0    (0<r<1).

Hence its maximum is at u=-1 or 1. Both real numerators are positive,
and

  |Phi(-r)|-Phi(r)=r^2/(1-r^2)>0.

Thus the negative real point is the unique maximum. In particular,
outside any fixed neighborhood of theta=0 the modulus ratio in (4)
is bounded by a constant strictly less than 1. In a sufficiently small
neighborhood it is bounded by exp(-c theta^2), c>0. The zeros -1+/-i
of the numerator of Phi lie outside this circle.

3. Determinant integral and its actual leading term

On this contour (2) becomes

  C_n(r,k)=1/(2*pi*k!) integral_(-pi)^pi
       z(theta)^(-r)/(1+z(theta))
       log(1+z(theta))^k Phi(z(theta))^n dtheta.           (6)

The elementary determinant integration identity (obtained just by
expanding both determinants and relabeling four integration variables)
gives

  det C_n = 1/[4! (2*pi)^4 product_(k=0)^3 k!]
    integral_[-pi,pi]^4 product_(j=1)^4
       [Phi(z_j)^n/(1+z_j)]
       Delta(1/z_1,...,1/z_4)
       Delta(log(1+z_1),...,log(1+z_4)) dtheta_1...dtheta_4,
                                                               (7)

where Delta(v)=product_(i<j)(v_j-v_i). Both Vandermondes have the
same orientation; their product introduces no minus sign.

Let y(theta)=1/z(theta) and ell(theta)=log(1+z(theta)). Then

  y'(0)=-i/z0,  ell'(0)=i*z0/s,  y'(0)ell'(0)=1/s.

There are six pairs in each Vandermonde. Thus, after setting
theta_j=u_j/sqrt(n*kappa),

  Delta(y(theta_j))Delta(ell(theta_j))
      ~ s^(-6) (n*kappa)^(-6) Delta(u)^2.

The four ordinary amplitudes tend to s^(-4), the four measures give
(n*kappa)^(-2), and the fourth power removes the sign of Phi(z0)^n:

  product_(j=1)^4 Phi(z_j)^n
      / Phi(z0)^(4n) -> exp(-sum_j u_j^2/2).

These limits can be integrated. On a fixed small saddle neighborhood,
analyticity bounds every difference y(theta_j)-y(theta_i) and
ell(theta_j)-ell(theta_i) by a constant times |theta_j-theta_i|.
Together with the Gaussian modulus bound following (5), this provides
an integrable polynomial times Gaussian majorant after scaling. If any
variable leaves that neighborhood, (5) bounds the whole contribution
by an exponentially small factor times |Phi(z0)|^(4n); the amplitudes
and Vandermondes are bounded on the compact contour. Dominated
convergence therefore applies to (7), including its complex phases.
This is a leading asymptotic, not an absolute-value upper estimate.

Finally,

  integral_R^4 exp(-sum_j u_j^2/2) Delta(u)^2 du
       =(2*pi)^2 product_(j=1)^4 j! = (2*pi)^2 * 288.

For completeness this is another instance of the elementary determinant
integration identity: replace the monomials in Delta by the monic
Gaussian-orthogonal polynomials 1,u,u^2-1,u^3-3u. Their squared norms
are sqrt(2*pi) times 1,1,2,6. Multiplication by 4! gives 288*(2*pi)^2.
The denominator 4! product_(k=0)^3 k! in (7) is also 288. Consequently

  det C_n ~ Phi(z0)^(4n) s^(-10)
               / [(2*pi)^2 kappa^8 n^8].                (8)

Substitution of (3) and the row factorials proves (T), with a strictly
positive constant.

4. Exact and interval checks

rank_checks.py rebuilds the centered matrix using integer Taylor
products, checks the row replacement exactly at selected small n, and
uses Arb only to compare the exact determinant with the asymptotic.
These checks do not enter the proof. At n=20,40,80 the ratio to the
right side of (T) is respectively approximately

  0.5662605455, 0.7482090644, 0.8636923692.

The same calculation records n=1 degeneracy and the negative n=3 sign.
The checks are finite evidence for normalization and sign; the proof
above supplies the eventual conclusion independently.
