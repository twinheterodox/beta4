1. Setup and the full endpoint system

Let N>=1, a,b>=0, a+b=2N. Let D in Z[x] have degree2, D(0)=1 and
no zero on [0,1]. In particular D(1)!=0. Write E=x*d/dx and h0=1+x^2.
The sources under consideration are

 G=R/D^N,  deg R<=2N,  R(0)=0,
 f=A/h0+E^4G/x,
 f=O(x^a) at0,   f=O((x-1)^b) at1.                         (1.1)

All coefficients A,R are rational. Four integrations by parts give the
exact endpoint form

 I(f)=(1/6)integral_0^1 f(x)(-log x)^3 dx
                           =A theta+G(1), theta=beta(4).   (1.2)

The regularity hypotheses justify the integral. The algebraic endpoint
system below only needs D(0)=1 and D(1)!=0. No numerical value of theta
enters its construction.

Set

 F(x)=sum_(j>=1 odd) chi4(j)x^j/j^4,
 R_low=trunc_(degree<=a)[-D^N F],
 L(z)=product_(j=a+1)^(2N)(z-j),
 D(x)^N=sum_(ell=0)^(2N) d_ell x^ell.                       (1.3)

Since E^4F=x/(1+x^2), the condition at0 is exactly

 R=A R_low+sum_(j=a+1)^(2N) r_j x^j.                        (1.4)

Use y=log x near x=1 and put

 T(y)=1/(2 cosh y),
 V_b(y)=-sum_(m=4)^(b+3) T^(m-4)(0)y^m/m!.                  (1.5)

An empty sum is zero. If g_k=(d/dy)^kG(exp y)|_(y=0), then the second
condition in (1.1) is equivalent to

 G(exp y)=sum_(k=0)^3 g_k y^k/k!+A V_b(y)+O(y^(b+4)).        (1.6)

Indeed x f=A T(y)+(d/dy)^4G(exp y), and multiplication by x and the
coordinate change preserve the order of the zero at1.

For r,k=0,1,2,3 define P_r(z)=z^r L(z) and

 K_(r,k)=sum_ell d_ell P_r^(k)(ell)/k!,
 v_r=(d/dy)^r L(d/dy)
        [R_low(exp y)-D(exp y)^N V_b(y)]|_(y=0).            (1.7)

The notation v is the inhomogeneous vector; it is called h in the saved
script and is unrelated to h0=1+x^2. A fully explicit formula is

 v_r=sum_j [x^j]R_low * P_r(j)
       +sum_ell d_ell sum_(s=4)^(b+r)
                      P_r^(s)(ell)/s! * T^(s-4)(0).        (1.8)

The derivatives of T are Euler integers divided by2; odd ones vanish.

THEOREM1. The rational source space (1.1) is naturally isomorphic to

                           K g=A v.                       (1.9)

The endpoint pair is exactly (A,g_0). This is a four-by-five kernel
problem ker[-v|K]; det K is allowed to vanish.

Proof. Put U(y)=D(exp y)^N G(exp y)-A R_low(exp y). Equation(1.4)
is equivalent to L(d/dy)U=0. Applying derivatives r=0,...,3 at0, the
jet(1.6) suffices because the operator order is b. The identity

 (d/dy)^r L(d/dy)[exp(ell y)y^k/k!]|_0=P_r^(k)(ell)/k!

gives (1.9).

Conversely assume b>0 and rational A,g satisfy(1.9). Let P(y) be the
polynomial in(1.6) without the remainder. Form the analytic germ
U_*(y)=D(exp y)^N P(y)-A R_low(exp y). There is a unique exponential
sum U=sum_(j=a+1)^(2N) r_j exp(jy) matching its first b derivatives:
the interpolation matrix is a Vandermonde matrix at b distinct integers.
Thus all r_j are rational. The four equations(1.9) say that
L(d/dy)U_* has its first four derivatives zero. Since L is monic, its
order-b recurrence forces U-U_*=O(y^(b+4)). Division by the nonzero
germ D(exp y)^N gives (1.6). Taking R as in(1.4) gives exactly(1.1).
The same recurrence proves uniqueness, including when b<4.

For b=0, L=1 and(1.9) simply says that the first four derivatives of
D(exp y)^N sum_(k=0)^3g_k y^k/k!-A R_low(exp y) vanish. Hence
R=A R_low and G=R/D^N have precisely those four jets. This separately
handles the order-zero boundary case. QED.

This isomorphism does not assert that the kernel has dimension one or
that its endpoint pair is nonzero. A nonzero source may have A=0, or
even have a zero endpoint pair. Those cases must be distinguished.


2. Integer clearing and actual primitive normalization

Put

 O_a=lcm(1,3,...,largest odd<=a),  O_0=1,
                           C=2 O_a^4.                     (2.1)

Each K entry is an integer: a divided derivative of an integer polynomial
at an integer is integral. Also C v is integral. For the low polynomial
this follows from its coefficient denominators. For the second sum in
(1.8), every summand is an integer times an Euler integer divided by2.
Factorials have already canceled in the divided derivatives.

At an index where the integer matrix [-C v|K] has row rank4, its signed
maximal minors give a kernel vector (u,g_0,g_1,g_2,g_3). The corresponding
target coefficient is A=C u. Expansion of the first two cofactors gives
the specified integer endpoint pair

 U=C det K,
 B=det K[ column0 replaced by C v ].                       (2.2)

The formula remains valid when det K=0, provided the augmented matrix
has rank4. If (U,B)!=(0,0), its actual primitive pair is

                  (U,B)/gcd(U,B),                         (2.3)

with the overall sign fixed to make the first nonzero coefficient positive.
This is an actual two-integer gcd, not an entrywise denominator estimate.
On a one-dimensional source space it agrees with the primitive pair from
the growing boundary-jet matrix, regardless of that matrix's source-vector
content. At an augmented-rank-defective index the whole kernel is needed;
the cofactor pair alone is not a prescription for a particular source.

The circular N=a=b=1 example has det K=0 but augmented rank4. It gives
A=0, G=x^2/(1+x^2), and primitive constant form1. The saved checks retain
this case instead of silently treating it as a theta approximation.


3. Exact factorial normalization

Let d_b=lcm(1,...,b), with d_0=1. For all four rows and columns,

                        d_b^k K_(r,k)/b! is integral.      (3.1)

Proof. L(z)/b!=binom(z-a-1,b) is integer-valued. At an integer ell,
Vandermonde's identity expresses binom(ell-a-1+t,b) as an integer linear
combination of binom(t,j), 0<=j<=b. For k>0 the t^k coefficient of

 binom(t,j)=(-1)^(j-1)t/j * product_(1<=s<j)(1-t/s)

has denominator dividing d_b^k; its constant coefficient is integral.
Multiplication by (ell+t)^r preserves that denominator bound. Summing
against the integral d_ell proves(3.1). The empty b=0 case is immediate.

Define

 Khat=K diag(1,d_b,d_b^2,d_b^3)/b!,   vhat=v/b!.             (3.2)

Khat is integral. Replacing g by diag(1,d_b,d_b^2,d_b^3)ghat preserves
g_0. Equivalently both endpoint determinants acquire exactly the same
factor

                           d_b^6/(b!)^4.                   (3.3)

Thus their primitive pair is unchanged. Removing this factorial common
scalar is an exact normalization, not evidence that the residual pair
has small height. The next section controls the denominator of vhat.


4. Four strict integration indices in the local endpoint germ

Let V(x) be the analytic germ at x=1 satisfying

 E^4 V=-x/(1+x^2),  E^jV(1)=0 for j=0,1,2,3.              (4.1)

Then V(exp y)=V_b(y)+O(y^(b+4)). Consequently the vector(1.7), whose
largest derivative order is b+3, is unchanged if V_b is replaced by
this full local germ. This is only a statement about jets at1; no global
branch or rationality of V is assumed.

Put z=x-1 and write

                1/(2+2z+z^2)=sum_(k>=0) q_k z^k.

The four ordinary integrations constructing V are explicit:

 H_3(z)=-integral_0^z dt/(2+2t+t^2),
 H_2(z)= integral_0^z H_3(t)/(1+t) dt,
 H_1(z)= integral_0^z H_2(t)/(1+t) dt,
 V(1+z)=integral_0^z H_1(t)/(1+t) dt.                       (4.2)

All four primitives vanish at z=0. They follow successively by dividing
the equations for Euler primitives by x=1+z.

For m>=0 put

 W4(m)=product_(r=1)^4 lcm(1,...,floor(m/r)),
                 lcm of an empty list=1.                  (4.3)

LEMMA2. If V(1+z)=sum c_m z^m, then c_0=...=c_3=0 and, for m>=4,

                  2^ceil((m-3)/2) W4(m)c_m is integral.    (4.4)

Proof. The first kernel has the exact period-four coefficients

 q_(4s)=(-1)^s/2^(2s+1),
 q_(4s+1)=(-1)^(s+1)/2^(2s+1),
 q_(4s+2)=(-1)^s/2^(2s+2),   q_(4s+3)=0.                  (4.5)

This follows either from its two-term recurrence or its Gaussian partial
fractions. Thus 2^(floor(k/2)+1)q_k is integral.

Expanding the four integrations in(4.2), every contribution to c_m has
strict increasing integration indices

                       1<=j_1<j_2<j_3<j_4=m.

Its denominator, apart from the first kernel's power of2, divides
j_1 j_2 j_3 j_4. The first kernel index is j_1-1, and j_1<=m-3.
Equation(4.5) therefore supplies a power of2 no larger than
2^ceil((m-3)/2).

For any prime p, the maximum total valuation of four distinct positive
integers at most m is

 sum_(e>=1) min(4,floor(m/p^e))
        =sum_(r=1)^4 floor(log_p floor(m/r)).               (4.6)

The right side is v_p(W4(m)). This proves that every product of the
four indices divides W4(m), and hence proves(4.4). The last index being
exactly m is not needed for this upper bound. QED.

The strictness here is essential and justified: all four integrations
are ordinary integrations of germs analytic at z=0. This differs from
an iterated-integral expression with singular dx/x letters at the origin,
where such a denominator saving cannot be assumed.


5. The sharpened normalized-column bound

For b>0 define

 Z_b=gcd(2 b!, 2^ceil(b/2) W4(b+3)),
 Chat=lcm(O_a^4,Z_b).                                     (5.1)

For b=0 put Z_0=1 and Chat=O_a^4.

THEOREM3. Every entry of Chat*vhat is integral.

Proof. Split v=v_R+v_V according to the two terms in(1.7), including
the minus sign in the V contribution. For the low polynomial,

 (v_R)_r/b!=sum_j [x^j]R_low * j^r binom(j-a-1,b),

so O_a^4 clears it.

For the other term use the operator identity

                 L(E)=x^(2N+1) (d/dx)^b x^(-a-1).          (5.2)

After applying E^r and evaluating at1, division by b! expresses
(v_V)_r/b! as an integer linear combination of ordinary Taylor
coefficients through degree b+r of

                         x^(-a-1)D(x)^N V(x).

For completeness, expanding E^r as an integer combination of
x^j(d/dx)^j gives derivatives of orders b+s, 0<=s<=r. The corresponding
ordinary Taylor coefficient is multiplied by (b+s)!/b!, an integer;
the other factors are derivatives of the integer-power monomial
x^(2N+1) at1. This proves the asserted integrality of the combination.

Both D(1+z)^N and (1+z)^(-a-1) have integer Taylor coefficients.
Multiplication by them cannot worsen a coefficient-denominator bound
that increases with the degree. Lemma2 therefore clears all the Taylor
coefficients needed here by

                         2^ceil(b/2) W4(b+3).              (5.3)

Independently, formula(1.8) shows v_V is half-integral. Thus v_V/b!
has denominator dividing 2b!. Combining these TWO bounds gives Z_b.
Taking its least common multiple with O_a^4 proves the theorem. For
b=0, V_b=0 and the assertion is just the low-polynomial bound. QED.

This theorem is uniform in the integer quadratic D. It is a sufficient
bound, and need not be the exact denominator of vhat. For example the
saved circular N=a=b=8 case has Chat=31116960000, whereas the actual
common denominator of its four vhat entries is only40516875.


6. A genuine common-content divisor, with exact scope

Form the integral pair

 Uhat=Chat det Khat,
 Bhat=det Khat[ column0 replaced by Chat vhat ].             (6.1)

By(3.3), the exact pair(2.2) satisfies

 (U,B)=q (Uhat,Bhat),
            q=C(b!)^4/(d_b^6 Chat)>0.                     (6.2)

Write q in lowest terms. Since both pairs are integral, the numerator
of q divides gcd(U,B), whenever that pair is nonzero. Indeed its reduced
denominator must divide both coordinates of (Uhat,Bhat); canceling it
leaves the numerator as a common factor of (U,B). This is a uniform,
proved divisor of common numerator content in the specified raw pair.

The saved calculations verify(6.2), this divisibility, and equality of
the two actual primitive pairs. They retain the additional common gcd
after this guaranteed factor has been removed. The factor can be weak
at small b (it is72 in the saved circular N=8 case), and it is not
asserted to exhaust common content.

The role of(6.2) is to remove factorial scale before assessing the actual
primitive coefficient height. Its content is normalization-dependent:
rescaling an input pair changes that content while leaving its primitive
pair unchanged. Neither this factor nor Chat alone proves a useful
upper or lower asymptotic bound for the final primitive target coefficient.


7. The local fourth p-layer is postponed, and the postponement is sharp

THEOREM4. Let p>=5 be prime. No c_m in Lemma2 with m<4p has a denominator
divisible by p^4. At m=4p its p-adic valuation is exactly -4, and

                    p^4 c_(4p) == -chi4(p)/48 mod p.        (7.1)

Proof. For m<=4p, every integration index is below p^2. A term can
therefore have four p factors only if all four strict indices are
multiples of p. This is impossible below4p. At4p there is exactly one
such chain:

                             (p,2p,3p,4p).

The three intervening coefficients of 1/(1+z) have degrees p-1, so
their signs are all positive. The first integration contributes
-q_(p-1). Hence the surviving residue after multiplication by p^4 is

                             -q_(p-1)/24.

The Gaussian partial fractions give

 q_(p-1)=(1/(2i))[(1-i)^(-p)-(1+i)^(-p)].

Modulo p in F_p[i], Frobenius sends i to chi4(p)i. The factors 1+i and
1-i are units because their norm is2. The displayed difference is then
chi4(p)i, so q_(p-1)==chi4(p)/2 modp. This proves(7.1), whose right
side is nonzero. Other chains have at most three p factors and vanish
after multiplication by p^4 and reduction. QED.

This exact residue statement is about V, not an assertion that every
entry of vhat has its fourth layer at every available prime. The later
linear combination may cancel it. Conversely the low polynomial in v
still carries O_a^4, so the theorem does not delete that separate source
of fourth prime powers in the full system.


8. Recorded denominator comparison and unresolved arithmetic gate

The earlier in-round observation about asymmetry can be stated precisely
without recommending or extending a construction. It concerns the explicit
bound Chat, not the actual endpoint pair. If a/N tends to alpha and b/N
tends to beta=2-alpha, the prime-number-theorem estimate for least common
multiples gives

 log Chat/N -> sum_(r=1)^4 max(alpha,beta/r)+(beta/2)log2.    (8.1)

The standard input is log lcm(1,...,m)=m+o(m). For the underlying prime
sum asymptotic see D. Zagier, "Newman's Short Proof of the Prime Number
Theorem," American Mathematical Monthly104 (1997), p707, stepVI,
 https://people.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/zagier/files/doi/10.2307/2975232/fulltext.pdf
Adding the prime-power terms changes that sum by o(m). The coordinator
checked this author-source input; the closing pass also reopened the
corresponding statement. No new estimate about primes is claimed here.

Prime-power corrections are o(N). For odd primes of order N, the bound
has the larger of four layers below a and min(4,floor(b/p)) layers from
W4; taking gcd with b! does not change that leading profile. The dyadic
exponent is beta*N/2+O(log N) when beta>0. The endpoint beta=0 agrees
with the limiting expression.

The elementary piecewise-linear expression in(8.1) is minimized at
alpha=1/2, beta=3/2, with value13/4+(3/4)log2. At symmetry it is
4+(1/2)log2. This only identifies the best sufficient clearing in this
one column. The scaled K entries, their determinant height, the actual
common gcd, and the real source error all remain relevant. No optimized
primitive family, and no actual-value no-go, follows from(8.1).

In particular, the required arithmetic step after the separate analytic
work is a uniform estimate for the ACTUAL reduced target coefficient
from(2.3). The results here do not prove such an estimate on either side
of a useful error threshold. They also do not establish rank or nonzero
error uniformly over all asymmetric endpoint choices and denominators.
Those are explicit gaps, not consequences of finite examples.
