INTEGER q-SERIES: A RATIONALITY-DEPENDENT BOUNDARY SUBTRACTION
29 October2026. Local/unpublished. No irrationality proof or credible route.

The source is classical, exactly Jouhet--Mosaki beta_q(4). The new attempted
step was an integrality bridge after assuming theta=beta4 rational. That
step fails in the specified direct rational-evaluation family by an exact
reduced-denominator calculation. A subsequent root-of-unity average has
an exact reduction, but does not produce the desired integer observable.
These are scoped gates, not a theorem against q-deformations or counting.

1. Exact source and the intended contradiction

Write chi=chi4 and define, for |q|<1,

  A(q)=sum_(n>=1) a_n q^n,
  a_n=sum_(d|n)chi(d)(n/d)^3 in Z,
  Phi(q)=q(1+4q+q^2)/(1-q)^4=sum_(n>=1)n^3 q^n.

Absolutely convergent rearrangement gives

  A(q)=sum_(k>=1) k^3 q^k/(1+q^(2k))
      =sum_(d>=1)chi(d)Phi(q^d).                         (1)

This is exactly equation1.1 of Jouhet--Mosaki [JM], not a new Lambert
series. Their Section6.1 proves its beta-value boundary calibration.

For t>0 the first expression in(1) is

  A(e^-t)=t^-3 sum_(k>=1) f(kt), f(x)=x^3/(2cosh x).

The integral of f is6theta: expand1/(2cosh x) as the alternating sum
of exp(-(2j+1)x), or first use finite partial sums with an integrable
remainder. All derivatives of f are integrable and decay at infinity.
Euler--Maclaurin with its integral remainder [EM] therefore gives

  A(e^-t)=6theta*t^-4+1/240+t^2/1008+O(t^4).            (2)

To check the constants directly, f(x)=x^3/2-x^5/4+O(x^7),
f'''(0)=3, f^(5)(0)=-30, B4=-1/30 and B6=1/42. In the formula
-B_(2r)f^(2r-1)(0)t^(2r-4)/(2r)!, these give1/240 and1/1008.
Apply the remainder formula with m=4: after the overall t^-3 factor,
its absolute value is bounded by a constant times t^4, since f^(8)
is integrable. This is a proved fixed-order expansion, not a divergent
series used outside its domain. Direct expansion of the rational kernel
gives

  Phi(e^-t)=6t^-4+1/120-t^2/504+O(t^4).                (3)

Assume theta=u/v, u,v positive integers, and prescribe

  H_(u,v)(q)=240v A(q)-240u Phi(q)-(v-2u).              (4)

It has INTEGER coefficients, including its constant coefficient, and
the actual real rationality assumption, through(2)-(3), implies

  H_(u,v)(e^-t)=c_(u,v)t^2+O_(u,v)(t^4),
  c_(u,v)=5(v+2u)/21>0.                               (5)

Thus this is a genuine assumption-dependent shrinking source. A valid
construction of nonzero integers from it, still tending to zero, would
contradict rationality. Integer coefficients do not make its values at
rational q integers. The next section tests that precise missing step.

Nor are its coefficients eventually of one sign. Under theta=u/v,
80/81<u/v<1, and for every r>=0,

  [q^(2^r)]H=240(v-u)2^(3r)>0,
  [q^(3*2^r)]H=240(26v-27u)2^(3r)<0.                  (6)

Hence a positivity-based boundary rigidity argument does not apply.

2. The direct rational-evaluation bridge fails exactly

Let p>5 be a prime not dividing v, put M=p^2 and q_p=(p-1)/p, and let
H^[M] be the polynomial consisting of coefficients0,...,M in(4).
For ANY positive integers u,v satisfying this prime restriction,

             den(H^[p^2](q_p))=p^(p^2).                (7)

This is the actual reduced denominator, not a sufficient multiplier.
Indeed

  a_(p^2)=p^6+chi(p)p^3+1,
  h_M=240v a_M-240u M^3 ==240v (mod p).

In p^M H^[M]((p-1)/p), every term except the top term is divisible
by p; the top term is h_M(p-1)^M, nonzero modulo p. Thus its numerator
is coprime to p, proving(7) with the complete possible gcd accounted for.

The truncation error is negligible on the real scale of(5). Indeed

  |a_n|<=n^3 sum_(d>=1)d^-3 <2n^3,
  |h_n|<=C_(u,v)n^3.

For M=p^2 and q_p=1-1/p, expand n=M+j. The four geometric power sums
show

  sum_(n>M)n^3 q_p^n=O(p^7 e^-p).                     (8)

Here q_p^M<=e^-p and the sums of j^r q_p^j are O(p^(r+1))
for r=0,1,2,3. Thus the upper estimate is sufficient for the difference,
not an unjustified lower bound for an error.

Under theta=u/v, t_p=-log(1-1/p)=p^-1+O(p^-2), and(5),(8) imply

  H^[p^2](q_p)=c_(u,v)p^-2(1+O_(u,v)(p^-1))>0

eventually. Combining with the EXACT denominator(7), its reduced numerator
N_p satisfies the conditional asymptotic

  N_p=c_(u,v)p^(p^2-2)(1+O_(u,v)(p^-1)) -> infinity.    (9)

This is a theorem about the stated truncation and rational point under
the hypothetical rationality assumption. It is not a no-go theorem for
arbitrary filters, optimal truncations, q-hypergeometric constructions,
or algebraic evaluation points. No extension to those was attempted.

The coefficient h_M also proves den(H^[p^e]((p-1)/p))=p^(p^e)
for any fixed e>=1, since a_(p^e)==chi(p)^e modulo p. Only e=2
is used for the real asymptotic and the closed gate above.

3. Root-of-unity averages: an exact follow-up, no integrality bridge

For every odd prime p and n>=1 the divisor definition gives

  a_(pn)=(p^3+chi(p))a_n-chi(p)p^3 a_(n/p),             (10)

where a_(n/p)=0 unless p divides n. To prove it, factor n=p^e m,
p not dividing m. Multiplicativity reduces(10) to the geometric sum
a_(p^e)=sum_(j=0)^e chi(p)^j p^(3(e-j)).

Let zeta be a primitive p-th root of unity. Filtering Fourier indices
and using(10) gives the analytic identity, for |q|<1,

  sum_(j=0)^(p-1) A(zeta^j q)
     =p[(p^3+chi(p))A(q^p)-chi(p)p^3 A(q^(p^2))].       (11)

All p terms are included. This is a root-of-unity AVERAGE, not an assertion
that the generally transcendental infinite sums belong to a number field.
For the finite polynomial A^[M], the same exact statement has cutoffs
floor(M/p), floor(M/p^2) on the two right-hand sides. At q=1 it is an
integer coefficient identity, but it contains finite divisor sums rather
than a small beta4 remainder.

The raw counting sequence S(N)=sum_(n<=N)a_n cannot have an expansion
S(N)=P(N)+o(1) with a polynomial P of degree at most4. Such an expansion
would give a_N=P(N)-P(N-1)+o(1), forcing a_N/N^3 to have one limit.
But this ratio is1 on N=2^r and26/27 on N=3*2^r. Thus this particular
integer count lacks the small polynomial remainder required by the
rationality argument. This says nothing about other weights or counts.

For the potentially cheaper algebraic point q=2^(-1/p), all p conjugate
arguments have the same radius and the principal argument approaches1.
Equation(11) becomes exactly

  p[(p^3+chi(p))A(1/2)-chi(p)p^3 A(2^-p)].              (12)

The principal boundary limit is calibrated to theta, but this complete
average transfers it to q-series values. Rationality of theta supplies
no algebraicity or integrality for those values. Evaluations of finite
polynomials at these algebraic points can support norms, but would require
all conjugate estimates and the factor v^p at the degree-p radical point.
None is supplied here. In particular(12) is not a norm estimate.

The frozen round11 sine-normalized family was read before considering
this follow-up. It proves growth for its own conjugate distribution, not
for(12) or all algebraic evaluation points. The present exact reduction
reveals no credible alternative integer bridge; a root substitution alone
would not resolve the missing arithmetic. No new norm scan was started.
