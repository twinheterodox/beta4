ROUND10: AN EXACT INTEGER-VALUED NORM AND A PROPER-SOURCE GATE
29 October 2026. Local and unpublished. No irrationality proof.

Outcome

The polynomial Euler functional has a sharp, elementary norm on the
integer-valued polynomial space. This is a different norm from the
Gauss norm bounded in round08. Its first three odd-prime denominator
layers start at degrees p+2, 2p+1, 3p, for p>=5.

Finite congruence conditions on a complete Gram source space change
only a rational scalar, so that proposed remedy is retired. A different
Fermat multiplier does change the proper source space. Its two prescribed
tests have larger actual primitive values than matched old controls,
and no local determinant improvement at the prime it was designed for.
This finite gate does not prove an asymptotic impossibility theorem.

1. Exact norm on integer-valued polynomials

Let E(x^d)=E_d(0), where sum E_d(0) z^d/d!=2/(exp(z)+1), and

  tau(P)=-(1/12)E(P''').

Fix p>=5. Equip Q_p[x] of degree at most n with
||P||_Int=sup_(x in Z_p)|P(x)|_p. For n>=3 define

  m_p(3q+r)=(3-r)p^q+r p^(q+1),  q>=0, r=0,1,2,
  H_p(n)=max{h>=0:m_p(h)<=n}.                         (1)

THEOREM. The operator norm of tau on that space is exactly p^H_p(n).
For n<3 it is zero. More explicitly, put a_j=tau(binomial(x,j)). Then

  min_(0<=j<=n) v_p(a_j)=-H_p(n),                     (2)
  p^h a_(m_p(h)) == -c_h/12 mod p,
  c_h=1 if h=0 mod3, and c_h=3 otherwise.             (3)

In particular, for every p>=5:

  v_p(a_(p+2))=-1,  v_p(a_(2p+1))=-2,
  v_p(a_(3p))=-3,  p a_(p+2)==-1/4 mod p.            (4)

Proof. Every polynomial P of degree at most n has a unique expansion
P=sum_(j=0)^n b_j binomial(x,j), with b_j=Delta^j P(0). Thus if P is
integer-valued on Z_p, every b_j is in Z_p. Conversely, binomial(x,j)
is integer-valued there: its integer values at nonnegative integers
extend by continuity to their dense subset closure Z_p. Consequently
||P||_Int=max_j |b_j|_p. This proves the needed basis assertion directly.

The formal generating function is

  sum_(j>=0) a_j z^j
    =-log(1+z)^3 / [12(1+z/2)].                     (5)

Indeed sum binomial(x,j)z^j=(1+z)^x, while
E((1+z)^x)=2/(2+z); three x derivatives give log(1+z)^3.

A summand of the coefficient of z^j on the right of (5) has three
positive log indices u,v,w with u+v+w<=j. Its possible p-denominator
exponent is v_p(u)+v_p(v)+v_p(w). If these valuations are a,b,c,
then u+v+w>=p^a+p^b+p^c. Among triples of nonnegative exponents with
sum h, the minimum of p^a+p^b+p^c is m_p(h). To prove this, whenever
a>=b+2 replace (a,b) by (a-1,b+1); the sum of exponents is unchanged
and the sum of powers strictly decreases. Thus every minimizing triple
has exponents differing by at most one, giving exactly (1).

It follows that v_p(a_j)>=-H_p(j). At j=m_p(h), a contribution with
denominator exponent h must use u,v,w equal to the three minimizing
powers themselves, and the geometric factor in (5) must use its
constant term. There are c_h such ordered triples. Every other summand
has valuation at least -h+1. Each minimizing index is odd, hence their
sum is odd; the sign and factor -1/12 give (3). Since c_h is a p-unit
for p>=5, this proves the sharp lower bound and then (2). The binomial
polynomial at that degree has norm one, so the operator norm is attained.
QED.

An exact universal denominator consequence: on the globally
integer-valued polynomial lattice of degree <=n, the part of the
denominator of tau supported at primes >=5 is cleared optimally by

  M_n=product_(p>=5) p^H_p(n).                       (6)

Only primes <=n-2 appear. The first three exponent layers have cutoffs
n-2, (n-1)/2, n/3; further layers at a prime p begin at p^2+2p.
No prime-number theorem or asymptotic estimate is needed here. The
statement concerns the polynomial part alone, not rational-source pole
values or final primitive coefficients.

There is no conflict with round08. That theorem bounds tau by one in
the Gauss COEFFICIENT norm on Q_p<x>; an integer-valued binomial
polynomial can have nonintegral monomial coefficients. The norms cannot
be interchanged. The exceptional primes 2 and3 are not included in
the new sharp statement.

2. Scope of the full-rank congruence gate

Fix rational functions f_1,...,f_h and a bilinear functional
B_X(f,g)=mu_X(fg/W), with rational W and values in Q[X]. Let M_X
be its Gram matrix. Replacing the source lattice by any full-rank
rational lattice with basis matrix T gives

  M'_X=T^t M_X T,  det M'_X=(det T)^2 det M_X.        (7)

Hence its positive primitive integer polynomial is unchanged. A finite
system of homogeneous congruences on the coefficients contains a
nonzero integer multiple of the original Z^h lattice; it therefore has
full rational rank and is covered by (7).

This covers only the complete h-dimensional determinant. Selecting
fewer particular vectors, taking a proper subspace, changing poles,
or choosing a different multiplier is outside the statement. In
particular it is not a no-go theorem for useful source lifts from a
lattice. The old block image-index warning remains applicable there.

The fixed checks use h=3, K=3,4 and W=D_K(t),
D_K=product_(j=0)^(K-1)(t+(j+1/2)^2). They compare monomials, the
integer-valued central basis D_i(t)/(2i)!, and the complete solution
lattice P(1)=P'(1)=0 mod5, P(2)=0 mod7. The latter has the basis

  -10+25t-10t^2,   10t-5t^2,   7-14t+7t^2.

Its determinant has absolute value175=5^2*7, equal to the constraint
index, so it is the full solution lattice. The raw determinant changes
by 1/2304 or30625 respectively; the exact primitive polynomial is
identical in each comparison. This part requires no larger experiment.

3. A proper space from Fermat divisibility

For an odd prime p, set e=(p-1)/2, epsilon=(-1)^e and

  F_p(t)=t(t^e-epsilon)^2.                            (8)

Then, identically,

  F_p(-x^2)=-x^2(x^(p-1)-1)^2.                      (9)

If x is a p-adic unit, Fermat's residue identity makes the square in
(9) divisible by p^2. If p divides x, the x^2 factor does so. Hence
F_p(-x^2)/p^2 is integer-valued on Z_p. The polynomial degree is p.
For p=1 mod4, F_p has a positive root at t=1, unlike D_p. This is a
specific new numerator shape within the archived csch functional.

There is no improved degree-for-integrality efficiency here. For every
integer-valued polynomial of degree2p in x, its leading coefficient
times (2p)! is in Z_p, by the binomial-basis proof above. Since
v_p((2p)!)=2, the leading coefficient of F_p(-x^2)/p^2 attains the
best possible p-denominator at that degree. But so does the old
D_p(-x^2)/(2p)!. The changed root placement is the only potential
advantage tested, not a stronger leading-coefficient arithmetic bound.

The prescribed gate uses just p=5,7 and

  K=2p+3,  V_p=(F_p/p^2)*span_Q{1,t},
  Delta_p(X)=det(mu_X(f_i f_j/D_K))_(0<=i,j<2).       (10)

The matched old control is V_p=(D_p/(2p)!)*span_Q{1,t}, with the
same K and matrix size. These are proper subspaces of the larger
degree-p+1 polynomial space, not alternative full bases. Their
primitive polynomials are different, checked exactly.

Here mu_X is precisely the archived csch functional, with

 mu_X(t^j)=(-1)^j tau(x^(2j+4)),
 mu_X(1/(t+(j+1/2)^2))
   =2(2j+1)^3(-1)^j(X-T_4(j))-1/(2j+1).

At theta=beta(4) it integrates against
w(y)=-(y^4/6)(d/dy)^3 csch(pi y)>0 on y>0. Both Gram matrices
are positive definite: a nonzero polynomial in V_p has only finitely
many real zeros, while D_K(y^2)>0 and w(y)>0. Thus Delta_p(theta)>0,
and the positively normalized primitive Q_p also has Q_p(theta)>0.
This proves actual nonvanishing without a numerical independence claim.

Exact polynomial division plus residues gives Q_p. Arb gives:

 p  K   multiplier   degree  maximum coefficient bits  log Q_p(theta)
 5 13   Fermat         2             331                152.581582
 5 13   central        2             267                118.451422
 7 17   Fermat         2             492                240.352689
 7 17   central        2             400                192.882663

Every displayed value is positive and greater than one. The JSON files
retain full exact integer coefficients and outward-rounded values.
For BOTH normalized designs and BOTH p, the minimum p-valuation of the
raw determinant's coefficients is -16. Thus the uniform pointwise p^2
divisibility of F_p(-x^2) gives no determinant advantage at its target
prime in this fixture. It cannot be counted as an extra primitive gain.
Removing either displayed source normalization leaves the primitive
polynomial unchanged, separately checked.

4. What a successful extension would have to prove

For any prescribed family of such positive determinants, let Q_n be
the actual primitive integer polynomial, d_n its degree. If theta=u/v,
then v^d_n Q_n(theta) is a positive integer. Therefore

  log Q_n(theta)/d_n -> -infinity                    (11)

would contradict rationality. At the fixed matrix size2 used here,
Q_n(theta)->0 would suffice. An isolated value below1 would not suffice.

A possible genuinely arithmetic-first extension would construct a
single small-degree multiplier that realizes useful local divisibility
at many primes while maintaining a favorable positive integral. Merely
multiplying (8) over primes pays the sum of their degrees; a CRT lift
may instead pay a large coefficient height. No bound overcoming either
cost was found. The actual primitive balance, not a chosen sufficient
clearing denominator, is the missing estimate. The two finite failures
give no asymptotic no-go theorem, but also no concrete reason for a scan.

The round08 affine parameter Y=chi(p)p^4 X+C_p remains a valid local
tool; its p-adic C_p is never identified with a real rational theta.
No new irrationality mechanism is established by this packet.
