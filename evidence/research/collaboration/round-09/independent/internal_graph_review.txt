INDEPENDENT ANALYTIC REVIEW OF THE NONUNIFORM INTERNAL GRAPH
29 October2026. Local and unpublished. No irrationality proof.

Reviewed sources: internal_graph/first-period.txt and coordinator/
internal-weight-analysis.txt. The derivation below was worked out without
reading either worker's code. The separate script uses rational point
certificates with degree bounds, rather than their symbolic reconstruction.

Outcome

The n1 identity is correct. I found no normalization, radial-endpoint,
angular-projection, or interchange gap. In particular,

 J1=301/384-503 beta2/64+243 beta4/32
                         -73 log2/24+27 zeta3/32.           (A)

The primitive displayed vector is(301,-3018,2916,-1168,324). This is a
five-period form. Its positivity and its value below1 do not prove the
irrationality of beta4. Independence of these periods is not assumed.

The review supplies a direct convergence bound independent of the printed
rational function's asymptotics. It also proves the fixed period space
Q+Q beta2+Q beta4+Q log2+Q zeta3 for EVERY fixed integer n>=0 in the
prescribed family. This latter claim concerns the exact decomposition,
not an arithmetic estimate or nonvanishing after auxiliary elimination.

1. Graph and projective normalization

Let the two external vectors A,B be orthogonal and have norm1. At n1,
the outer propagator powers are2 and the central power is5 in dimension12.
The Schwinger Gaussian matrix is

 [[a1+a2+e,-e],[-e,a4+a5+e]],

with determinant U. Put a1+a2+a4+a5=1 and set a1=r(1-x),a2=rx,
a4=(1-r)(1-y),a5=(1-r)y. Then U=r(1-r)+e. Completing the square gives

 F=U(a2+a5)-a2^2(a4+a5+e)-a5^2(a1+a2+e),

because A dot B=0. With e=t r(1-r)/(1-t), direct substitution gives
F=U D with precisely the printed D. The absolute outer Jacobian is
r(1-r); multiplied by de/dt it is U^2. In the coordinate order
(a1,a2,a4) versus(r,x,y) the signed Jacobian is negative; the measure uses
its absolute value. This is an orientation convention, not an error.

For two loops, sum(alpha)=13 and dimension12, the scale integral gives
Gamma(1) U^-5/F. The Schwinger denominator is Gamma(2)^4 Gamma(5)=24.
Thus a1 a2 a4 a5 e^4 U^2 U^-6 is exactly the cube numerator, and

       normalized graph K=J1/24.

More generally, b=2n+1,q=n gives dimension8n+4, outer power n+1 and
central power4n+1. The corresponding normalized graph is
J_n/[(n!)^4(4n)!]. All uses of Schwinger integration have nonnegative
integrands; Tonelli is applicable even before finiteness is established.
The cube bound by[2sqrt(r(1-r)x(1-x)y(1-y))]^-1 proves finiteness.

2. Spherical expansion and angular normalization

DLMF18.12.4 is the Gegenbauer generating function. Substitute mu=2,
lambda=5 into DLMF18.18.16, and in the connection sum write its original
degree as k+2j. The coefficient becomes

 (k+5)/5 * (2)_(k+j)/(6)_(k+j) * (-3)_j/j!
   =24/[(k+2)(k+3)(k+4)]
          * (k+2)_j(-3)_j/[(k+6)_j j!].

The sum stops at j3. This proves the printed d_k,P_k and the two radial
expressions for f_k. No angular dimension is being continued: it is12.

The degree-k spherical harmonic space in dimension12 has dimension
 h_k=(k+5) C_k^5(1)/5.
Its orthogonal projector kernel is consequently
 (k+5)C_k^5(u dot v)/(5 Omega_12).
Composing projectors gives the printed convolution identity. Since
Omega_12=pi^6/60, the two angular integrations, together with pi^-12,
give exactly1/[144(k+5)^2]. The Gegenbauer generating function at0 gives
C_(2ell)^5(0)=(-1)^ell binom(ell+4,4) and zero in odd degree. These checks
confirm both the angular constant and the alternating sign.

3. All radial regions, including the logarithm

For r<s<1, the r exponent is2k+7+2j. The inner integral gives
s^[2(k+4+j)]/[2(k+4+j)]. The remaining s exponent is2k+5+2j+2l.
This yields1/(L_j T_jl).

For r<1<s, the same inner integral ends at1. The outer exponent is
-2k-7-2l, giving1/(L_j M_l).

For1<r<s, the r exponent is3-2j, so its integral is
(s^(4-2j)-1)/(4-2j), with log(s) when j2. The outer exponent remains
-M_l-1. For M>0 and M-N>0,

 integral_1^infinity s^(-M-1)(s^N-1)/N ds
       =1/[M(M-N)].

At N0, differentiating integral s^(-M-1)=1/M gives the same value1/M^2.
Here N=4-2j and M-N=V_jl>0 for every k>=0,j,l in{0,1,2,3}.
This verifies the third term. Symmetry of the original r,s integral
supplies the factor2. There is no omitted endpoint contribution.

4. Independent justification of the integrated series

Euler's hypergeometric integral gives

 P_k(s)=E[(1-sT)^3], T~Beta(k+2,4), 0<=s<=1.

Hence P_k>=0, and using(a+b)^3<=4(a^3+b^3) gives

 P_k(s)<=4[(1-s)^3+120/((k+6)(k+7)(k+8))].              (B)

Set r=e^x,s=e^y. Direct substitution into the complete radial integral
gives, before any finite expansion of P,

 I_k=d_k^2 integral_(R^2)
   exp[x+y-(k+2)(|x|+|y|)-(k+5)|x-y|]
   P_k(e^(-2|x|)) P_k(e^(-2|y|)) dx dy.                 (C)

Bound exp(x+y)<=exp(|x|+|y|), drop exp[-(k+5)|x-y|]<=1,
and use1-exp(-2|x|)<=2|x| in(B). Then

 integral_R e^(-(k+1)|x|) P_k(e^(-2|x|)) dx=O(k^-4).

Since d_k=O(k^-3), (C) yields I_k=O(k^-14). Projector Cauchy--Schwarz
gives |C_k^5(z)|<=C_k^5(1)=binom(k+9,9). The absolute integrated terms
after angular projection are therefore O(k^-14 k^-2 k^9)=O(k^-7).
Their sum converges. This estimate uses no cancellation in the j,l sum
and no prior use of the claimed rational function R.

For fixed r,s away from r1,s1,r=s, all generating series converge
absolutely and uniformly in the angular variables. Their finite-radius
angular identity can thus first be obtained there. The nonnegative
majorant just proved allows its radial integration by Tonelli/Fubini.
The excluded radial equalities have measure0. This validates the exchange
through the potentially singular loci as well.

5. Exact rational certificates and period reduction

The script independently computes the radial sum at k0,...,30 using
fractions and compares it with the printed R(k). This is a rational-
identity proof, not numerical sampling: the common polynomial

 Q(k)=product_(q=1)^9(k+q)^4

clears both expressions. In each uncombined radial term, d_k^2 contributes
at most double poles at2,3,4; the p_j p_l factors contribute at most double
poles at6,7,8. Each radial denominator contributes two simple linear
factors, and the angular denominator has a double pole only at5. Thus no
pole exceeds order4. Each uncombined expression for R is O(k^-6), so
Q times their difference is a polynomial of degree at most30. Its31
distinct zero values certify that it is zero identically.

Likewise the printed partial-fraction table is checked at36 values.
After multiplication by Q, its difference from R has degree at most35.
These are exact equality certificates with explicit degree costs.

The separate period aggregation gives

 rational301/384, beta2 -503/64, beta4 243/32,
 eta1 -73/24, eta3 9/8,

and zero for beta1,beta3,eta2,eta4. All partial-fraction alternating sums
converge, including the s1 terms, so a finite sum of their limits is valid.
Using eta1=log2 and eta3=3zeta3/4 proves(A). No PSLQ, numerical integral,
or fitted coefficient was used in this review.

6. General fixed-n period-space theorem

This is an analytic extension of the review, not a claim of a useful
arithmetic sequence. Fix n>=0 and put a=n+1,lambda=4n+1, Dspace=8n+4.
The outer expansion has

 d_(n,k)=(k+lambda)/lambda * (n+1)_k/(4n+2)_k,
 P_(n,k)(s)=2F1(k+n+1,-3n;k+4n+2;s).

The same connection formula proves it. Write a_j(k)=[s^j]dP,0<=j<=3n.
Cancelling the initial Pochhammer factors gives the useful exact form

 a_j(k)= [(4n)!/n!] (-1)^j binom(3n,j)
  /[product_(q=n+j+1)^(4n)(k+q)
    product_(q=4n+2)^(4n+j+1)(k+q)].                    (D)

Empty products mean1. In particular each pole of a_j is simple, and
k+lambda is absent from its denominator.

The radial sum is2 sum_(j,l)a_j a_l times the same three fractions,
now with

 L_j=2(k+3n+1+j),       M_l=2(k+2n+1+l),
 T_jl=2(k+2n+1+j+l),    V_jl=2(k+1+j+l).                (E)

For the outer region the logarithmic case is j2n. The same limiting
identity used above applies. Define this complete radial sum as I_n(k),
and put

 C_n(k)=product_(r=1)^(4n)(k+2r)/(2^(4n)(4n)!),
 R_n(k)=4(n!)^4 C_n(k) I_n(k)/[(4n)! (k+lambda)^2].       (F)

The angular and Schwinger constants derived above give exactly

                 J_n=sum_(ell>=0)(-1)^ell R_n(2ell).

The n0 specialization is R_0(k)=6/(k+1)^4, independently confirming
J_0=6 beta4 and the full normalization.

Absolute convergence for fixed n is proved as follows. Euler's integral
writes P_(n,k)(s)=E[(1-sT)^(3n)] with
T~Beta(k+n+1,3n+1). For n>=1,
P_(n,k)(s)<=C(n)[(1-s)^(3n)+k^(-3n)] for k>=1.
The log-coordinate version of(C) has exponent

 n(x+y)-(k+n+1)(|x|+|y|)-(k+4n+1)|x-y|.

The first term is bounded by n(|x|+|y|). Thus its one-variable bound is
O_n(k^(-3n-1)), and d_(n,k)=O_n(k^-3n). Consequently

 I_n(k)=O_n(k^(-12n-2)).

Since C_k^lambda(1)=O_n(k^(8n+1)), the absolute angular terms are
O_n(k^(-4n-3)). For n0 the same estimate follows with P=d=1 directly.
These estimates are for each fixed n; no uniform-in-n conclusion follows.

By(D)--(F), R_n has only poles k=-q for integers1<=q<=8n+1, and every
pole has order at most4. Away from the angular pole, a_j a_l supplies
at most2 orders and the radial fraction at most2. At the angular pole
the a_j factors are regular, leaving at most2+2. Polynomial factors can
only cancel poles. Also R_n is proper: even the uncombined summands are
O_n(k^(-2n-4)). Thus its partial fractions use only orders1,...,4.

There is an exact reflection. Put k'=-2lambda-k and j'=3n-j. Formula(D)
gives a_j(k')=a_j'(k): reflection permutes its3n denominator factors,
and its sign is compensated by(-1)^j binom(3n,j). Formula(E) gives

 L_j(k')=-M_j'(k),  M_l(k')=-L_l'(k),
 T_jl(k')=-V_j'l'(k),  V_jl(k')=-T_j'l'(k).

The first and third radial terms exchange after swapping the two summation
indices; the middle one is preserved. Therefore I_n(k')=I_n(k).
The polynomial C_n and the squared angular factor are also invariant,
so R_n(k')=R_n(k).

If c_(q,s) denotes a partial-fraction coefficient, this reflection says
c_(2lambda-q,s)=(-1)^s c_(q,s). For paired odd q, the beta-shift signs
are equal; hence odd beta orders cancel. For paired even q, the eta-shift
signs are opposite; hence even eta orders cancel. The middle pole q=lambda
is odd and has no odd-order coefficient. Consequently only beta2,beta4,
eta1,eta3 and rational finite-tail terms remain. This proves the stated
five-period space for every fixed n.

7. Coordinator's auxiliary analytic claims

The maximum of P is uniquely at r=x=y=1/2,t1, with value1/256. The printed
expansion log(256P)=-8u^2-4v^2-4w^2-4s+O(u^4+v^4+w^4+s^2) is correct.
The Gaussian factor pi^(3/2)/(8sqrt2), endpoint factor1/4 and denominator
value8/3 give exactly pi^(3/2)/(12sqrt2). The integrable1/D majorant controls
the complement of a fixed neighborhood, so the stated Laplace argument is
valid. The beta-integral normalization K_n also simplifies to the printed
factorial expression. These give nonzero signed decay of J_n itself, not
of a signed combination chosen to eliminate auxiliary periods.

I did not review the three-coordinate Jacobian used to compare with the
old external moments; that is separate from the identity reviewed here.
