ROUND 06 FRESH — A FULL-RANK DIFFERENCE-VARIABLE GRAM CONSTRUCTION
2026-10-29. Local and unpublished.

There is no irrationality proof here. A concrete positive determinant has
exact degree n in theta=beta(4), an unusually simple leading coefficient,
and a genuine real support gap. However, an exact large-prime calculation
proves that its ACTUAL primitive integer values grow at least as

  exp((6-log 4)n^2+o(n^2)).

This retires the specified unshifted principal Gram family. It is not an
obstruction to other frames, moment combinations, weights, determinants,
or beta(4) constructions. In particular, it does not convert the failure
of a sufficient upper denominator bound into an impossibility assertion.

1. Independent start, overlap, and the constructive change

The independent candidate list is 00_initial_ideas.txt. It was saved before
reading the README or archive. The initial ordinary orthogonal-polynomial
proposal for moments (2k+1)^-4 turned out to be exactly the canonical lower
boundary ray in collaboration/round-01/fresh/findings.txt. It was therefore
not pursued again. The elementary translated-Mellin rank-one determinant
was not pursued either.

01_pivot.txt records a move to explicit multiple orthogonality. For

  Q_n(x)=sum_(k=0)^(4n) (-1)^k binom(4n,k)
                       ((k+1/2)_n/n!)^4 x^k,

partial fractions give the exact Mellin formula

  integral_0^1 x^(s-1/2)Q_n(x)dx
    = (4n)!/(n!)^4 * product_(j=0)^(n-1)(s-j)^4
                         / product_(k=0)^(4n)(s+k+1/2).       (1.1)

Consequently Q_n is simultaneously orthogonal to x^j log^l x with
0<=j<n and 0<=l<=3, against x^(-1/2)dx. This formula and 144 exact
orthogonality conditions at n=1,...,8 were checked. All eight primitive
beta(4) scalar forms evaluated in this small diagnostic exceed one in
absolute value. No general sign, arithmetic obstruction, or new advantage
is claimed for this multiple-orthogonal scalar family. It remains a
separate explicitly defined construction, not an irrationality sequence.

The more useful constructive change was to use the archive's cyclotomic
period-isolation kernel with a new DIFFERENCE-VARIABLE Gram frame. Its
period coefficient is full rank, unlike an elementary translated Mellin
Hankel. The kernel itself and its scalar moments are credited to
cyclotomic-integrals.txt; the new work below is the determinant construction,
its exact real rate, and its actual primitive prime law.

2. Positive moments and the exact theta-only formula

For a polynomial P(u,v), put

  J(P)=integral_[0,1]^2
       P(u,v) log^2(uv)/(sqrt(uv)(1+uv)) du dv.

The kernel is integrable and strictly positive in the open square. Define

  mu_m=J((u-v)^(2m))/96,    m>=0,
  T_s(j)=sum_(0<=k<j) (-1)^k/(2k+1)^s,
  C_m=binom(2m,m).

Then

  mu_m=C_m theta+B_m,                                      (2.1)

  B_m= -C_m T_4(m)
        +(1/6) sum_(0<=j<=2m, j!=m)
                         binom(2m,j) T_3(j)/(j-m).           (2.2)

In particular mu_0=theta, B_0=0, and
mu_1=2theta-149/81. All coefficient statements use this FORMAL map;
none assumes theta irrational.

For a self-contained derivation, expansion of 1/(1+uv) and integration
of monomials give

  J(u^i v^i)/96=(-1)^i(theta-T_4(i)),

  J(u^i v^j)/96=-(1/6)(-1)^i
                    (T_3(i)-T_3(j))/(j-i),  i!=j, i+j even.

The log^2 integral is the second common-parameter derivative of
1/((t+i+1/2)(t+j+1/2)). The possible beta(3) terms cancel because
(-1)^i=(-1)^j. Expanding (u-v)^(2m), the diagonal term yields the first
term of (2.2). Pairing j with 2m-j in the off-diagonal terms yields the
second term. Absolute convergence after the two derivatives justifies
the termwise integrations.

3. The full-rank positive determinant

For n>=1 define the rational polynomial

  P_n(X)=det[ C_(i+j) X+B_(i+j) ]_(0<=i,j<n),
  D_n=P_n(theta)=det[mu_(i+j)].                              (3.1)

Under y=(u-v)^2, the mu_m are the moments of a positive measure rho on
[0,1]. Its density, for s=sqrt(y), is

  rho(y)=1/(96s) integral_0^(1-s)
          log^2(v(v+s))/(sqrt(v(v+s))(1+v(v+s))) dv.          (3.2)

It is strictly positive throughout (0,1), so every nonzero polynomial
has positive squared norm. Thus D_n>0 for EVERY n, without an
irrationality assumption.

In contrast, C_m is the moment sequence of the probability arcsine
measure on [0,4]:

  C_m=integral_0^4 y^m/(pi sqrt(y(4-y)))dy.

The monic integer polynomials

  c_0(y)=1,   c_j(y)=2 T_j((y-2)/2), j>=1,

have squared norms 1,2,2,... for this measure and are orthogonal.
Their coefficient change from monomials is unit lower triangular over Z.
Therefore

  det[C_(i+j)]_(i,j<n)=2^(n-1),                             (3.3)

and P_n has EXACT degree n, with leading coefficient 2^(n-1).
The same integral basis change transforms the pencil to

  X diag(1,2,...,2)+R_n,   R_n rational symmetric.

It does not alter P_n or its primitive normalization. This is a genuine
full-rank theta coefficient, not a scalar power or a rank-one determinant.

4. A two-sided real estimate

The support difference [0,1] versus [0,4] produces

  log D_n=-(log 4)n^2+O(n log(n+1)).                        (4.1)

Both sides follow by elementary estimates, rather than an unverified
strong asymptotic theorem.

Upper bound. The monic shifted Chebyshev polynomial of degree j>=1 on
[0,1] has sup norm 2/4^j. Apply the determinant-preserving monic basis
change and Hadamard's Gram inequality. Since rho([0,1])=theta<1,

  D_n<=theta^n 4^(-(n-1)^2)<=4^(-(n-1)^2).                 (4.2)

Lower bound. In (3.2), restrict v to [(1-s)/4,(1-s)/2]. On this interval
v(v+s)<=1/2; hence -log(v(v+s))>=log 2>=1/2, and the denominator of the
integrand is at most 2. The integrand is therefore at least 1/8. It follows
that

  rho(y)>=(1-y)/6144,   0<y<1.                             (4.3)

Gram-matrix ordering, followed by the determinant monotonicity of positive
definite matrices, gives

  D_n>=6144^(-n) det[1/((i+j+1)(i+j+2))]_(i,j<n)
     =6144^(-n) product_(j=0)^(n-1)
                  1/[(2j+2)binom(2j+1,j)^2]
     >=1/[6144^n n! 2^(2n^2+n)].                           (4.4)

The product formula follows from the monic shifted Jacobi polynomials for
weight 1-y. It may also be checked directly by Rodrigues integration by
parts: their squared norms are
j!^2(j+1)!^2/((2j+1)!(2j+2)!). The last inequality uses
binom(2j+1,j)<=2^(2j+1). Equations (4.2) and (4.4) prove (4.1).

5. Exact large-prime residues and ranks

Fix n>=1 and a prime p>4n. All moments used have 0<=m<=2n-2. In this
range m<p/2, 2m<p, and |j-m|<p. The T_4(m) term is p-integral. A T_3(j)
term contains at most one odd denominator divisible by p, namely p itself.
Thus p^3 B_m is p-integral and

  p^3 B_m = chi_4(p)/6 * sum_(j=h)^(2m)
                           binom(2m,j)/(j-m)       (mod p), (5.1)
  h=(p+1)/2,    chi_4(p)=(-1)^((p-1)/2).

The sum is empty if 2m<h. Put

  m0=floor(p/4)+1.

Consequently the leading residues vanish for m<m0. At m=m0 they are
NONZERO whenever m0<=2n-2. Indeed, without the nonzero chi_4(p)/6 factor,
the first residue is

  1/m0,                                  p=4m0-1,
  (2m0-1)(m0+1)/(m0(m0-1)),               p=4m0-3.          (5.2)

The first case has only j=2m0. The second has j=2m0-1,2m0, giving
2m0/(m0-1)+1/m0. None of the displayed numerator or denominator factors
vanishes modulo p in the stated prime range (the potentially small p=5
case is excluded by p>4n and m0<=2n-2).

Let

  r=max(0,2n-1-m0).

The matrix of residues [p^3B_(i+j) mod p] has exactly r nonzero rows and
columns, in its bottom-right r-by-r block. That block is anti-triangular:
entries before its anti-diagonal vanish and the anti-diagonal is the
nonzero first residue (5.2). It therefore has EXACT rank r.

More precisely, every entry outside this bottom-right block is actually
p-integral, not merely of zero leading residue: its index m<m0, so no
T_3 tail in (2.2) contains p. This stronger fact is needed in Section 6.

6. Exact primitive exponent; no unproved content assumption

Let kappa_n be the unique positive rational number for which

  Z_n(X)=kappa_n P_n(X) in Z[X]

has gcd of coefficients one. Since the leading coefficient of P_n is
2^(n-1), for every odd prime p,

  v_p(kappa_n)=-min_j v_p([X^j]P_n).                       (6.1)

The minimum is nonpositive because the leading coefficient is a p-unit.

Theorem. For every prime p>4n,

  v_p(kappa_n)=3 max(0,2n-2-floor(p/4)).                   (6.2)

Proof. In each determinant term at most r entries can have valuation -3,
because only the bottom r rows have nonintegral entries. Therefore every
coefficient of P_n has valuation at least -3r.

After multiplying by p^(3r), the reduction modulo p can receive a
nonzero contribution only by selecting all r bottom rows against all r
bottom columns from the leading residue block. Its complement is the
upper-left pencil of size s=n-r. Thus

  p^(3r)P_n(X) = det(B_lead)*det[X A_top+B_top] (mod p).     (6.3)

For r>0, det(B_lead) is nonzero by (5.2). The coefficient of X^s on the
right is det(B_lead)*2^(s-1), by (3.3), and is nonzero modulo p. Here
s>=1 because p>4n. Hence the valuation -3r is attained by an actual
coefficient. If r=0, all entries are integral and the leading coefficient
already has valuation zero. Equation (6.1) proves (6.2). QED.

This exact formula accounts for full global primitive normalization.
It cannot be removed by an unrecorded coefficient gcd or rational
rescaling. A change to the monic Chebyshev frame likewise leaves it intact.

7. The actual primitive-value obstruction

Sum (6.2) over 4n<p<8n-8. Partial summation and the prime number theorem
give

  sum_(4n<p<8n-8) 3(2n-2-floor(p/4)) log p
       =6n^2+o(n^2).                                     (7.1)

For example, write the sum as
3 integral_(4n)^(8n)(2n-t/4) d vartheta(t)+O(n log n);
its main term is 3 integral_(4n)^(8n)(2n-t/4)dt=6n^2.

At every other odd prime, v_p(kappa_n)>=0 because of the p-unit leading
coefficient. The ONLY possible negative valuation of kappa_n is at 2,
and integrality of the leading coefficient bounds it below by -(n-1).
Consequently

  log kappa_n>=6n^2+o(n^2).                                (7.2)

Combining this ACTUAL primitive multiplier lower bound with the ACTUAL
positive determinant lower bound (4.4) proves

  log Z_n(theta)>=(6-log 4)n^2+o(n^2).                     (7.3)

In particular Z_n(theta)>1 for all sufficiently large n and
Z_n(theta)^(1/n) tends to infinity. The degree-sensitive irrationality
criterion would need small nonzero values relative to degree n; these
values go in the opposite direction. This conclusion is confined to
(3.1), not to arbitrary weights, projections, sparse frames, or filters.

8. A narrow constraint on full-layer positive measure filters

The arithmetic worker pointed out a triangular consequence of the exact
onset in Section 5; the following includes the necessary shifted-moment
integrality restriction. Suppose W(y)=sum_(j=0)^d w_j y^j has p-integral
coefficients, not all zero modulo p, and d<=m0-2 with m0<=2n-2. Then W
cannot make the p^-3 leading residue of EVERY weighted moment
integral y^k W(y)d rho(y), 0<=k<=2n-2, vanish.

To prove this, use successively k=m0-d,m0-d+1,...,m0. All moment indices
in these equations are at most m0+d<=2m0-2<p/2. Thus every one of these
moments has valuation at least -3, and reduction after multiplication by
p^3 is legitimate even when a higher coefficient of W is divisible by p.
The first equation gives w_d=0 mod p because all earlier residues vanish.
The subsequent equations give w_(d-1)=0,...,w_0=0, a contradiction.

If the ACTUAL leading coefficient of W is a p-unit, the first equation
alone gives the stronger obstruction for degree d<=m0. In general the
naive version with d<=m0 and arbitrary p-divisible higher coefficients
would need additional bookkeeping: such coefficients may multiply
shifted moments of valuation -4 and still affect the third layer. That
stronger version is NOT asserted.

Thus a p-primitive weight deleting the full moment layer needs degree
at least m0-1, approximately p/4, in this scoped setting. This says
nothing by itself about partial content savings or selected determinant
subspaces. Positivity of W, including the case W is a square, is allowed
but not needed in this algebraic argument. This result is not used in
the proof of (7.3).
