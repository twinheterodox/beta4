BETA(4): TWO COMPLETE-FAMILY IDENTITY CANDIDATES
30 October 2026. Local and unpublished. No irrationality proof.

Conclusion

Neither candidate supplies a credible improved arithmetic construction.
Candidate A is exactly an already archived family. Candidate B gives a
complete moved-base identity, a positive coefficient formula, and eventual
nonvanishing, but the proposed complementary denominator estimates do not
improve one another. In fact one proved multiplier divides the other for
every n. Even perfect removal of odd-prime denominators cannot rescue
integer clearing of its fixed cofactor normalization.

The last statement is NOT an obstruction to primitive rational rescaling:
a sufficiently large common numerator factor remains an unexcluded way
to change the balance. No such factor is established, and the six exact
primitive examples are unfavorable. The appropriate outcome is to stop
this bounded assessment, not to run a larger parameter scan or claim an
impossibility theorem for hypergeometric methods.

1. Candidate A: the symmetric quadratic deformation is old work

KZ18 Theorem2, specialized before differentiation, identifies the split
half-pole Catalan family with the zero-preserving quarter-pole family.
All parameter-dependent gamma factors cancel exactly. Its second jet
and subsequent beta2 elimination give exactly the primitive beta4 pair
already evaluated in experiments/quarter_pole_deformation.py.

During source comparison the more precise match was located in
half-pole-arithmetic.txt, Sections2-3: that note already contains the
complete identity, the meromorphic formal-coefficient argument, and
stronger denominator estimates. This phase does not claim them as new.

The independent checker confirmed the full normalization and formal
coefficients at n=2,4,8. The n=4,8 primitive pairs agree with the saved
archive; that particular saved table has no n=2 entry. The identity
holds for the whole even-index family, not just
these examples; its derivation imports KZ18. The earlier large primitive
errors are not repaired by another presentation of the same pairs.
No additional large-index scan was performed.

2. Candidate B: a complete transformation with a polynomial part

Write (x)_m for the rising factorial. For integers n>=1 set

 U_n(x,e)=2(x-n+1)_n(x+2n+1)_n /
          [(x+1/2+e)_(2n+1)(x+1/2-e)_(2n+1)],
 D_n(x,e)=(2x+n+1)(x-2n+1)_(2n)(x+n+1)_(2n) /
          [(x+1/2)_(n+1)(x+1/2+e)_(n+1)(x+1/2-e)_(n+1)],
 I_n(e)=sum_(x>=0)(-1)^x U_n(x,e),
 J_n(e)=Abel-sum_(x>=0)(-1)^x D_n(x,e).

Whipple's transformation followed by a numerator permutation gives the
complete identity

 J_n(e)=(-1)^n (n+1)_n(n+1/2+e)_n(n+1/2-e)_n I_n(e).     (1)

The transformed base point differs from the one in round10, so the old
fixed-base stabilizer test does not cover it. The D summand has degree
n-2. Its polynomial Abel contribution is retained, including in every
finite arithmetic check. Simple fixed integer zeros permit recentering
both sums without a boundary correction. This is a parameter deformation,
not the archived triple-zero second-derivative Abel construction.

Both complete families reduce to A(e)H(e)+B(e) with A,B rational and

 H(e)=sum_(k>=0)(-1)^k/[(k+1/2)^2-e^2]
     =4 beta2+16e^2 beta4+... .

Reflection removes the other simple-pole periods. The infinitely many
poles of H prove uniqueness over Q(e), so no independence assumption
about numerical beta values is needed to compare the formal coefficients.

For G_n(e)=(2n)! I_n(e)/8, a terminating transformation gives

 G_n(e)=T_n(e)H(e)/4+B_n(e),
 T_n(e)=sum_(j=0)^n w_(n,j) product_(k=1)^j(1-e^2/k^2)^(-1),
 w_(n,j)=binom(n,j)binom(2n-1/2,j)binom(n+j-1/2,j)>0.     (2)

The exact prefactor derivation is in coordinator/moved-whipple.txt.
Let G0=q beta2+b and G2=4q beta4+c beta2+d, where subscripts0,2 denote
Taylor coefficients. Then q=T_n(0)>0 and c/q is the positive weighted
average of H_j^(2). The beta4-only cofactor form is

 W_n=qG2-cG0=4q^2 beta4+qd-cb.                          (3)

3. Primewise comparison: the apparent improvement disappears

Put C=binom(2n,n), R=(n+1/2)_n/n!, and L_m=lcm(1,...,m).
Elementary binomial/Laurent coefficient bounds from the U presentation,
using the sharper positive formula(2) for q,c, prove that

 M_U W_n has integer coefficients,
 M_U=4 C 16^(2n)L_(4n)^4.                               (4)

Write F=J/n! and kappa(e)=(-1)^n8(n+1/2+e)_n(n+1/2-e)_n/(n!)^2.
Then F=kappa G, and the corresponding exterior form satisfies

 W_F=kappa(0)^2 W_n,       kappa(0)^2=64R^4.

The derivative kappa_2 cancels in the exterior product. The constant
kappa(0)^2 does not disappear from the arithmetic comparison.
The D presentation, INCLUDING its polynomial part, supplies

 M_D=2^(18n+1)L_(2n)^5 kappa(0)^2.                      (5)

The two bounds must be combined with gcd(M_U,M_D). They give no saving:

                         M_U divides M_D.             (6)

For odd p, set a=v_p(L_(2n)), b=v_p(L_(4n)). Legendre's formula gives
v_p(C)<=a and v_p(R)>=b-a. Therefore

 v_p(M_U)=4b+v_p(C)<=4b+a<=5a+4v_p(R)=v_p(M_D).

The explicit power-of-two comparison gives the same ordering. This is
an all-index, all-prime statement about these two proved multipliers.
It is not a claim that either multiplier is the best possible one.

The polynomial part is handled by interpolation at half integers, where
Q_n(k+1/2)=n!k!/(n+k+1)! has denominator dividing L_(n+k+1).
Its Newton basis elements have rational dyadic Abel sums. The complete
argument and all exponents are in coordinator/primewise-comparison.txt.

4. Size, nonvanishing, and an optimistic arithmetic gate

An Euler double integral for F has nth-power factor

 f(x,y)=x^2(1-x)y(1-y)^2/(1-xy)^2.

Its unique maximum occurs at
 x0=(sqrt17-1)/4, y0=(sqrt17-3)/2,
 f0=(85sqrt17-349)/32=0.0457493180469... .

Laplace estimates and the positive sum(2) give

 log|G0|/n -> log f0,
 log q/n -> -log f0,
 G2/G0 ->0,             c/q ->zeta(2).

Thus the beta4-only form(3) is nonzero for all sufficiently large n,
with sign(-1)^(n+1), and log|W_n|/n ->0. Nonvanishing here concerns
the actual signed value, not merely its leading coefficient.
The explicit bound(4) gives

 log|M_U W_n|/n ->16+10log2=22.9314718...>0.

There is also an optimistic test independent of the odd-prime bounds.
The j=n term in(2) has the unique least2-valuation, proving

 v2(q)=-4n+2s2(n),

where s2 counts binary ones. Every positive INTEGER m_n clearing the
coefficients of the fixed form(3) must have

 m_n>=2^(8n-4s2(n)-2),
 liminf log|m_n W_n|/n >=8log2>0.                       (7)

Even removal of every odd-prime denominator would therefore leave this
integer-clearing construction unsuccessful. The qualification is vital:
one may clear denominators and then divide large common integer content,
which corresponds to a RATIONAL multiplier of W_n. Equation(7) does
not exclude that possibility. A large, quantified numerator gcd is the
missing arithmetic lemma; no such all-index estimate is supplied here.

Arb intervals exclude0 in each case. These are finite failures, not a
proof of asymptotic primitive growth.

5. Checks, sources, and remaining scope

One independent model checker audited the complete identities, formal
support, polynomial contributions, positive coefficient formula, primewise
comparison, and analytic/nonvanishing argument. It used a separate exact
D-side implementation; the main agent's implementation uses U residues.
This is an independent model cross-check with exact computations, not
formal verification or an external human audit.

Primary source inputs are KZ18 Theorem2 for A, and the classical Whipple
transformations as stated/derived in Zudilin2002 and Karp--Prilepkina2022
for B. Relevant proofs were read; standard imported summations and the
inaccessible Bailey/Rahman dependencies are explicitly recorded in
coordinator/sources.txt. No claim is made to have checked every dependency
from first principles, or to establish priority for these constructions.
