OPTIMIZED SAME-MAP COMPARISON: INDEPENDENT AUDIT
30 October 2026. Local and unpublished. No irrationality proof.

Verdict
-------
The proposed clipped-envelope inequality is correct under the inherited
CDT hypotheses, with independently known holonomicity. No assumption
beta_l<=d is required. There is no missing endpoint term when the integral
of the actual lower envelope is used. A closed, un-clipped version of
(7.6.2) WOULD generally miss an endpoint correction when beta_l>d.

For the same fixed weights and radii, B_c(s)<=B_m(s) for c<m. Consequently
the companion comparison is valid at the full family's chosen weights
s_m. At the weights furnished by CDT (7.6.5), with r_l=1, B_m(s_m) is
exactly the numerator in (7.6.6) and (8.0.5). This extends the previously
audited no-gain comparison to those numerators for the stated flat
denominator profiles. It does not require comparing separately optimized
weights for the two dimensions.

The explicit primitive I(t)=integral_0^t A0(u)du is also valid: it gives
one genuinely new unconditional companion, a conditional rank18 family,
and the stated total cost 931/324.

2. Precise statement, including hypotheses
-----------------------------------------
Let f1,...,fd be Q(t)-linearly independent elements of Q[[t]], each
independently known to be holonomic. Give them certified denominator
types satisfying the flat cap-column hypotheses of CDT Sections6-7,
with the allowed fixed initial factors/offsets treated as in Remark6.0.12.
Let tau_d=tau_flat(b)+tau_sharp(e) for that family.

Let phi:(D,0)->(C,0) be holomorphic with phi'(0) nonzero and suppose all
fi(phi(z)) are germs of meromorphic functions throughout D. Set

  a=log|phi'(0)|.

Fix FINITELY MANY positive radii 0<r0<...<r_l<=1 and a fixed vector
s_h>=0, sum s_h=1. These choices are independent of the auxiliary
polynomial degree D used in the slopes proof. Write

  G_hk = L_(r_h) . L_(r_k)
       = integral_T^2 log|phi(r_h z)-phi(r_k w)| dmu(z)dmu(w),
  u_k  = (G s)_k,
  Q_s  = s^T G s,
  E_s(x)=min_k [u_k-x log r_k],
  B_d(s)=-Q_s+(2/d) integral_0^d E_s(x) dx.

The intersection numbers must be well-defined and finite for a finite
numerical bound. The inherited limiting interpretation at radius1 is
the one in Lemma7.4.1: first work on compact subdiscs, then pass to the
outer-radius limit. If needed, impose the usual neighborhood regularity
first and use that limit. No boundary regularity stronger than the
source's limiting setup is being asserted here.

Then the UNDIVIDED inequality

  d(a-tau_d) <= B_d(s)                                      (A)

holds. It does not need beta_l<=d. With holonomicity supplied independently,
it also does not need a>tau_d: positivity of this denominator is needed
only to express (A) as a quotient upper bound on d.

For the identification with the exact printed numerator of (7.6.6),
the final radius must be r_l=1, as it is in CDT. Positive radii are
essential: r=0 cannot be included in the logarithms or covering metric.
Nonnegative weights are essential to the inherited metric/convexity
setup. No positive-semidefinite matrix assumption on G is used.

3. Proof of the clipped inequality and sign check
------------------------------------------------
Use CDT's evaluation lattice E_D for the d-function family, but equip
its archimedean summands with the weighted metric

  L_s=product_h L_(r_h)^(s_h).

It is still the underlying bundle O(1); its curvature is a convex
combination of the nonnegative curvatures. The mixed intersections are
L_s.L_(r_k)=u_k and L_s.L_s=Q_s. The finite-place lattice construction
and finite-height estimates do not depend on this change of metric.

The proof of Lemma7.4.1, as explicitly invoked in Section7.6, gives for
each fixed k and every filtration jump n:

  h_infinity(psi_D^(n))
    <= -n a -n log r_k +D u_k +o(D+n).

Because there are finitely many radii, the errors may be bounded
uniformly over k. Choose the best k separately for each n. Then

  h_infinity(psi_D^(n))
    <= D[-a(n/D)+E_s(n/D)] +o(D+n).                        (B)

This is the direct lower-envelope estimate; it is valid regardless of
where the envelope's breakpoints fall relative to d.

The jump-set point is important. Linear independence gives
#V_D=d(D+1). Independent holonomicity and Theorem3.2.13 give, for every
epsilon>0 and large D,

  V_D subset [0,(d+epsilon)(D+1)+C(epsilon)].

Hence its difference from the integer interval [0,dD] has at most
O(epsilon D)+O_epsilon(1) elements. The function

  F(x)=-a x+E_s(x)

is a finite lower envelope of affine functions plus an affine function;
it is continuous and bounded on each [0,d+epsilon]. In particular,
both positive and negative omitted terms are controlled. One must not
silently assume the height main terms are nonnegative. Bounding their
absolute value on this compact interval shows that replacing the
jump-set sum of D F(n/D) by the full interval sum costs only
O(epsilon D^2)+o_epsilon(D^2). The endpoint D versus D+1 also contributes
only O(D). First take D->infinity and then epsilon->0. Thus (B) yields

  limsup D^(-2) sum_(n in V_D) h_infinity(psi_D^(n))
    <= -d^2 a/2 + integral_0^d E_s(x) dx.                  (C)

There is no surviving term from n>dD, nor from clipped slope intervals.

For completeness, retain the arithmetic parameters y_h and xi used in
Section7.3. The degree and finite-height terms are

  deg(E_D)/D^2
    = (d/2)Q_s +sum_h u_h^cap y_h -xi sum_i e_i +o(1),

  limsup D^(-2) sum_(n in V_D) h_fin(psi_D^(n))
    <= (max_i e_i) I_xi^d(xi)
       +(1/2)[sigma_d d^2+sum_h y_h^2/b_h].

Here u_h^cap is the number of initial zero rows in denominator column h;
it is unrelated to the notation u_k=(Gs)_k. Combining these inequalities
with deg(E_D)<=sum h_infinity+sum h_fin gives

  d^2(a-sigma_d)
    <= -d Q_s+2 integral_0^d E_s
       +sum_h(y_h^2/b_h-2u_h^cap y_h)
       +2[xi sum_i e_i+(max_i e_i)I_xi^d(xi)].

Take y_h=b_h u_h^cap and optimize xi exactly as in CDT. This is

  d^2(a-tau_d)<=-d Q_s+2 integral_0^d E_s,

which is (A). In particular the minus sign in front of Q_s and the
factor 2/d in the definition of B_d are correct.

An equivalent explicitly clipped formula is available. For r_l=1 set
beta_k=(u_k-u_(k-1))/(log r_k-log r_(k-1)), beta_0=0. Convexity gives
0<=beta_1<=...<=beta_l. Set b_0=0, b_k=min(beta_k,d), b_(l+1)=d.
Then

  B_d=-Q_s+(2/d)sum_(k=0)^l [
       (b_(k+1)-b_k)u_k
       -(b_(k+1)^2-b_k^2)log r_k/2].                     (D)

Intervals whose left endpoint is beyond d have zero length. Formula(D)
or the envelope integral should be used for the companion dimension;
one cannot automatically reuse the un-clipped square-savings formula
from (7.6.2).

4. Monotonicity and the selected full-family weights
--------------------------------------------------
Each function u_k-x log r_k is nondecreasing in x because r_k<=1.
Their minimum E_s is therefore nondecreasing. This requires no sign
assumption on u_k or E_s. The average of a nondecreasing function on
[0,d] is nondecreasing in d; equivalently, where differentiable,

  d/dd [(1/d)integral_0^d E_s]
    =[d E_s(d)-integral_0^d E_s]/d^2 >=0.

Since Q_s is fixed,

  B_c(s)<=B_m(s),             0<c<m.                      (E)

Now take r_l=1 and let s_m be the actual nonnegative solution of CDT
(7.6.5) for dimension m, under that theorem's existence/uniqueness
hypothesis. Its equations say

  beta_k=m sum_(h<k) s_h,
  beta_(k+1)-beta_k=m s_k,

where beta_0=0 and beta_(l+1)=m. Thus beta_l<=m automatically.
Writing u=Gs_m and Q_s=s_m^T G s_m, the integral formula and a telescoping
sum give

  B_m(s_m)
    =u_l-Q_s+(1/m)sum_k(beta_(k+1)-beta_k)u_k
    =u_l-Q_s+sum_k s_k u_k
    =u_l
    =sum_h s_h G_hl.                                      (F)

This is precisely the numerator of (7.6.6). It is also the numerator
of (8.0.5), because that numerator is

  G_ll-sum_(h<l)s_h(G_ll-G_hl)=sum_h s_h G_hl.

If r_l<1, the analogous identity is B_m=u_l-m log r_l, so the condition
r_l=1 must be retained for the literal identification (F).

The word 'optimized' should here mean the weights selected by (7.6.5).
Remark7.6.7 presents a stationary-action motivation and explicitly says
the general existence/positivity property is not known. The comparison
does not need a claim that these weights are a global minimizer. It
actually works for every fixed admissible s through (A) and(E).

5. Consequence for the companion comparison
-------------------------------------------
Use the SAME phi, SAME radii, and SAME weights s_m for the c-function
unconditional companion and m=c+r function full family. Applying (A)
to the companion, then(E), gives

  c(a-tau_c)<=B_c(s_m)<=B_m(s_m).                          (G)

A contradiction from the full-family optimized bound would require

  B_m(s_m)<m(a-tau_m),       with a>tau_m.

Together with(G), this would force

  r a>m tau_m-c tau_c.                                    (H)

For the audited profiles with c cheap companions, r functions requiring
four LCM layers, and a certified fixed collection of first primitives,

  tau_m>=4(1-c^2/m^2)+tau_sharp(m,k),
  tau_c=tau_sharp(c,k),
  m tau_sharp(m,k)>=c tau_sharp(c,k).

The last relation, including k=c, was proved in the first audit. Thus
the right-hand side of(H) is at least

  4r(1+c/m)>4r.

It follows that this contradiction would need a>4. The mandatory-cusp
bound a<=log Rstar<13/10 excludes it. Since the base inequality(G) is
undivided and its family is independently holonomic, there is no need
to add a separate a>tau_c hypothesis in this argument.

For the flat cap-column profiles, Remark8.0.6 proves
tau_flatflat(b)=tau_flat(b). Therefore (8.0.5) uses the same arithmetic
cost in this comparison and is covered as well. This audit does not
extend that conclusion to arbitrary denominator arrays or new types.
