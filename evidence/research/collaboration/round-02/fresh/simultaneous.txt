A COMPATIBLE THREE-BLOCK APPROXIMATION AFTER THE COMPLETED LADDER
28 October 2026. Local and unpublished; companion to findings.txt.

Status
------
This is a genuinely different construction from the scalar Taylor truncation
retired in findings.txt. It uses different polynomial multipliers for the
three interior arguments, with matched values at the evaluation point, and
a shared approximation to log2 and log^2 2. I derive:
  * exact beta(4)-only rational forms with common leading coefficient 25^n;
  * a uniform, explicitly positive leading error asymptotic, hence eventual
    nonvanishing, before integer clearing;
  * an explicit integer clearing factor retaining some dyadic savings.
That factor is too large for an irrationality proof. All twelve primitive
forms checked have absolute value >1. These finite checks DO NOT prove
that the simultaneous family fails asymptotically. No structured determinant,
optimally chosen simultaneous Pade system, or other arbitrary approximation
is ruled out. I have no credible favorable primitive arithmetic estimate.

1. Matched pole polynomials
--------------------------
Use z_1=i/2, z_3=(1+i)/4, z_5=(1-i)/8 and the coefficients c_j,alpha_j
of findings.txt, (5)--(6). The polynomials with zeros at 1/z_j and
1/conj(z_j) are
 P_1(t)=t^2+4,
 P_3(t)=t^2-4t+8,
 P_5(t)=t^2-8t+32.
Crucially,
 P_1(1)=P_3(1)=5,       P_5(1)=25.
Thus their powers (2n,2n,n) ALL evaluate to 25^n. Independent approximants
to the three blocks can be combined without multiplying three unrelated
leading coefficients.

Let T_N denote truncation at degree N about t=0, evaluated at t=1 when
followed by '(1)'. Let I_s(z_j t) be the rational analytic imaginary-part
germ defined in findings.txt. Set
 m_1=m_3=2n, m_5=n,
 N_j=2m_j+2n,  so (N_1,N_3,N_5)=(6n,6n,4n),
 A_(j,s)=[T_(N_j)(P_j(t)^(m_j) I_s(z_j t))](1), s=2,3,4.     (S1)
All A_(j,s) are rational.

For the logarithms use ell(t)=-log(1-t/2), and
 B_r=[T_(8n)((2-t)^(4n) ell(t)^r)](1), r=1,2.                 (S2)
The multiplier has value 1 at t=1. In particular B_2 is a direct
approximation to log^2 2, not the square of a separately approximated log.
Define
 R_n=sum_j c_j [A_(j,4)+alpha_j B_1 A_(j,3)
                            +(alpha_j^2/3)B_2 A_(j,2)],
 Lambda_n=25^n beta(4)-R_n.                                  (S3)
This is an exact beta(4)-only rational form by the classical identity (5).
It does not insert a numerically selected rational approximation to beta(4).

2. Exact errors for the individual polylogarithm blocks
-----------------------------------------------------
For any of the three z=z_j, P=P_j, put eta=z/conj(z), m=m_j,
N=2m+ell, and
 E_s=25^n I_s(z)-[T_N(P(t)^m I_s(zt))](1).
Direct summation of the Taylor tail, using the integral for 1/k^s, gives

 E_s=Im{ z^(ell+1)/(s-1)! integral_0^1
          x^ell [(1-x)(1-eta x)]^m (-log x)^(s-1)/(1-zx) dx }. (S4)

Indeed
 P(1/(zx))=(1-x)(1-eta x)/(z^2 x^2),
and for a polynomial Q of degree D the tail is
 z^(N+1) integral_0^1 x^N Q(1/(zx))(-log x)^(s-1)/(1-zx) dx/(s-1)!.
All interchanges are justified by absolute convergence for |z|<1.
Formula (S4) also shows why truncating merely at N=2m is inadequate:
there is no vanishing x^ell factor at x=0, and the absolute source error
then decreases only polynomially. The extra 2n orders in (S1) are essential.

3. Logarithm errors and a common nonvanishing asymptotic
------------------------------------------------------
Write l=log2, delta_r=l^r-B_r. For k=4n and h>k the exact coefficients are
 [t^h](2-t)^k ell(t)
    =(-1)^k 2^(k-h)/[h binom(h-1,k)] =: b_h,
 [t^h](2-t)^k ell(t)^2
    =2b_h(H_(h-k-1)-H_k).                                    (S5)
One derivation differentiates 2^k(1-t/2)^(k-a) once or twice at a=0.
For h>8n, b_h>0 and the harmonic difference is nonnegative. Consequently
 delta_1>0, delta_2>=0. Also
 delta_1=2^(-4n-1) integral_0^1 x^(4n)(1-x)^(4n)/(1-x/2) dx
         <=4096^(-n).
Since b_(h+1)/b_h=(h-k)/(2(h+1))<1/2 and
 H_(h-k-1)-H_k <= (h-8n-1)/(4n+1), summing a geometric first moment gives
 0<=delta_2 <=4 delta_1/(4n+1).                               (S6)

The exact error decomposition from (S3) is
 Lambda_n=sum_j c_j [E_(j,4)+alpha_j l E_(j,3)
                   +(alpha_j^2/3)l^2 E_(j,2)
                   +alpha_j delta_1 A_(j,3)
                   +(alpha_j^2/3)delta_2 A_(j,2)].             (S7)
The A values are O(25^n), so the last two terms are O((25/4096)^n).

For j=3, formula (S4) gives a uniform bound O(64^(-n)) for each s=2,3,4:
 |z_3|^(2n)=2^(-3n), and
 x^2(1-x)^2(1+x^2)/8 <=1/64.
For j=5 it similarly gives O(144^(-n)), using
 x^2(1-x)<=4/27 and sqrt(1+x^2)<3/2, so
 x^2(1-x)sqrt(1+x^2)/32 <=1/144.
The constants absorb |z_j|, 1/(1-|z_j|), and the integrable log powers.

For j=1, eta=-1 and ell=2n. The completed error is exactly

 E_(1,4)+l E_(1,3)+(l^2/3)E_(1,2)
 =(-1)^n 2^(-2n-1) integral_0^1 [x^2(1-x^2)^2]^n
        [(-log x)^3/6+l(-log x)^2/2+l^2(-log x)/3]
                         /(1+x^2/4) dx.                     (S8)

The integrand is positive. Its exponential factor has a unique interior
maximum at x_0=1/sqrt3, equal to 4/27. For
 f(x)=log[x^2(1-x^2)^2], one has f''(x_0)=-18.
Ordinary one-dimensional Laplace asymptotics (Taylor expansion at the
unique maximum and exponential domination away from it) therefore gives

 Lambda_n ~ (-1)^n K 27^(-n)/sqrt(n),                          (S9)
 K=(225/884)sqrt(pi) [u^3/6+l u^2/2+l^2 u/3] >0,
 u=(log3)/2.

Here the j=3, j=5, and logarithm errors are exponentially smaller than
27^(-n)/sqrt(n). Thus Lambda_n is nonzero, has sign (-1)^n, and tends to
zero for every sufficiently large n. This is a source-form statement,
NOT a shrinking-integer-form statement.

4. An explicit compatible integer clearing factor
------------------------------------------------
Put L_M=lcm(1,...,M). Then

 D_n=17 * 2^(9n+4) * L_(6n)^2 * L_(8n)^2                       (S10)

satisfies D_n R_n in Z. Hence
 D_n 25^n beta(4)-D_n R_n
is a nonzero integer linear form for all sufficiently large n.

Proof of the dyadic saving: for j=1,3,5 the polynomial P_j(t)^m has
coefficient valuation at least ceil(alpha_j(2m-r)) at t^r. Check this
on the three coefficients of P_j and use convolution. Since
2^ceil(alpha_j k) Im(z_j^k) is an integer, every summand of a coefficient
of total degree <=2m+ell has dyadic denominator dividing
2^ceil(alpha_j ell), apart from the denominator k^s. Thus
 2^(2alpha_j n) L_(N_j)^s A_(j,s) is an integer.                (S11)
The same argument with (2-t)^(4n) and ell(t)^r gives
 2^(4n) L_(8n)^r B_r in Z.                                   (S12)
The largest extra dyadic cost in products is 5n+4n=9n.
The odd and remaining small-prime denominators divide
L_(6n)^4, L_(6n)^3 L_(8n), or L_(6n)^2 L_(8n)^2, respectively;
the last one is a common multiple of all three. The fixed rational
coefficients c_j alpha_j^r / (3 if r=2 else 1) have common denominator
17*16. This proves (S10).

Without matching the pole multipliers, the elementary termwise bound for
this same choice of truncation orders would have an extra dyadic factor
as large as 2^(18n); (S11)--(S12) reduce that to 2^(9n). This is a real
arithmetic compatibility, but still far from the required estimate.
By the prime number theorem,
 log D_n=(28+9 log2)n+o(n),
whereas -log|Lambda_n|=(log27)n+O(log n). The displayed clearing bound
therefore DOES NOT yield shrinking integer forms. This comparison is
not a lower bound for primitive heights and is not an impossibility
theorem for this simultaneous construction.

5. Exact finite diagnostics and remaining gap
--------------------------------------------
Run from beta4/:
 .venv/bin/python collaboration/round-02/fresh/simultaneous.py
This constructs one fixed family at n=1,...,12; it does not scan
parameter grids. It reduces the two integer coefficients by their gcd
and evaluates the primitive forms using Arb at 1200 bits.
All twelve source forms have the predicted sign and all twelve primitive
absolute values exceed one. Their log absolute primitive values run
from about 17.8007 at n=1 to 317.7174 at n=12. The integer-pair gcd in
these twelve cases is one. Exact coefficients and certificates are in
simultaneous_results.json.

The theorem (S9) establishes eventual nonvanishing and exponential decay
before arithmetic clearing. The missing estimate is a favorable bound
for the primitive coefficients, or a different simultaneous numerator
construction preserving the matched value 25^n while saving much more
than (S10). Period cancellation by itself does not furnish that saving.
The n=1,...,12 data do not establish an asymptotic primitive obstruction.
No general Pade, block-determinant, or independent-embedding construction
has been excluded. In particular, the scalar dyadic denominator theorem
of findings.txt says nothing by itself about determinant content or the
rank of a scaled leading residue matrix.

The match P_1(1)=P_3(1)=5,P_5(1)=25 and formulas (S4),(S9),(S10) give a
concrete starting point should another round find a structural arithmetic
mechanism. I do not have a credible reason at present to enlarge this
fixed family's numerical tests or to claim an irrationality result.
