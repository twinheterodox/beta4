CAYLEY TRANSPORT, A COMPLETED TETRALOGARITHM LADDER, AND ITS ARITHMETIC
28 October 2026. Local, unpublished fresh-construction investigation.

Status and scope
----------------
No irrationality proof is obtained. This is an independent path, not an audit
of the slow-filter determinant. The first idea was saved in 00_initial_idea.txt
before reading the archive. The useful exact output is a beta(4)-only identity
in three completed tetralogarithms, with all arguments of modulus at most 1/2.
For one natural rational Taylor realization I prove its reduced denominator
exactly at 2 and prove that EVERY sufficiently late primitive integer form
grows. The obstruction is to that specified truncation family only.

The completed identity follows algebraically from classical Broadhurst
identities. I make no novelty claim for it. The Taylor construction and its
valuation calculation were not found in the archive sections actually read;
this is not a literature-wide priority determination. Numerical checks are
checks, not proofs of a classical functional identity or of irrationality.

A subsequent, structurally different simultaneous construction is saved in
simultaneous.txt. Its three pole multipliers share the evaluation 25^n;
its rational source form has a proved alternating nonzero asymptotic
K*27^(-n)/sqrt(n). Its clearing bound is unfavorable and twelve primitive
examples fail, but no asymptotic primitive obstruction is asserted for it.

1. The initially proposed Cayley transport
----------------------------------------
Put r=sqrt(2)-1, L=-log(r)=log(1+sqrt(2)), and
 F_s(x)=sum_{k>=0} (-1)^k x^(2k+1)/(2k+1)^s.
The involution phi(t)=(1-t)/(1+t) fixes r and satisfies
 dx/(1+x^2)=-dt/(1+t^2),       dlog(x)=-2dt/(1-t^2).
Splitting the defining logarithmic integral at r gives EXACTLY

 beta(4)= F_4(r)+L F_3(r)+(L^2/2)F_2(r)+(L^3/6)F_1(r)+J(r),       (1)
 J(r)=(1/6) integral_0^r log^3((1+t)/(1-t))/(1+t^2) dt,
 F_1(r)=pi/8.

Proof: beta(4)=(1/6) integral_0^1 (-log x)^3/(1+x^2) dx. On
[0,r], integrate the geometric series; the incomplete gamma integral gives
the first four terms in (1). On [r,1], make the Cayley substitution.
The new differential 2dt/(1-t^2), absent from the original Euler tower,
explains the new mixed iterated integral J. Merely using the fixed point
does not express beta(4) in the four values F_s(r) alone.

There is also an arithmetic problem with a naive quadratic trace. The
conjugate of r is r'=-1/r. Consequently
 Tr(r^N)=r^N+(-1/r)^N
is an integer of size asymptotic to r^(-N), and the traced Taylor terms of
F_s(r) do not even tend to zero. Analytic continuation of F_s at r' cannot
be exchanged with this divergent term-by-term trace.
More generally, if theta is rational with denominator d and
 a,b in Z[r], d(a theta-b) is an algebraic integer. If nonzero, its norm
implies
 |a theta-b| |a' theta-b'| >= d^(-2).
Thus an r^N gain at one real embedding is exactly offset by r^(-N) at the
other for the basic unit powers. This does NOT rule out simultaneous
approximants that control both embeddings; it rules out the proposed free
trace shortcut. No unjustified independence of the periods in (1) is used.

2. Classical input and explicit cancellation of product periods
---------------------------------------------------------------
For |z|<1 define
 D_4(z)=Im[Li_4(z)-log|z| Li_3(z)+(log^2|z|/3)Li_2(z)].           (2)
This is the usual completed (single-valued) classical tetralogarithm on
this disk. Only the displayed formula and the classical identities below
are needed; no theorem about single-valued periods is being assumed to
remove products for free.

Put l=log 2, b1=pi/4, b3=pi^3/32,
 w=(1+i)/2, u=(1+i)/4, v=(1-i)/8, and a=i/2.
Write I_s(z)=Im Li_s(z), and use RAW (unbarred) Broadhurst ladders
 P_s=2^(s-1) I_s(w),
 Q_s=(2/3)^(s-1) I_s(u)+I_s(a),
 R_s=(2/5)^(s-1) I_s(v)+2 I_s(a).
The conjugations in Q and R turn the paper's minus I_s(-i/2) terms into
plus I_s(i/2). Equations (39)--(46), (54)--(55) of [B] give:

 Q_2-(2/3)P_2 = -b1*l/3,
 Q_3-(2/3)P_3 = -b3+b1*l^2/6,
 Q_4-(2/3)P_4 = -(80/27)beta(4)+b3*l-b1*l^3/18;                 (3)

 R_2-(4/5)P_2 = -b1*l/5,
 R_3-(4/5)P_3 = -(23/25)b3+b1*l^2/10,
 R_4-(4/5)P_4 = -(384/125)beta(4)+(23/25)b3*l-b1*l^3/30.        (4)

The source uses corrected ladders with L_j=(-l)^j/j!, so the conversion
to raw ladders matters for these signs and coefficients.
For any of P,Q,R set Xhat_4=X_4+l X_3+(l^2/3)X_2. Summing (3) and
(4) with precisely these coefficients cancels every displayed product:
 Qhat_4-(2/3)Phat_4=-(80/27)beta(4),
 Rhat_4-(4/5)Phat_4=-(384/125)beta(4).
The cubic coefficients cancel because
 -1/18+1/6-1/9=0,           -1/30+1/10-1/15=0.
Also
 Phat_4=8D_4(w),
 Qhat_4=(8/27)D_4(u)+D_4(a),
 Rhat_4=(8/125)D_4(v)+2D_4(a).
Eliminating Phat_4 leaves the exact identity

 beta(4)=(225/136)D_4(i/2) -(25/34)D_4((1+i)/4)
                                      +(9/68)D_4((1-i)/8).    (5)

The largest argument w has disappeared. Its elimination is useful for
fast evaluation, but (2) still contains logarithms multiplying lower
polylogarithms. It is not a pure ordinary-polylogarithm BBP expansion.

3. A rational Taylor realization
--------------------------------
Let z_1=i/2, z_3=(1+i)/4, z_5=(1-i)/8 and set
 (c_1,c_3,c_5)=(225/136,-25/34,9/68),
 (alpha_1,alpha_3,alpha_5)=(1,3/2,5/2),
 ell(t)=-log(1-t/2).
Use I_s(z_j t) to mean the rational-coefficient analytic germ
 [Li_s(z_j t)-Li_s(conj(z_j)t)]/(2i), not complex conjugation of t.
Define
 H(t)=sum_{j=1,3,5} c_j [I_4(z_j t)+alpha_j ell(t) I_3(z_j t)
                          +(alpha_j^2/3)ell(t)^2 I_2(z_j t)]
     =sum_{n>=1} h_n t^n.                                    (6)
Every h_n is rational, H is analytic for |t|<2, and H(1)=beta(4).
This follows since log|z_j|=-alpha_j log2. Its singularity set includes
 2, +/-2i, 2+/-2i, 4+/-4i.
The first coefficient is h_1=341/544.
Let S_N=sum_{n=1}^N h_n=p_N/q_N in lowest terms, q_N>0.
Then q_N beta(4)-p_N is a genuine integer linear form, not a
continued-fraction encoding and not a positive-integral re-encoding.

4. Exact dyadic denominator theorem
----------------------------------
For N>=1, write N=4m+r, 0<=r<4. Then

 v_2(q_N)=E_N,  where
 E_(4m)  =10m+4  (m>=1),
 E_(4m+1)=10m+5  (m>=0),
 E_(4m+2)=10m+11 (m>=0),
 E_(4m+3)=10m+12 (m>=0).                                      (7)

There are NO exceptions at positive N: E_1=5,E_2=11,E_3=12,E_4=14.
For N=0 separately S_0=0,q_0=1,E_0=0. In particular the displayed
r=0 formula is NOT valid at N=0.
In fact v_2(h_N)=-E_N. Since E_N strictly increases, summing coefficients
cannot cancel the last, uniquely most negative, valuation.

Proof. The z_5 contribution controls the valuation. For k not divisible
by 4,
 v_2(Im z_5^k)=-ceil(5k/2),        v_2(k) in {0,1};
for 4|k the imaginary part is zero. Also
 [t^j]ell(t)=1/(2^j j),
 [t^j]ell(t)^2=2^(1-j) H_(j-1)/j  (j>=2),
 v_2(H_m)=-floor(log_2 m).
The last equality holds because among 1,...,m exactly one denominator
has maximal 2-adic valuation.
After including c_5=9/68 and alpha_5=5/2, the nonzero summands contributing
to h_n have the following valuations (k=n-j):

 T0(n)=-ceil(5n/2)-4v_2(n)-2,
 T1(k,j)=-ceil(5k/2)-3v_2(k)-j-v_2(j)-3,
 T2(k,j)=-ceil(5k/2)-2v_2(k)-j-v_2(j)
                                      -floor(log_2(j-1))-3.    (8)

T0 requires 4 not dividing n; T1 requires j>=1 and 4 not dividing k;
T2 requires j>=2 and the same condition on k. The unique minimum is:
 n mod4=1: T0(n);         n mod4=2: T0(n);
 n mod4=3: T1(n-1,1);     n mod4=0: T1(n-2,2).
These values are exactly -E_n.
Here is a finite check sufficient to certify the small-offset part of
that assertion. Entries are the gaps above -E_n; a dash means zero term.
For each n residue, the columns are
   T0; T1(j=1,2,3,4); T2(j=2,3,4):
 r=0: --;  2,0,5,-- ; 1,4,--
 r=1:  0; --,1,1,3  ; 1,1,2
 r=2:  0;  4,--,7,4 ; --,6,4
 r=3:  2;  0,3,--,5 ; 3,--,4.
Only terms with positive k are included for small n; the unique minimum
listed above exists for every n>=1.

For completeness, all larger offsets are rigorously above the minimum.
Relative to -(ceil(5n/2)+2), the T1 gap is at least
 floor(3j/2)-v_2(j)-4,
and the T2 gap is at least
 floor(3j/2)-v_2(j)-floor(log_2(j-1))-3.
For j>=5 the first is positive (use v_2(j)<=j/2). The second is positive
for j=5,...,9 by direct evaluation; for j>=10 use both
 v_2(j)<=j/2 and floor(log_2(j-1))<=j/2.
Since E_n>=ceil(5n/2)+2, these are also above -E_n.

No other ladder can cancel this unique term. The corresponding z_3
summand (same k,j and log power) has valuation larger by k+1. This
follows from v_2(Im z_3^k)=-ceil(3k/2), v_2(c_3)=-1 and
v_2(alpha_3)=-1. For z_1 only odd k occur. Its pure Li4 term has valuation
larger than the corresponding z_5 term by ceil(3k/2)-1>=1; the one-log
and two-log differences are ceil(3k/2) and ceil(3k/2)+1. Thus all other
summands have strictly higher valuation. This proves (7).

5. Real error asymptotic and a complete failure theorem for (6)
--------------------------------------------------------------
Write H=C+ell B+ell^2 A, where
 A(t)=sum_j c_j alpha_j^2 I_2(z_j t)/3.
The coefficient sequences of A,B,C obey respectively
 O(2^(-n)/n^2), O(2^(-n)/n^3), O(2^(-n)/n^4).
Their weighted sums at t=2 are therefore absolutely convergent. Since
 [t^n]ell^2=2^(1-n)H_(n-1)/n,
an elementary convolution estimate gives

 h_n=2^(-n)[2 A(2) log(n)/n+O(1/n)],
 beta(4)-S_N ~ 2 A(2) 2^(-N) log(N)/N.                         (9)

One way to justify the convolution estimate without invoking a transfer
theorem is to split the sum over the A index k at n/2. For k<=n/2,
expand H_(n-k-1)/(n-k) around H_(n-1)/n, using
 |2^k[t^k]A|=O(k^-2). The accumulated change is
O(log^2 n/n^2). For k>n/2, sum O(n^-2) times
H_(n-k-1)/(n-k), again O(log^2 n/n^2). The constant term is A(2).
The B contribution is O(2^-n/n), and C is smaller. Summing the geometric
tail yields the second part of (9): sum_{n>N}2^-n log(n)/n is asymptotic
to 2^-N log(N)/N.

The constant is strictly positive without assuming anything about Catalan:

 A(2)=(75/272)[pi log2/4-Im Li_2((1+i)/4)].                     (10)

Indeed direct substitution first gives
 A(2)=(75/136)[G-Im Li_2((1+i)/2)]
                         -(75/272)Im Li_2((1+i)/4),
and the classical weight-two identity gives
 Im Li_2((1+i)/2)=G-pi log2/8.
For u=(1+i)/4, |u|=1/(2sqrt2)<3/8, and
 Im Li_2(u) <= Im u + sum_{k>=2}|u|^k/k^2
            <=1/4+|u|^2/[4(1-|u|)] < 3/10.
Also pi>3 and log2>2/3 (the first term of 2atanh(1/3)), so
 pi log2/4>1/2. Hence A(2)>15/272>0.
Numerically A(2)=0.07166185354607449552915678623357006933... .
The number is a diagnostic; (10) and the inequalities prove positivity.

Combining (7) and (9), the primitive integer forms satisfy eventually

 q_N beta(4)-p_N >= A(2) 2^(E_N-N) log(N)/N

for every sufficiently large N. In particular it is at least
c 2^(3N/2) log(N)/N for a fixed c>0,

so they tend to +infinity, at least exponentially at rate
 (3/2)log2 per N. This theorem uses only the prime 2; odd-prime
denominators make the actual recorded values much larger.
The exact asymptotic is asserted for the real error, not for q_N.
