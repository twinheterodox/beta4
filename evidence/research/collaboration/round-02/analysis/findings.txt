Round 02: slow-filter monodromy and a limitation of absolute contour estimates
28 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

Status. These are mathematical derivations with exact rational and Arb
checks by a model worker, subsequently discussed with the coordinator and
the arithmetic worker. They are not an irrationality proof, a lower bound
for the actual endpoint determinants, independent human verification, or a
priority claim. Writes were confined to this analysis directory.

The main result is an obstruction for a specified analytic METHOD, not
for every use of the endpoint forms. For the SAME fixed-k determinants
and primitive normalization used by the arithmetic worker, every
admissible reciprocal-plane cut K satisfies

          k=3: cap(K)>6/5;        k=5: cap(K)>7/20.           (A)

More precise certified bounds are 1.2208991079... and 0.3586216225....
The k=3 statement excludes any negative n^2 exponent supplied by the
unweighted, absolute-value Cauchy--Vandermonde/capacity estimate. For k=5
that method could supply at most an exponent about 1.02549 of decay.
Neither can meet the available primitive-multiplier UPPER rates
C_3=5.6257703... and C_5=5.1389924... from the arithmetic worker.

These are not just failures of radial cuts. The proof controls EVERY
compact filled cut supporting a single-valued continuation of the germ.
It does NOT assert that the true determinants have those lower exponents.
Complex cancellations in their integral are not bounded from below here.
It also does not assume that the primitive multiplier is an integer or
that numerator content has only coefficient-denominator primes.

There are explicit upper estimates on the actual raw determinants:

 limsup log|L_n(theta)|/n^2 <= log(13/6),      k=3,
 limsup log|L_n(theta)|/n^2 <= log(19/10),     k=5,            (B)

with log 0=-infinity. They come from containing ellipses; the constants
are positive. No matching lower asymptotic is obtained.

1. The exact determinant and the analytic germ

Write theta=beta(4), fix odd k>=3, and put

 u(t)=log((1+t)/(1-t)),  v(t)=log((1+t^k)/(1-t^k)),
 h'''(x)=x^2 sech'(x)/8,  h(0)=h'(0)=h''(0)=0,

 r_k(t)=2h(u)+4h(v)-4h(u+v)-2h(2v)+2h(u+2v),
 R_k(z)=r_k(sqrt(z))=sum_(j>=1) a_j z^j,  a_0=0.

For an arbitrary constant X,

 C_X(z)=(6X+R_k(z))/(1-z),
 c_j(X)=6X+sum_(l=1)^j a_l,
 G_X(w)=C_X(1/w)/w=(6X+R_k(1/w))/(w-1)
       =sum_(j>=0)c_j(X)w^(-j-1) near infinity.              (1.1)

The raw polynomial is exactly

 L_n(X)=det[c_(i+j)(X)]_(0<=i,j<=n)
       =det [[6X,b^T],[b,H]],
 b_i=a_i, H_ij=a_(i+j)-a_(i+j-1), 1<=i,j<=n.                (1.2)

The unit lower-bidiagonal difference congruence proving (1.2), and
Kronecker's consequence that L_n(theta) is nonzero infinitely often for
each fixed k, are already recorded in kronecker-nonvanishing.txt. They
are not rederived as a new result here.

For a nonzero polynomial, P_n=kappa_n L_n is primitive integral with
kappa_n>0. This is exactly the arithmetic worker's definition. In
particular kappa_n may have negative prime valuations. The analytic
monodromy below is independent of the constant X and hence applies to
the actual theta without a rationality hypothesis.

2. A finite possible branch divisor, and all the outer branches

For (a,b) in {(1,0),(0,1),(0,2),(1,1),(1,2)}, let

 A_(a,b)(t)=(1+t)^a(1+t^k)^b,
 B_(a,b)(t)=(1-t)^a(1-t^k)^b.

Since exp(au+bv)=A/B, the preimages of poles of sech(au+bv) are zeros
of (A^2+B^2)/2. This polynomial is even in t. In z=t^2 it becomes

 P10=1+z,
 P01=1+z^k,
 P02=1+6z^k+z^(2k),
 P11=(1+z)(1+z^k)+4z^((k+1)/2),
 P12=(1+z)(1+6z^k+z^(2k))+8z^((k+1)/2)(1+z^k).             (2.1)

All these polynomials are reciprocal: their coefficient lists read the
same in both directions. Thus their roots are also the reciprocal-plane
w-roots. The logarithms u,v can have singularities only when z^k=1 or
at infinity. It follows that G_X is continuable along paths avoiding
the finite set

 E_k={0} union {w:w^k=1} union zeros(P10 P01 P02 P11 P12).   (2.2)

This gives a complete finite set of POSSIBLE singular locations on every
sheet. It is enough for the explicit admissible ellipses in section 6.
The proof does not mistake an infinite list of sech poles in the x-plane
for infinitely many points in the w-plane: all have exp(x)=+i or -i,
which is exactly the finite polynomial equation used above.

The part outside |w|<=1 is fully identified as genuine. Indeed, for
|t|<1 both u and v have imaginary parts strictly between -pi/2 and pi/2.
Consequently h(u),h(v) are regular there. The only possible poles of
the other three arguments are at x=+i*pi/2 and x=-i*pi/2. For k=3,5,
the roots of P02,P11,P12 are simple, and these three polynomials have
no common roots with one another. These assertions follow from exact
polynomial gcds and are checked independently in checks.py. Thus every
root of these three polynomials in |z|<1 produces a nonzero first-order
logarithmic branch of r_k; no other summand is singular at that point.
Squaring t, then reciprocating z, has nonzero derivative there, so the
corresponding |w|>1 point is a genuine branch of G_X.

These outer points belong to EVERY admissible cut, not just a cut of
the principal sheet. On another logarithm sheet the unique singular
argument at the same point is x0=(sigma+4ell)i*pi/2 for an integer ell
and sigma=+1 or -1. It is never zero. Its double-pole coefficient in
h''' is nonzero, so the resulting local (x-x0)log(x-x0) coefficient is
nonzero too. Simplicity of the polynomial root makes the argument map
locally invertible. All other summands remain regular there because
their exponential arguments are unchanged by the logarithm shifts.
Polynomial branch increments of h are locally analytic. Thus no sheet
is meromorphic at this exclusive outer point. An admissible continuation
domain cannot contain it. Section 3.1 makes the shifted-sheet calculation
explicit for loops enclosing more than one point.

The isolated-root counts, with exact squarefreeness and reciprocal
symmetry and Arb enclosures, are:

 k    polynomial       |w|<1       |w|=1       |w|>1
 3      P01               0           3           0
 3      P02               3           0           3
 3      P11               2           0           2
 3      P12               2           3           2
 5      P01               0           5           0
 5      P02               5           0           5
 5      P11               3           0           3
 5      P12               3           5           3

P10 adds only -1, already a root of P01 and P12. The only pairwise
common roots among all five polynomials are precisely this -1 in
P10,P01,P12. Unit-circle classification in the script is not based on
rounding a modulus to 1: it uses uniqueness of isolated roots under
w -> 1/conjugate(w).

For clarity, the remaining possible points are also well understood:

* Nonshared roots in (2.1) have a genuine logarithmic branch by the
  same local double-pole argument, on any sheet on which u,v are regular.
* At z=-1 choose t=i from the disk. For k=3 the three singular arguments
  are u=i*pi/2, v=-i*pi/2, u+2v=-i*pi/2. For k=5 they are u=v=i*pi/2
  and u+2v=3i*pi/2. Their quadratic monodromy parts, in the normalization
  of section 3, are respectively

     2u^2+4v^2+2(u+2v)^2,
     2u^2+4v^2-6(u+2v)^2.

  Neither is zero, so the shared point is not cancelled.
* At z^k=1, z!=1, choose the t-lift with t^k=1. Then v tends to
  positive real infinity along a suitable local approach while u is
  regular. With T(x)=[3+(3/2)x+x^2/4]e^(-x)+O(x^2 e^(-3x)), the
  positive-tail identity for J=6theta+r gives the nonzero term
  (1-e^(-u)) v^2 e^(-v). Since t!=0, its coefficient does not vanish.
  It is a (t-t0)log^2(t-t0) branch. At t=1, v=u-log k+o(1), and

          C_theta(t^2) ~ (2k+1)u^2/8,

  which proves a logarithmic branch there too. Adding 6(X-theta)/(1-z)
  cannot remove it.
* At w=0 there is a square-root branch on an explicit continuation.
  Take t to negative real infinity through the upper half-plane, so
  u=i*pi+2/t+O(t^-3), v=i*pi+O(t^-k), approaching all arguments from
  Re x<0. Differentiating the known polylogarithmic expression for h,
  with G=beta(2) and C3=pi^3/32, gives

  h'(i*pi*l)= -3C3+(i*pi*l/2)G+pi^3*l^2/16   for odd l,
               -(3i*pi*l/2)G-pi^3*l^2/16       for even l.

  Hence 2h'(i*pi)-4h'(2i*pi)+2h'(3i*pi)=15pi^3/8+16i*pi*G !=0.
  The nonzero 1/t term in r gives a sqrt(w) term in G_X. This identifies
  a genuine branch on that continuation; no claim that every sheet at
  w=0 is ramified is needed below.

Only the completely controlled |w|>1 branches are used in the lower
capacity bounds. The extra discussion is not a shortcut around the
monodromy calculation in the next two sections.

3. Exact local monodromy and its noncancellation in the disk

Let x_m=i*pi*(m+1/2), m any integer. The principal part is

 h'''(x)= [i(-1)^m x_m^2/8]/(x-x_m)^2
                +[i(-1)^m x_m/4]/(x-x_m)+analytic.

Integrating three times shows that one positively oriented circuit
around x_m changes h by the EXACT polynomial

 M_m(x)=-pi*(-1)^m*x_m*(x-x_m)*(x-2x_m)/4.                  (3.1)

Analytic terms have no monodromy. There is no unspecified polynomial
in (3.1): the double and simple pole parts determine it. For x_m=+i*pi/2
and -i*pi/2, respectively,

 M_+(x)=-(i*pi^2/8)(x-i*pi/2)(x-i*pi),
 M_-(x)=-(i*pi^2/8)(x+i*pi/2)(x+i*pi).                     (3.2)

The coefficient of x^2 is the SAME, namely -i*pi^2/8, at both poles.
This sign is essential. Opposite imaginary poles do not cancel their
quadratic monodromy in the present argument.

Consider a positively oriented boundary cycle in |t|<1, avoiding all
poles, with winding number 1 on some collection of pole preimages and
0 on the others. Let N02,N11,N12 count the enclosed preimages, with
their positive multiplicities, for 2v,u+v,u+2v. The quadratic part of
the resulting monodromy of r_k is -i*pi^2/8 times

 Q(u,v)=-8N02 v^2-4N11(u+v)^2+2N12(u+2v)^2.               (3.3)

No h(u) or h(v) singularities are available inside the disk to cancel
these terms. Since u,v are single-valued there, continuation of a
previous quadratic branch increment produces no additional increments.
Thus boundary cycles add exactly as in (3.3).

For k>=3, the six functions 1,u,u^2,v,uv,v^2 are linearly independent
over the constants. Their initial degrees in t, successively, are
0,1,2,k,k+1,2k; eliminating the least initial degree successively proves
the claim. Consequently a nonzero quadratic polynomial in u,v cannot
be cancelled identically by the lower-degree monodromy terms.

The coefficient matrix of (u^2,uv,v^2) in (3.3), with columns ordered
N02,N11,N12, is

            [  0  -4   2 ]
            [  0  -8   8 ],        determinant = 128.
            [ -8  -4   8 ]

In particular zero monodromy forces N02=N11=N12=0 on the principal
logarithm sheet. This calculation alone is NOT enough for arbitrary
admissible cuts: a continuation to their boundary can first leave the
unit disk and shift the logarithms. That issue was identified by the
arithmetic worker in adversarial review and is addressed explicitly next.

3.1 The shifted-sheet repair for k=3 and k=5

On any one closed loop lying in |t|<1, the continued logarithms have
the form u+2pi*i*A and v+2pi*i*B for fixed integers A,B, where u,v
denote the principal disk functions. Their shifts stay constant along
that loop. For a term h(au+bv), put ell=aA+bB. Its pole corresponding
to a principal value sigma*i*pi/2, sigma=+1 or -1, now has argument

                   x0=sigma*i*pi/2+2pi*i*ell.

The sech residue is still -sigma*i. Applying the same principal-part
integration as in (3.1), the quadratic monodromy coefficient is

                    -i*pi^2*(1+4sigma*ell)/8.              (3.4)

Previous branches of h differ by quadratic polynomials in its argument;
these are single-valued on the closed loop and do not alter (3.4).
If N_ab is the number of enclosed pole preimages of a type and
S_ab=N_ab,+ - N_ab,-, replace its count in (3.3) by

                    W_ab=N_ab+4(aA+bB)S_ab.               (3.5)

In particular the three weights are

 W02=N02+8B S02,
 W11=N11+4(A+B)S11,
 W12=N12+4(A+2B)S12.

The SAME invertible quadratic coefficient matrix forces all three W
to vanish if the monodromy of r vanishes. But for 1<=N<=5 and
S congruent to N modulo 2, N+4ell*S cannot vanish for an integer ell.
It would first require N to be divisible by 4, hence N=4; then S is
even and 4ell*S is divisible by 8, so it cannot equal -4.

For k=3,5 an enclosing loop in the w-plane which does not surround 0
counts at most five outer w-branches of any one type. The reciprocal
polynomial P02 has k such roots. P11 has degree k+1, so has at most
(k+1)/2 outer roots. P12 is reciprocal of degree 2k+1 and has the unit
root -1, leaving at most k outer roots. These bounds count multiplicity;
squarefreeness for k=3,5 is checked separately. A single conformal
t-lift of that w-loop counts one preimage per w-root, not both lifts.

Therefore for k=3,5, on EVERY logarithm sheet, a positively oriented
single loop whose interior lies in |t|<1 and corresponds to such a
w-loop cannot enclose any nonempty collection of these outer branches
while leaving r single-valued. The single-loop condition matters:
different boundary components could carry different shift pairs.
No all-odd-k extension of this shifted-sheet argument is asserted.

4. A cut component containing an outer branch must reach the unit disk

Call a compact set K in the w-plane admissible if the germ G_X at
infinity has a single-valued holomorphic continuation to the unbounded
component of its complement. The same argument allows meromorphic
continuation. Replace K by its filled hull, the complement of that
unbounded component. This preserves logarithmic capacity and admissibility.
From now on K is filled, so its complement is connected.

Theorem (k=3 or k=5). Every connected component of such a K that contains
a genuine branch point w0 with |w0|>1 intersects the closed unit disk.

Proof. Suppose a component A containing w0 misses that disk. In a compact
Hausdorff space a component is the intersection of its relatively clopen
neighborhoods. Applying this fact to K and to the compact set K intersect
the closed unit disk gives a relatively clopen compact subset K' of K
which contains A and is disjoint from that disk. (If K has no points in
the disk, take K'=K.) The two compact sets K', K\K' have positive distance,
as do K' and the closed disk.

The relatively clopen subset K' is itself filled. To see this, a bounded
component O of its complement could contain no point of C\K: otherwise
that point's path to infinity in the connected open set C\K would cross
K'. Thus O would lie in K\K'. But its boundary lies in K', contradicting
the positive separation of those two compact sets.

It follows that K' has arbitrarily small filled polygonal neighborhoods,
with finitely many components and Jordan outer boundaries. Here is a
useful direct justification of the approximation step. Take decreasing
finite square-grid neighborhoods of K' and fill their bounded holes.
The filled sets decrease to K': any point outside the filled K' has a
path to infinity avoiding K', and a sufficiently thin grid neighborhood
also avoids a positive-clearance compact portion of that path. Compactness
then ensures that, for a sufficiently fine grid, the filled neighborhood
misses both the closed unit disk and K\K'. Small perturbations or rounded
corners remove polygonal point contacts while preserving these separations.
The boundary also misses K.

Take the ONE positively oriented outer Jordan boundary gamma of a filled
component of that neighborhood containing w0. Its whole closed interior
lies in |w|>1, and gamma lies in C\K. Thus G_X has zero monodromy along
gamma. Since its interior is simply connected and avoids 0, select one
analytic branch t=1/sqrt(w) on a neighborhood of that closed interior.
It maps gamma conformally to a closed Jordan loop in |t|<1. Its interior
contains one t-preimage for each enclosed w-root, with positive winding
number one. In particular it encloses a nonempty branch collection.

Continue the original germ to the start of this lifted loop along any
path corresponding to a path in C\K. Such a path need NOT stay in the
unit disk. At its endpoint the logarithms are u+2pi*i*A,v+2pi*i*B, and
the same shift pair persists around the whole lifted loop because the
principal u,v are single-valued on the disk. The continued r is the sum
of the five h compositions on their continued branches; polynomial
branch increments of h have no new monodromy on this loop. Its zero
monodromy therefore forces the three weighted counts (3.5) to vanish.
The counts of each type are at most five. Section 3.1 shows that every
actual count must then be zero, contradicting the enclosed w0.

This proves the theorem for all compact filled K, including infinitely
many components. The clopen and filled-neighborhood steps produce one
enclosing Jordan curve; the proof does not sum monodromies from boundary
components carrying possibly different logarithm shifts. Filledness also
ensures that a boundary avoiding K lies in the chosen continuation domain.

The theorem does NOT prove that all the symmetric branch orbit, or all
branches, lie in one connected component. Components may reach different
parts of the disk. No Chebotarev lower bound requiring a connected cut
is being applied without proving its connectivity hypothesis. The weaker
statement just proved is enough for the following projection argument.

5. Capacity lower bounds valid for every admissible cut

Two elementary capacity facts suffice, with no specialized extremal-cut
theorem. Orthogonal projection cannot increase transfinite diameter:
lift any finite set of projected points to K; every pairwise distance
can only increase. Passing to maximal Vandermonde products and their
limits gives cap(proj K)<=cap(K).

A line interval of length d has capacity d/4. Also

 cap([-b,-a] union [a,b])=sqrt(b^2-a^2)/2, 0<a<b.            (5.1)

For (5.1), the polynomial w^2 maps this set onto [a^2,b^2]. Half the
Green function of that interval evaluated at w^2 is its Green function;
comparison at infinity gives the displayed capacity. These formulas
could equally be obtained from Chebyshev polynomials.

5.1 Exact k=3 certificate

Here

 P12(w)=(w+1)(w^6+8w^4-2w^3+8w^2+1).

Putting x=w+1/w reduces the sextic to x^3+5x-2=0. Its unique real
root r lies in (3/8,2/5): the endpoint signs are opposite and the
derivative is positive. The other roots are

                x=-r/2 +/- i sqrt(3r^2+20)/2.

Choose the upper root and the reciprocal quadratic solution w=a+ib
with |w|>1. Then b>0 and

 a(1+1/|w|^2)=-r/2,     b(1-1/|w|^2)=sqrt(3r^2+20)/2>sqrt5.

In particular |a|<1/5. If b<=13/5, then |w|^2<34/5 and the second
left side is less than (13/5)(29/34)=377/170<sqrt5, contradiction.
Thus b>13/5. The conjugate point a-ib is another genuine outer branch.
This exact certificate was supplied by the coordinator and checked by
this worker; it does not rely on the numerical root calculation.

By section 4, the components containing these two points each meet
|w|<=1. Their imaginary projections therefore contain [1,b] and [-b,-1].
This holds whether the two components coincide or are distinct. Hence

        cap K >=sqrt(b^2-1)/2 >sqrt((13/5)^2-1)/2=6/5.       (5.2)

Arb isolation gives b=2.6386319423143175898... and the sharper bound
cap K>=1.2208991079324929448.... The exact rational lower bound is
already sufficient to rule out cap K<1.

5.2 Exact k=5 certificate

Here P12(w)=(w+1)Q(w), where

 Q(w)=w^10+8w^7-8w^6+14w^5-8w^4+8w^3+1.

Direct rational substitution gives

 Q(-5/2)=918649/1024>0,
 Q(-12/5)=-3392998151/9765625<0.

There is a real root w0 in (-5/2,-12/5). It is an exclusive P12 root,
so it is a genuine outer branch. Its cut component must meet the unit
disk. Projecting onto the real line shows that the cut contains in
projection [w0,-1]. Consequently

                 cap K >=(-w0-1)/4 >7/20.                 (5.3)

The isolated root is -2.4344864903484775549..., giving the improved
lower bound 0.3586216225871193887.... This result does NOT settle whether
the optimal admissible capacity for k=5 is above or below 1. It does
settle the compatibility test with the available arithmetic rate.

5.3 Why the earlier symmetric-star calculation was insufficient

The known orbit w^k=-q^-2, q=sqrt2-1, has a radial-star capacity
(q^-2/4)^(1/k)>1. That is an exact capacity of one chosen star. It
cannot lower-bound every admissible cut without a connectivity or
extremality argument. The new proof uses additional P12 branches and
the component theorem; it does not promote the radial-star calculation
into a general minimal-capacity theorem.

6. An admissible contour estimate for these exact determinants

If Gamma is a contour in a single-valued domain surrounding an
admissible compact K, coefficient extraction and the determinant
integral identity give, with N=n+1,

 L_n(X)=1/[N!(2pi i)^N] int_(Gamma^N)
           product_j G_X(w_j) product_(i<j)(w_j-w_i)^2 dw_1...dw_N.
                                                                  (6.1)

Taking absolute values bounds its n^2 rate by log cap(K), after
approximating K by contour neighborhoods. More precisely,

              limsup |L_n(X)|^(1/n^2) <= cap(K).             (6.2)

Zeros cause no difficulty. The factor N rather than n changes no
leading n^2 constant. Formula (6.2) is the one-variable Polya inequality
in exactly the coefficient convention (1.1); a precise primary-paper
statement is Gunyuz--Zakharyuta [P], Theorem 2.1, pp. 2-3. Our G_X is
holomorphic at infinity and vanishes there, as required. K is filled,
and isolated poles, if permitted in the definition of admissibility,
can be added to K without changing capacity.

For actual upper bounds take the filled ellipses

 k=3: ((Re w+1/5)/(5/3))^2+(Im w/(8/3))^2 <=1;
 k=5: ((Re w+2/5)/(41/20))^2+(Im w/(7/4))^2 <=1.             (6.3)

Every point of (2.2) lies strictly inside the relevant ellipse. The
script proves the polynomial part using certified root enclosures,
also checks all w^k=1 roots and 0, and records every enclosure and
quadratic-form bound. Thus the germ is holomorphic and single-valued
outside each ellipse by the monodromy theorem. These are concrete
admissible compacts, not numerically guessed extremal cuts.

The capacity of an ellipse with semiaxes a,b is (a+b)/2, as seen from
the coefficient of the exterior conformal map
w=center+((a+b)/2)zeta+((a-b)/2)/zeta. Therefore their capacities are
13/6 and 19/10. Applying (6.2) gives (B), with constants about
0.7731898882 and 0.6418538862, respectively.

This bounds the raw determinants, not their primitive polynomials.
The bounds are uniform in n for each fixed k; the constant X affects
only the subquadratic terms. No joint limit in k,n is claimed.

7. Compatibility with actual primitive normalization

The arithmetic worker's findings.txt, sections 1 and 6, proves

 log kappa_n <= C_k n^2+o_k(n^2),
 C_3=10042/1785=5.6257703081...,
 C_5=37232/7245=5.1389924086....                              (7.1)

The worker derives this directly as an upper bound on the multiplier
in P_n=kappa_n L_n. It includes cancellation in the correct direction
and makes no false claim that numerator primes are confined to those
in the coefficient denominator. This analysis worker read that packet
and coordinated the determinant indexing explicitly.

Using (6.2) and (7.1), a sufficient capacity criterion for shrinking
integer forms would be

                         C_k+log cap(K)<0.                 (7.2)

No admissible cut can satisfy (7.2) for these proved constants:

 C_3+log(6/5) >0,        C_5+log(7/20) >0.                  (7.3)

The latter positive gap is over 4.08; this is not a close numerical
optimization issue. The refined all-cut lower bounds give possible
analytic decay exponents at most -log(1.220899...)<0 for k=3 and
-log(0.358621...) about 1.02549 for k=5. The explicit ellipses supply
positive combined upper exponents C_3+log(13/6) and C_5+log(19/10).

The precise conclusion is that this absolute contour-capacity strategy
cannot prove shrinking forms when paired with the CURRENT rigorous
multiplier upper bounds. A smaller actual multiplier, perhaps caused
by a large maximal-minor gcd, could change the arithmetic side. A
determinant estimate exploiting complex cancellation rather than the
absolute Vandermonde envelope could change the analytic side. Neither
possibility is ruled out by (A). In particular there is no inference
from a multiplier lower bound plus a determinant upper bound, and no
inference from an unsuccessful upper estimate to an actual-value no-go.

More specifically, the excluded strategy combines the fixed-cut Polya
bounds (6.2), or their infimum over admissible cuts, with (7.1). No claim
is made here about n-dependent weighted contours or estimates below the
absolute Vandermonde envelope. Constants attached to a fixed contour
contribute only subquadratic terms; an n-dependent construction would
have to account for its changing weights and constants separately.

9. What was and was not obtained about two-sided asymptotics

No two-sided determinant asymptotic, pointwise Padé error lower bound,
or uniform lower bound for the primitive values was found in this
round. The integral (6.1) is complex; there is no fixed-sign product
as there was for the positive kernel in round 01. Capacity lower
bounds on its integration geometry do not remove cancellation.

The primary-paper Polya statement [P, Theorem 2.1] is explicitly an
upper inequality. Its sharpness results concern existence of functions
or functionals achieving a bound, not automatic equality for this G_X.
Stahl's paper [S] was inspected for its admissibility framework, unique
minimal-set theorem, and topology, not imported as a determinant theorem.
Theorem 1 as stated there is a convergence-in-capacity theorem for the
particular algebraic example (1.1). It does not supply a pointwise
endpoint estimate for the present logarithmic functions, or a lower
bound for their Hankel determinants. No unverified strong-asymptotic
theorem for non-Hermitian orthogonality is being invoked.

The symmetric branch orbit has therefore not been proved to force one
connected extremal cut. The component-to-disk theorem is the precise
connectivity conclusion obtained. For k=5 even whether the least
admissible capacity exceeds one remains open in this packet. Neither
uncertainty affects the proved incompatibility with (7.1).

10. Ordinary cubic-tail Loewner sign counterexample

This archives the earlier diagnostic, with exact and Arb certificates,
to prevent a repeated assumption of sign regularity. Define

 f(x)=sum_(j>=0)[(4j+1+4x)^(-3)-(4j+3+4x)^(-3)],
 L(a,b)=-f[a,b]/12.

The double-Laplace density is proportional to h0(u+v), with
h0(t)=t^2 sech t. Its fourth confluent Hankel determinant satisfies

 D4(0)=det[h0^(i+j)(0)]_(0<=i,j<=3)=-624,

 D4(t)=8192(t^2-6t+15/2)e^(-6t)+O(t^8 e^(-8t))>0
                                      for sufficiently large t.   (10.1)

The value at zero uses derivatives (0,0,2,0,-12,0,150). For the second
formula write h0=2t^2 e^(-t)-2t^2 e^(-3t)+O(t^2 e^(-5t)). The leading
rank-three part has zero 4-by-4 determinant. Replacing one column by
the next exponential term gives exactly the polynomial in (10.1).
checks.py verifies this as a rational polynomial determinant identity.
Continuity makes the sign negative for small positive t, so the density
kernel fails sign regularity already at order four.

Smoothing does not restore global sign regularity for L. Its Taylor
coefficient at (x,x), with i+j=j0, is

 [a^i b^j] L(x+a,x+b)
   =(-1)^(i+j)/1536 * (i+j+2)(i+j+3)
       *[zeta(i+j+4,x+1/4)-zeta(i+j+4,x+3/4)].             (10.2)

For n=4 the row and column signs cancel in the determinant. If M_x is
the matrix in (10.2) without the signs and the factor 1/1536, its Arb
determinant is rigorously

 x=0:  -2426864805998170.876257380707...,
 x=1:        0.000231587931005586531800712287...,
 x=10:      -7.810149160124735396674574380... *10^-34.       (10.3)

The certified intervals at 256 bits have widths far smaller than the
displayed values. A determinant on small ordered clustered row and
column frames has the sign of this confluent determinant, since its
leading coefficient is the product of two positive Vandermondes times
det M_x/1536^4. Choosing one sufficiently small fixed spacing and moving
the cluster from x=0 to x=1 therefore gives a zero determinant by
continuity. There is no universal nonvanishing/sign-regularity theorem
over all real increasing frames for this ordinary L.

This does NOT exhibit a zero on integer frames and does not rule out a
special separated integer-frame theorem. It does rule out the generic
sign-regularity proof attempted through this kernel or its double-
Laplace density. Arithmetic problems on shifted integer frames remain
separate; no new integer-frame scan was performed here.
