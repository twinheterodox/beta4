Round 02: primitive endpoint arithmetic, content, and determinant clearing
28 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

Status and main results

This is a mathematical derivation with exact computational checks, not an
irrationality proof, a general no-go theorem, independent human verification,
or a priority claim. The worker wrote only in this arithmetic directory.
No external literature was needed for the new elementary arguments below.
The inherited coefficient results are from round 01; the nonvanishing input
is the separate kronecker-nonvanishing.txt, which was read in this round.

The work gives three structural results for the fixed-k endpoint forms.

* A maximal-minor gcd controls the ENTIRE common numerator content, including
  higher nullity and prime powers. If D clears the coefficient prefix,
  T=DH, u=Db, and Delta is the maximal-minor gcd of [T|u], then the gcd g of
  the corresponding integer endpoint pair satisfies

                     Delta | g | 6D Delta^2.                 (A)

  Thus D^n/(6 Delta^2) <= kappa_n <= D^(n+1)/Delta. Away from 6D,
  a prime occurs in the common content exactly when a rectangular Hankel
  matrix loses rank. This is equivalent to a specific modular Pade defect.

* The tempting assertion that there are no new common numerator primes is
  FALSE when "new" means outside the ACTUAL coefficient denominator.
  At k=3,n=6, the actual D is 4729725 and the minimally cleared pair has
  content 2^14*23. The prime 23 does not divide D. An explicit degree-five
  Pade defect over F_23 proves this without relying on factorization data.

* Using the proved first occurrences of the denominator layers inside
  individual determinant entries gives a uniform UPPER bound for kappa:

  limsup_(n, L_n != 0) log(kappa_n)/n^2 <= C_k,
  C_k=13/3+3/k+1/(k+2)+4/(3k+8)-1/(k+4).                    (B)

  In particular C_3=10042/1785=5.6257703081... and
  C_5=37232/7245=5.1389924086.... These replace the previous sufficient
  raw-clearing rates 7.7333333333... and 7.0857142857.... The bound does
  not assume favorable numerator content.

There is also an exact reduction in the one-layer prime band. If p>2n,
s=(p+2k+3)/2, R=2n-s+1>0, and m=n-R, then

 p^R L_n(X) = (-1)^[R(R-1)/2] (-32/3)^R L_m(X) mod p.         (C)

Both sides are coefficientwise p-integral. Consequently the upper bound
v_p(kappa_n)<=R is exact unless the smaller pencil has a modular Pade
defect at p. This links a positive denominator exponent at one index to
common numerator content at a smaller index.

None of these results bounds the actual real value of L_n(beta(4)) from
below. A lower bound on a multiplier together with an upper bound on a
real determinant would not prove a no-go. The analytic worker is using
exactly the normalization below; its real estimates must be combined
with (B) in the appropriate direction.

1. Conventions and scaling

Fix an odd k>=3 and write

 R_k(z)=r_k(sqrt(z))=sum_(j>=1) a_j z^j,  a_0=0,
 b_i=a_i,  H_ij=a_(i+j)-a_(i+j-1),  1<=i,j<=n.

The raw endpoint polynomial is

 L_n(X)=det [[6X,b^T],[b,H]]=A_n X+C_n,
 A_n=6 det H,  C_n=-b^T adj(H)b.                             (1.1)

The definitions make sense when H is singular. A nonzero polynomial can
be a rational constant. If both coefficients vanish, the polynomial is
zero and its primitive multiplier is not defined; put P_n=0 separately.

For a nonzero polynomial, let

 M=lcm(den A_n,den C_n),
 g_min=gcd(M A_n,M C_n)>0,
 kappa_n=M/g_min,  P_n=kappa_n L_n.

Then P_n has primitive integer coefficients. No assumption about beta(4)
is involved. In particular kappa_n can be rational and can have negative
prime valuations.

Let D be ANY positive integer clearing a_0,...,a_(2n). In the computations
D is the actual coefficient LCM, not a sufficient theoretical multiple.
Put

 T=DH, u=Db, d=det T, m=adj(T)u, Q=u^T adj(T)u.

The scaled integer endpoint pair is

                   (6D d,-Q),                              (1.2)

and, for g=gcd(6D d,Q)>0,

                   kappa_n=D^(n+1)/g.                       (1.3)

Although g and g_min can differ by a large factor, they have identical
prime parts away from D. Formula (1.3) is used to check the normalization
against the minimally cleared rational pair.

2. Content versus a rectangular Hankel determinantal divisor

Define

 Delta=gcd(d,m_1,...,m_n)>=0,
 F=(D a_(i+j))_(0<=i<=n, 1<=j<=n).                           (2.1)

Delta is the gcd of all n-by-n minors of [T|u], and also of F. Indeed,
the maximal minors of [T|u] are d and the entries of adj(T)u, up to sign.
The lower bidiagonal row operation on F which retains its first row and
replaces each later row by its difference with its predecessor gives
[u^T;T]. This is unimodular over the integers, so maximal-minor ideals
are preserved. Symmetry of T identifies the transpose of this matrix
with [u|T].

The following result applies to EVERY symmetric integer matrix T and
integer vector u, not just the present coefficients.

Lemma. Put g0=gcd(d,Q). If the bordered pencil is nonzero, then

                       Delta | g0 | Delta^2.                (2.2)

Proof. Delta divides d and every m_i, hence divides Q=sum u_i m_i.
For each i, the elementary bordered-minor identity is

 m_i^2-Q adj(T)_(ii) = -d Q_i,                              (2.3)

where Q_i is the quadratic adjugate expression after deleting coordinate
i from T and u. For n=1 take Q_i=0. This is the Desnanot--Jacobi identity
on the symmetric bordered matrix; alternatively it follows by expansion
when det T is nonzero and then, as a polynomial identity, for all T.
All quantities on the right are integers. Thus g0 divides m_i^2 for
every i. At each prime, v_p(g0)<=v_p(d)<=2v_p(d), and also
v_p(g0)<=2v_p(m_i). Taking the minimum gives
v_p(g0)<=2v_p(Delta). This proves (2.2).

The zero case is exact: Delta=0 implies d=m=Q=0. Conversely d=Q=0
forces m_i^2=0 by (2.3), hence Delta=0. Thus Delta=0 if and only if
the bordered polynomial is identically zero over Q. No invertibility
assumption or odd-prime diagonalization is needed.

Since g0 divides g and gcd(a d,Q) divides a*gcd(d,Q), (2.2) gives

                      Delta | g | 6D Delta^2.               (2.4)

This proves (A), including primes dividing 6D. More precisely,

 v_p(Delta) <= v_p(g) <= v_p(6D)+2v_p(Delta).                 (2.5)

The factors 1 and 2 are sharp even in dimension one. T=[p],u=[p]
gives v_p(Delta)=v_p(g0)=1; T=[p^2],u=[p] gives exponents 1 and 2.
Singular T is allowed: T=diag(1,0),u=(0,p) has Delta=p and g0=p^2.

For p not dividing 6D, (2.4) yields the equivalence

 p divides the common integer content
   iff rank_Fp(F mod p)<n.                                  (2.6)

This includes every nullity. When rank(T mod p)=n-1 it specializes to
the initially proposed condition u perpendicular to ker T. At larger
nullity the rank criterion remains valid without a separate case split.

3. Exact Pade interpretation and a new-prime counterexample

For a field in which D is invertible, a nonzero kernel vector
(x_1,...,x_n) of F is equivalent to a nonzero polynomial

 q(z)=sum_(j=1)^n x_j z^(n-j),  deg q<=n-1,

and a polynomial p(z) of degree at most n-1 such that

                  q(z) R_k(z)-p(z)=O(z^(2n+1)).             (3.1)

Indeed the coefficient at z^(n+i), 0<=i<=n, is
sum_j x_j a_(i+j). Thus the common numerator question is an EXACT modular
Pade defect question, rather than a generic probabilistic rank assertion.
The constant coefficient of q need not be nonzero; (3.1) deliberately
allows this, so it remains equivalent in degenerate cases.

At k=3,n=6,p=23, the coefficient residues a_0,...,a_12 are

            0,0,0,0,0,19,8,6,19,13,22,17,18.

The rectangular matrix F over F_23 has rank five and nullvector

                        (5,1,13,17,2,1).

In polynomial form,

 (1+2z+17z^2+13z^3+z^4+5z^5) R_3(z)
                         =19z^5+O(z^13) mod 23.             (3.2)

The coefficient denominator through a_12 is D=4729725, prime to 23.
The exact raw endpoint coefficients are

 A_6=3170597854812063115968512/111190552238390625,
 C_6=12154765979998928896/4189282972875.

Both have 23-valuation exactly 1. The minimally cleared content is
376832=2^14*23 and

 kappa_6=333571656715171875/376832,
 P_6(X)=25241469844482924348 X+2568314902018445125.             (3.3)

This is a counterexample to absence of common content outside the actual
coefficient denominator, not a good approximation. It does NOT show that
content primes can exceed 4n: here 23<24. The stronger proposed support
restriction to primes <=4n is neither proved nor disproved by this example.

The eight larger targeted cases in section 7 happen to have no common
prime outside 6D. The explicit counterexample shows why those observations
cannot be elevated into a uniform theorem. The finite-field normality
checks in the script are likewise checks, not a proof of normality.

4. An exact one-layer reduction and its exceptional set

Fix k and let p>2n be prime. Suppose

 s=(p+2k+3)/2 <=2n,  R=2n-s+1>0,  m=s-n-1=n-R.              (4.1)

The first-layer theorem of round 01 gives

 a_j is p-integral for j<s,
 p a_s=-32/3 mod p.                                        (4.2)

All a_j through j=2n have valuation at least -1: the second layer begins
only at p+k+delta_p>2n, where delta_p=1 for p=1 mod4 and 0 otherwise.
The hypotheses of that onset theorem hold automatically in the nonempty
range (4.1). In particular p>=5 and 2p+2k+2<kp there.

Separate the indices 0,...,n of the endpoint matrix into
0,...,m and m+1,...,n. Every entry involving a row in the first group is
p-integral, since its largest sum index is m+n=s-1. The lower-right
R-by-R block W has pW integral. In pW mod p the entries before the
anti-diagonal vanish, and the anti-diagonal has the constant unit -32/3.
Multiplying just the last R rows of the full endpoint matrix by p gives
a block upper-triangular reduction modulo p. Its upper-left block is
exactly the smaller endpoint matrix for L_m(X). Therefore

 p^R L_n(X)=epsilon_R (-32/3)^R L_m(X) mod p,
 epsilon_R=(-1)^[R(R-1)/2].                                 (4.3)

This proves (C), as a coefficientwise polynomial congruence, not merely
a rank bound. The actual coefficient denominator D_m is prime to p.
Consequently

 v_p(kappa_n)=R  iff  L_m(X) is nonzero modulo p
                 iff  p does not divide Delta_m.           (4.4)

If the smaller polynomial vanishes modulo p, the exponent is at most
R-1. The precise further loss depends on higher p-adic terms; (4.3) does
not claim to determine those terms.

The same block argument works slightly beyond p>2n whenever s>n,
the second layer has not yet appeared by 2n, and all entries have
valuation at least -1. This explains boundary losses caused by the
identically zero very small pencils. In the main band p>2n, m>=k+1,
so those forced zero pencils no longer occur, although modular defects
still can.

For example k=3,n=9,p=23 gives s=16,R=3,m=6. The defect (3.2) forces
the exponent below 3; its exact value is 2. This was the sole main-band
exception in the 544 polynomial congruence checks in the script. Its
location was found from (4.3), not by presuming that an anti-triangular
residue block always gives the full determinant valuation.

5. A determinant assignment bound using coefficient layer onsets

Let N=n+1. If nonnegative integers e_p(j) bound the possible denominator
exponents of a_j, and are nondecreasing in j, then every endpoint matrix
entry at indices i,j in {0,...,n} has denominator exponent at most
e_p(i+j). The (0,0) entry has exponent zero. Consequently

 v_p(kappa_n) <= max_permutation pi sum_(i=0)^n e_p(i+pi(i)), (5.1)

with the (0,0) cost set to zero. This follows term by term in the
determinant expansion; cancellation can only improve the bound. A
coefficient that vanishes identically may safely be assigned cost zero.

The finite script uses the strongest round-01 prefix bound, then caps
it by zero before the first onset and by one before the second onset.
It computes (5.1) by an exact Hungarian algorithm and checks its integer
dual certificate. The following closed form yields an explicit uniform
asymptotic theorem without invoking an unproved optimization conjecture.

Two-layer matching lemma. On indices 1,...,N, give edge (i,j) weight
1_(i+j>=s)+1_(i+j>=t), where s<t. Put

 R=min(N,max(0,2N-t+1)),  m=N-R.

The maximum perfect-matching weight is exactly

             M_N(s,t)=2R+min(m,max(0,2m-s+1)).               (5.2)

Proof. There are at most R edges of weight two. For a matching attaining
the displayed lower bound, reverse the last R indices, giving R edges
of weight two; within the remaining 1,...,m use a maximum matching in
the threshold graph i+j>=s. Its size is min(m,max(0,2m-s+1)).

For the upper bound, R=0 and R=N are immediate. Otherwise t=N+m+1.
If s<=m+1, the elementary bound N+R applies. If m+1<s<=2m+1, choose
integers a,b in [0,m] with a+b=s-1. Assign weight one to each row index
>a and each column index >b, and zero to the other vertices. Every
positive edge has at least one selected end; every weight-two edge has
both ends selected, since both its indices exceed m. This dual cover
has total weight 2N-s+1. If s>2m+1, select just the last R row and column
vertices. It covers every positive edge at least once and every
weight-two edge twice, with total weight 2R. These three bounds agree
with (5.2). This proves optimality over all permutations.

For the endpoint indices 0,...,n, replace N by n+1 and both thresholds
by s+2,t+2. This accounts for the target row and column explicitly.

6. Uniform multiplier upper rates for fixed k=3 and k=5

For sufficiently large p with p^2>4n, the four proved possible layers
of a_j, j<=2n, have lower cutoffs

 s1=(p+2k+3)/2,
 s2=p+k+delta_p,
 s3=ceil(kp/2),
 s4=(k+2)p.                                                 (6.1)

The first two are the round-01 onset theorems. Before s3 the slow-variable
argument permits at most two layers. Before s4 the Frobenius congruence
permits at most three layers. Regular length-four words give at most
four layers throughout this prime range. Hence an admissible entry cost
is the sum of the four threshold indicators in (6.1).

Maximizing the sum is bounded above by the sum of the maxima for the
pairs (s1,s2) and (s3,s4). This is an UPPER bound; it does not assert
that the two optimal permutations coincide. Thus the finite exponent

 B_(n,k,p)=M_(n+1)(s1+2,s2+2)+M_(n+1)(s3+2,s4+2)            (6.2)

clears every polynomial coefficient locally in this prime range. At
the excluded small primes use (n+1) times the round-01 coefficient
exponent. These finite bounds are checked in the script, including
the strict cutoff and onset hypotheses.

Here is the weighted prime integral for (6.2). For thresholds alpha*p,
beta*p with beta>=2alpha>0, let x=p/n. Up to O(1) in the matching weight,
M/n is the following continuous piecewise-linear function:

 2                 for 0<=x<=1/beta;
 4-2beta*x         for 1/beta<=x<=2/(2beta-alpha);
 2-alpha*x         for 2/(2beta-alpha)<=x<=1/(beta-alpha);
 3-beta*x          for 1/(beta-alpha)<=x<=2/beta;
 1                 for 2/beta<=x<=1/alpha;
 2-alpha*x         for 1/alpha<=x<=2/alpha;
 0                 afterwards.

Intervals of length zero are harmless. Integration gives

 I(alpha,beta)=3/(2alpha)+1/beta+2/(2beta-alpha)
                                      -1/(2(beta-alpha)).  (6.3)

For the first pair, (alpha,beta)=(1/2,1), this is 13/3. For the second,
(alpha,beta)=(k/2,k+2), it is

                  3/k+1/(k+2)+4/(3k+8)-1/(k+4).

The prime number theorem and partial summation turn the weighted sums
into these integrals. The omitted primes p<=sqrt(4n), plus finitely many
k-dependent primes, cost only O_k(n^(3/2) log n)=o(n^2), even using the
crude four-word bound. The shifts O_k(1) in the cutoffs and the one extra
matrix row change the prime sum by o(n^2). Therefore

                 log kappa_n <= C_k n^2+o_k(n^2)            (6.4)

for every nonzero endpoint polynomial at each FIXED k. Numerator content
only lowers kappa and was not discarded with an incorrect sign. This
is not a jointly varying-k statement and does not assert the existence
of a limit for log kappa_n/n^2.

8. What remains unknown and what this round changes

The content support has been reduced exactly to modular Pade defects,
but the iterated-integral/differential structure has not yet yielded a
uniform bound on the maximal-minor gcd Delta beyond the inequalities
above. The explicit F_23 defect prevents replacing that missing argument
by an assertion of universal modular normality. There is no proof here
that common primes beyond 4n are absent, that the total content is
subquadratic, or that the primitive height has a specific asymptotic rate.

The new matching theorem does give a stronger uniform upper bound on the
actual primitive multiplier, independent of those unknowns. The exact
one-layer factorization identifies precisely what must be proved to turn
many local upper exponents into equalities; it does not suppress the
exceptional smaller Hankel pencils.

The same-family Kronecker result supplies infinitely many nonzero values
at beta(4) for each fixed k. To prove irrationality from this family,
one still needs a uniform real estimate compatible with the primitive
normalization, including all sufficiently large indices or another proved
nonvanishing-compatible choice. Neither the finite primitive heights nor
the positive upper constants in (B) are a no-go theorem.

The useful next analytic/arithmetic compatibility test therefore concerns
the exact L_n and kappa_n above. Enlarging a raw coefficient-denominator
scan, or assuming the observed lack of new content persists, would miss
the structural issue exposed by (3.2) and (4.3).
