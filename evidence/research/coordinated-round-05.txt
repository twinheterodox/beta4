COORDINATED ROUND05: BALANCED MODULAR DELETION AND THE POLES IT LEAVES
29 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

No irrationality proof has been obtained. This round transfers exact
prime deletion into the modular/Eichler construction, with a precise
theta-only endpoint and a genuine determinant arithmetic saving. It
also identifies why three proposed modular coordinate/prefactor repairs
fail to provide the intended raw decay. A separate fresh investigation
proves a uniform primitive-value obstruction for Euler--Boole truncations.

All full proofs and checks are under collaboration/round-05/. The two

1. The minimal Fricke-compatible prime filter

Use the archived Lambert/Eichler series

 F(q)=sum_(d odd) chi_4(d)q^d/[d^4(1+q^d)],
 G(q)=sum_(d odd) q^d/[d^4(1+q^(2d))],

and V_p h(q)=h(q^p). Their exact outer-index deletions are

 (1-chi(p)p^-4 V_p)F,
 (1-p^-4 V_p)G.

Both have p-integral Fourier coefficients. A single deletion factor
does not respect the cubic coefficient reversal needed by the Fricke
transformation. The unique minimal simultaneous filter, up to scalar,
has degree two:

 C_p(V)=(1-chi(p)p^-4 V)(1-pV),
 C_p^*(V)=chi(p)(1-p^-4 V)(1-chi(p)pV).

For a degree-r F filter C, its paired G filter is
p^(-3r/2)V^r C(p^3/V). Bounded p-denominators on both sides require
C(chi(p)p^4)=C(p^-1)=0, explaining the minimal degree. With C_p(0)=1,
both filtered Fourier series are actually p-integral.

Apply these to the F and G components of the old H32 period filter.
For selected primes S, P=product S, the new level is32P^2 and

 S_2=7 product_(p in S)(1-p)(1-chi(p)/p^4).

The exact new Fricke value is S_2 theta/2, theta=beta(4). The doubled
rational F component alone has the theta-only cusp value S_2 theta
at q=1. The period-killing moments remain zero, so no other period
has been silently introduced at these stated endpoints.

The arithmetic gain must be assessed for the actual target. Scalar
coefficient forms have sufficient multiplier P^4 d^4, where d is the
LCM of the odd integers in the coefficient prefix coprime to P. For
large selected primes this can equal the old scalar clearing factor.
For an order-n endpoint determinant, however, the bound is

  kappa <= P^4 d_(2n,S)^(4(n+1)).

The selected fourth powers are paid once in the endpoint, rather than
once per determinant row. Deleting exactly y<p<=2n gives
log^+kappa=o(n^2) when pi(y)log n=o(n). The exact n=8,p=11 comparison,
using h_1=32 in every case, gives actual primitive exponent24 before
filtering and4 after the quadratic filter.

A quartic self-dual extension also exists, but is not a free improvement.
At the same constant-dilation normalization its endpoint grows by p,
its level grows by another p^2, and entry denominators of order p^3
return. The same determinant then has exponent21, worse than4.
Dividing the entire germ by an endpoint scalar does not change its
primitive determinant pair: J_(h/a)(X)=a^(-(n+1))J_h(aX).

Full proof: arithmetic/findings.txt. The arithmetic/analysis reviews
corrected one quantifier: the cutoff set is exactly the primes in the
specified band, so its product cannot contain uncontrolled larger primes.

2. A cusp alternative with a uniform nonzero error

For U=16F-10V_2F+V_4F, retain only minimal physical deletion:

 U_S=product_(p in S)(1-chi(p)p^-4 V_p)U,
 alpha_S=product_(p in S)(1-chi(p)/p^4).

Its endpoint is(7/2)alpha_S theta. Unlike the sparse cyclotomic germ
of round04, its Fourier coefficients at multiples of a selected prime
are retained through the other Lambert divisors. The exact transformed
identity is

 U_S(tau)-(7/2)alpha_S theta
  =-8i tau^3 sum_(r=1,2,4; d|P)
        a_r r^3 mu(d)chi(d)/d G(-1/(8rd tau)),
 (a_1,a_2,a_4)=(16,-10,1).

Put x=pi/(16Py), sigma=product_(p in S)(1+1/p). Along tau=iy,

 U_S(iy)-(7/2)alpha_S theta
  =-512 mu(P)chi(P)y^3 e^-x/P * (1+epsilon),
 |epsilon|<=2e^(-2x)+5P sigma e^-x.

Thus x>=log(20P sigma) gives a uniform two-sided bound and known
nonzero sign. The dependence on P is explicit: the sufficient cusp
approach scale is y of order at most1/(P log P). These analytic
evaluations are not automatically rational approximants of small height.

For each FIXED finite S, the fourth derivative is a nonzero holomorphic
positive-weight modular form. A cusp Fourier expansion proves it cannot
be meromorphic through any finite rational cusp. Hence the q germ has
the unit circle as a natural boundary. Its cumulative generating germ
is nonrational, and Kronecker gives infinitely many nonzero endpoint
determinants for that same fixed S. This does not prove nonvanishing
when S varies with the determinant order.

The natural boundary forces every filled compact supporting continuation
from reciprocal infinity to contain the unit disk. Its minimum capacity
is therefore1. This limits the usual unweighted absolute-contour bound;
it is NOT a lower bound for actual determinant values. An endpoint-fixing
Möbius substitution seems to improve capacity, but multiplies the whole
Hankel pencil by exactly a^(-n(n+1)), leaving its primitive pair unchanged.

Full proof: analysis/findings.txt. A draft sentence incorrectly calling
all q=exp(-2pi y) values transcendental was removed during review; no
transcendence assertion about the sampled q values is needed.

3. Two integral coordinates have accessible uncanceled singularities

The naive higher-level trace T_P=t(q)+t(q^(P^2)) has integral q expansion
and formal inverse. Its Fricke fixed-point value is2t(q0^P), where
q0=exp(-pi/(2sqrt2)). For P>=3 this is below2/19, whereas
T_P(q0)>t0>3/20. Starting from q=0, the real inverse therefore meets an
earlier critical point. The desired fixed point is on another branch.

The coordinator proves the first critical q_c lies between q0 and2/5.
For the quadratic single-prime filter p>=7, |H_p(q_c)|<13 while its
theta target has magnitude greater than20. Thus the pole of
J_p^5/(D log T_P) is actually uncanceled there. This is a genuine
continuation obstruction, not just a poorly chosen estimate.

The analysis worker tested a different integral parameter,

 T_m=t(q)t(q^m)/(t(q)+t(q^m))^m,

with m=p^2 or p^4. It has q-leading coefficient1, integral coefficients,
and integral inverse. Nevertheless mT_m(z/m) converges to z exp(2z),
including derivatives on compact sets. Its principal negative-real
critical point is -1/(2m)+O(m^-2), with value-1/(2em)+O(m^-2).
The theta remainder does not cancel the resulting inverse-square-root
singularity. This gives actual raw coefficient limsup growth of at
least2em(1+O(1/m)), not a primitive-value obstruction.

Proofs: coordinator/trace_coordinate.txt and analysis/findings.txt.
Exact rational certificates and separate interval checks accompany them.

4. A better prefactor cancels one pole, but not the next pair

The fresh worker proposed keeping q and replacing the derivative
prefactor by

 Atilde_p=J_p^3 [t(q)+t(q^(p^2))]/[t(q)-t(q^(p^2))].

It has integral q coefficients in Z[sqrt2], correct Fricke weight, and
avoids the artificial earlier positive-real pole. Its nearest pole is
the intended Fricke point, and H_p minus its theta target cancels it.
This is a valid improvement in the geometric construction.

However the complete degree-two fiber description of t on X0(32)
identifies the next denominator zeros. They form the conjugate pair

 tau_+=1/(2p^2)+i sqrt(p^2-8)/(sqrt32 p^2),
 tau_-=-conjugate(tau_+),
 q_+=rho_p exp(pi i/p^2), q_-=conjugate(q_+),
 rho_p=exp[-pi sqrt(p^2-8)/(2sqrt2 p^2)]<1.

They are the unique next pair for every odd p>=3. The p=3 tie that
appears in a crude height bound is ruled out by the determinant
congruence for Gamma0(32). For large p these zeros are simple and
the numerator is nonzero; small selected primes are checked separately.

An exact shifted-period identity gives

 H_p(tau_+)-target -> -(1/2)H32'(i/sqrt32).

The derivative is rigorously enclosed as i times7.6426869042..., so
the pair remains uncanceled for all sufficiently large primes.
Direct certificates additionally cover3,5,7,11,17,31,101; no effective
threshold for all other small primes is claimed.

For each covered fixed p, the q-series remainder has radius exactly
rho_p. Its Fricke and reality symmetries force the leading residue
phase to be a real multiple of tau_+. The resulting periodic coefficient
factor cannot vanish: otherwise a rational cosine of a rational
multiple of pi would equal1-16/p^2, outside its possible rational values.
Consequently EVERY sufficiently late raw coefficient has absolute
value at least c(p)rho_p^(-n), c(p)>0.

This all-index statement is still about raw real coefficient forms in
one embedding. Splitting their two rational components and removing
common integer content remains a different arithmetic question.
There is no primitive-value no-go theorem for this prefactor here.

Proof: fresh/alternative_prefactor.txt. The coordinator and fresh worker
jointly derived the pole/phase mechanism; a third worker audited it.
The degree of t was clarified during review: degree2 on X0(32), degree1
on its Fricke quotient. Exact symmetry proves the residue's imaginary
direction; interval checks support consistency and certify nonzero size.

5. A uniform primitive obstruction for Euler--Boole approximants

The separate fresh construction uses the classical midpoint alternating
tail expansion. Let S_N=sum_(k<N)(-1)^k/(2k+1)^4 and

 A_(N,m)=S_N+(-1)^N/12 sum_(j<m)
             E_(2j)(2j+1)(2j+2)(2j+3)/(2N)^(2j+4),

where E_(2j) are integer Euler numbers. Write A=a/q in lowest terms.
The exact sech partial fractions give a strictly signed integral error
and, for every m>=1,

 |theta-A_(N,m)|>=pi^2 exp(-pi N)/(144N).

The lower exponential rate is sharp: at m=pi N/2+O(1), the error is
asymptotic to pi^3 exp(-pi N)/(48sqrt(2N)). Increasing the truncation
order arbitrarily cannot evade the uniform lower bound.

The correction has no denominator primes outside those dividing6N.
Large primes in the original partial sum therefore retain their fourth
layers in the ACTUAL reduced fraction. A fixed-prime-band argument,
followed by a fixed-K-then-liminf limit, proves uniformly in m

 liminf log q_(N,m)/N >=8,
 liminf log|q_(N,m)theta-a_(N,m)|/N >=8-pi>0.

The primitive values diverge whenever N+m tends to infinity, including
bounded N and growing m. The finitely small examples are recorded and
do not contradict the theorem. Gauss's lemma also rules out repairing
this family merely by multiplying its primitive factors: their
degree-normalized values have a positive lower bound. General sums,
resultants, or nonfactoring determinants are not excluded.

Full proof: fresh/findings.txt; separate coordinator review in
coordinator/boole_review.txt. No novelty claim is made for either the
classical summation method or the proved family obstruction.
