Block arithmetic: positive moments, exact recurrences, and denominator bounds
28 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

Outcome and scope
-----------------
This investigation establishes several uniform identities for the coupled
Catalan/beta(4) family, but does not prove the irrationality of beta(4).

The main analytic result is a positive moment representation for BOTH
normalized error sequences, on the same interval [0,phi^(-10)]. It gives
an explicit bound for arbitrary block combinations that retains cancellation
between indices. This replaces the earlier unspecified contour constant.
An exact rational telescoping certificate also proves the mixed recurrence
and the formerly conjectural order-four recurrence. The positive moments
give the previously missing slit continuation directly.

On the arithmetic side, there are exact formulas for the powers of 2 in
all four coefficient sequences, and a sharper uniform residue denominator
lemma. These results separate some of the arithmetic costs; they do not
yet bound the size of the source coefficients needed in a growing block.

Combining exact lattice calculations with the new analytic bound gives
two integer forms A beta(4)+B with nonzero coefficient determinant, each
of absolute value below 3*10^(-34). These are finite examples, with 426-
and 432-bit coefficients, not an irrationality argument. No improvement
over numerical continued fractions at comparable height is claimed.

The missing theorem is still a uniform small-lift construction: as blocks
grow, produce two independent integral coefficient images whose source
polynomials tend to zero in the stated norm. A small image-lattice index
does not supply that theorem. No priority claim, independent review, or
formal verification is made; the symbolic and numerical checks here were
performed by the same agent.

1. Conventions and the source family
------------------------------------
Write G=beta(2), B4=beta(4), phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2, and

    rho=phi^(-10)=(123-55 sqrt(5))/2 < 1/122.

Throughout this note m is the BLOCK index, corresponding to source index
n=2m in the preceding notes. To avoid repeated subscripts, put

    (a_m,b_m,c_m,d_m)=(a_(2m),b_(2m),c_(2m),d_(2m))_old.

Thus a row numbered m in results/coupled_coefficients.json has the four
coefficients used here. They are rational, and the two small forms are

    R_m=a_m G+b_m,
    S_m=16a_m B4+c_m G+d_m.                                  (1)

Their construction comes from the classical Catalan approximants [Z] and
the quarter-pole hypergeometric family [KZ]; the pole splitting is the
experiment developed in hypergeometric-arithmetic.txt and coupled-holonomy.txt.
Specifically, for m>=0 set

    p_j=(2j+1)/4,   0<=j<=4m+1,
    K_(2m)=(4m+1)!/2^(4m+2),
    F_m(t,e)=K_(2m) (2t+2m+1)
                * product_{ell=-3m}^{m-1}(t-ell)
                / product_{j=0}^{4m+1}((t+p_j)^2-e^2).

An empty numerator product is one. Let F_m(t)=F_m(t,0), and
H_m(t)=sum_j(t+p_j)^(-2). Then

    R_m=sum_{t>=0} F_m(t),
    S_m=sum_{t>=0} F_m(t) H_m(t).                            (2)

The formal partial fractions give (1); no assumption of linear independence
of 1,G,B4 is used. Initial data are

    (a_0,b_0,c_0,d_0)=(8,0,0,0),
    (a_1,b_1,c_1,d_1)=(649/8,-10699/144,2445/8,-2026399/1296).

In particular R_0=8G and S_0=128B4. The earlier bounds
0<R_m<8rho^m and 0<S_m<(32/m)rho^m, m>0, remain valid.

2. Positive moments for both normalized sequences
--------------------------------------------------
For x=(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4) in (0,1)^4 put z=x_1 x_2 x_3 x_4 and

    Psi(x)=z product_i(1-x_i)^2/(1-z)^4,
    omega(x)=(1+z)/(1-z)^2 * (x_1 x_2)^(-3/4)(x_3 x_4)^(-1/4),
    ell(x)=-log x_1+log x_2-log x_3+log x_4.

Let nu be the arcsine probability measure on (0,1),
dnu(u)=du/(pi sqrt(u(1-u))). On six variables x,u,v define

    T=16(uv)^2 Psi(x),
    dmu=(1/4)omega(x) dx dnu(u)dnu(v),
    deta=(1/8)omega(x)ell(x)^2 dx dnu(u)dnu(v).

Then, for every integer m>=0,

    R_m/(4m+1)^2 = integral T^m dmu,
    S_m/(4m+1)^2 = integral T^m deta.                        (3)

Both measures are positive and finite. Their masses are 8G<8 and
128B4<128, and their pushforwards by T have infinite support in [0,rho].

2.1 Derivation by beta integrals

The integer zeros in the numerator allow t=m+k, k>=0. Splitting the
quarter progressions in the denominator gives the four rising factorials

    product_{b in {1/4-e,1/4+e,3/4-e,3/4+e}} (k+m+b)_(2m+1).

Use

    1/(a)_(2m+1)=1/(2m)! * integral_0^1 x^(a-1)(1-x)^(2m) dx

and the elementary power-series identity

    sum_{k>=0}(2k+4m+1)(k+1)_(4m) z^k
       =(4m+1)! (1+z)/(1-z)^(4m+2).

Consequently

    sum_t F_m(t,e)=C_m integral Psi(x)^m omega(x) exp(e ell(x)) dx,
    C_m=((4m+1)!)^2/[2^(4m+2)((2m)!)^4].                    (4)

This identity is analytic for |e|<1/4. At zero endpoints all exponents
remain greater than -1 on smaller closed e-disks. Near x_i=1 for all i,
the singular factor is O((sum_i(1-x_i))^(-2)), integrable in four
dimensions. Logarithmic derivatives are also integrable. These estimates
justify coefficient extraction, or one can first use positive real e and
then analytic continuation on a smaller disk.

The arcsine moments give

    integral u^(2m)dnu(u)=binom(4m,2m)/16^m,
    C_m=(4m+1)^2/4 * 16^m * (integral u^(2m)dnu(u))^2.

Taking the e^0 and e^2 coefficients in (4), with [e^2]exp(e ell)=ell^2/2,
proves (3). In particular, the second measure is positive; it is not a
signed measure inferred from a recurrence. Swapping x_1,x_2 and x_3,x_4
changes ell to -ell and explains the evenness in e.

2.2 The sharp support endpoint

At a fixed product z=x^4, concavity of log(1-exp(s)), s<0, gives

    product_i(1-x_i) <= (1-x)^4.

It follows that

    Psi(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)
       <= [x(1-x)/((1+x)(1+x^2))]^4.

With s=x+x^(-1)>=2, the square of the bracket is
(s-2)/(s^2(s+2)). Differentiation gives its maximum at
s=1+sqrt(5)=2phi, with value 1/(4phi^5). Therefore 16Psi<=rho,
and hence 0<=T<=rho. Equality is approached when the four x_i coincide
at the maximizer and u,v approach one. Although ell then vanishes, every
neighborhood of this configuration contains points where ell!=0, so the
second measure has the same support endpoint. Both pushforwards have
infinite support. Their normalized Hankel matrices are positive definite.
No favorable arithmetic bound for those Hankel determinants is asserted.

2.3 Cancellation, continuation, and a polynomial filter

For any two real polynomials P(t)=sum x_m t^m and Q(t)=sum y_m t^m,

 |sum_m (x_m R_m+y_m S_m)/(4m+1)^2|
       <= 8 ||P||_[0,rho] +128 ||Q||_[0,rho].                (5)

Unlike a sum of absolute source terms, (5) retains cancellation in P,Q.
The normalized generating functions are Stieltjes transforms of mu,eta,
and continue holomorphically to C minus [1/rho,infinity). Applying
(4theta+1)^2, theta=z d/dz, gives the same continuation for the original
generating functions. Explicitly, their kernels are

    sum_{m>=0}(4m+1)^2 (zT)^m=(1+22zT+9(zT)^2)/(1-zT)^3.

This closes the slit-continuation gap for S in coupled-holonomy.txt. It
does not establish Q(z)-linear independence, or fix the negative arithmetic
margin of the holonomy templates tested there.

A simple integer polynomial illustrates the analytic gain. For
p(t)=t(t-1)(t-2),

    sup_{0<=t<=256rho}|p(t)|=2/(3sqrt(3)) < 1.

The two interior extrema have that magnitude. For the endpoint beyond 2,
use 256rho<128/61<17/8 and p(17/8)^2<4/27. Hence replacing P(T) by
p(256T)^N in (5) produces exponentially decreasing integrals, bounded by
8*(2/(3sqrt(3)))^N or 128 times that power. These are explicit analytic
filters. Their coefficients have NOT been shown to meet the required
integrality and auxiliary-period conditions; the filter alone is not an
irrationality construction.

3. A computable norm and finite integer forms
---------------------------------------------
Use the circle with center h=1/256 and radius r=1/224, and its normalized
L2 norm. The interval [0,rho] lies strictly inside the circle. Since
rho<1/122,

    |(t-h)/r| <= 469/488   (0<=t<=rho).

Cauchy--Schwarz on the Taylor coefficients about h gives
|P(t)| <= ||P||_circle /sqrt(1-((t-h)/r)^2) < 4||P||_circle.
Thus (5) implies the entirely explicit inequality

 |sum_m (x_m R_m+y_m S_m)/(4m+1)^2|
       < 514 sqrt(||P||_circle^2+||Q||_circle^2)              (6)

for nonzero P,Q. Indeed the two separate constants are 32 and 512,
and 32^2+512^2<514^2. The monomial Gram matrix is rational:

    G_ij=sum_{k=0}^{min(i,j)} binom(i,k)binom(j,k)
                             h^(i+j-2k) r^(2k).             (7)

In a block the indices i,j are the actual m=N,...,N+w-1, not local
indices starting at zero.

For reference, an explicit constant also applies without dividing source
forms by (4m+1)^2. Write D=(4t d/dt+1)^2=16t^2 d^2/dt^2+24t d/dt+1.
For a polynomial in the unit-circle coordinate, delta=1-(469/488)^2>1/14
gives evaluation bounds 4,84,3920 for it and its first two derivatives
times its L2 norm. These follow by differentiating the geometric-series
kernel; the squared derivative norms are (1+s^2)/(1-s^2)^3 and
4(1+4s^2+s^4)/(1-s^2)^5. Since t/r<2, |DP(t)|<255000||P||_circle.
Using sqrt(8^2+128^2)<129 gives the safe constant 33,000,000 for the
unnormalized pair. This retroactively makes the old circle-norm results
absolute certificates as well; the normalized bound is much cleaner.

3.1 Exact lattice construction and certification

At each source m, the normalized coefficient rows in coordinates (G,B4,1)
are

    (a_m,0,b_m)/(4m+1)^2,
    (c_m,16a_m,d_m)/(4m+1)^2.

Adjoining two integer-coordinate slack variables and taking an integer
kernel after clearing matrix denominators enforces zero G coefficient
and integer B4 and constant coefficients. Exact LLL on the Gram matrix
(7) seeks short source polynomials. Every coefficient image is then
recomputed exactly. Zero images are discarded, nonzero images are divided
by their complete gcd, and two with nonzero coefficient determinant are
selected. The same divisor is applied to the source polynomials and norm.

The selection uses the source coefficients and the rational norm, not a
numerical value of beta(4). The final error bounds follow from (6).

    N     w      upper bound for BOTH errors, normalized construction
    8    16                  2.55*10^(-19)
    8    24                  1.40*10^(-27)
   16    16                  6.06*10^(-17)
   16    24                  3.00*10^(-34)

For the last row, the individual bounds are below 2.408*10^(-34) and
2.563*10^(-34). Coefficient sizes are 426 and 432 bits. A separate
Fraction computation checks every coefficient coordinate, directly
translates each polynomial to the circle, and checks the norm and the
squared rational error inequality. Arb evaluations also enclose the
actual errors, around 8.79*10^(-41) and 7.32*10^(-42), but these
evaluations are not used to derive (6) or the displayed certificates.

If two independent integer coefficient pairs with such bounds could be
constructed with errors tending to zero along unbounded blocks, beta(4)
would be irrational: at a rational value p/q at least one form would be
a nonzero multiple of 1/q. The present finite table supplies no such
uniform construction. The table is not monotone in N, and tiny errors
alone are uninformative without controlling coefficient size or proving
a limiting theorem. Earlier numerical continued-fraction comparisons
were substantially better at comparable heights.

4. A uniform mixed recurrence, certified by telescoping
--------------------------------------------------------
Let

    L f_m=f_(m+2)+u(m)f_(m+1)+v(m)f_m,
    u=q_1/q_2,  v=q_0/q_2,

where the degree-14 polynomials q_j are the exact coefficient blocks in
results/coupled_guess_a.json. This base recurrence was already derived
from [Z] by eliminating odd source indices. Despite the discovery filename,
it is proved, and La=Lb=LR=0.

There are explicit rational functions P(m),Q(m), stored as rational
coefficient lists in results/block_recurrence.json, for which

    L S_m+P(m)R_m+Q(m)R_(m+1)=0,                            (8)
    L c_m+P(m)a_m+Q(m)a_(m+1)=0,
    L d_m+P(m)b_m+Q(m)b_(m+1)=0.

Both rational functions have numerator degree 26 and denominator degree
28, with P(m)~2/m^2 and Q(m)~-398/m^2. They were first reconstructed
from finite data. The proof of (8) is the following rational-function
certificate, not agreement with further terms.

4.1 Certificate specification

Put e_j=t+2m+(2j+1)/4. There are polynomials Tnum,Znum in t over Q(m),
of degrees 13 and 25, and rational functions

    T_m(t)=(t-m)Tnum(t)/[(2t+2m+1) product_{j=2}^7 e_j^2],
    Z_m(t)=(t-m)Znum(t)/[(2t+2m+1) product_{j=2}^7 e_j^4].

Their complete factored coefficient lists are in
results/block_telescoping.json. They satisfy the identities

    F_(m+2)+uF_(m+1)+vF_m = Delta_t(F_m T_m),

    F_(m+2)H_(m+2)+uF_(m+1)H_(m+1)+vF_m H_m
           +P F_m+Q F_(m+1)
        = Delta_t(F_m (T_m H_m+Z_m)).                        (9)

Here Delta_t g(t)=g(t+1)-g(t). These are identities of rational functions
of t for each m, proved using rational shift quotients over Q(m).

For a compact way to reproduce or verify the certificate, let

    A=(t+3m+1)(t+1/4)^2(t+3/4)^2,
    B=(t-m)e_8^2 e_9^2,
    D2=product_{j=2}^9 e_j^2,
    r_i=F_(m+i)/F_m.

The first polynomial equation is

    A Tnum(t+1)-B Tnum(t)=(2t+2m+1)D2 (r_2+u r_1+v).

The right side is polynomial after cancellation. With
tau=F_m(t+1)/F_m(t), dH_i=H_(m+i)-H_m, and
dH_t=H_m(t+1)-H_m(t), the second is

    A e_2^2 e_3^2 Znum(t+1)-(t-m)e_8^4 e_9^4 Znum(t)
       =(2t+2m+1)D2^2
          * [r_2 dH_2+u r_1 dH_1+P+Q r_1-tau T_m(t+1)dH_t].

It too is polynomial. Solving coefficients from the top down has diagonal
entries d-4m-15, 0<=d<=13, and d-4m-27, 0<=d<=25, respectively.
These never vanish at m>=0. The residual lower coefficients all vanish
identically over Q(m), as checked by experiments/block_telescoping.py.
The displayed coefficient files and this algorithm are the exact algebraic
certificate; a finite numerical fit is not being used as a substitute.

At t=0 the boundary is zero: F_m(0)=0 for m>=1, and T_0,Z_0 have a
factor t. At infinity F_m=O(t^(-4m-3)), H_m=O(t^(-2)), and T_m,Z_m=O(t),
so the upper boundary also vanishes. All relevant denominators are
nonzero for m>=0 and t>=0. Summing (9) proves the real recurrence.

The coefficient recurrences in (8) require a little care. They are not
inferred from an unproved independence of G,B4,1. Apply the formal
partial-fraction summation map instead. A difference g(t+1)-g(t) cancels
its complete quarter-progression period coefficients and leaves the
rational boundary -g(0), which is zero here. Thus its three formal
coordinates vanish separately.

4.2 The formerly conjectural order-four equation

The operator in results/coupled_guess_c.json (also the d candidate) has
the base L as a right factor. Substituting (8) into that exact factorization
and reducing the remaining shifts of R using LR=0 leaves two zero rational
functions. This is checked symbolically in experiments/block_symbolic_checks.py.
Consequently that order-four, degree-52 recurrence is now proved for
c,d,S, as well as a,b,R. The old finite-discovery files retain their
original status text; the proof is supplied by the later certificates.

Its generating-function equation has theta order 52, with highest-theta
coefficient, up to a nonzero constant, (z^2-123z+1)^2. Thus the only
possible finite differential singularities are 0,rho,1/rho. No minimality
or functional-independence conclusion is inferred. Section 2 supplies a
simpler direct proof of the desired continuation of the error functions.

5. Exact powers of 2
---------------------
Let s_2(m) be the binary digit sum and nu_2 the 2-adic valuation. For m>=1,

    nu_2(a_m)=3+2s_2(m)-8m,
    nu_2(b_m)=nu_2(a_m)-1,
    nu_2(c_m)=nu_2(a_m)-2 floor(log_2 m),
    nu_2(d_m)=nu_2(c_m)-1.                                 (10)

In particular, the deformation adds only logarithmically many powers of 2
to these coefficient denominators. This is a uniform theorem about the
individual coefficients, distinct from the finite image-index pattern
in Section 7.

5.1 Rational-function inputs

Put

 h(m)=225280m^6+856064m^5+1328384m^4+1076736m^3
                         +480784m^2+112176m+10699.

The factorizations are

 q_2=64(m+2)^2(2m+3)^2(4m+5)^2(4m+7)^2 h(m),
 q_0=64(m+1)^2(2m+1)^2(4m+1)^2(4m+3)^2 h(m+1),
 D=(m+2)^4(2m+3)^4(4m+5)^4(4m+7)^4 h(m)^2,
 P=(m+1)^2(2m+1)^2 P22(m)/D,
 Q=-Q26(m)/(64D).

Each of h,-q_1,P22,Q26 is an integer polynomial with odd constant
coefficient and all other coefficients even. These are exact polynomial
identities checked from the stored coefficients, so for every integer m>=0,

 nu_2(u)=-6-2nu_2(m+2),
 nu_2(v)=2nu_2(m+1)-2nu_2(m+2),
 nu_2(P)=2nu_2(m+1)-4nu_2(m+2),
 nu_2(Q)=-6-4nu_2(m+2).                                    (11)

5.2 Induction

Let A_m=3+2s_2(m)-8m, with A_0=3. The identity
s_2(m+1)-s_2(m)=1-nu_2(m+1) gives

    A_(m+1)-A_m=-6-2nu_2(m+1).

In the recurrence for a, the term u a_(m+1) has lower valuation than
v a_m by 12+4nu_2(m+1). There is therefore no cancellation at the
least valuation, and the initial data prove the first formula. The same
argument gives b with the extra -1; b_0=0 is handled directly.

For c at target index n=m+2, compare u c_(n-1) with Q a_(n-1).
Writing ell=floor(log_2(n-1)), their valuations differ by
2(nu_2(n)-ell). If n is not a power of 2, the u term is strictly lower.
If n is a power of 2, the Q term is strictly lower. The P term has
valuation 12+4nu_2(n-1) greater than the Q term, and the v term is
strictly above the relevant minimum. For n>=3 its comparison with the
u term has gap 12+4nu_2(n-1)+2(floor(log_2(n-1))-floor(log_2(n-2))).
The case n=2 follows directly since c_0=0. This proves the c formula,
including the extra factor 4 at each power-of-2 index. Replacing a,c by
b,d shifts the relevant valuations by -1 and proves the d formula;
the zero terms at index zero are again handled directly.

6. A sharper uniform residue denominator lemma
-----------------------------------------------
For this section only return to the ORIGINAL even source index n=2m.
Let M=2n+1 and L_j=lcm(1,...,j). Let H_j(e) be the moving-pole residue
numerator from coupled-holonomy.txt, so that A_j(e)=16K_n H_j(e) and

 A_j(e)=F_j(e) product_{h!=j}(1+4e/(h-j))^(-1),
 F_j(e)=8/4^n * Q_j(e)/(2n)! * binom(M,j)^2 (M-2j+4e)/M,
 Q_j(e)=product_{ell=-3n/2}^{n/2-1}(4e-(2j+1)-4ell).

For every j=0,...,M and every k>=0,

              16^n L_M^k [e^k] A_j(e) is an integer.        (12)

Taylor coefficients, not derivatives, are used, so the factorial costs
of differentiation have not been discarded.

6.1 Odd primes

For an odd prime p, s=3n/2-(2j+1)/4 is p-integral, and
Q_j(e)/(2n)!=4^(2n) binom(s+e,2n). Vandermonde's polynomial identity
expands this as a sum of binom(s,2n-r)binom(e,r). The first factor is
p-integral: approximate s by nonnegative integers modulo arbitrarily high
p-powers and use continuity of this fixed rational polynomial.

For r>=1,

    binom(e,r)=e/r product_{i=1}^{r-1}(e/i-1).

An e^k coefficient has a denominator dividing a product of k integers
at most 2n. Hence L_(2n)^k clears it. The constant part of the remaining
factor is integral because

 binom(M,j)^2 (M-2j)/M
      =binom(M,j)[binom(M,j)-2binom(M-1,j-1)].

Its linear term has denominator M, costing one additional L_M for one
additional Taylor order. Finally, the inverse factors in A_j have
coefficients (-4/(h-j))^k, also cleared order by order by L_M^k.
This proves (12) at every odd prime.

6.2 The prime 2 and coefficient consequences

At 2 the polynomial Q_j has integer coefficients, M is odd, and
16^n F_j has integral coefficients: its possible 2-denominator is bounded
by 2n+nu_2((2n)!)-3<=4n-4 at positive even n. The inverse factors are
again cleared by L_M^k. The case n=0 is immediate from the displayed
products. This completes (12).

Let alpha_(j,k)=[e^k]A_j(e), and let T_(j,s) be the finite quarter-tail
sum of order s removed in shifting p_j to 1/4 or 3/4. Explicitly, with
r=1+2(j mod 2),

    T_(j,s)=sum_{i=0}^{floor(j/2)-1}4^s/(4i+r)^s.

The formal coefficients at original source n are

 a_n=sum_{j even}alpha_(j,0),
 c_n=sum_{j even}alpha_(j,2),
 b_n=-(1/16)sum_j[alpha_(j,0)T_(j,2)+alpha_(j,1)T_(j,1)],
 d_n=-(1/16)sum_j sum_{k=0}^3 alpha_(j,k)T_(j,4-k).

Every odd tail denominator is at most 4n-1. Combining (12), the total
Taylor-plus-tail orders, and the exact 2-valuations (10), gives, for even
n>=2,

    16^n a_n in Z,
    16^n L_(2n+1)^2 c_n in Z,
    16^n L_(4n)^2 b_n in Z,
    16^n L_(4n)^4 d_n in Z.                                (13)

For b_n the classical stronger L_(2n)^2 bound is already available [Z];
no improvement for b is claimed here. For the deformation, (13) improves
the earlier proved L_(6n+3) bounds and their extra constant factors.
In block notation the c,d multipliers are 256^m L_(4m+1)^2 and
256^m L_(8m)^4, respectively.

The still sharper inclusions with L_(2n)^2 for c and L_(2n)^4 for d
observed in the old coefficient table were unproved at this stage. The
subsequent half-pole-arithmetic.txt proves the stronger L_n^2 inclusion
for c, at every index, and a gcd bound for d with the same exponential
rate as L_(2n)^4. The exact L_(2n)^4 inclusion for d remains unproved.
Neither these individual bounds nor (13) establish inexpensive block
combinations.

7. What the coefficient-image computations say
-----------------------------------------------
For the UNNORMALIZED source rows (a_m,0,b_m),(c_m,16a_m,d_m), form

    Gamma_(N,w)=(sum of their Z-spans, m=N,...,N+w-1) intersect Z^3.

This can be computed in three dimensions by duality, avoiding the costly
source lift. If Lambda is the rational row lattice generated by the
sources, then (Lambda intersect Z^3)^*=Lambda^*+Z^3. Hermite forms on
both sides give an exact basis for Gamma. A separate construction using
the full integer kernel agrees in nine small cases.

In all 128 cases N=1,...,64 with widths w=2 and w=3N, the observed index
has

    nu_2([Z^3:Gamma_(N,w)])=8N+3-2s_2(N).                   (14)

For w=3N, its odd part is one in all 64 cases, and G coefficient one is
attainable. At width two, G coefficient one is NOT always attainable:
N=53 and N=58 have first-coordinate generator 97. A separate 31-case
grid also tests intermediate widths and larger N. Width 2N does not
always remove the odd index factors in that grid.

Equation (14) and the saturation at w=3N are finite patterns, NOT uniform
theorems. In particular the simpler guess 8N+1 for the valuation fails
away from one-bit N; the binary digit sum matters.

Even a proof of these index patterns would leave the key difficulty.
An image basis does not control the source polynomials realizing it.
There are 2w-3 exact-identity directions in a rank-three source block,
and a short lattice vector often has zero coefficient image. A volume
argument that counts those directions as useful approximants is invalid.

The next substantive target is therefore a constructive bound for two
independent images together with their source lifts in (6), or an explicit
arithmetically admissible polynomial family in (5). This requires control
of the real size as well as congruence solvability. The new recurrences
and moments supply tools to study that target, not its solution.

8. Reproduction and evidence
-----------------------------
Run from beta4. The 360-row coupled coefficient file is an existing input.

  .venv/bin/python experiments/block_recurrence.py
  python3 experiments/block_telescoping.py
  python3 experiments/block_symbolic_checks.py
  python3 experiments/block_valuation_checks.py
  .venv/bin/python experiments/block_residue_checks.py
  .venv/bin/python experiments/block_circle_lattice.py --normalized \
      --output results/block_circle_normalized.json
  python3 experiments/block_moment_checks.py
  .venv/bin/python experiments/block_image_lattice.py
  .venv/bin/python experiments/block_lattice_checks.py

The mixed telescoper is checked over Q(m), including all residual
coefficients. Separate direct rational checks cover 50 summand identities
and five zero-boundary examples. Exact Ore reduction has two zero residuals.
The valuation checks include four parity-polynomial identities and 1436
coefficient valuations. The residue checks include 392 Taylor inclusions,
seven fully rebuilt coefficient quadruples, and 1077 global inclusions.

The moment checks compare 105 beta-integral summands and 31 arcsine
prefactors. They separately rebuild all 16 saved circle forms, including
their coefficient triples, exact norms, nonzero pair determinants, and
rational absolute-error bounds. The lattice checker reproduces the 128
image patterns and nine kernel-versus-dual comparisons. These finite
checks support the displayed uniform proofs; they do not replace them.

The older discovery files retain their original experimental status text.
Use block_telescoping.json and block_symbolic_checks.json for the later
recurrence proof, and block_moment_checks.json for the later absolute
bounds on the original unnormalized circle experiments.
