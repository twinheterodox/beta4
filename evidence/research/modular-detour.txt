MODULAR DETOUR: A FALSE PREMISE, A PERIOD FILTER, AND AN APERY LIMIT
27 October 2026. Local and unpublished research record.

Status
------
There is no irrationality proof here. This investigation began by deliberately
pretending that beta(4) might inherit the arithmetic of a weight-two problem.
That premise fails for the direct modular change of coordinates: fourth powers
of odd primes remain in the denominators. Pursuing it nevertheless produced
an explicit filter that cancels the unwanted pi^4 period polynomial identically,
and a different pair of rational approximation sequences.

The construction is an application of classical modular/Eichler methods, not
a claim of priority. The exact period filter and the resulting sequences are
new to this investigation. The literature check in Section 8 identifies the
classical starting identity and approximation mechanism; no exact match for
the level-32 sequences was found, but their novelty has not been established.
The first 160 terms do not give shrinking primitive integer
linear forms. No independent audit or formal verification is claimed.

1. Definitions and the classical input
--------------------------------------
Write chi(n)=0 for even n and chi(n)=(-1)^((n-1)/2) for odd n. Set
q=exp(2 pi i tau), with Im(tau)>0, and define

  LF(tau) = sum_{n>=1} chi(n) q^n / (n^4(1-q^n)),
  LG(tau) = sum_{n>=1} q^n / (n^4(1+q^(2n))),
  F(tau)  = LF(tau)-2 LF(2 tau),
  G(tau)  = LG(tau)-LG(2 tau)/16.

Equivalently,

  F(tau) = sum chi(n) q^n / (n^4(1+q^n)),
  G(tau) = sum_{n odd} q^n / (n^4(1+q^(2n))).

All these series converge absolutely in the upper half-plane. The factor
1/16 in G is essential: four q-derivatives of LG(2 tau)/16 give the
unscaled Fourier series at 2 tau.

With D=q d/dq, put

  EF = D^4 LF,             EG = 5/4 + D^4 LG,
  f  = EF(tau)-32 EF(2 tau),
  g  = EG(tau)-EG(2 tau).

Then D^4 F=f and D^4 G=g. EF and EG are the two weight-five Eisenstein
series whose nonconstant Dirichlet series are beta(s) zeta(s-4) and
zeta(s) beta(s-4), respectively.

The general setting is classical: Berndt--Straub [BS, Sections 4--6,
especially Example 6.4] treats the relevant level-four Eisenstein series,
their Eichler integrals, and the resulting L-value identities. Yang [Y]
explains the modular construction of Apéry limits through cancellation of
singularities. Neither source is evidence that the particular sequences
below are new, or that their irrationality estimates work.

The normalizations used here give

  f(-1/(8 tau)) = 64 i tau^5 g(tau),
  g(-1/(8 tau)) = 512 i tau^5 f(tau).

Bol's fourth-derivative identity shows that

  tau^3 F(-1/(8 tau)) - i G(tau)/64

is a polynomial of degree at most three. Its constants can be determined
without numerical fitting. The Mellin transform of LF(is/(2 pi)) is
Gamma(z) zeta(z) beta(z+4), and its small-s algebraic part is

  beta(5)/s - beta(4)/2 + beta(3)s/12 - beta(1)s^3/720.

All subsequent algebraic terms disappear through the trivial zeros. Hence

  F(is/(2 pi)) = beta(4)/2 - pi^3 s/128 + pi s^3/192
                 + a remainder smaller than any fixed power of s.

Substitution at s=pi i/(4 tau) determines the exact period identity

  tau^3 F(-1/(8 tau)) - i G(tau)/64
    = beta(4) tau^3/2 - i pi^4 (tau^2/512 + 1/12288).       (1)

The Fourier/theta identities behind the transformation laws, the
normalizations, and numerical enclosures of (1) are checked in the scripts.
Those enclosures are consistency checks, not the proof of (1).

2. First cancellation: a special point
--------------------------------------
At tau0=i/sqrt(24), the pi^4 term in (1) vanishes and -1/(8 tau0)=3 tau0.
Consequently

  beta(4) = 2 F(3 tau0) + (3 sqrt(6)/2) G(tau0).            (2)

For a completely real form, set alpha=pi/sqrt(6). Then F(3 tau0) is
sum chi(n)/(n^4(exp(3 alpha n)+1)), while

  G(tau0) = sum_{n>=1} [1/(2 cosh(alpha n))
                         -1/(32 cosh(2 alpha n))]/n^4.

Equation (2) is a rapidly convergent identity at a transcendental q. It
does not by itself provide rational approximations with controlled height.

There is a useful extra cancellation. If

  H24(tau) = F(3 tau) + (3 sqrt(6)/4) G(tau),

its Fricke transformation has pi^4 correction

  (sqrt(24) tau-i)^2 (sqrt(24) tau+i) / (12288 tau^3).

This has a double zero at tau0. A double zero is not the same as the
absence of a period polynomial: it does not automatically remove an entire
branch singularity. That distinction prompted the next construction.

3. Cancelling the period polynomial identically
-----------------------------------------------
Let M be a positive integer and let a_r, indexed by divisors r of M, obey

  sum a_r r = 0,       sum a_r r^3 = 0.

Put S=sum a_r, K=sqrt(M^3/8), and

  b_s = K a_(M/s)/s^3,
  H(tau) = sum a_r F(r tau) + sum b_s G(s tau).

Applying (1) and its inverse to the individual summands gives

  H(-1/(8 M tau))-S beta(4)/2
    = i/((8M)^(3/2) tau^3) [H(tau)-S beta(4)/2].           (3)

This is an identity for every tau in the upper half-plane, with no pi^4
correction. The two moment conditions cancel all four coefficients of
that correction; the two conditions on the b_s follow from those on a_r.
At the Fricke fixed point, the multiplier in (3) is -1, so

  H(i/sqrt(8M)) = S beta(4)/2.

The small choice a_1=16, a_2=-10, a_4=1 has S=7. Its two moment conditions
are 16-20+4=0 and 16-80+64=0. For M=4 this gives

  H32 = 16F(tau)-10F(2tau)+F(4tau)
        +(sqrt(2)/2)[4G(tau)-5G(2tau)+G(4tau)].

For M=8 it gives a version with rational Fourier coefficients:

  H64 = 16F(tau)-10F(2tau)+F(4tau)
        +G(2tau)-(5/4)G(4tau)+(1/4)G(8tau).

The transformation identities for both are checked symbolically and by
interval arithmetic. A pure Fricke transformation does NOT imply that
H-S beta(4)/2 is a modular form for the whole congruence group. Periods
associated with other transformations have not disappeared automatically.

4. A concrete level-32 approximation construction
-------------------------------------------------
Use the usual theta convention theta3(z)=sum exp(pi i z n^2). Define

  t = eta(tau)^2 eta(4tau) eta(8tau) eta(32tau)^2
      /[eta(2tau)^3 eta(16tau)^3],
  E = eta(4tau)^2 eta(8tau)^2,
  Y = D log t,
  J_+ = theta3(2tau)^2 + 2 sqrt(2) theta3(16tau)^2,
  A_+ = J_+^5/Y,             B_+ = A_+ H32.

Here t=q-2q^2+2q^3-4q^4+..., so its inverse q(t) has integer
coefficients. Expressing A_+, B_+ in powers of t gives

  A_+ = sum (a_(n,0)+sqrt(2) a_(n,1)) t^n,
  B_+ = sum (b_(n,0)+sqrt(2) b_(n,1)) t^n.

The a_(n,j) are integers. If d_n=lcm(1,...,n), then

  2 d_n^4 b_(n,j) is an integer.                            (4)

This follows directly from the product/Fourier series: Y is an integer
power series with constant term 1; J has integral coefficients in
Z[sqrt(2)]; and 2d_n^4 clears the coefficients of H32 through degree n.
Integer series inversion and multiplication preserve this bound.

The geometry and local cancellation can be made explicit. The eta cusp
orders of t at denominators 1,2,4,8,16,32 are respectively

  1, -1, 0, 0, -1, 1.

Thus t has degree two on X0(32), is invariant under its Fricke involution,
and is a coordinate on the quotient. The eta form E is the nonzero
holomorphic differential on this genus-one curve, up to d tau. One has

  (Dt/E)^2 = P(t),
  P(t)=1-8t+12t^2-16t^3+4t^4.                              (5)

There is a short finite proof of (5): both sides are meromorphic modular
functions; their difference has poles of total degree at most eight,
at the two poles of t. Matching through q^8 gives a zero of order at least
nine at infinity and forces equality. The program checks through degree
160 instead. The eta modularity conditions and cusp orders are checked
separately with exact rational arithmetic.

For another explicit description, write J_-=theta3(2tau)^2-
2sqrt(2)theta3(16tau)^2. Then

  t J_+^2/E = R0(t)+sqrt(2) R1(t),
  R0(t)=(4t^4-24t^3+44t^2-28t+9)/(2t^2-2t+1),
  R1(t)=4/(2t^2-2t+1),                                     (6)

with the conjugate identity for J_-. After multiplication by the displayed
denominator, the possible pole degree is at most twelve, so agreement
through q^12 proves (6) by the same argument. These identities identify
the other finite cusp values as (1+i)/2 and (1-i)/2.

The four branch values, numerically, are

  t0 = 0.153332807156510...,
       3.26088075521659...,
       0.292893218813452... +/- 0.643594252905583... i.

The positive smallest one is t(i/sqrt(32)). The last two and the two
finite nonzero cusp values have modulus 1/sqrt(2).

The modular transformation laws give

  A_+(-1/(32tau)) = -i 32^(3/2) tau^3 A_+(tau),
  [B_+-(7/2)beta(4) A_+](-1/(32tau))
       = B_+-(7/2)beta(4) A_+.

At tau=i/sqrt(32), A_+ has a simple pole in the local coordinate before
taking the quotient, while H32-(7/2)beta(4) vanishes. Their product is
holomorphic and invariant under the local involution, and therefore
holomorphic in t at t0. For the conjugate construction, J_- vanishes
there, and the conjugate product is holomorphic and invariant as well.
Consequently both rational components B_j-(7/2)beta(4) A_j extend across
the first branch point.

These observations give the Apéry limits

  2 b_(n,j)/(7 a_(n,j)) --> beta(4),  j=0,1.                (7)

For completeness, the analytic justification is as follows. The only
finite obstructions to continuation are branch points or cusp values of
the modular parameter. The first has just been removed in B_j-c A_j.
For every r<1/sqrt(2), these two remainders are holomorphic on |t|<r.
Meanwhile A_+ has a nonzero inverse-square-root singularity at t0, and
A_- has no such leading term. Thus each rational component A_j has
coefficients asymptotic to a nonzero constant times t0^(-n)/sqrt(n).
Cauchy's estimate for the remainder proves (7), with relative error
O(sqrt(n)*(t0/r)^n) for each t0<r<1/sqrt(2). This is a limit argument,
not an estimate that proves irrationality.

The alternative level-64 filter is recorded in Section 3. A corresponding
level-64 coefficient construction has not been carried out here.

5. What fails arithmetically
----------------------------
The fast relative convergence in (7) is insufficient. Irrationality needs
small nonzero INTEGER linear forms after all denominators are cleared.
For each component, reduce

  7 a_(n,j) beta(4)-2 b_(n,j)

to primitive integer coefficients A beta(4)-B. The exact finite results
include the following natural logarithms of their absolute values:

       n       component 0       component 1
      20          77.0629            74.6052
      80         334.5580           336.4585
     160         667.7496           668.7483

They grow, rather than shrink, in the tested range. At n=160 even the
uncleared forms have logarithms 66.7717 and 66.3728. All reported values
are evaluated with rigorous real enclosures, which exclude zero.
This finite test is not a theorem ruling out all modifications or every
possible asymptotic phenomenon in the construction.

There is also an exact obstruction to the original cheap-denominator
premise. In the coordinate

  u=theta2(4tau)^2/theta3(4tau)^2=4q-16q^3+56q^5-...,
  H(u)={}_2F_1(1/2,1/2;1;u^2),

form BF=H(u)^3 F(tau(u)) and BG=H(u)^3 G(tau(u)). For every odd prime p,

  v_p([u^p]BF)=v_p([u^p]BG)=-4.                            (8)

Indeed, coefficients below q^p are p-integral, while the coefficient
at q^p is chi(p)/p^4 plus a p-integral number for F, and 1/p^4 plus a
p-integral number for G. The inverse q(u) is p-integral, with leading
term u/4, and H(u)^3 is a p-integral unit series. Thus the residues after
multiplication by p^4 are chi(p)4^(-p) and 4^(-p), respectively, modulo p.

The same argument applies to the level-32 coefficients. Since
A_+(0)=401+298sqrt(2), one has

  p^4 b_(p,0) = 6416chi(p)+1192 (mod p),
  p^4 b_(p,1) = 4768chi(p)+802  (mod p).                    (9)

The congruences are in the p-integral local ring. Each right side is
nonzero for all but finitely many primes, so the fourth-power cost also
persists here. Combined with (4), this shows that the least COMMON
denominator for the coefficients through degree n has logarithm
4n+o(n). It does NOT supply that lower bound for each individual reduced
coefficient, or for every more sophisticated linear combination.

6. The direct fourth-order recurrence
-------------------------------------
To make the initial modular experiment independently reproducible, let
theta=u d/du and define

  L = theta^4
      -u^2(3theta^4+12theta^3+26theta^2+28theta+12)
      +u^4(3theta^4+24theta^3+80theta^2+128theta+81)
      -u^6(theta+3)^4.

This is the symmetric cube of the differential equation for H(u), in a
polynomial normalization. It gives

  L(H^3)=0,
  L(BF)=u(u^2-3u+1)/(4(u+1)),
  L(BG)=(u-5u^3)/4.

The coefficient recurrences are obtained by equating powers of u. A
separate Sympy implementation derives L by differentiating the basis
y^3,y^2y',y(y')^2,(y')^3 and using the second-order equation for y.
Exact coefficients through degree 100 agree with independent Fourier
composition in the Flint implementation. The first 24 odd primes obey
(8); the level-32 experiment checks (9) through degree 160.

7. Interpretation and next mathematical issue
---------------------------------------------
The false premise was productive because it led to a construction that
was not simply another adjustment of the earlier determinant families.
The useful surviving fact is the global period cancellation (3), and
its exact removal of the first branch singularity in Section 4.

The cheap-arithmetic conclusion did not survive. The direct recurrence
keeps its fourth-power prime denominators, and the first complete
modular approximation family fails the tested integer-form budget.

A further attempt would need additional arithmetic cancellation or
control of the remaining cusp/branch contributions. Merely optimizing
the point in (2), observing many matching decimals in (7), or repeating
the same construction at larger n would not address that deficiency.
No proof that all modular approaches fail is asserted. Nor is a pure
Fricke symmetry being confused with vanishing of all other periods.

8. Literature and novelty check
--------------------------------
Checked 27 October 2026. The appropriate description is a possibly
unrecorded explicit specialization of an established method. The search
does not establish priority, and this record makes no such claim.

The starting transformation is classical, not a new identity. Bradley
[B, Corollary 4 and the following remarks] gives the even-beta formula and
attributes it to Ramanujan, with the first published proof due to Chowla.
Setting his integer parameter to 2 and writing his positive parameter as
-i pi tau gives, in our normalization,

  LF(tau)+beta(4)/2
    = i tau^3 LG(-1/(4tau))/2
      +i pi^4 [5/(3072tau)-tau/192-tau^3/360].             (10)

Initially this substitution is on the positive imaginary axis; holomorphic
continuation extends it to the upper half-plane. Subtract twice (10) at
2u from (10) at u, and set u=-1/(8tau). This gives exactly (1), including
its 1/64, 1/512 and 1/12288 constants. The polynomial reduction was also
checked with exact symbolic arithmetic. Thus (1) is a dilation consequence
of the classical beta transformation, rather than merely an analogy to it.

Berndt--Straub [BS, Example 6.4] gives the general character version through
Eisenstein Eichler integrals, recovering Katayama's 1974 formulas. Their
Example 7.7 also uses roots of a period polynomial to remove the explicit
pi-power contribution in special-value identities. This is a precedent
for the special-point cancellation in Section 2; it is not a claim that
their displayed special point is identical to ours.

The global filter also has an elementary explanation. Let T act by
Th(tau)=h(2tau). Then

  16-10T+T^2=(T-2)(T-8).

On the monomials tau and tau^3, T has eigenvalues 2 and 8. The filter
therefore kills precisely these two powers, while multiplying a constant
by 7. This explains the moment equations in Section 3. Combining the
paired F and G transformations then gives (3). The particular formula
was not located in the sources searched, but this algebra alone is not
a new cancellation principle.

The approximation mechanism is likewise established. Yang [Y, Section 2,
especially equation (8), and Section 3] develops Beukers's modular method:
a transformation law for an Eichler integral makes B-cA invariant at an
elliptic point, removes its dominant singularity, and identifies the limit
of coefficient ratios with an L-value. This is the mechanism used in
Section 4. It does not identify our specific level-32 parameter, theta
prefactor, or two rational component sequences as previously published.

Searches covered beta(4) and L(4,chi_{-4}) in connection with Eichler
integrals, Lambert series, Apéry limits, level 32, period cancellation,
and the initial coefficient strings. No exact match for H32, H64, or the
two level-32 sequences was found. Searches of indexed coefficient strings
are incomplete; changed normalizations or modular coordinates can conceal
equivalent constructions.

Additional comparisons included the Apéry-limit catalog of Almkvist,
van Straten and Zudilin [AVZ], the Chamberland--Straub survey [CS], and
the recent level-8 rederivation [S]. None supplied an identified exact
match. These comparisons support a cautious classification, not proof
of absence from the literature. Fast beta-value series are also already
available by other methods [HP], so rational approximation to beta(4)
by itself is not a novelty claim.

Assessment: the exact specialization might be worth recording as an
example, but it is not presently evidence of a new irrationality method.
The arithmetic deficiency in Section 5 remains the substantive obstacle.
