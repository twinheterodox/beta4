Beta(4): structural follow-up to the first investigation
27 October 2026 — unpublished local working record
Research note prepared with Codex

Outcome. A modified construction has a positive integral representation,
which proves nonvanishing at beta(4) for every determinant size. Its finite
arithmetic balance is worse than the original interlaced construction.
The original construction has not acquired a uniform nonvanishing proof
or the negative asymptotic bound needed for irrationality. No beta(4)
irrationality theorem is claimed.

This file extends research-notes.txt without replacing its record of the
first investigation. All statements labeled proofs below are mathematical
arguments in this working note, not formally verified or independently
reviewed results. Priority has not been established.

1. The two weights come from a known base system

Write theta=t d/dt. Define the base functionals

  B_1(R)=integral_0^infinity R(y^2) y csch(pi y) dy,
  C_1(R)=integral_0^infinity R(y^2) sech(pi y) dy.

In the t-coordinate their density ratio is

  h_1(t)=tanh(pi sqrt(t))/sqrt(t)
        =(2/pi) sum_{j>=0} 1/(t+(j+1/2)^2).

Thus the base pair is a Nikishin system. This exact hyperbolic-weight
pair, after rescaling, is the multiple Pollaczek system treated by
Aptekarev, Lopez Lagomasino, and Martinez-Finkelshtein [P1, Section 1;
P2, Section 3.3]. This connection is existing mathematics, not a new
property of our beta(4) construction.

Three integrations by parts give the exact functional identities

  B_4 = B_1 o [(2theta+2)(2theta+3)(2theta+4)/6],
  C_4 = C_1 o [(2theta+1)(2theta+2)(2theta+3)/6].

They hold for the polynomials and negative-square-pole rational functions
used here. At zero the boundary terms vanish: in the B case the factors
are y^4 R(y^2), with derivatives paired with a csch singularity; all three
boundary products are O(y). In the C case the corresponding factor is
y^3 R(y^2) and sech is regular. At infinity exponential decay dominates
every polynomial factor. The remaining differential expressions are

  y^(-1) d^3[y^4 R(y^2)]/dy^3
      =[(2theta+2)(2theta+3)(2theta+4)R](y^2),
  d^3[y^3 R(y^2)]/dy^3
      =[(2theta+1)(2theta+2)(2theta+3)R](y^2).

These identities explain why the coefficients of the two weights are
related, but they do not transfer the base system's positivity theorem.

2. Two precise obstacles to transferring that theorem

2.1 An explicit loss of normality

The first three moments are

  B_4: 1, 5/2, 14;
  C_4: 1/2, 5/4, 175/32.

If P(t)=c_0+c_1 t+c_2 t^2 satisfies B_4(P)=C_4(P)=0, subtracting
half the first equation from the second gives

  -(49/32)c_2=0.

Hence every such P has degree at most one, and the nonzero solutions are
multiples of t-5/2. In particular there is no monic degree-two polynomial
orthogonal to both weights at multi-index (1,1). The differentiated pair
is not perfect in the way the base Nikishin pair is. This does not assert
that our Cauchy-column determinants vanish; those are different matrices.

2.2 The ratio has nonreal poles

Put h=tanh(pi y). Direct differentiation gives

  w_C(y)/w_B(y) = h^5(6h^2-5)/(y(6-5h^2)).

Let u_0=artanh(sqrt(5/6)). At y=u_0/pi+i/2 we have
tanh(pi y)=coth(u_0)=sqrt(6/5). The denominator of the ratio vanishes
while the numerator does not. Thus its meromorphic continuation as a
function of t=y^2 has a nonreal pole. It cannot be the Stieltjes transform
of a positive measure supported on the negative real axis, unlike h_1.
Adding a polynomial to the ratio does not remove this pole. This rules
out that particular direct Nikishin argument, not all possible positivity
or nonvanishing arguments.

3. The original matrix also has a useful rank-two identity

For M_ij=F_j(t^i/(t+a_j^2)), where F_j is B_4 or C_4,

  M_(i+1),j + a_j^2 M_ij = F_j(t^i).

The right side has only two different columns. This is a rank-two
displacement relation and gives a possible link with simultaneous
rational approximation. It is an exact identity, not yet a recurrence
or asymptotic estimate for the determinant. No such estimate has been
deduced from it in this investigation.

4. A modified family with a proved nonvanishing property

The signed weight C_4 can be combined with B_4 in a controlled way.

Lemma. For all y>0,

  w_C(y) >= -(pi/4) w_B(y).

Proof. w_B is strictly positive. When w_C>=0 the assertion is immediate.
Otherwise put h=tanh(pi y) and u=h^2, so 0<u<5/6. Since
pi y=artanh(h)>=h,

  -w_C/w_B
    = pi h^5(5-6u)/(artanh(h)(6-5u))
    <= pi u^2(5-6u)/(6-5u).

The identity

  u(1-u)(6-5u)-u^2(5-6u)
       =u(11u^2-16u+6)
       =u[11(u-8/11)^2+2/11]>0

shows that the last expression is at most pi u(1-u)<=pi/4.
This proves the bound. In particular pi/4<1.

For an integer n>=1 define

  D_B(t)=prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(t+(j+1/2)^2),
  D_C(t)=prod_{j=1}^n(t+j^2).

Every factor of D_B(t) is smaller than its paired factor of D_C(t)
when t>=0. For any rational lambda with 0<=lambda<=1, define

  F_n(R)=B_4(R/D_B)+lambda C_4(R/D_C).

Its integral weight is

  W_n(y)=w_B(y)/D_B(y^2)+lambda w_C(y)/D_C(y^2)
        >=(1-pi/4) w_B(y)/D_B(y^2)>0.

For any integer h>=1 form the Hankel determinant

  H_(n,h)=det[F_n(t^(i+j))]_(0<=i,j<h).

Proposition. H_(n,h)>0 for every n,h>=1 and rational 0<=lambda<=1.
Every entry lies in Q+Q beta(4).

Proof. For any nonzero real polynomial p of degree less than h, the
quadratic form of the matrix is

  integral_0^infinity p(y^2)^2 W_n(y) dy > 0.

All integrals converge and the weight is strictly positive, so the
matrix is positive definite. Equivalently its determinant is

  1/h! integral_(0,infinity)^h
       prod_{i<j}(y_j^2-y_i^2)^2 prod_i W_n(y_i) dy_i > 0.

For arithmetic, polynomial division and simple partial fractions reduce
each B term to rational B moments and its half-integer pole values, and
each C term to rational C moments and its integer pole values. The
formulas in research-notes.txt then give Q+Q beta(4). The coefficient
matrix of beta(4) is a sum of at most 2n rank-one matrices, so the
determinant polynomial has degree at most min(h,2n).

This solves nonvanishing for this MODIFIED family. It neither proves
nonvanishing for the original interlaced Cauchy-column determinants nor
establishes the required arithmetic/real size inequality.

5. Numerical outcome for the positive family

As before, normalize each determinant polynomial to a primitive integer
polynomial Q, preserving its sign at beta(4). Measure

  Lambda = log Q(beta(4))/h^2.

At lambda=1, with h=2n, the comparison is:

  h    original interlaced    positive Hankel variant
  12      0.8535084278           2.0226481294
  24      1.1946000911           2.4196659277
  48      1.4381616267           2.6465210108

The comparison uses the same number 2n=h of pole locations. Nonvanishing
is now proved, but the primitive arithmetic balance is worse. Sixteen
designs were tested in total: n=6,12,24; h/n in {1/2,1,3/2,2}; and
lambda=1, with lambda=1/2 also tested at n=24. All Lambda values were
positive. This is finite evidence, not an impossibility theorem.

The implementation checks include exact agreement with the B-only
primitive polynomial when lambda=0 and h=n, at n=2,4,6. Six direct
integral comparisons for the combined weight at n=1,2 agree with the
rational formulas within 2.8e-59 using 60 working digits. The polynomial
coefficients and their primitive content are exact. Numerical evaluation
uses two precisions, not interval certification.

6. Adapted polynomial rows do not improve the tested designs

The ordinary Meixner--Pollaczek recurrence [D] at angle pi/2, with
H_m=m! P_m, is

  H_0=1, H_1=2y,
  H_(m+1)=2y H_m-m(m+2lambda-1)H_(m-1).

For lambda=1/2 and lambda=1 its even polynomials H_(2d)(y) are rational
polynomials in t=y^2, adapted respectively to the C_1 and B_1 weights.
The implementation verified 72 exact scalar orthogonality identities.

Replacing monomials of degrees 0,...,K-1 by any other rational basis
of that same polynomial space only multiplies the determinant by a
nonzero rational number. It therefore leaves the primitive integer
polynomial unchanged. The zero-offset tests confirm this exactly.

To change the row space, we tested sequences of consecutive adapted
polynomials with positive degree offsets, including inverse images under
the Euler operators in Section 1. We also used the genuine two-weight
base multiple Pollaczek polynomials, computed from their exact rational
moment equations. All their row denominators were powers of two in the
tested range. Thus their failure was not caused by introducing large odd
denominators in the row coefficients.

There were 40 designs including controls, at K=12 and 24. Every
positive-offset design was worse than the zero-offset baseline. For
example, at K=24 an offset of 6 in the true two-weight base sequence
gives Lambda=1.52637, versus 1.19460 at zero offset. The corresponding
individual-weight sequences give 1.58827 and 1.61862. These tests do not
exclude other row spaces; they do exclude a straightforward benefit
from this particular orthogonal-polynomial adaptation at these sizes.

7. Where the extra weight-four cost occurs

For a fixed matrix basis let D clear all rational entry coefficients.
Write g for the positive gcd of the coefficients of det(D M(X)). Then

  Q(X)=(D^K/g) det M(X)

is primitive. Thus

  log|Q(beta(s))| = log|det M(beta(s))| + K log D-log g.

The normalization term can be split into its 2-adic contribution and
all odd-prime contributions. This split uses the same monomial basis
at every weight; the individual terms are basis-dependent, while their
sum and Q are invariant under rational basis changes.

For the original mixed construction at K=24, raising the weight from
s=2 to s=4 changes the normalized quantities by

  real determinant size:             +0.1768086771
  2-adic normalization:              -0.0433216988
  odd-prime normalization:           +0.7761298201
  total:                             +0.9096167985

Here Lambda rises from 0.2849832926 to 1.1946000911. The same qualitative
pattern occurs at K=12. The EXTRA weight-four cost is dominated by odd
primes in these tests. This does not mean that the entire final gap is
an odd-prime term, or that an asymptotic prime-cost theorem has been
proved. The tested weight-two construction itself is already positive.

This provides a concrete reason to demand a new arithmetic cancellation
before pursuing larger versions of the same determinant search.

8. Finite prime tests and their limits

For the original primitive Q_K, exact reduction modulo p=K-1 gives

  K=6, p=5:    roots in F_p = {0};
  K=12, p=11:  roots in F_p = {7};
  K=24, p=23:  no roots in F_p;
  K=48, p=47:  no roots in F_p.

Thus Q_24(a/b) is nonzero for rational a/b with 23 not dividing b,
and similarly Q_48 with 47. This is a finite certificate, not a uniform
nonvanishing theorem: a hypothetical denominator may contain both primes.
The 2-adic Newton polygons also allow integral root valuations, so they
did not supply a simple unconditional exclusion of rational roots.

9. What has and has not been gained

We now have a structural connection with existing approximation theory,
an explicit demonstration of why its most immediate theorem does not
transfer, and a different determinant family for which nonvanishing has
a short proof. None of these supplies the shrinking primitive integer
polynomials required to prove beta(4) irrational. The positive-family
modification and the tested adapted row spaces do not close that gap.

The current evidence supports a change in the arithmetic construction,
specifically one that alters the odd-prime cost, before another large
optimization run. This is a research judgment based on these families,
not a theorem that all determinant methods must fail. The work remains
private and no release or announcement is warranted by these results.
