Beta(4): logarithmic finite differences and a positive cubic-tail kernel
28 October 2026. Local research record; unpublished.

Status: mathematical derivations with exact computational checks by the
same agent. No irrationality proof, independent audit, formal verification,
or priority claim is made. B=beta(4), G=beta(2), C3=beta(3)=pi^3/32.

1. Outcome and next research target

Two constructions give different parts of what a proof needs.

The first uses a fixed finite difference in two logarithmic variables.
One variable is evaluated at t and the other at t^k, with k odd. It gives
a positive function J_k(t) on 0<t<1, tending to zero at 1, whose Taylor
series is 6B plus rational coefficients. For k>=3 the rational part starts
at -4 t^(2k+4). More significantly, through degree N its odd-prime
denominators have at most two layers when p>N/k. Only the smaller primes
need the general four-layer allowance. The resulting explicit denominator
bound has logarithmic growth (2+2/k)N+o(N) for fixed k, instead of 4N+o(N).

This is a coefficient bound, not a bound for primitive determinant
polynomials. All 62 tested diagonal Pade constructions from this filter
still have primitive integer value above one. Function positivity does
not prove nonvanishing of those Pade linear forms. Increasing k also
moves the change from 6B to zero into a narrower neighborhood of t=1.
There is consequently no demonstrated improvement in the final
arithmetic-versus-decay inequality.

The second construction is a positive Cauchy-integral kernel at squared
integer nodes. Its entries contain only B and rational numbers, and every
ordered square minor is strictly positive. Explicit Cauchy determinant
bounds control the real size. Thus period isolation, determinant
nonvanishing, and a usable real upper bound are all available for this
family. Its arithmetic remains bad in the tested frames: 47 of 48
primitive values exceed one; the other is the polynomial X itself.
A local calculation explains one cost: the positive transform can turn
vanishing residue blocks into full-rank cubic-denominator Cauchy blocks.

The leading new arithmetic question is whether the two-layer saving of
the first construction can survive in a determinant with sufficient real
decay and a uniform nonvanishing proof. The positive kernel is a concrete
comparison family and a separate controlled test of node designs. These
properties have NOT been combined into one construction. Broadly enlarging
the same Pade or consecutive-node scans is not supported by the results.

2. A logarithmic form of the ladder restriction

Put b_j(w)=Im Li_j(i exp(w)) for real w, continued along this real path.
The harmonic restriction of the two-loop ladder from
two-loop-numerators.txt and radial-cancellation.txt can be written

 F(w)=3[B+b_4(w)]-(3/2)w[C3+b_3(w)]+(w^2/4)[G+b_2(w)].             (2.1)

It is even and satisfies

 F(w)=6B+(G/2)w^2+h(w),
 h'''(w)=w^2 sech'(w)/8,   h(0)=h'(0)=h''(0)=0.                  (2.2)

These identities follow by differentiating (2.1), or from the inversion
identities for the polylogarithms. In particular h is a rational Taylor
series. If E_j are the Euler numbers in sech(w)=sum E_j w^j/j!, then

 [w^j]h(w)=(j-3)(j-4) E_(j-4)/(8 j!)     (even j>=6),

and all other coefficients are zero. Thus h(w)=-w^6/960+O(w^8).

There is a useful positive tail for w>=0:

 T(w)=3b_4(-w)+(3/2)w b_3(-w)+(w^2/4)b_2(-w)
     =(1/8) integral_w^infinity (2u^3-3wu^2+w^2u) sech(u) du,

 F(w)=3B+(3/2)C3 w+(G/4)w^2+T(w).                              (2.3)

Indeed the integral follows by the Laplace representations of b_j(-w).
It has the same third derivative as F; checking the first three values
at zero gives the polynomial in (2.3). The integrand is
u(u-w)(2u-w), so T>0. Differentiating under the integral gives T'<0,
T''>0, and T'''=w^2 sech'(w)/8. Also T(w)=O((1+w^2)exp(-w)).

Integrating (2.2) by parts gives two formulas used below:

 h(x)=(1/8) integral_0^x u(x-u)(2u-x) sech(u) du,                 (2.4)
 h''(x)=x^2 sech(x)/8-(1/4) integral_0^x u sech(u) du.            (2.5)

3. Ordinary dilations: useful identities, poor finite Pade forms

For an integer m>=3 define

 J_m(w)=2 sum_(j=1)^m (-1)^(j-1) binom(m,j) F(jw/m)-F(0).

The elementary finite-difference identities for powers 0,1,2 show that
its beta coefficient is 6, its Catalan coefficient is zero, and its
polynomial part at positive infinity vanishes. Thus J_m(w)->0. For m=3,

 J_3(w)=6T(w/3)-6T(2w/3)+2T(w)>0.

Writing w=2 atanh(t) gives rational Taylor coefficients except for the
constant 6B. The rational part starts in degree max(6,2 ceil(m/2)).
The power-sum multiplier on w^j is
2(-1)^(m+1)m! S(j,m)/m^j, with S(j,m) a Stirling number of the second kind.
Using jw instead of jw/m also gives a valid filter.

Rational dilations introduce denominators at primes dividing m; integer
dilations move complex singularities inward. Among 112 diagonal Pade
tests (m=3,4,5,6,7,8,10,12; both scalings; orders through 32), four systems
were singular and all 108 nondegenerate primitive integer forms exceeded
one. These outcomes motivated the next construction rather than a larger
scan of the same filters.

4. A slow logarithmic variable and a reduced denominator bound

Let k>=1 be odd and set

 u=log((1+t)/(1-t)),    v=log((1+t^k)/(1-t^k)),

 J_k(t)=-F(0)+2F(u)+4F(v)-4F(u+v)-2F(2v)+2F(u+2v).             (4.1)

This is F(0)-2(1-E_u)(1-E_v)^2 F(0), where E denotes translation.
It is a new construction in this investigation, not an asserted new
identity in the literature. The coefficient of G vanishes because the
third finite difference annihilates quadratics. The constant coefficient
of B is 6. Hence

 J_k(t)=6B+r_k(t),   r_k(t) in Q[[t]],

with r_k even. For k>=3,

 r_k(t)=-4 t^(2k+4)+O(t^(2k+6)).                               (4.2)

To see the leading term, the rational part of (4.1) is

 r_k=2[Delta_v^2 h(u)-Delta_v^2 h(0)]
    =2 sum_(j>=2) ((2^j-2)/j!) v^j [h^(j)(u)-h^(j)(0)].         (4.3)

The first term is 2v^2 h''(u)=-u^4 v^2/16+... . Since u=2t+...
and v=2t^k+..., this gives (4.2). For k=1 the leading term is -72t^6.

Applying (2.3) to (4.1), all polynomial terms cancel and

 J_k=2T(u)+4T(v)-4T(u+v)-2T(2v)+2T(u+2v).

This is the sum of
2[T(u)-T(u+v)], 2[T(v)-T(u+v)], 2[T(v)-T(2v)], 2T(u+2v).
Every summand is positive for 0<t<1. All tend to zero as t->1, proving

                         J_k(t)>0,   J_k(1-)=0.              (4.4)

Here is an arithmetic benefit beyond delayed contact. Let L_N=lcm(1,...,N)
and e_p(N)=floor(log N/log p). Then all coefficients through degree N of
r_k have denominators dividing

 D_(N,k)=8 L_N^2 product_(p prime, p<=N/k) p^(2 e_p(N)).         (4.5)

Proof of the general four-layer bound. For x=a u+b v, with the nonnegative
integer a,b occurring in (4.1), write exp(x)=A(t)/A(-t), where
A(t)=(1+t)^a(1+t^k)^b. The series x' has integer coefficients and

 sech(x)=A(t)A(-t) / ((A(t)^2+A(-t)^2)/2)

also has integer coefficients, since its denominator has integer
coefficients and constant term one. Put Omega=sech(x)x' dt and
I_j=integral_0^t x(s)^j Omega(s). Formula (2.4) becomes

 h(x)=(3x I_2-x^2 I_1-2I_3)/8.

Through degree N, x has denominator dividing L_N; I_j has denominator
dividing L_N^(j+1). Consequently 8 L_N^4 clears h(x), hence r_k.

Proof of the sharper large-prime bound. Fix an odd p>N/k. In (4.3), only
j<=N/k can contribute through degree N, so j! is p-integral. The
coefficients of v through degree N are also p-integral: their possible
denominators are odd integers at most N/k. For j=2, formula (2.5) shows
that h''(u(t)) is a two-iterated-integral expression, plus u(t)^2 times
a p-integral rational series. Its coefficients have valuation at least
-2 e_p(N). More explicitly u'=2/(1-t^2) and sech(u)u'=2/(1+t^2), both
integer series. For j>=3, differentiating h'''(x)=x^2 sech'(x)/8 shows
that h^(j)(u(t)) is a polynomial of degree at most two in u(t), with
coefficients in Z[1/2][[t]]. It has the same valuation bound. The
constants h^(j)(0) have no odd denominators. Thus (4.3) proves the
two-layer bound for every odd p>N/k. At smaller primes use the general
four-layer bound. If any coefficient in question is nonzero then
N>=2k+4 (or N>=6 for k=1), so 2<=N/k and the four-layer allowance already
covers p=2. This proves (4.5).

Using the prime number theorem, for each fixed k,

 log D_(N,k)=(2+2/k)N+o(N).                                   (4.6)

Indeed log L_N=N+o(N), and sum_(p<=N/k)e_p(N)log p=N/k+o(N);
the contributions from prime powers of exponent at least two are o(N).
The exact finite formula (4.5) does not require the prime number theorem.

What this does NOT show. The saving is per Taylor degree, before solving
a Pade system or clearing a determinant's coefficients. Reindexing a
series can itself change such rates, so (4.6) must not be advertised as a
halving of the cost of an irrationality proof. This construction is not
just r(t^k): after its first term it has the fast u-variable as well.
Nevertheless its analytic behavior changes with k. For every fixed
0<=t<1, J_k(t)->6B as k->infinity, while J_k(1-)=0 for every k. A direct
calculation at t=1-c/k, c>0 fixed, gives the limiting boundary profile

 4T(2 atanh(exp(-c)))-2T(4 atanh(exp(-c))).

Thus the arithmetic saving has an analytic tradeoff that a proof must
quantify. All 62 tested [n/n] Pade forms (odd k through 45, n through 48)
remain above one after exact primitive normalization. Their uniform
nonvanishing has not been proved.

5. Frobenius explains what is postponed, not abolished

The logarithmic-word argument proved in radial-cancellation.txt applies
to these germs. For p>=5 and degrees <p^2,

 p^4 r_k(t) = chi_4(p) r_k(t^p) mod p.                         (5.1)

For the rational-dilation filters in section 3, the same statement holds
when p does not divide m, with r replaced by that filter's rational part.
In each case the right side uses only original coefficients of degree <p.

For completeness, formula (2.4) is a sum of length-four logarithmic
iterated integrals. There are three letters dx and one letter

 sech(x) dx = -i d log((1+i exp(x))/(1-i exp(x))).

The logarithmic derivative Cartier identity from the preceding note
applies to these p-integral units. The dx letters are fixed by Frobenius;
the last letter acquires chi_4(p). In the rational-dilation case the
power-series units remain p-integral for p not dividing 2m. The leading
four-fold denominator term therefore gives (5.1). Equivalently one can
apply the earlier word proof to the formula for h in terms of I_j.

In particular, when k>=3 and p>2k+4,

 v_p([t^((2k+4)p)] r_k)=-4,
 p^4 [t^((2k+4)p)] r_k = -4 chi_4(p) mod p.

The fourth layer survives at higher degrees. This is consistent with
(4.5): at N=(2k+4)p, that prime is among the smaller primes p<=N/k.

6. A positive kernel with only beta(4) in its integer-node entries

The divided-difference direction was prompted by the rectangular digamma
Loewner frames in S. Litsey's working repository:
https://github.com/slitsey/catalan-irrationality-measure
The introduction and selected frame definitions were consulted, not the
whole manuscript or its Lean development. An attributed TeX snapshot and
its hash are recorded in literature/dilation-source.json. The following
cubic-tail transform and its proofs were developed here; no claim that
Litsey's arithmetic estimates transfer to it is made.

Define, for Re z>=0,

 f(z)=sum_(j>=0)[(4j+1+4z)^(-3)-(4j+3+4z)^(-3)]
     =(1/4) integral_0^infinity u^2 sech(u) exp(-4zu) du.

Then f(0)=C3 and f'(0)=-12B. Let

 S_l(a)=sum_(j=0)^(a-1)[(4j+1)^(-l)-(4j+3)^(-l)].

For a nonnegative integer, f(a)=C3-S_3(a), f'(a)=-12[B-S_4(a)].
The ordinary normalized Loewner kernel -f[a,b]/12 therefore has rational
off-diagonal entries and diagonal B-S_4(a), with no Catalan elimination.
It is not a positive Gram kernel. Its 48 finite tests gave 46 values above
one, plus X and 81X-80; no uniform nonvanishing theorem was obtained.

To obtain positivity instead put, for x>=0,

 g(x)=sqrt(x)[f(0)-f(sqrt(x))]/6,   g(0)=0,
 K(x,y)=(g(y)-g(x))/(y-x),   K(x,x)=g'(x).                      (6.1)

The elementary cosine integral

 sqrt(x)(1-exp(-a sqrt(x)))
      =(2/pi) integral_0^infinity x(1-cos(as))/(x+s^2) ds

and the positive Laplace representation of f give, by Tonelli,

 g(x)=(1/(3pi)) integral_0^infinity x[f(0)-Re f(is)]/(x+s^2) ds.

Differentiating the Fourier transform of sech twice gives, with
A(s)=sech(2pi s),

 Re f(is)=C3 A(s)(2A(s)^2-1),
 f(0)-Re f(is)=C3 (1-A(s))(2A(s)^2+2A(s)+1)>0   (s>0).

Consequently

 K(x,y)=integral_0^infinity omega(s)/((x+s^2)(y+s^2)) ds,
 omega(s)=pi^2 s^2/96 [1+A(s)-2A(s)^3]>0.                      (6.2)

Near zero omega(s)=O(s^4), and at infinity omega(s)=O(s^2), so all
entries with x,y>=0 are finite, including K(0,0)=2B.

At integer squared nodes there are no periods other than B:

 K(a^2,b^2)=[b S_3(b)-a S_3(a)]/[6(b^2-a^2)]       (a!=b),
 K(a^2,a^2)=B-S_4(a)+S_3(a)/(12a)                 (a>0),
 K(0,0)=2B.                                                    (6.3)

Uniform nonvanishing. For any increasing distinct nonnegative x_i and
y_j, with the same number n of each, the integral determinant identity
gives

 det K(x_i,y_j)=(1/n!) integral product_k omega(s_k)
     det[1/(x_i+s_k^2)] det[1/(y_j+s_k^2)] product_k ds_k.

On ordered distinct positive s_k, both Cauchy determinants have the same
strict sign (indeed the standard positive sign with these orderings).
Their product is positive on a set of positive measure. All terms in
the expanded integral are absolutely integrable, being products of
finite entries from (6.2). Thus every such determinant is strictly
positive. This proof does not assume B irrational and includes
rectangular row/column configurations and nodes equal to zero.

For integer node sets with r common nodes, the determinant is a polynomial
in B of degree exactly r. The top coefficient is, up to nonzero factors
1 or 2 and a permutation sign, the determinant left after deleting the
common rows and columns. That remaining matrix has disjoint node sets,
so it is rational and its determinant is strictly positive by the same
integral proof. With no common nodes the polynomial is a positive
rational constant, whose primitive normalization is 1.

7. Explicit real bounds, including the node zero

Let M=1+2/(3 sqrt(6)). For 0<=A<=1,

 0<=1+A-2A^3<=M.

Comparing the density in the preceding determinant integral with
(pi^2 M/96)s^2 gives, for positive integer node sets a_i,b_j,

 0<det K(a_i^2,b_j^2)<=C^n V(a)V(b)/product_(i,j)(a_i+b_j),
 C=pi^3 M/192,   V(a)=product_(i<j)(a_j-a_i).                   (7.1)

Here integral_0^infinity s^2/((s^2+a^2)(s^2+b^2)) ds
=pi/(2(a+b)), and the remaining determinant is the usual Cauchy
determinant. Density comparison is legitimate for these nonsymmetric
minors because the product of the two Cauchy determinants is positive.

For a common node zero, regularize the comparison. Since
1-sech(v)<=v^2/2, the same density factor is at most 10pi^2 s^2.
For any tau>0 this implies

 omega(s)<=(pi^2/96)(M+10pi^2 tau^2) s^4/(s^2+tau^2).

The comparison kernel integrates explicitly to a constant times

 [ab+tau(a+b)]/[(a+b)(a+tau)(b+tau)],

with value 1/tau at a=b=0 by continuity. If a_0=b_0=0 and all other
nodes are positive, its determinant, with the common constant removed,
is

 (1/tau) product_(i>0) a_i/(a_i+tau)
         product_(j>0) b_j/(b_j+tau)
         V(a_+)V(b_+)/product_(i,j>0)(a_i+b_j).                 (7.2)

This follows by multiplying rows and columns by a_i+tau,b_j+tau:
the matrix becomes tau+ab/(a+b); subtracting the zero row leaves a
Cauchy determinant on the positive nodes. The full upper bound is
(7.2) times C_tau^n, where C_tau=pi^3(M+10pi^2 tau^2)/192.
The checks use the rational choice tau=1/8.

These give a precise sufficient arithmetic target. Write P_n(X)=H_n
det K_n(X) in primitive integer coefficients, with H_n>0, degree r_n>=1.
Let U_n denote either explicit determinant upper bound. A node family
satisfying

                   log(H_n U_n)/r_n -> -infinity              (7.3)

would prove irrationality: if B=a/b, positivity and integrality force
P_n(B)>=b^(-r_n). No family meeting (7.3) has been found.

8. What the local arithmetic of the positive kernel costs

Let all integer nodes lie in [0,M0] and let p>M0, p>=5. Set

 D_l(a)=sum_(ell in {1,3}, ell*p<4a) chi_4(ell)/ell^l.

For the rational coefficient matrix R in (6.3), direct reduction of the
prefix sums gives

 p^4 R_(a,b) = -chi_4(p) D_4(a)                    if a=b,
              chi_4(p)[D_3(a)+D_3(b)]/12          if a+b=p,
              0                                  otherwise, mod p.  (8.1)

For the off-diagonal exceptional case use b=-a mod p in (6.3).
No other denominator can introduce a fourth layer in this prime range.

There is also a simple full-rank cubic block. Take disjoint row nodes
a_i and column nodes b_j all in (p/4,p/2). Then

 p^3 R_(a_i,b_j) = chi_4(p)/(6(a_i+b_j)) mod p.                  (8.2)

The Cauchy determinant is nonzero modulo p: all node differences and
all sums in its formula are nonzero. An n-by-n block therefore has
determinant valuation exactly -3n. If it is the auxiliary block left
after deleting common nodes, the leading coefficient of the B-polynomial
has this valuation, so its integer multiplier H must have v_p(H)>=3n.

This matters because the ordinary Loewner kernel of f has zero leading
cubic residue when both prefix sums are in this same constant region.
The positive transform replaces that cancellation with a full Cauchy
block. It is therefore not safe to import favorable rank counts from
the digamma or ordinary cubic-tail frames. Equations (8.1)-(8.2) are
local statements, not a no-go theorem for every positive-kernel design.

9. Computations, provenance, and reproduction

All determinant polynomials were cleared to primitive integer
coefficients, including their full coefficient gcd. The recorded size
tests are Arb interval evaluations, not floating-point sign guesses.
Quadratures below are high-precision diagnostics, separately identified.

Ordinary logarithmic dilations:
  112 Pade tests: 108 nondegenerate values above one, four singular.
  325 coefficients matched a separate Euler-number expansion;
  37 matched the earlier bivariate ladder implementation;
  9,114 coefficients checked the logarithmic Frobenius relation.

Slow-variable filters:
  62 nondegenerate primitive Pade values, all above one;
  195 coefficients matched a separate Euler-number expansion;
  1,136 nonzero coefficients checked against (4.5);
  42,859 coefficient-prime checks of the two-layer bound;
  13,631 Frobenius coefficient checks, plus surviving fourth-layer cases;
  numerical checks of the positive-tail identity at twelve (k,t) pairs.

Divided-difference families:
  48 ordinary cubic-tail frames: 46 above one, plus X and 81X-80;
  48 positive-kernel frames: 47 above one, plus X;
  121 entries reproduced with a separate Python Fraction implementation;
  seven Cauchy-integral quadratures and four Fourier identities checked
    numerically to better than 10^(-58);
  25,915 entry-prime checks of (8.1);
  27 exact full-rank cubic blocks checking (8.2);
  all 48 positive-frame values certified positive and below (7.1)/(7.2).

For example, the primitive degree-eight determinant on common nodes
0,...,7 has log value about 388.8323. The explicit regularized Cauchy
upper bound has log about 396.4051. The upper bound is therefore already
reasonably close in that case; a hidden enormous improvement in the
real estimate is not indicated by this example.
