Direct Catalan cancellation and a uniform fourth-denominator congruence
28 October 2026. Local research record; unpublished.

Status
======
There is still no irrationality proof for B=beta(4).

This pass replaces the previous matrix projection by a direct identity
between harmonic and radial moments. The source coefficients have only
a fixed factor 1/2: Catalan cancellation itself introduces no large
odd-prime denominator. Nevertheless, fourth-power denominators survive.
We now prove a uniform formula for that layer, rather than just exhibit
individual primes in a finite matrix.

The resulting rational sequence s_n satisfies, for every prime p>=5,

                   p^4 s_(2p) = -chi_4(p)/6  (mod p).     (A)

Thus v_p(s_(2p))=-4 exactly. More generally, p^4 s_(mp)=chi_4(p)s_m
modulo p when 1<=2m<p. The formula comes from a Frobenius congruence
for the entire rational Taylor germ of the ladder integral.

We also prove that a fixed polynomial in the Euler derivative cannot
remove the fourth layer of the harmonic rational coefficients while
retaining their constant B coefficient. This is a restricted obstruction,
not a theorem excluding arbitrary differential or numerator constructions.

The direct Hankel and filtered variants have unfavorable finite results:
179 of 192 primitive evaluations exceed one in absolute value, and the
remaining 13 are the trivial polynomial -X. These evaluations include
overlapping cases. No asymptotic impossibility theorem for their
determinants, nor a uniform determinant nonvanishing theorem, is claimed.

The proofs and checks are same-agent work. There has been no independent
audit or formal verification, and no priority claim is made.

1. Input and notation
======================
The source is the classical two-loop ladder evaluation, in the
normalization of Loebbert and Stawinski, arXiv:2408.15331v1, equation
(2.73). The cube substitution, positivity, moment normalization and
source attribution are proved in two-loop-numerators.txt. This pass
does not add a new literature survey or another source PDF.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15331v1
  literature/papers/2408.15331v1.pdf

Recall the positive measure nu of mass 6B, supported inside
W>=0, |Z|+W<=1. Write mu(P)=integral P dnu, and set

 Q=1-Z^2-W^2, T=Q/4, C_n=Re(Z+iW)^(2n).

For polynomials even in Z and W, the specified ladder evaluation gives

 mu(P)=r(P)+a(P)B+g(P)G,  G=beta(2),                    (1)

with rational coefficient functionals. This is a chosen exact
decomposition, not an assumption that the periods are linearly independent.

The previous note proves

 a(P)=6 times the normalized unit-circle average of P,
 g(TP)=(1/pi) integral_(xi^2+eta^2<=1) integral_0^1
                                     P(xi,t eta) dt dxi deta.   (2)

Denote the second functional by h(P). The analytic Cauchy germ is

 C(z,w)=mu(1/(1-zZ-wW))
       =sum_ij binom(i+j,i)mu(Z^i W^j)z^i w^j.

Let Rcal(z,w) be its rational coefficient and Gcal(z,w) its G coefficient.
The binomial factor in this definition matters for denominator arguments.
With K=(1-z^2-w^2)^(-1/2),

 Gcal=-K[atanh(z)^2+3asin(w/sqrt(1-z^2))^2],              (3)

and the first nonzero homogeneous term of Rcal is

                  Rcal_4=(z^2+w^2)^2/8.                 (4)

The full germ also contains B, zeta(3), and log(2). On even Z,W moments
the last two coefficients vanish. Its rational part is explicitly
computed from the known polylogarithmic ladder formula.

2. Cancellation without a matrix inverse
=========================================
Put H_n=sum_(j=0)^(n-1)1/(2j+1). For n>=1,

             g(C_n)=2H_n/n,  g(Q^n)=4H_n/n.              (5)

For the first formula, restrict (3) to (z,w)=(t,it). Then K=1 and
Gcal(t,it)=2atanh(t)^2. Its coefficient of t^(2n) is 2H_n/n.

For the second formula, (2) gives g(Q^n)=4h(Q^(n-1)). To evaluate h,
write xi=x and eta=sqrt(1-x^2)y in the disk integral. If
b_n=binom(2n,n)/4^n, the x integral is pi*b_n, while

 integral_0^1 integral_0^1 (1-t^2 y^2)^(n-1) dt dy
     =integral_0^1 (-log u)(1-u^2)^(n-1) du
     =H_n/(2n b_n).

The last equality follows by differentiating the beta integral at its
first parameter 1/2, or by integrating its finite binomial expansion.
The factor two from y in [-1,1] proves h(Q^(n-1))=H_n/n.

Define

                 s_n=mu(C_n-Q^n/2).                     (6)

At n=0 this is 3B. At every positive n its B coefficient vanishes by
the circle formula in (2), and its G coefficient vanishes by (5).
Therefore s_n is rational for n>=1. In particular,

 s_0=3B,
 s_1=0, s_2=-1/6, s_3=-4/15, s_4=-9/35,
 s_5=-3793/14175, s_6=-117176/467775.

This cancellation does not multiply a rational Catalan coefficient by
another rational approximation to Catalan. It is an exact identity of
source polynomials with a fixed denominator two. The integrand in (6)
is signed, so the positive scalar nonvanishing proof from the previous
pass does not apply to it or to its Hankel determinants.

3. The uniform Taylor-germ congruence
====================================
For a prime p>=5 put chi=chi_4(p)=(-1)^((p-1)/2) and

                  A_p(z,w)=(1-z^2-w^2)^((p-1)/2).

Proposition. Coefficientwise through total degree strictly below p^2,

 p^4 Rcal(z,w) = chi A_p(z,w) Rcal(z^p,w^p)  (mod p),    (7)
 p^2 Gcal(z,w) =     A_p(z,w) Gcal(z^p,w^p)  (mod p).    (8)

The left sides have p-integral coefficients in the stated range.
On the right, only original coefficients of degree below p occur;
these are p-integral. Thus the displayed reductions are well-defined.

Proof of the logarithmic-word mechanism.
First let f(t) be a formal power series with p-integral coefficients
and invertible constant coefficient. If
f'(t)/f(t)=sum_(j>=0)b_j t^j, then

                      b_(mp-1)=b_(m-1)^p  (mod p).       (9)

One elementary proof factors f/f(0) formally as product_(n>=1)(1-a_n t^n).
The coefficient of t^(m-1) in its logarithmic derivative is
-sum_(n|m)n a_n^(m/n). For mp, terms with p dividing n vanish modulo p;
the remaining terms are precisely the pth powers of the preceding sum.
This works over a characteristic-p coefficient ring, including Gaussian
coefficients and polynomial parameters. Formal factorization is obtained
recursively by matching one coefficient at a time.

Consider an r-fold iterated integral of logarithmic differentials, all
integrals based at t=0. A coefficient of degree N is a sum over strictly
increasing positive integration indices k_1<...<k_r=N, with denominator
k_1...k_r and p-integral differential coefficients. If N<p^2, each
index contributes at most one factor p. Multiplying by p^r and reducing
modulo p leaves only terms in which every index is a multiple of p.
Dividing those indices by p and applying (9) gives the Frobenius copy
of the original iterated word, now in t^p. The reduced indices are
less than p, so their remaining denominators are units.

Products of words can be treated by the shuffle identity, which has
integer coefficients. Multiplication by an algebraic p-integral
prefactor is then handled separately.

Application to the ladder germ.
Insert z=alpha*t,w=beta*t and write

 zeta_+(t)=[beta*t+i sqrt(1-(alpha^2+beta^2)t^2)]/(1+alpha*t),
 zeta_-(t)=[beta*t-i sqrt(1-(alpha^2+beta^2)t^2)]/(1+alpha*t),
 u(t)=(1-alpha*t)/(1+alpha*t).

These are the two polylogarithm arguments of the ladder formula. Define
omega0=dlog(zeta_+), omega1=-dlog(1-zeta_+), and their conjugates.
Their coefficients are p-integral for odd p: all initial denominators
are powers of two or units 1+i and 1-i, and the square-root series has
only powers of two in its denominators.

Let I_k^+ be the word with k-1 copies of omega0 followed by omega1,
and let I_k^- be its conjugate. Subtracting the initial polylogarithm
values leaves these words as the rational remainders of Li_k.
Consequently

 Rcal(alpha*t,beta*t)=K(alpha*t,beta*t)/(2i) times
   [6(I_4^+-I_4^-)-3log(u)(I_3^+-I_3^-)
                         +(log(u)^2/2)(I_2^+-I_2^-)].   (10)

The bracket is a sum of words of length four. The other terms of the
ladder formula have the initial constants B,G,zeta(3),log(2), or pi
powers; the pi contributions cancel as in the previous note. This
separation is also checked by an independent implementation of (10).

Apply the word mechanism. Frobenius sends i to i^p=chi*i, so the
outside factor 1/(2i) contributes chi relative to the Frobenius copy
of the full rational germ. The remaining prefactor ratio is

 K(z,w)/K(z^p,w^p)=(1-z^2-w^2)^((p-1)/2)  (mod p).

This proves (7). Formula (3) is a rational combination of products of
two logarithms, with the squared 1/i factor rational. The same argument
therefore gives (8), without chi. Using indeterminates alpha,beta proves
the bivariate coefficient statements, not just a statement along one line.

Formula (4) can be checked by expanding (10) to degree four. Equivalently,
put rho=-atanh(z) and delta=-asin(w/sqrt(1-z^2)). The fourth Taylor
combination in these log coordinates is (rho^2+delta^2)^2/4, and
Im Li_0(i)=1/2.

4. Passing from Taylor coefficients to rational moments
========================================================
Because C includes binomially weighted moments, (7) must not simply be
read as an entrywise moment congruence. The following argument accounts
for that normalization.

Proposition. If p>=5 and 1<=2m<p, then

                    p^4 s_(mp)=chi_4(p)s_m  (mod p).     (11)

For the harmonic term, substitute (z,w)=(t,it) in (7) and take real
parts. The multiplier A_p becomes 1. Changing i^p from i to -i leaves
the real even coefficients unchanged. Hence the rational harmonic
coefficients h_n=r(C_n) satisfy

                    p^4 h_(mp)=chi h_m  (mod p).         (12)

For the radial term expand Q^(mp). Its rational moment is a sum of
Taylor coefficients Rcal_(2j,2k-2j) with multipliers

 W_(k,j)=(-1)^k binom(mp,k) binom(k,j)/binom(2k,2j),
                       0<=j<=k<=mp.                    (13)

These multipliers are p-integral. Indeed,
binom(k,j)/binom(2k,2j) is the normalized circle moment
of cos^(2j)theta*sin^(2k-2j)theta divided by
b_k=binom(2k,k)/4^k. The circle moment is dyadic. Since 2mp<p^2,
v_p(b_k) is at most one, and is zero when k is a multiple of p.
When k is not a multiple of p, v_p(binom(mp,k))=1, covering that
possible denominator. This also proves integrality when a circle
moment happens to have additional p divisibility.

In the product on the right side of (7), a contribution to an even
radial coefficient has

 k=l*p+d, j=a*p+d_1, d=d_1+d_2, 0<=d<=(p-1)/2.

Here the original germ coefficient has bidegree (2a,2l-2a), while
the multiplier A_p supplies bidegree (2d_1,2d_2). If d>0, b_k is
a p-adic unit, whereas binom(mp,k) is divisible by p, so (13) kills
the contribution modulo p. Only d=0 survives. Lucas reduction of
the remaining binomial factors gives exactly the multipliers for Q^m.
The bound 2m<p ensures all binomial factors at those reduced indices
are units. The sign (-1)^(lp)=(-1)^l is also preserved.

Thus p^4 r(Q^(mp))=chi r(Q^m) modulo p. Combine this with (12) and
(6) to prove (11).

Taking m=2 and s_2=-1/6 proves (A). The residue is nonzero for p>=5,
so the denominator exponent is exactly four. In particular, if
L_N=lcm(1,...,N), then L_(2n)^3 s_n is not integral at n=2p for
every prime p>=5: p occurs only once in L_(4p), and its third power
does not clear s_(2p). This is an infinite family of failures of that
specific denominator bound, not an obstruction to determinant content
or to other combinations of moments.

5. A restricted obstruction to fixed derivative filters
========================================================
The harmonic rational coefficients begin h_1=h_2=0, h_3=-1/15.
By (12), for every prime p>=7,

                    p^4 h_(3p)=-chi_4(p)/15  (mod p).    (14)

Let A be a fixed integer polynomial and multiply the nth harmonic
coefficient by A(n). This is the effect of A(theta), theta=x*d/dx,
on the generating series indexed by n. If A(0)!=0, the constant B
coefficient is retained. But for all sufficiently large primes,

 p^4 A(3p)h_(3p)=-A(0)chi_4(p)/15  (mod p)

is nonzero. Hence this fixed Euler filter cannot lower the uniform
fourth denominator layer. The same conclusion holds for the already
cancelled sequence: (A) gives p^4 A(2p)s_(2p)=-A(0)chi/6 modulo p.
This statement does not cover p-dependent
filters, general shifted recurrences, rational-function substitutions,
or arbitrary families whose order grows with their index.

6. Finite determinant and filtering experiments
================================================
The simplest determinant uses the moments (6):

                  det(s_(i+j))_(0<=i,j<n).

Only its upper-left entry contains B, so its polynomial degree in B
is at most one. We also tested the weight (1-x)^h, which replaces
s_k by sum_j(-1)^j binom(h,j)s_(k+j), preserving the B coefficient
in the zeroth moment. These give 24 configurations through n=24.

Further tests replace s_k by w_k*s_(d*k), with stride d=1,2,3.
The weights were Euler beta moments

 w_k=[(ak)!(bk)!/((a+b)k+1)!]^r,

and multinomial weights [(ak)!/(k!)^a]^r. The former correspond to
adding positive beta-integral variables; the latter test factorial
divisibility directly. Twelve weight choices and the available matrix
sizes give 168 further configurations.

Every nontrivial primitive evaluation is larger than one. The 13
exceptions are -X, arising at size two from s_1=0. The raw unfiltered
rational approximation also remains poor: at n=24 its value is about
0.5990753, compared with B about 0.9889446. Thus this direct cancellation
has not retained the strong approximation behavior of the positive
scalar construction in the preceding note. No convergence rate for
these Hankel approximants is asserted.

The uniform moment congruence does not by itself explain every determinant
failure: taking primitive coefficient content can cancel large common
denominators. Conversely, determinants can introduce large new factors
into their coefficient ratios. The finite determinant results and the
uniform moment theorem are separate conclusions.

A modular search of 65 low-order polynomial-coefficient recurrence
templates found no recurrence for the sampled rational sequence. This
is not a non-holonomicity result and does not exclude larger recurrences.

7. Checks and reproducibility
=============================
Before any previous record was edited, all 431 hashes from the previous
manifest matched. See results/radial_integrity_baseline.json.

The exact checks cover 70 harmonic/radial Catalan cancellations, 88,780
Taylor coefficients in (7)-(8), nine instances of (A), 29 instances
of (11), and five instances of (14). A separate iterated-integral
implementation verifies 488 rational/G coefficients through degree 60
in four directions, without using the earlier negative-polylogarithm
and Eulerian-number computation. This is computational independence of
an implementation, not an independent human or model audit.

All primitive determinant coefficients are computed exactly, reduced
by their integer gcd, and evaluated with Arb at coefficient-dependent
precision. The 192 recorded values exclude zero. No uniform determinant
nonvanishing argument follows from these finite checks.

8. What has changed in the research target
==========================================
We have removed one avoidable source of arithmetic loss: Catalan can
be cancelled without multiplying two second-order denominator layers.
But the resulting sequence still has its own fourth layer, with the
explicit Frobenius multiplier A_p in (7). That layer is not merely an
artifact of the old matrix inverse.

The useful target is now a family of source rows whose leading residues
under (7) have controlled rank, while retaining a usable real integral
and nonvanishing argument. The congruence specifies what has to be
cancelled. No family satisfying all three requirements has been found.
The fixed Euler filters in section 5 cannot do it; the direct Hankel
and beta-filter prototypes in section 6 do not show favorable finite
evidence. Neither statement excludes new variable-dependent rows or
a different representation of beta(4).

This pass strengthens the arithmetic diagnosis and gives a direct
period-cancellation construction. It does not establish that an
irrationality proof is close.
