The deformed Catalan coefficient and a smaller denominator budget
28 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

This note continues coupled-holonomy.txt and block-arithmetic.txt. It proves
an exact terminating sum for the full deformed Catalan coefficient. The
sum improves its denominator bound at every Taylor order. A second pole
representation also improves the proved denominator bound for the rational
term of the beta(4) form. These are arithmetic results for our existing
family, not an irrationality proof. All checks are by the same agent;
neither independent verification nor priority is claimed.

The main tools are classical hypergeometric transformations. In particular,
the undeformed terminating sum is Krattenthaler--Rivoal [KR08, Lemma 3],
up to our normalization. The transfer between pole representations is a
direct specialization of Krattenthaler--Zudilin [KZ18, Theorem 2]. We give
the parameter substitutions and normalization rather than crediting the
resulting arithmetic improvement to an unexplained computational identity.

1. Conventions and the result

Throughout this note n is the ORIGINAL EVEN source index, not the block
index m=n/2. Put G=beta(2), L_N=lcm(1,...,N), and L_0=1. Our source forms
are

  r_n=a_n G+b_n,
  s_n=16a_n beta(4)+c_n G+d_n.

They are the coefficients of 1 and e^2 in

  E_n(e) = (2n+1)!/2^(2n+2) * sum_(t>=0)
      (2t+n+1) product_(ell=-3n/2)^(n/2-1)(t-ell)
      / product_(j=0)^(2n+1)((t+(2j+1)/4)^2-e^2).          (1)

The formal partial-fraction decomposition, established in coupled-holonomy,
is E_n(e)=a_n(e)T(e)/16+b_n(e), where a_n(e),b_n(e) are rational functions
regular and even near zero, and

  T(e)=sum_(t>=0)[1/((t+1/4)^2-e^2)-1/((t+3/4)^2-e^2)]
      =16G+256 beta(4)e^2+4096 beta(6)e^4+...,
  a_n(e)=a_n+c_n e^2+...,
  b_n(e)=b_n+d_n e^2+....

No linear-independence assumption about these periods is used to identify
the coefficients: they are defined by partial fractions and finite tails.

For 0<=j<=n set

  U_(n,j)=binom(n,j) binom(n-1/2,j) binom(n+j-1/2,j).

The exact parameter identity proved below is

  a_n(e)=8 sum_(j=0)^n U_(n,j)
                     product_(k=1)^j (1-4e^2/k^2)^(-1)
        =8 3F2(-n,n+1/2,1/2-n;1-2e,1+2e;1).             (2)

In particular, writing H_j^(2)=sum_(k=1)^j 1/k^2,

  a_n=8 sum_j U_(n,j),
  c_n=32 sum_j U_(n,j) H_j^(2),
  16^n L_n^2 c_n is an integer.                           (3)

More generally, for EVERY k>=0,

  16^n L_n^(2k) [e^(2k)]a_n(e) is an integer.              (4)

The improved rational-term bound is

  16^n D_n d_n is an integer,
  D_n=gcd(L_(2n)^5,L_(4n)^4).                             (5)

It obeys

  L_(2n)^4 | D_n | L_(2n)^4 L_floor(sqrt(4n))^4.           (6)

The sharper exact conjecture 16^n L_(2n)^4 d_n in Z remains
UNPROVED here. It holds at all 359 positive even indices in the existing
table, through n=718. In contrast, (3) is now proved, not just observed.

2. Transferring the full parameter, not just its value at zero

Define the rational function

 F_n(t,e)=n!(2t+n+1)
   product_(ell=0)^(n-1)(t-ell) product_(ell=n+1)^(2n)(t+ell)
   / product_(j=0)^n [(t+j+1/2)((t+j+1/2)^2-4e^2)].

Then, uniformly on a sufficiently small closed disk about e=0,

                   E_n(e)=sum_(t>=0)(-1)^t F_n(t,e).     (7)

Here is a normalization check for applying [KZ18, Theorem 2]. Put z=2e
and substitute c=n+1/2+z, d=n+1/2-z in that theorem. In the alternating
sum the first nonzero summand is t=n; in (1) it is t=n/2. Their initial
factors are respectively

 H_n=(n!)^2(3n+1)(2n+1)_n
     /[(n+1/2)_(n+1)(n+1/2+z)_(n+1)(n+1/2-z)_(n+1)],

 Q_n=2^(2n+2)((2n+1)!)^2
     /[(n+1/2+z)_(2n+2)(n+1/2-z)_(2n+2)].

The remaining series are exactly the 6F5(-1) and 6F5(1) in that theorem.
Its gamma prefactor after this substitution is

 Gamma(4n+3) Gamma(2n+3/2-z) Gamma(2n+3/2+z) Gamma(2n+2)
 ----------------------------------------------------------------.
 Gamma(3n+2) Gamma(3n+5/2-z) Gamma(3n+5/2+z) Gamma(n+1)

Multiplying it by H_n gives Q_n, by the gamma duplication formula.
There is no residual parameter-dependent factor. The sign (-1)^n is 1
because our source indices are even. Absolute locally uniform convergence
justifies extracting Taylor coefficients in (7).

3. A terminating transformation for the Catalan coefficient

For generic nonzero z near zero, let D_j(z) be the residue of F_n(t,z/2)
at t=-j-1/2+z, for 0<=j<=n. Put B(u)=sum_(t>=0)(-1)^t/(t+u).
Shifting the alternating sums and using the reflection t -> -n-1-t
shows that their non-rational part is

  C(z)[B(1/2-z)-B(1/2+z)],   C(z)=sum_j (-1)^j D_j(z).

The fixed half-integer poles give zero total coefficient of B(1/2).
Moreover B(1/2-z)-B(1/2+z)=(z/2)T(z/2). Thus the formal coefficient is

                         a_n(z/2)=8z C(z).               (8)

One can identify this with the coefficient from (1) without presuming
independence of any numerical periods. The corresponding rational
coefficients multiply the same meromorphic T(e). If their difference
were nonzero, (7) would express T(e) as a rational function. But T(e)
has simple poles at every positive odd quarter-integer, with nonzero
residues. It is not rational. The coefficient functions therefore agree.
Alternatively the quarter-residue sum can be transformed directly.

Here are the finite transformations in full. Write (x)_n for a rising
factorial and set

 t0=(n+2z)(1/2-z)_n(n+1/2+z)_n /[(z)_(n+1)(2z)_(n+1)],
 a=-n-2z, b=n+1/2-z, x=-n-z, y=1/2-n-z.

The ratio of consecutive residues gives

 C(z)=(-1)^n t0 H,
 H=6F5(a,1+a/2,b,x,y,-n;
       a/2,1+a-b,1+a-x,1+a-y,1+a+n;-1).

Use the terminating Whipple transformation recorded as (3.2) of [KR08]:

 H=W 3F2(-n,-n-z,1/2-n-z;1/2-2n,1/2-2n-z;1),
 W=(1-n-2z)_n(n+1/2)_n /[(1-z)_n(1/2-z)_n].

Next use the terminating identity

 3F2(-n,A,B;C,D;1)
   =(C-A)_n/(C)_n *
      3F2(-n,A,D-B;A-C-n+1,D;1).

This identity follows by expanding a terminating 2F1 transformation and
interchanging finite sums (or directly by Chu--Vandermonde). For our
series take A=-n-z, B=1/2-n-z, C=1/2-2n-z, D=1/2-2n. The new factor is

 T0=(1/2-n)_n/(1/2-2n-z)_n,

and the series is 3F2(-n,-n-z,-n+z;1/2,1/2-2n;1).
Reverse its finite sum. This introduces the factor

 R0=(-1)^n(-n-z)_n(-n+z)_n /[(1/2)_n(1/2-2n)_n]

and leaves 3F2(-n,1/2-n,n+1/2;1+z,1-z;1). Finally,

                       (-1)^n z t0 W T0 R0=1.

Together with (8), this proves (2). All manipulations are identities of
rational functions in z. Apparent singularities at z=0 are removed by
continuation, so taking Taylor coefficients there is legitimate.

At e=0 formula (2) specializes to the sum in [KR08, Lemma 3]. Their a_n
is -4 sum U_(n,j); ours is 8 sum U_(n,j). The parameter extension above
is the step used here to control the new Taylor coefficients.

4. Arithmetic and positivity of the coefficient

For any integer v and j>=0, 4^j binom(v+1/2,j) is an integer. At every
odd prime, binom(v+1/2,j) is p-integral by the integer-valued-binomial
property. At 2, its denominator divides 2^j j!, whose 2-valuation is
at most 2j. Hence 16^n U_(n,j) is integral for j<=n.

The coefficient of e^(2k) in the product in (2) is 4^k times the complete
homogeneous polynomial of degree k in 1,1/2^2,...,1/j^2. Multiplication
by L_n^(2k) clears each monomial denominator. This proves (4) and (3).
These are Taylor coefficients; no derivative factorial is being omitted.

Every U_(n,j) is positive. Therefore

  a_n>=8,
  0<c_n/a_n<4 zeta(2)=2pi^2/3<8    (n>=1),               (9)
  c_0=0.

The strict bound <8 follows already from sum_(k>=1)1/k^2<2 and needs
no numerical optimization. In particular the unwanted coefficient has
the same exponential scale as a_n, with a uniformly bounded ratio.
This is useful in the limiting-metric argument in quotient-investigation.

5. Laurent coefficients at half-integer poles

Put R_n(t)=F_n(t,0). Differentiating the product as a Taylor series gives

 [e^2]F_n(t,e)=S_n(t)
       =4R_n(t) sum_(j=0)^n (t+j+1/2)^(-2).               (10)

Let S_(j,s) be its Laurent coefficient of (t+j+1/2)^(-s), s=1,...,5.
For all j,s,

                 16^n L_n^(5-s) S_(j,s) is integral.     (11)

To prove this, factor

 R_n(t)=(2t+n+1) binom(t,n) binom(t+2n,n) Q_n(t)^3,
 Q_n(t)=n!/product_(h=0)^n(t+h+1/2).

(The rational function Q_n(t) here is unrelated to the initial series
factor Q_n of Section 2.) The residue of Q_n(t) at x_j=-j-1/2 is
(-1)^j binom(n,j), an integer. Its partial fractions imply that the
y^k coefficient of y Q_n(x_j+y) is cleared by L_n^k.

The y^k coefficient of either binomial factor at x_j is cleared by
4^n L_n^k. At odd primes use Vandermonde's identity with

 binom(y,r)=y/r product_(i=1)^(r-1)(y/i-1):

an order-k coefficient has a denominator dividing a product of k
integers at most n. The other binomial factor in Vandermonde is
p-integral. At 2 the entire polynomial numerator is integral after
multiplying by 2^n, and n+v_2(n!)<=2n, so 4^n suffices before any L_n.

Thus the regular part y^3 R_n(x_j+y) has its order-k coefficient
cleared by 16^n L_n^k. By (10),

 y^5 S_n(x_j+y)=4 y^3 R_n(x_j+y)
          *[1+y^2 sum_(h!=j)(y+h-j)^(-2)].

The bracket has its order-k coefficient cleared by L_n^k as well.
Taking k=5-s proves (11).

In summing the partial fractions, the alternating finite tail at j is

 T_(j,s)=sum_(t=0)^(j-1) (-1)^t 2^s/(2t+1)^s.

The formal rational coefficient is

                d_n=-sum_(j=0)^n sum_(s=1)^5
                              (-1)^j S_(j,s) T_(j,s).

For n>=1, tail denominators are at most 2n-1 and L_n divides L_(2n-1).
The total Laurent-plus-tail order is five. Consequently

                16^n L_(2n-1)^5 d_n is integral.         (12)

Weakening L_(2n-1) to L_(2n) and intersecting with the proved quarter-pole
bound 16^n L_(4n)^4 d_n in block-arithmetic, Section 6, proves (5).
Intersection is valid: if two integers M,N both clear a rational number,
then gcd(M,N) clears it, by Bezout's identity. At n=0, d_0=0 directly.

6. Size of the saving and its limits

For a prime p let a=v_p(L_(2n)), b=v_p(L_(4n)). Then

 v_p(D_n)=min(5a,4b)>=4a,
 v_p(D_n)-4a=min(a,4(b-a)).

Here b-a is 0 or 1. A positive excess requires a>=1 and p^(a+1)<=4n,
so p<=sqrt(4n); the excess is at most 4. This proves (6), and even the
slightly stronger surplus bound product_(p<=sqrt(4n)) p^4.

Using log L_N=N+o(N) just to express exponential rates gives

       log L_n^2=2n+o(n),        log D_n=8n+o(n).

The preceding proved multipliers for c_n,d_n had respective rates
4n+o(n) and 16n+o(n). Thus both odd-prime exponential costs are halved.
Excluding the prime 2 changes these rates only by O(log n). The common
factor 16^n remains; it has NOT been halved. Equivalently the full
upper-bound rates change from log(16)+4 and log(16)+16 to log(16)+2 and
log(16)+8, respectively. These are denominator bounds, not asymptotic
equalities for the actual reduced denominators.

This does not by itself make the final integer forms small. Multiplying
or combining forms to cancel G still incurs large coefficients, and
the exact finite searches already used the actual reduced denominators.
Their poor arithmetic is therefore not repaired merely by improving a
proof of a clearing bound. Likewise the earlier unfavorable holonomy
budgets allowed optimistic denominator estimates already. A proof of the
remaining exact d_n conjecture would remove a subexponential surplus in
(5), rather than supply another exponential saving.

The useful lesson is more specific: transforming the FULL parameter
family can reveal cancellation invisible in termwise residue estimates.
The undeformed rational coefficient was treated with Andrews' multiple
sum by [KR08, Section 4]. A corresponding parameter-level formula for
b_n(e), with explicit arithmetic, is still missing from this investigation.
We have not assumed that its derivative inherits their theorem for free.

7. Reproducible checks and attribution

  .venv/bin/python experiments/half_pole_transfer.py
  .venv/bin/python experiments/catalan_parameter_identity.py

The first script independently rebuilds 11 quadruples (a,b,c,d), n=0,2,
...,20, from half-pole Laurent coefficients and verifies 605 inclusions
(11). It compares the two full sums at 12 nonzero parameter choices by
512-bit Arb digamma evaluation; differences contain zero and have size
below 10^(-110). The analytic identity is proved by (7), not by these
numerical comparisons. It also tests the global inclusions and surplus
bounds at every positive even index through 718.

The second checks (2) at 39 exact rational parameter choices, verifies
156 transformation/prefactor identities, and compares 28 quarter-pole
Taylor coefficients through e^6. It rebuilds all 720 saved a,c values
from the positive sum and checks all 360 instances of (3). The all-index,
all-Taylor-order claims rest on Sections 2--4, not finite testing.
