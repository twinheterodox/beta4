Beta(4): coordinated round 03
28 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

Status and the main change

This round obtains a proved improvement in the arithmetic/analytic balance:
a new common-base determinant family has infinitely many nonzero values
and tends to zero even after ALL powers of 2 in its rational coefficients
are cleared. Odd-prime denominators remain. This is not an irrationality
proof, a bound for the whole primitive multiplier, or evidence that a
complete proof is close.

The arithmetic and analysis workers continued their contexts. A newly
created fresh worker selected an independent construction before reading
the archive, then cross-reviewed the joint results. Root separately
reconstructed coefficients, primitive pairs, residue ranks, prime-power
congruences, and analytic certificates. These are model cross-checks,
not a human audit or formal verification. No priority claim is made.

1. Preserve the common powers of the arguments

The completed identity inherited from the classical Broadhurst ladders is

 theta=beta(4)=225/136 D4(i/2)-25/34 D4((1+i)/4)
                                      +9/68 D4((1-i)/8),
 D4(z)=Im[Li4(z)-log|z| Li3(z)+(log|z|)^2 Li2(z)/3].

Its source equations and algebraic derivation are recorded in
collaboration/round-02/fresh/findings.txt, with the primary reference
https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9803067. It is not a new functional identity.

The crucial observation is that, with q=(1-i)/2,

 z_2=i/2=-q^2, z_3=(1+i)/4=-q^3, z_5=(1-i)/8=-q^5.

Instead of replacing every z_m by z_m*t, preserve those different powers.
Write (c_2,c_3,c_5)=(225/136,-25/34,9/68), L(t)=-log(1-t^2/2), and

 H(t)=sum_(m=2,3,5) c_m [I4(z_m*t^m)+(m/2)L(t)I3(z_m*t^m)
                                  +(m^2/12)L(t)^2 I2(z_m*t^m)]
     =sum_(j>=1) h_j t^j.                                  (1)

Here I_s is the analytic imaginary-part germ, obtained by subtracting
the conjugate-argument polylogarithm and dividing by 2i; t itself is
not conjugated. All h_j are rational and H(1)=theta. This is a different
parametrization, not a uniform reindexing of the old Taylor series.

Define h_0=0 and, for 1<=i,j<=n,

 T_ij=h_(i+j)-h_(i+j-1), b_i=h_i,
 J_n(X)=det [[X,-b^T],[b,T]]=A_n X+C_n,
 A_n=det T, C_n=b^T adj(T)b, J_0(X)=X.                      (2)

For nonzero J_n let kappa_n>0 make kappa_n J_n primitive integral.
Then v_p(kappa_n)=-min(v_p(A_n),v_p(C_n)). This multiplier is rational,
and its valuation can be negative. Zero polynomials are handled separately.
There is no factor 6 in this round's normalization.

2. An elementary contour bound and infinitely many nonzero determinants

The possible polylogarithm singularities of (1) are the roots of

 t^4+4, t^6-4t^3+8, t^10-8t^5+32.

They share the quadratic t^2+2t+2, giving 16 distinct points. The two
logarithm points are +/-sqrt(2). All 18 points have modulus sqrt(2),
and all their arguments lie in pi*Z/60.

Put G_X(w)=(X-H(1/w))/(w-1). Its Laurent coefficients at infinity are
c_j(X)=X-sum_(l<=j)h_l. Their leading (n+1)-Hankel determinant equals
(-1)^n J_n(X), by elementary row and column differences.

At X=theta the apparent pole at w=1 is removable: H is analytic near
t=1 and H(1)=theta. Every remaining possible finite singularity lies in

 K={w : w^120 belongs to [0,2^(-60)]},

the 120-ray star of radius 1/sqrt(2), including its center. Its complement
on the sphere is simply connected, so it is an admissible cut for the
germ from infinity. Polynomial inverse-image capacity gives

 cap(K)=(2^(-60)/4)^(1/120)=2^(-31/60).

The classical Polya Hankel estimate, in the same normalization as (2),
therefore yields

 limsup log|J_n(theta)|/n^2 <= -(31/60)log 2.                (3)

This is an upper bound, not a two-sided asymptotic. Exact polynomial
division independently verifies that the full branch divisor divides
t^120-2^60 and that its reciprocal divides w^120-2^(-60).

Nonvanishing does not require positivity. At t=sqrt(2), all polylogarithm
blocks are analytic, while the coefficient of L(t)^2 is

 (75/272)[2G-3 Cl2(pi/4)]<0,

where G=beta(2). The inequality follows directly from G<1 and a rational
lower bound for the first positive eight-term block of the Clausen series.
Thus H has a genuine logarithmic branch. G_X is nonrational for EVERY
fixed X, and Kronecker's rationality criterion gives infinitely many
nonzero J_n(X). This does not assert nonvanishing at every index.

3. Dyadic normalization now fits inside the analytic decay

The arithmetic proof uses Q_2(i), with uniformizer pi=1-i, only as a
valuation tool. The final determinant coefficients remain rational.
Set x=q*t. After multiplication by -64, the three completed blocks have
the form

 -64 H(x/q)=C(x)+L_0(x)B(x)+L_0(x)^2 A(x),
 L_0(x)=-log(1-i*x^2),
 A(x)=B(x)=C(x)=x^10/(1-x^20) mod pi.                       (4)

All coefficients of A,B,C are integral at pi. The difference between
the two polylogarithms kills the high powers of 2 in their summation
indices. The remaining logarithms have only logarithmic denominator
depth. This is proved coefficientwise, not inferred from scalar data.

For n>=8 put r=floor(log_2 n), delta=2r-2, and

 R=max(0,2n-2^(r+1)-9).

After the natural q row/column scaling and multiplication by
-64*2^delta, the full bordered matrix in (2) has EXACT residue rank R.
The resulting bound for the actual primitive multiplier is

 v_2(kappa_n) <= n(n+1)/2+(6+delta)(n+1)
                                -ceil((n+1-R)/2).          (5)

In particular limsup v_2(kappa_n)/n^2<=1/2. The improvement from the
old sufficient leading rate 5/2 comes mainly from the common-power
parametrization. The residue-rank theorem supplies the additional
explicit divisibility saving in (5); these are distinct improvements.

Let d_n=2^max(0,v_2(kappa_n)), or d_n=1 for a zero polynomial. Combining
(3) and (5) gives the genuine positive result of the round:

 limsup log|d_n J_n(theta)|/n^2 <= -(log 2)/60 <0.           (6)

Every coefficient of d_n J_n has ODD denominator, and infinitely many
of its values at theta are nonzero. The forms shrink faster than any
fixed exponential in n. They are still rational forms, not integer
forms. Odd denominators permit nonzero arbitrarily small values even
when theta is rational, so (6) supplies no irrationality contradiction.

4. Exactly where large odd primes remain

Let p satisfy n/2<p<=n, p^2>2n and p not dividing 2*3*5*17. The
coefficients before h_(2p) are p-integral, all coefficients through
h_(2n) have valuation at least -4, and

 p^4 h_(2p)=chi_4(p)*225/272 mod p.                         (7)

Only the pure m=2 Li4 term supplies the first fourth-power denominator.
Let R=2n-2p+1 and m=2p-n-1. Scaling the last R rows of (2) gives

 p^(4R)J_n(X)=(-1)^[R(R-1)/2](chi_4(p)*225/272)^R
                                           J_m(X) mod p. (8)

The fresh review sharpened this to a coefficientwise congruence modulo
p^4: if S is p^4 times the lower-right R-by-R block of T, then det S
is a p-unit and

                    p^(4R)J_n=det(S)J_m mod p^4.           (9)

Both cross-blocks were p-integral before row scaling, so the Schur
complement differs from the smaller pencil by a p^4-integral matrix.

Writing mu_m=min(v_p(A_m),v_p(C_m)), if mu_m<4 then

                    v_p(kappa_n)=4R-mu_m.                 (10)

When mu_m>=4, only the upper bound 4R-4 follows. Thus common content in
a smaller pencil can cancel part of the expected fourth layer.
For n=10,p=7, m=3 and mu_3=1, the actual exponent is 27, not 28.
At n=2p-1 the smaller pencil is J_0=X, giving the unconditional local
exponent v_p(kappa_(2p-1))=4(2p-1).

These are local statements. They do not supply a uniform normality
theorem, a lower bound for the whole multiplier, or a lower bound for
actual determinant values. Large positive valuations can coexist with
negative valuations at other primes. The root checks 94 recursions
modulo both p and p^4, including the actual defect.

5. A shared Mellin kernel and a more structured numerator problem

The same common powers combine all three blocks into ONE rational kernel:

 U(x)=900*x^2/[17*(x^4+4)]
       -2025*x^3/[17*(x^6-4*x^3+8)]
       -5625*x^5/[17*(x^10-8*x^5+32)].

With l=log 2, r=-log x and W(r)=r^3/6+l*r^2/4+l^2*r/12,

                    theta=integral_0^1 U(x)W(r) dx/x.     (11)

Let D be the monic least common multiple of the three denominators of U,
without 17. It has degree 16. Put N=D*U. For P=A+D*V, define
N*V=sum b_j*x^j and impose

                  sum b_j/j^3=sum b_j/j^2=0.              (12)

These are exact rational linear constraints, and they give

 I(P):=integral U(x)P(x)W(r) dx/x
                         =A*theta+sum b_j/j^4.            (13)

There is a signed Rodrigues subspace with genuinely cheaper arithmetic.
Write N0=17N in Z[x], E=x*d/dx, and choose any S in Z[x]. Set

 F=x(1-x)^2 N0^4 S, V=17 E^3F/N0.

V is an integer polynomial: three Euler derivatives retain at least
one factor N0. Conditions (12) follow from F(1)=E F(1)=0. The rational
remainder in (13) becomes

                    sum f_j/j=integral_0^1 F(x) dx/x,

whose denominator divides lcm(1,...,deg F), rather than its fourth
power. This is an exact structural saving for this subspace. However,
A remains free, and no choice of S and A producing small nonzero forms
has been proved. Selecting them by already knowing approximations to
theta would not solve the problem.

Two review results prevent easy shortcuts in (13):

* U has exactly one zero x0 in (3/5,5/8), with sign positive then
  negative. The preserved moments M1=integral U*r and M2=integral U*r^2
  are positive. For every nonzero P>=0 satisfying (12), a dual quadratic
  polynomial gives I(P)>A*(1309/2600), with A>0. After any positive
  normalization making the theta coefficient an integer, I(P)>1/2.
  Thus these nonnegative source forms cannot shrink at all.

* For EVERY signed P satisfying (12),
  integral |P U| W >= (log 2)^2 |A| M1/12.
  Consequently an absolute-source estimate cannot establish shrinking
  normalized forms with nonzero theta coefficient. Signed designs must
  control actual cancellation, not merely uniform smallness of P.

Neither result excludes signed source values themselves or the
common-base Pade determinants. A dimension count or short lattice
vector is also insufficient: it can lie in the exact A=B=0 kernel.

6. The independent construction: a uniform obstruction after a pivot

The fresh agent first proposed integer polynomial Mellin weights, then
recognized their overlap with round 01 and pivoted to rational Euler
kernels. Four integrations by parts give J(E^4 H)=H(1), with
J(R)=integral (-log t)^3 R(t)dt/(6t). This cancels unwanted periods
exactly, but Taylor osculation of a rational H reduces at the endpoint
to a scalar polynomial weight.

For repeated quadratic poles the weight is
P_n(y)=y^n(u_n-v_n*y)^n. For even n and positive integers u_n,v_n with
log(u_n+v_n)=o(n), its ACTUAL primitive positive form L_n satisfies

 liminf log(L_n)/n >= 8/3-log(5+4sqrt(2))
                    =0.3004633900...>0.                   (14)

Primes in (4n/3,2n) not dividing u_n+v_n retain their fourth powers
after primitive normalization. An exact minimax calculation over ALL
real pole ratios, plus a uniform integral lower bound, proves (14).
It is an actual obstruction for this family, including moving rational
poles of subexponential height; it is not a statement about all rational
kernels. The proof uses the prime number theorem for the interval sum.

7. Finite diagnostics, review, and what has not been achieved

Root checked 14 prescribed indices for each of the old equal-speed
germ, an alternative classical ladder, and the common-base germ.
All nontrivial primitive forms tested exceed one. For the common-base
germ the n=1 exception is merely X. At n=20 the log absolute primitive
value falls from about 2305 for the old germ to 962 for the new germ;
neither is remotely small. Those numbers are diagnostics, not asymptotics.

The substantive positive theorem is (6), despite the unfavorable finite
values. Odd-prime integrality is unresolved. The shared Mellin kernel
and one-LCM signed subspace give a more concrete design problem, but no
small, nonzero integer family has emerged from them. Merely extending
the existing scans or improving the star constant is not the missing
mathematical mechanism.
