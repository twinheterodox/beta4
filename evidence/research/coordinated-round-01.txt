Beta(4): first coordinated research round
28 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

The user requested multiple coordinated instances after the investigation
in dilations-and-positive-kernels.txt. Three workers started with separate
contexts and the parent model's inherited configuration. The coordinator
assigned arithmetic, construction/analysis, and a fresh approach, with
separate write directories. Initial findings were developed separately;
specific arguments were then exchanged for adversarial cross-review.

This is a record of mathematical derivations and model cross-checks.
It is not independent mathematical verification, formal verification,
a priority claim, or an irrationality proof. The first round produced
sharper bounds and several uniform reductions/obstructions. It did not
produce a compatible construction proving beta(4) irrational.

Full proofs, scripts, data and individual reviews are under
collaboration/round-01/. The record below explains what changes the
research assessment. Write theta=beta(4).

1. The coefficient-denominator benchmark is substantially sharper

The arithmetic worker independently confirmed the earlier slow-filter
denominator bound and found a better baseline. For regular integer
power-series differential letters, a word of length r has coefficient
denominators through degree N dividing

             W_r(N)=product_(j=1)^r lcm(1,...,floor(N/j)).

The reason is that its integration indices are strictly increasing.
This replaces the crude four-word rate 4N by (25/12)N. The earlier bound
was valid; comparisons with it overstated the saving relative to this
sharper baseline.

For the slow filter r_k from the preceding note, combine this lemma with
the two-word bound at p>N/k and the Frobenius congruence. If Q_(N,k) is
the ACTUAL common denominator of its rational Taylor coefficients through
original t-degree N, then for each fixed odd k>=3,

 3/2+1/(k+2) <= liminf_N log Q_(N,k)/N
              <= limsup_N log Q_(N,k)/N
              <= 3/2+1/k+1/(2k+4).                           (1)

A limit is not asserted. No uniform statement for jointly varying k,N
is inferred from these fixed-k asymptotics. The exact finite bounds and
their hypotheses are in arithmetic/findings.txt.

The lower estimate is supported by proved first occurrences of the
first two prime layers and a surviving fourth layer, not extrapolation
from finite data. Thus the base rate approaching 3/2 as k increases
is an actual feature of these coefficients.

The worker also obtained an explicit residue-rank lemma for an endpoint
Pade determinant. It improves a raw clearing bound but does not control
the primitive height or prove nonvanishing. This distinction matters:
the multiplier kappa in P(theta)=kappa L(theta) still costs real size,
even when its primes divide neither final primitive coefficient.

The construction worker cross-reviewed the word argument, Frobenius cap,
both onset proofs, and the degree normalization and found no gap. The
coordinator separately reviewed those arguments.

2. The positive kernel's real estimate is already sharp at the main scale

For the cubic-tail kernel K from the preceding note and ANY increasing
positive integer row/column nodes a_i,b_j, the construction worker proved

 log det K(a_i^2,b_j^2)
     =log det[1/(a_i+b_j)]+O(n),                             (2)

with absolute constants independent of node positions, gaps, or
intersections. A common zero node has the analogous statement using
the Cauchy determinant on the remaining positive nodes.

The proof adds a lower density comparison to the earlier upper bound.
Its regularized Cauchy determinant has an explicit rank-one formula,
proved by rational interpolation. This is a two-sided theorem, so a
better real estimate cannot hide an additional n^2-scale gain for a
fixed node design.

Combining that lower bound with exact prime valuations gives a complete
obstruction for consecutive principal nodes. Let P_(n,m)(X) be the
primitive integer polynomial obtained from the kernel determinant on
m+1,...,m+n, with positive normalization. Then for EVERY sequence of
nonnegative integer offsets m=m_n,

 liminf_(n->infinity) log P_(n,m_n)(theta)/n^2
                           >=2-2 log 2 >0.                  (3)

The same leading lower bound holds for nodes 0,...,n-1. These primitive
values grow; larger scans of these complete families are unwarranted.
Invertible rational row/column changes do not evade (3), because they
only multiply the unnormalized determinant by a rational scalar.

The proof does not exclude different subspaces, nonprincipal frames,
sparse node designs, or another kernel. Its full scope is recorded in
construction/findings.txt. The coordinator independently checked the
key determinant identity on 144 rational frames and reviewed the
prime-valuation and offset arguments.

3. A direct real-moment bridge for the slow filter is obstructed

Put R_k(z)=r_k(sqrt(z)) and q=sqrt(2)-1. For odd k>=3, every root of

                         z^k=-q^2                           (4)

is a genuine logarithmic branch point of R_k. In particular k-1 of
them are nonreal. The source is the -2h(2v) summand: at t^k=iq,
2v=i pi/2 is a sech pole, and the other summands are locally regular.

This logarithmic monodromy survives removing the initial zero
coefficients, multiplying by any nonzero algebraic function, and adding
an algebraic function. A Cauchy transform of a measure supported on a
compact real set has no nonreal singularity. Consequently these
same-variable modifications cannot provide the desired real-support
moment representation. Merely inserting an arcsine-type algebraic
factor does not repair this problem.

This is stronger than noticing that the initial zero moments prevent a
direct positive Hankel realization. It still leaves nonlinear changes
of independent variable, complex contours, bivariate kernels, and other
nonvanishing mechanisms open. No successful such replacement was found.

4. The scalar positive-polynomial search has an exact two-dimensional form

The fresh worker independently considered

 I(P)=(1/6) integral_0^1 (-log x)^3 P(x^2)/(1+x^2) dx.

The underlying identity overlaps earlier cyclotomic/tail work. The new
result in this investigation is a complete description of its positive
polynomial image, rather than another fixed-family scan.

Define FORMAL coefficients, without assuming theta irrational,

 A(P)=P(-1),
 B(P)=integral [P(t)-P(-1)]/(1+t) dnu(t),
 dnu(t)=(-log t)^3/(96 sqrt(t)) dt.

Then I(P)=A(P)theta+B(P), and the moments of nu are the rationals
nu_j=(2j+1)^(-4).

For every degree bound D>=1 there are explicit rationals l_D<theta<u_D
such that the entire image of polynomials nonnegative on [0,1] is the
cone generated by

                     (1,-l_D),  (-1,u_D).                   (5)

The rational points of this cone have explicit rational polynomial
witnesses. The endpoints are rational Gram-matrix solves, and the
boundary witnesses are rational weighted squares. A self-contained
interval sum-of-squares argument proves that no other nonnegative
polynomial of that degree enlarges the cone. For example

 l_1=1/2,  u_1=1;
 l_2=81/82, u_2=161/162.

Thus, for an integer final coefficient-height bound H, the exact least
positive represented value is

 min(1,
     min_(1<=q<=H)[q theta-floor(q l_D)],
     min_(1<=q<=H)[ceil(q u_D)-q theta]).                     (6)

This includes primitive normalization: if a minimizing pair has a
common divisor, dividing by it remains in the cone and reduces the
value and height. A high-dimensional polynomial search can therefore
be replaced by this two-dimensional integer problem.

There is a useful warning. With degree and source coefficient heights
unrestricted, these polynomials represent EVERY positive rational
linear form in theta. Clearing their source coefficients also represents
every primitive positive integer form after primitive normalization.
The least witness degree for a prescribed L=A theta+B>0, A!=0, satisfies

 degree =log(|A|/L)/log(3+2sqrt(2))
                            +O(log log(|A|/L))              (7)

as |A|/L tends to infinity. The proof uses explicit Chebyshev witnesses
and a lower bound for every positive polynomial.

Neither (6) nor (7) creates the needed small integer form. In particular,
choosing an ordinary continued-fraction form first and encoding it as
a positive polynomial is circular as an irrationality argument. Large
primitive values on the two canonical rays also do not exclude small
primitive integer directions between them. The worker gives an exact
example of that distinction, explicitly labeled as an encoding of an
ordinary continued-fraction approximation.

Full proofs, boundary cases, integer lifts, and limitations are in
fresh/findings.txt. The coordinator separately derived the four parity
Gram formulas and verified all nine recorded endpoint pairs and the
formal coefficients of their extremizers.

5. Consequences for the next round

The arithmetic improvement remains valid, with a more realistic and
stronger denominator benchmark. Two proposed easy routes for exploiting
it are now excluded: the consecutive principal positive kernels, and
same-variable real-moment repairs of the slow filter using algebraic
multipliers. These exclusions should remain in the shared research
record so future workers do not repeat their finite scans.

A subsequent construction must address compatibility explicitly. A
complex-contour or genuinely bivariate nonvanishing mechanism could
avoid the branch obstruction, but no viable one has been constructed.
Nonprincipal kernel frames remain open, with their real determinant
energy now known to O(n); their arithmetic must justify any further
search. The scalar cone theorem gives a complete reduction for its
particular family, but another pass over unrestricted positive scalar
weights would merely re-encode the approximation problem.

The first coordinated round has therefore narrowed the search and
improved its exact arithmetic inputs. It has not supplied a shrinking
sequence of nonzero primitive integer forms.
