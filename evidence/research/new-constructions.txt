Beta(4): asymmetric congruence lattices and cancellation without dilation
27 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

Status

This pass follows the instruction to invent and pursue new constructions
without waiting for approval between research steps. Two new directions
were implemented and then combined. Neither proves beta(4) irrational.
The old obstruction is not being asserted for these new constructions.

There are useful structural improvements: denominator cancellation can
be imposed exactly on one side of a mixed determinant; nonvanishing can
be guaranteed algebraically for a collection of minors; and beta(2) can
be removed without doubling the exponent range. Some resulting primitive
polynomial values are less than one, but a continued-fraction benchmark
does substantially better at the same degree and coefficient bit length.
No asymptotic advantage or irrationality estimate has been established.

1. An asymmetric construction with an algebraic nonvanishing guarantee

Use the positive matrix from arithmetic-followup.txt,

  M(beta)=A-6 beta I_N,  beta=beta(4),  N=binom(H,3).

Here A is rational. Its entries come from the vector-valued functions

  Phi_u=(U_u+V_u, 2(U_u^2-V_u^2)),
  u=(0,a,b,c), 0<a<b<c<=H,
  U_u=y^u, V_u=y^(c*1-u), y_i=x_i^4,

paired against dmu=dx/(1+(x_1 x_2 x_3 x_4)^2).

Select m rows by an integer matrix U, with 2m<=N. Instead of using the
same functions for columns, choose a full-rank rational column matrix V
with at least 2m columns. Define

  Z(X)=U(A-6X I)V=E+XF.

The following sufficient nonvanishing condition is elementary but useful:

  rank [E;F]=2m.                                    (1)

Indeed, if w^t Z(x)=0 for any real x, then
(w^t,xw^t)[E;F]=0. Full row rank of [E;F] implies w=0. Hence Z(x) has
rank m for EVERY real x, independently of any arithmetic information
about beta(4).

Consequently

  P(X)=det(Z(X)Z(X)^t)
      =sum_J det(Z_J(X))^2                         (2)

is strictly positive on the real axis. J runs through m-column subsets.
Its degree is at most 2m. Clearing denominators and dividing the gcd of
the coefficients preserves strict positivity. This replaces positivity
of an individual mixed determinant with a simultaneous nonvanishing
guarantee for its maximal minors.

For a full invertible N-by-N V, it suffices to check
rank[UA;U]=2m. This condition was checked exactly in every experiment.
It also reveals the trap in counting extra variables: the common kernel
of UA and U can have dimension N-2m. Short vectors there produce zero
polynomials. The rank condition prevents such vectors from supplying a
false success. An argument based only on the total number of variables
or on the first successive minimum would not suffice.

2. Impose divisibility before estimating real size

Let D be the least common multiple of the denominators of UA and
F_0=D UA, an integer matrix. For a chosen divisor q of D, form

  L_q={v in Z^N : F_0 v=0 mod q}.                  (3)

For q=D, every entry of UA V is an integer when V consists of lattice
vectors. Thus Z(X) has integer coefficients from the start. The other
tests impose just the primes in 4H<p<8H, or impose no congruences.

The lattice is computed exactly using duality. If B_* is an integer
row basis, obtained by Hermite normal form, for the lattice generated by
the rows of [q I_N; F_0], then a row basis of L_q is

  B=q B_*^(-t).

Integrality and F_0 B^t=0 mod q are checked exactly. This is a new choice
of column functions, not a basis change of the old full determinant.

To control the coefficient cost, use the rational upper Gram matrix

  R_ij=integral Phi_i dot Phi_j dx,
  M(beta)<=R.

Each entry of R is a finite rational sum of products 1/(4a_i+1).
LLL reduction uses the exact Gram matrix B R B^t. No decimal value of
beta(4) is used to choose these columns. The experiments also test
selecting just 2m coefficient-independent columns, in order of increasing
R-norm, and removing individual column coefficient gcds.

There is an exact rational upper bound for (2). If G=V^t R V, then

  P(beta)<=det(U R U^t) e_m(G),                    (4)

where e_m is the m-th elementary symmetric function of the eigenvalues.
For each minor this is determinant Cauchy--Schwarz; sum the bounds and
use Cauchy--Binet. Divide the right side by the coefficient content when
passing to the primitive polynomial. If a common denominator was needed
for E, include its power 2m. The implementation accounts for each factor.

3. What the asymmetric experiments actually give

With m approximately N/2, imposing all denominator congruences makes a
large finite improvement over using unconstrained columns, but leaves
the wrong sign. For H=6, N=20, m=10, the natural logarithm of the
primitive value is

  no congruences:        3924.5965
  upper prime band:     3152.0430
  all denominators:     1821.3335.

At H=8, N=56, m=28, the all-denominator value still has log 7063.2796.

Keeping one or two fixed left rows while increasing the available right
space is more favorable. For the first left row, m=1, the all-column
construction gives:

  H   N     log primitive value     log rational upper bound
  8  56          1.4915                    2.7899
  9  84         -2.4593                   -1.1694
 10 120         -2.4593                   -1.7182
 11 165         -0.1172                    0.8850

At H=9 the primitive polynomial is explicitly

  Q(X)=3125301641748 X^2-6181500389592 X+3056580727769.

It is strictly positive for every real X, and the construction proves
Q(beta(4))<1 using the rational bound (4). Its value is approximately
0.0855. This is a finite certificate, not an irrationality theorem.
The repetition at H=9 and H=10 is an exact repetition of the polynomial.
The later values do not establish a decreasing sequence.

The selected-column m=2 variant at H=11 produces a degree-four primitive
polynomial with log value -1.9485. Its H=12 successor has log value
-1.1934. Here the simple rational upper bounds remain larger than one;
Arb interval evaluation certifies the stated actual values. The selected
m=1 variant at H=12 has log value -1.5053 and a rational upper bound below
one. None of these observations supplies a uniform decay estimate.

As an arithmetic diagnostic, the coefficient-image lattices for the
fixed first row have indices

  H=4: 13369345911922; H=5: 342075734; H=6: 14872858;
  H=7,8,9,10: 2124694=2*11*13*17*19*23.

These are the images in (UA v,Uv), with integer coordinates and v in
L_D. They show that extra function variables do not automatically create
equally many independent arithmetic approximation directions. No general
stabilization theorem is claimed from these finite indices.

4. Cancellation without doubling the exponent range

The previous positive construction eliminated beta(2) by combining scales
1 and 2. This new route uses only the complement-odd functions

  g_u=U_u-V_u,

on the same ordered index set. Their Gram matrix has the exact form

  A_H+K_H beta(2)-2 beta(4) I_N.                   (5)

Beta(3) cancels by complement reflection; same-side exponent sums have
four distinct coordinates. A cross term has four coincident exponents
precisely when u=v. That gives the full-rank target coefficient -2 I_N.

Find an integer full-row-rank matrix W such that

  W K_H W^t=0.

Then the projected positive Gram matrix is

  W A_H W^t-2 beta(4) W W^t.                      (6)

Its beta(4) coefficient has rank exactly rank W. This avoids the target
rank loss in the old full-cube isotropic seed and the increased exponent
range in the dilation construction. Positivity gives nonvanishing of
its determinant at beta(4).

The rational quadratic-form search first tries sparse vectors. When those
fail, PARI/GP qfsolve [P] supplies additional rational isotropic vectors.
Every returned vector is checked by exact substitution. Rational
orthogonal complements are replaced by their full integer lattices
before further searches. This saturation matters: merely clearing each
row denominator can introduce very large, artificial indices.

The saturation uses a unimodular HNF transformation of the transpose:
if T B^t has its last N-r rows zero, the first r columns of T^-1,
transposed, span span_Q(B) intersect Z^N. This is subsequently reduced
by LLL. All restrictions remain exactly isotropic.

The complete spaces constructed in this pass have dimensions

  H        5    6    7    8    9
  N       10   20   35   56   84
  rank W   4   10   18   28   42.

This is finite evidence for large cancellation spaces, not a proof of
a uniform three-parameter family with controlled height for all H.
The primitive determinant values still grow:

  H       log primitive value
  5             242.9562
  6             804.4619
  7            1909.9869
  8            3985.1733
  9            6917.9635.

Some raw solver bases have hundreds of bits per coefficient. Much of
that is representational: final saturation and metric reduction reduce
the H=9 basis from 790 bits to 25 bits. The primitive full determinant is
exactly unchanged, as it must be. Thus large solver coefficients alone
are not an explanation of failure, and reducing a basis cannot be
reported as an improvement in the full primitive polynomial.

Proper subspaces can change the polynomial. These were tested after
metric reduction, with 1,...,6 rows, approximately half the rows, and
all rows. Every tested primitive value is greater than one. For example,
at H=9 the 21-row choice has log value 3334.0896. The full 42-row result
remains 6917.9635, checking basis invariance explicitly.

The quadratic-form implementation initially had an empty-matrix shape
edge case when a complement became zero-dimensional. This was corrected
in isotropic_mellin.py and checked on a one-dimensional zero form, a
hyperbolic plane, and the old seed. Saturation is optional, so previous
default experiment results are unchanged. One unsaturated H=7 PARI run
hit its 45-second limit; it is not treated as a mathematical obstruction.
Saturated searches completed through H=9.

5. Combining both new constructions

For each space (6), keep g_(0,1,2,3) as the fixed left function and apply
the congruence-lattice construction to its right functions. Its inclusion
in the isotropic space is checked exactly. The resulting one-row pencil
has integer coefficients and rank two as a coefficient map, so its sum
of squared entries is again positive for every real X.

The primitive quadratic logs for H=5,...,9 are

  13.2576, 3.4324, 0.0105, -1.6245, 1.9189.

Thus this combination also produces subunit examples but no observed
uniform decay. The single-scale cancellation does not automatically
translate into a better irrationality construction after all costs are
included.

6. Why the small values are not being advertised as a breakthrough

A numerical continued-fraction benchmark was run at the same polynomial
degree and no greater maximum coefficient bit length. If p/q and r/s
are consecutive convergents of beta(4), then

  (qX-p)^2+(sX-r)^2

is positive for every real X because ps-qr=+/-1. Its square supplies a
degree-four comparison. These are diagnostic numerical constructions;
their existence in finite examples supplies no irrationality proof.

All 31 low-degree cases in the comparison are beaten by this baseline.
At the 43-bit budget of the H=9 quadratic above, the baseline is

  3599167551145 X^2-7118754281016 X+3520026630697,

with log value -28.8628, versus -2.4593 for the new construction. This
prevents mistaking a large improvement over earlier failed constructions
for a new Diophantine estimate. The important feature of the research
construction is that its choices use exact moments and rational norm
bounds; what is still missing is a useful asymptotic theorem for them.

There is also no overlooked success in the obvious factors. Exact
factorization of 35 distinct same-scale/subspace polynomials gives 44
factors, all greater than one in absolute value at beta(4). In addition,
123 primitive divided derivatives of three representative Gram
polynomials were tested; all are greater than one. Their nonvanishing
at beta(4) follows from positivity of the corresponding principal-minor
sums, since the target coefficient is negative definite. These are
finite checks, not a general impossibility result for derivatives.

7. The unresolved estimate

The asymmetric construction separates two requirements that used to be
entangled: integer coefficients and nonvanishing. What remains is an
asymptotic estimate for the size of a coefficient-independent collection
of lattice vectors, after all normalization costs.

The exact target is worth stating. For primitive nonzero Q_H of degree
at most 2m_H, it suffices to prove

  log |Q_H(beta(4))|/m_H -> -infinity

along a subsequence. If beta(4)=a/b, every nonzero such value is at least
b^(-2m_H), which contradicts that limit. For fixed m, values tending to
zero suffice. Isolated values below one do not meet either criterion.

For m=1 this requires TWO independent images under v -> (UA v,Uv), not
just one small vector. For general m it requires rank 2m. The large
common kernel means that a generic dimension count or a bound on the
first successive minimum cannot provide this estimate. Any proposed
Siegel-lemma argument must explicitly control the last required minimum
in the coefficient quotient and its real norm.

For the same-scale approach the missing ingredient is a structured
choice of isotropic subspaces with a favorable COMPLETE arithmetic/real
balance. A small-looking basis, a large dimension, or existence of
rational isotropic vectors is insufficient. The examples show all three
can coexist with very large primitive values.

Neither missing estimate was proved in this pass. The two constructions
escape the precise hypotheses of the previous obstruction, but do not
yet yield a result about irrationality, an irrationality measure, or
linear independence. The work remains private; no new release or paper
has been published.

The separate symbolic implementation reconstructs 92 dilation moment
entries and two complete mixed pencils, including their integer
coefficients, rank and primitive polynomials. It independently verifies
the rational size bound in the small example, sixteen single-scale
moment entries and their isotropic projection, and the five combined
one-row pencil certificates. This is a second implementation by the same
agent, not an independent model/human audit or formal verification.
