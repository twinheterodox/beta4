New avenues: block arithmetic, a fixed auxiliary period, and matrix measures
28 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

Status and assessment
---------------------
There is no irrationality proof. The useful new direction from this pass is
to combine a block of the coupled hypergeometric forms BEFORE clearing
their denominators individually. Exact congruences can then cancel
denominators across the block. This was motivated by retaining a fixed
coefficient of Catalan's constant instead of eliminating it immediately.

The experiments give both forms G+A beta(4)+B, with A,B integers, and pairs
of independent integer forms in beta(4) alone. One checked pair has each
absolute value less than 10^(-30), using a rational analytic error bound
that does not evaluate beta(4). These are finite examples, not a uniform
construction theorem. Their coefficients have about 130 decimal digits;
numerical continued fractions give much smaller errors at comparable
height. Smallness by itself is therefore not evidence of a breakthrough.

The precise next problem is to bound the cost of two independent arithmetic
directions in the block, including the cost of lifting them to combinations
of the original forms. Most of the apparent high dimension consists of
exact identities. A naive dimension or volume count would be misleading.

A second construction completes two old scalar weights to a positive
matrix weight. It gives nonvanishing determinants with both pole grids and
only beta(4) remaining. Its tested primitive polynomials evaluate above one.
This is a structural result, but the present version has poor arithmetic.

A third, more speculative direction is to realize a gamma-function filter
geometrically, with integral approximants built into the construction.
The filter isolates beta(4) without Catalan or its square. No suitable
geometric realization with the required arithmetic and convergence has
been supplied. Ordinary truncated gamma products do not work.

No priority claim, independent model check, or formal verification is made.

1. A fixed auxiliary period changes the target
----------------------------------------------
Write G=beta(2). Suppose, using the irrationality of G as an external input,
that one constructs

    E_N = G + A_N beta(4) + B_N -> 0,     A_N,B_N in Z.             (1)

Then beta(4) is irrational. Indeed, beta(4)=p/q would give

    q E_N = qG + p A_N + q B_N,

whose distance from zero is at least dist(qG,Z)>0. No upper bound on
|A_N| is required, and there is no separate nonvanishing obligation.
A nonzero G coefficient from any fixed finite set also suffices, by taking
a subsequence. The external Catalan result used for this conditional
criterion is [O]; this note does not reprove it.

The distinction from the previous work is in the arithmetic task. Earlier
we made the G coefficient zero separately at each index, often dividing
by a large coefficient. Here it is allowed to remain equal to one, while
all integrality conditions are solved jointly across a block. This still
requires genuine arithmetic control; the criterion alone proves nothing
about whether such a sequence exists.

1.1 The exact block calculation

Use the already proved coupled family from coupled-holonomy.txt. For even
n>0,

    r_n = a_n G + b_n,
    s_n = 16 a_n beta(4) + c_n G + d_n,

where all four coefficients are rational, and

    0 < r_n < 8 phi^(-5n),
    0 < s_n < (64/n) phi^(-5n),     phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2.              (2)

The strict inequalities use G<1. Since phi^5>11, the simpler bounds
8/11^n and 64/(n 11^n) are valid as well.

For m=N,...,N+w-1, take both source forms at n=2m, with integer weights
x_m,y_m. The desired affine arithmetic conditions are

    sum (x_m a_(2m) + y_m c_(2m)) = 1,
    A = sum 16 y_m a_(2m) in Z,
    B = sum (x_m b_(2m) + y_m d_(2m)) in Z.                       (3)

If (3) holds, the analytic certificate is simply

    |G+A beta(4)+B|
      < sum_m [8|x_m| + (64/(2m))|y_m|] / 11^(2m).              (4)

This bound is rational. It does not use a numerical approximation to G
or beta(4), except that their defining values enter the original analytic
proof of (2). In particular, (4) does not select weights by looking at the
decimal expansion of beta(4).

Let V be the 2w by 3 rational matrix with source rows
(a_n,0,b_n) and (c_n,16a_n,d_n), in the order (G,beta(4),1).
Choose a common denominator D. Adjoin (0,D,0) and (0,0,D) to the integer
row matrix DV. Hermite normal form determines whether (D,0,0) is in its
row lattice, and supplies a lift when it is. The appended rows enforce
the integrality of A and B. They are auxiliary arithmetic variables,
not extra small analytic forms.

The homogeneous kernel has 2w-1 source-coordinate directions. Weighted
LLL, followed by exact projection and an integer lattice embedding, looks
for a small representative of the affine target. The weights

    W_m = 123^(N+w-1-m)

approximate the relative analytic decay, since phi^10 is about 122.992.
The integer 123 is only a search weight: the certificates use (2) or (4),
and do not replace phi^10 by 123 in an inequality.

All final coefficients are recomputed exactly. The floating-point Babai
variant used in some exploratory runs is likewise only a search heuristic;
it does not certify integrality or the analytic bound.

1.2 Finite results and what they do not establish

The following table uses the coarser, entirely rational certificate (4).
The last two columns concern the direct beta(4) pairs explained below.
Each entry k means a proved strict upper bound 10^(-k).

   N     w       fixed-G form       first beta(4) form    second form
   8    16            15                   17                 16
   8    24            23                   26                 24
  16    16            14                   15                 15
  16    24            29                   30                 30

Additional Arb calculations include N=16,w=32, with a fixed-G error bound
less than 2.680*10^(-38), and N=32,w=32, with a bound less than
3.740*10^(-35). These have exact coefficient and interval checks, but were
not included in the separate Fraction recombination table above.

There is no monotonicity theorem. The short block N=32,w=16 has a large
error, despite the small errors of several earlier blocks. Allowing larger
blocks matters, and a choice of block size cannot be justified from the
successful examples alone.

The finite solvability of (3) is also not a proof that coefficient one is
always attainable. An asymptotic argument must prove both solvability and
a sufficiently inexpensive lift, or use the homogeneous version below.

1.3 A useful consequence: two independent pure beta(4) forms

The same calculation gives the lattice

    L_(N,w) = { z in Z^(2w): (zV)_G=0,
                            (zV)_beta and (zV)_1 are integers }.

Map z to its last two coefficients (A,B). The rational coefficient map V
has rank three for a block containing two consecutive even source indices:
the classical Catalan recurrence [Z] gives independent (a_n,b_n) and
(a_(n+2),b_(n+2)), and s_n has nonzero beta coefficient 16a_n.
Consequently the image of L_(N,w) has rank two. The exact-identity kernel
has rank 2w-3.

For completeness, the recurrence rank statement uses the classical
second-order recurrence for u_n,v_n with u_0=1,u_1=7/4 and
v_0=0,v_1=13/8. Its adjacent Casoratian is nonzero because both recurrence
coefficients are nonzero. The two-step Casoratian is a nonzero multiple
of the adjacent one. At even indices a_n=8u_n and b_n=-8v_n, as identified
in the preceding coupled-family work. This uses the known classical
identification, not a new inference from the finite coefficient table.

For each reduced lattice row, compute (A,B) exactly, discard (0,0), and
divide by gcd(A,B). Select two rows whose coefficient determinant is
nonzero. The corresponding forms cannot both vanish at ANY real number.

If their two certified bounds tend to zero for an unbounded sequence of
blocks, beta(4) is irrational: for beta(4)=p/q, at least one of the two
forms is a nonzero multiple of 1/q. This direct criterion does not use
the irrationality of G at all.

The selected pair for N=16,w=24 has coefficient bit lengths 428 and 430.
Both errors are less than 10^(-30) by rational inequalities. A separate
Fraction calculation verifies the three coefficient coordinates and the
nonzero determinant. Numerical continued fractions, with no larger
coefficient height, beat all eight selected homogeneous forms in the
four-row table. That benchmark is not part of the construction or a proof.

1.4 The mathematical hurdle worth investigating next

Most short source vectors can be identities. For example, at N=16,w=24,
41 of the 47 reduced homogeneous basis vectors have image (0,0); the
remaining six span an image of rank two. Increasing the number of source
vectors does not create that many independent useful approximations.

A sufficient next theorem would give, for an explicit unbounded choice
of N and w, two independent image directions with source lifts z_1,z_2
such that

    max_i sum_m [8|x_(i,m)|+(64/(2m))|y_(i,m)|]/11^(2m) -> 0.     (5)

This must account for the saturated congruence lattice and the cost of
integer lifts through the exact-identity kernel. Computing its index or
invoking a high-dimensional volume bound alone does not prove (5).

One route is to use contiguity/recurrence identities to give an explicit
basis for the identity kernel, then compute the remaining arithmetic
conditions and bound two independent lifts. Another is an inhomogeneous
argument for the particular fixed-G target (3), which can succeed even
when a bound on the entire lattice covering radius is too large.

The experiments also save a deliberately loose universal covering bound.
For a reduced weighted kernel basis B_i, let c be the vector of source G
coefficients and W the diagonal weight matrix. The squared distance from
zero to the real affine hyperplane c.x=1 is 1/||W^(-1)c||^2. Babai rounding
gives a point with weighted squared norm at most

    1/||W^(-1)c||^2 + (1/4) sum_i ||B_i||^2.                     (6)

This replaces orthogonalized row norms by larger ordinary row norms.
Cauchy--Schwarz converts (6) to an error bound using (2). The calculation
is a finite bound for the stated lattice, not a theorem about its growth.
For fixed N=8, these bounds stay around 10^(-15) as w increases from
16 to 32, even though the particular affine errors get much smaller.
This illustrates why the particular target and universal covering questions
must be kept separate.

2. Positive matrix completion of the two pole grids
---------------------------------------------------
The old interlaced determinant had useful half-integer and integer poles,
but its second scalar weight was signed. Define t=y^2 and

    b(y)=-(y^4/6) d^3/dy^3 csch(pi y),
    c(y)=-(y^3/6) d^3/dy^3 sech(pi y),

and let B,C be integration against b,c on y>0. We have b>0 and, with
h=tanh(pi y),

    c/b = h^5(6h^2-5) / [y(6-5h^2)].                            (7)

Since |6h^2-5|<=6-5h^2 and h<=min(pi y,1), |c/b|/y<pi^2<10.
Thus, for any rational m>=10,

    W(y) = [[b(y), c(y)], [c(y), m^2 y^2 b(y)]]                 (8)

is positive definite. A separate exact polynomial certificate improves
this to m>=2. It uses atanh(h)>=h sum_(k=0)^12 h^(2k)/(2k+1), pi^2<10,
and proves positivity on [0,1] of both polynomials

    (6-5h^2)(sum_(k=0)^12 h^(2k)/(2k+1))^2
                     +/- 5h^3(6h^2-5).

All 3,264 Bernstein coefficients on 32 rational subintervals exceed 1/10.
This establishes |c|<2yb. The elementary m=10 argument needs no such
finite certificate.

2.1 The pole identities

Put F_4(a)=sum_(k>=0)(-1)^k/(a+k)^4. Integration by parts three times,
followed by the elementary sine/cosine transforms of csch and sech, gives

    B(1/(t+a^2)) = a^3 F_4(a) - 1/(2a),
    C(1/(t+a^2)) = a^2 F_4(a+1/2).                              (9)

At a half-integer, the first expression is rational+beta(4), and the
second is rational+zeta(4). At an integer the roles reverse. All polynomial
moments are rational; their formulas are in interlaced_determinants.py.

2.2 Killing zeta(4) while preserving positivity

For n>=1, put

    D_h(t)=product_(j=0)^(n-1) (t+(j+1/2)^2),
    D_i(t)=product_(j=1)^n (t+j^2),       D=D_h D_i.

On polynomial vectors f=(f_1,f_2), use the positive form

    <f,g> = B((f_1g_1+m^2 t f_2g_2)/D)
                       + C((f_1g_2+f_2g_1)/D).                (10)

Take components of degree <2n and impose, at the roots of D,

    f_2(-(j+1/2)^2)=0,
    f_1(-j^2)=epsilon_j m j f_2(-j^2),  epsilon_j in {1,-1}.

This is a 2n-dimensional rational subspace. At each half-integer pole,
the unwanted zeta(4) off-diagonal term vanishes. At integer poles, its
diagonal form f_1g_1-m^2 j^2 f_2g_2 vanishes. Every Gram entry therefore
belongs to Q+Q beta(4), and the Gram determinant is strictly positive.

There is a particularly simple basis. For each pole a, let
P_a=D/(t+a^2). Use (P_a,0) at a half-integer pole and
(epsilon_a m a P_a,P_a) at an integer pole. Off-diagonal entries are
rational because P_a P_b/D is a polynomial for a!=b. The beta(4)
coefficient matrix is diagonal, with nonzero entries

    16 a^3 (-1)^j D'(-a^2),           a=j+1/2,
    32 epsilon_a m a^3 (-1)^a D'(-a^2), a integer.

The determinant polynomial has degree exactly 2n. Its primitive integer
multiple is nonzero at beta(4). This proves nonvanishing uniformly, but
says nothing about the magnitude after primitive normalization.

2.3 Arithmetic outcome

All 58 distinct tested configurations fail the shrinking-value test
(62 records include four repetitions). Tests include m=2 and m=10,
constant and alternating signs, dimensions up to 24, and lower polynomial
degree subspaces. For m=10 and the full constant-sign subspace,

    dimension K        log |primitive P(beta(4))| / K^2
          2                         1.38313
          4                         1.75615
          8                         2.21813
         16                         2.49318
         24                         2.58361

The m=2, alternating-sign example at K=24 still gives about +2.51823.
Arb encloses all evaluations, including the signs of the Gram determinants.
Fifteen numerical integral comparisons check (9) and sample Gram entries;
16 exact basis comparisons and three determinant-polynomial comparisons
check the algebra by different calculations. These do not constitute an
independent audit or a general asymptotic impossibility theorem.

There are additional rational maximal isotropic subspaces for the unwanted
zeta(4) coefficient form; the nodewise choice above does not exhaust them.
A search over such subspaces is a genuine remaining freedom, but these
poor first arithmetic results give it lower priority than block arithmetic.

3. A gamma-function filter as an arithmetic design target
--------------------------------------------------------
Define, near s=0,

    H(s)=Gamma(1/4+s) Gamma(1/4-s) Gamma(3/4)^2
          /[Gamma(3/4+s) Gamma(3/4-s) Gamma(1/4)^2].

The polygamma expansion gives the convergent identity

    log H(s)=sum_(m>=1) [16^m/m] beta(2m) s^(2m)
            =16G s^2+128 beta(4)s^4+O(s^6).

Consequently the fixed multiplicative filter

    K(s)=H(2s)/H(s)^4 = 1+1536 beta(4)s^4+O(s^6)               (11)

removes both the G term and the decomposable G^2 contribution. This is a
parameter identity, not an irrationality theorem. In particular, applying
it to rational approximants may square their denominators. The fixed
formula does NOT justify ignoring multiplication or normalization costs.

The structural research target is a geometrically integral family of
approximants K_N(s), regular on a fixed circle |s|=r<1/8, with a uniform
error epsilon_N, such that an explicitly proved integer D_N clears
[s^4]K_N(s), D_N epsilon_N ->0, and the extracted error is nonzero.
The Cauchy coefficient estimate contributes only the fixed factor r^(-4).
These conditions include the actual denominators AFTER applying the filter.

Bloch--Vlasenko [BV] explain gamma quotients as hypergeometric Frobenius
connection constants. Their Example 23 and Proposition 26 supply a natural
setting for asking this question. They do not supply the required fast,
arithmetically inexpensive approximants to (11).

The simplest implementation is definitely unhelpful: replace H by the
finite product

    H_N(s)=product_(k=0)^(N-1)
        [1-s^2/(k+3/4)^2]/[1-s^2/(k+1/4)^2].

Then [s^4](H_N(2s)/H_N(s)^4) is exactly 1536 times the ordinary beta(4)
series truncated after N positive-negative pairs. There is no new decay
or denominator saving. A useful realization must exploit additional
geometry or transformations, with parameter-uniform arithmetic control.

Kerr's geometric framework [K] is another way to formulate an arithmetic-
first search. His Theorem 3.2 has strong geometric and integrality hypotheses
and a critical-value inequality; it does not assert irrationality for an
arbitrary four-variable period. His extension in Proposition 3.3 suggests
looking over Q(i). One must identify a calibrated REAL regulator equal
to a rational multiple of beta(4): nonrationality of i beta(4), merely
because it is imaginary, would say nothing about our target. No explicit
Laurent polynomial meeting all the needed conditions has been found here.

4. Shortcuts screened out in this pass
--------------------------------------
Fixed-divisor bonuses. If P is a primitive integer polynomial of degree d,
gcd{P(m):m in Z} divides d!. Indeed, finite differences express P in the
binomial basis; if every value is divisible by M, every coefficient of
d!P is divisible by M. Primitivity then gives M|d!. Thus a universal
fixed-divisor gain is only exp(O(d log d)). At degree O(N), it cannot
repair a positive exp(cN^2) determinant gap. This does not exclude
adaptively chosen rationality-dependent constructions or other degrees.

Unrelated p-adic limits. Rationality of the real number beta(4) does not
identify it with a p-adic L-value. A product-formula argument needs a
proved common sequence of rational/algebraic objects and its local limits.
Frobenius congruences could control coefficients in a construction such as
(11); they cannot simply be asserted for the real period itself.

Fast polylogarithmic ladders. Broadhurst [B], equations (54)--(55), gives
fast combinations involving beta(4), pi^3 log 2, and pi log^3 2. Those
formulas do not isolate a pure beta(4) BBP-type expansion with an adequate
irrationality estimate. Fast computability is not the missing arithmetic
argument. This is a statement about the displayed family, not a claim
that every possible ladder has been excluded.
