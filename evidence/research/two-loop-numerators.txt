Coupled ladder numerators, a circle coefficient, and their arithmetic cost
28 October 2026. Local research record; unpublished.

Status
======
There is no irrationality proof here. The new construction does give
positive integral linear forms in 1 and B=beta(4), with a proved exponential
rate for the resulting rational approximations. It escapes the particular
positive-weight obstruction in ladder-integrals.txt. Clearing denominators
still destroys the required smallness in the tested examples.

The useful structural results are a coupled four-cube integral, exact
five-period bivariate moments, a circle formula for the B coefficient,
and a positive disk formula for the Catalan coefficient after multiplication
by a polynomial T. Two Euler derivative identities give auxiliary Gram
matrices with integer inverses. Full two-variable bases can also make the
B coefficient rank much smaller than the matrix dimension.

The arithmetic test is unfavorable. In a 24-by-24 projected matrix the
fourth denominator layer has full rank modulo 11 and 13. Consequently
the primitive polynomial's leading coefficient contains 11^96 and 13^96.
This is an exact finite certificate, not an asymptotic impossibility theorem.

There are 217 recorded primitive evaluations, including repeated or
overlapping cases. Of these, 214 have absolute value greater than one.
The three exceptions are X twice and 2X-1 once; evaluated at B, these are
B and 0.977889..., respectively. No tested family exhibits the needed
decay of nonzero integer forms. All checks are same-agent work, not an
independent mathematical audit or formal verification. No priority claim
is made for these identities or constructions.

1. Source and normalization
===========================
The input is the classical two-loop ladder evaluation, presented in the
normalization of F. Loebbert and S. Stawinski, arXiv:2408.15331v1 (2024),
equations (2.3), (2.5), (2.8)-(2.9), and (2.73):
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15331v1
  literature/papers/2408.15331v1.pdf

Their reference [8] credits Usyukina and Davydychev's 1993 ladder formulas.
The underlying beta-value evaluation is therefore an existing input.
The source was read selectively; no full review or new literature survey
is claimed. The previous pass recorded its PDF, hash, and reading scope.

For u=z*zbar and v=(1-z)(1-zbar), write

 F(u,v) = [6(Li4(z)-Li4(zbar))
           -3 log(u)(Li3(z)-Li3(zbar))
           +(log(u)^2/2)(Li2(z)-Li2(zbar))]/(z-zbar).

At u=1,v=2, take z=i,zbar=-i; then F(1,2)=6B.
To check the integral normalization, set the external points in (2.5)
to a,0,b,infinity, with |b|=1, |a|^2=u and |a-b|^2=v. For two loops
and unit powers the prefactor is 1/(x14^2*x23^2*x24^2). Multiplication
by the fourth power of the point tending to infinity leaves

 F(u,v) = integral_R4 integral_R4
   d^4y d^4z / [pi^4 y^2(y-a)^2(y-z)^2 z^2(z-b)^2].

This formula initially uses Euclidean kinematics near (u,v)=(1,2).
Only its analytic germ there is needed below.

2. A positive four-cube integral
================================
Put Schwinger parameters alpha1,alpha2,e,alpha4,alpha5 on the five
propagators in the displayed order. Let

 A=alpha1+alpha2+e,  C=alpha4+alpha5+e,  U=AC-e^2,
 F_S=(u alpha2+alpha5)U-u C alpha2^2-A alpha5^2
       -e alpha2 alpha5(u+1-v).

Gaussian integration and projectivization give the integrand 1/(U F_S).
Use the projective gauge alpha1+alpha2+alpha4+alpha5=1 and substitute

 alpha1=r(1-x), alpha2=rx,
 alpha4=(1-r)(1-y), alpha5=(1-r)y,
 e=t r(1-r)/(1-t).

The projective Jacobian is r(1-r), de/dt=r(1-r)/(1-t)^2, and
U=r(1-r)/(1-t). Thus the Jacobians cancel U^2, giving

 F(u,v)=integral_[0,1]^4 dr dx dy dt / D_(u,v),

 D_(u,v)=u r x(1-x)+(1-r)y(1-y)
          +t r(1-r)[u x^2+y^2-(u+1-v)xy].                 (1)

At (u,v)=(1,2), abbreviate this denominator to D and define

 Nu=r x(1-x)+t r(1-r)x(x-y),
 Nv=t r(1-r)xy,
 N0=(1-r)y[1-(1-tr)y-trx].

Then D=N0+Nu+2Nv. All three numerators are nonnegative: for example
Nu=rx[1-(1-t(1-r))x-t(1-r)y], and the two coefficients subtracted
from 1 are nonnegative and sum to 1. Set

 X=Nu/D, Y=Nv/D, dnu=dr dx dy dt/D.

The measure is positive, has total mass 6B, and satisfies
X>=0, Y>=0, X+2Y<=1. Its moments obey

 F(1+s,2+t)=sum_ij (-1)^(i+j) binom(i+j,i) mu(X^i Y^j) s^i t^j,

where mu(P)=integral P dnu. The symbol t in this Taylor formula is
an external perturbation, separate from the cube coordinate in (1).

The Schwinger identities and the Jacobians are checked separately with
SymPy. Direct Gauss-Legendre quadrature, after integrating the cube's
t coordinate analytically, also converges towards 6B. This numerical
convergence is diagnostic only, not a certified proof of the evaluation.

3. Symmetric coordinates and the five periods
============================================
Use

 Z=2(X+Y)-1, W=2Y, T=(1-Z^2-W^2)/4.

The physical support is contained in W>=0, |Z|+W<=1. In particular
T>=0 and T<=1/4. The cube symmetry r -> 1-r, x <-> y sends Z to -Z
and fixes W, so all odd Z moments vanish. The image contains an open
set: at r=x=y=t=1/2 the (x,y) Jacobian of (Z,W) is 32/125.

Let C(z,w)=mu(1/(1-zZ-wW)). Substitution in the source formula gives
the following analytic germ at (z,w)=(0,0):

 rho=-atanh(z),
 delta=-asin(w/sqrt(1-z^2)),
 K=(1-z^2-w^2)^(-1/2), h=rho+i delta,

 C=K[6B-(rho^2+3delta^2)G-(9/16)delta*zeta(3)
       +(delta/2)(rho^2+delta^2)log(2)+R(rho,delta)],       (2)

where G=beta(2). The Taylor coefficients of R are rational. Explicitly,

 H_k=6h^k/k! -6rho*h^(k-1)/(k-1)!
                       +2rho^2*h^(k-2)/(k-2)!,
 R=sum_(k>=4) Im[H_k Li_(4-k)(i)].                       (3)

Every nonpositive polylogarithm in (3) is a rational function at i,
hence a Gaussian rational. The four preceding terms follow from
Im Li4(i)=B, Re Li3(i)=-3 zeta(3)/32, Im Li2(i)=G and
Re Li1(i)=-log(2)/2. The potential pi contributions cancel algebraically.

For polynomials even in Z and W, (2) gives a decomposition

                 mu(P)=r(P)+a(P)B+g(P)G,                (4)

with r,a,g rational linear functionals. These are the coefficients
specified by (2)-(3); their definition does NOT assume linear independence
of the constants. The zeta(3) and log(2) germs are odd in w and therefore
drop out. For the full polynomial space the five periods are
1, B, G, zeta(3), log(2).

The W-only edge is exactly the previous hyperbolic family:
mu(W^(2k))=6I_k with I_k as in ladder-integrals.txt. The mixed Z,W
weights extend it; the entire construction should not be described as
unrelated to the previous family.

4. The circle and the Catalan disk identity
==========================================
The B generating function is 6/sqrt(1-z^2-w^2), so

 a(P)=6/(2pi) integral_0^(2pi) P(cos theta,sin theta) dtheta.  (5)

In particular a(TP)=0 for every polynomial P. For nonnegative integers
i,j define

 c_ij=(2i)!(2j)!/[4^(i+j) i! j! (i+j)!].

Then a(Z^(2i)W^(2j))=6c_ij. These numbers are dyadic: expand the
circle coordinates as Laurent polynomials in exp(i theta).

There is also an exact positive formula for the Catalan functional:

 g(TP)=h(P):=(1/pi) integral_(xi^2+eta^2<=1) integral_0^1
                          P(xi,t eta) dt dxi deta.       (6)

Consequently

 h(Z^(2i)W^(2j))=c_ij/[(i+j+1)(2j+1)],                  (7)

and odd moments are zero. Equivalently, h has density
pi^(-1) log(sqrt(1-Z^2)/|W|) on the unit disk, with the density
understood almost everywhere. Its total mass is one.

Here is a proof of (6), not just recognition of numerical moments.
Homogenize the G germ to

 Gcal(a,z,w)=-[atanh(z/a)^2
              +3asin(w/sqrt(a^2-z^2))^2]
                         /sqrt(a^2-z^2-w^2).

Direct differentiation yields

 (partial_a^2-partial_z^2-partial_w^2)Gcal
       =8/[(a^2-z^2)sqrt(a^2-z^2-w^2)].                  (8)

The left side equals 8g(T/(a-zZ-wW)^3). On the other hand, the
normalized uniform disk integral of (a-z xi-w t eta)^(-3) is
(a^2-z^2-t^2 w^2)^(-3/2). Integrating t from zero to one produces
the right side of (8), divided by 8. Comparing Taylor coefficients
proves (6). The differentiation in (8) was also checked with SymPy.

5. Positive rational approximations with exponential convergence
================================================================
For i+j>0, the coefficients of the negative of the G germ in (2)
are positive at all even bidegrees. Thus g(Z^(2i)W^(2j))<0. Let

 P_ij=Z^(2i)W^(2j),
 kappa_ij=-g(P_ij)/h(P_ij)>0,
 L_ij=mu(P_ij(1+kappa_ij T)).

Equations (4)-(7) cancel Catalan exactly and give

       L_ij=6c_ij B+b_ij>0,  b_ij in Q.                 (9)

Strict positivity follows from the open image of the cube map and the
nonzero nonnegative polynomial weight. Thus these forms have a uniform
nonvanishing proof, without any assumption about B.

For i=j=n write c_n=binom(2n,n)/16^n, kappa_n=kappa_nn, and
q_n=-b_nn/(6c_n). Since |ZW|<=1/4 on the physical diamond,

  0<B-q_n <= B(1+kappa_n/4)16^(-n)/c_n.                 (10)

For completeness, kappa_n grows at most polynomially. Evaluate the
positive-coefficient G germ at z=w=sqrt(n/(2n+1)). Its numerator is
at most 9 and its denominator is (2n+1)^(-1/2). Coefficientwise
positivity and binom(4n,2n)>=16^n/(4n+1) imply

 -g(P_nn) <=9e(4n+1)sqrt(2n+1)/4^n.

By (7), h(P_nn)=c_n/(2n+1)^2, while
c_n>=1/[4^n(2n+1)]. Hence

 kappa_n <=9e(4n+1)(2n+1)^(7/2),
 0<B-q_n <=(2n+1)(1+kappa_n/4)4^(-n)
                         =O(n^(11/2)4^(-n)).            (11)

These bounds are deliberately loose but prove exponential convergence.
They also explain why the old positive-weight obstruction does not
extend to this whole two-variable family: the physical diamond and
the coefficient circle are different supports. No claim is made that
the approximations or this proof are new to the literature.

The arithmetic failure is large. For n=1 the primitive form is
18B-13. For n=20 the approximation error is about 1.71931e-12,
but its reduced numerator and denominator each have 95 digits. The
corresponding primitive integer form is about 3.38997e82. Thus accurate
rational approximation is emphatically not the required small integer
linear form. The table tests n=1,...,20 and 56 off-diagonal choices.

6. Derivative companions and determinants
=========================================
Define E_W=1+W partial_W and E_R=1+(Z partial_Z+W partial_W)/2.
The scaled-coordinate integral in (6) gives, by the fundamental theorem
of calculus,

 g(T E_W P)=d(P),  normalized uniform disk average of P,
 g(T E_W E_R P)=c(P), normalized circle average of P.      (12)

For the second identity integrate radially, or check homogeneous
polynomials. Both Euler operators commute. These are identities of
polynomial functionals, with no asymptotic or numerical qualification.

Let C_k=Re(Z+iW)^(2k). On the disk their Gram matrix is diagonal:
d(C_0^2)=1 and d(C_k^2)=1/[2(2k+1)] for k>0. Its inverse is the
integer diagonal matrix diag(1,6,10,14,...). On the circle the inverse
is diag(1,2,2,...). This removes the large extra primes introduced by
inverting the logarithm-weighted disk Gram in the first prototype.

For a chosen basis define the main matrix

 M_ij=mu(P_i P_j)=A_ij B+D_ij G+R_ij.

The auxiliary matrix from either operator in (12) is H_ij G+U_ij,
with no B coefficient. Consequently

           M-D H^(-1)(H G+U)=A B+R-D H^(-1)U             (13)

contains only B and rationals. Its determinant supplies a polynomial
in B. This equality includes the full arithmetic cost; division by H
is not being ignored. The projected entries are not in general a
positive Gram matrix. Uniform nonvanishing for these determinant
families remains unproved; each recorded nonzero value is checked by Arb.

For a genuinely two-variable basis use the integer Zernike polynomials

 Q_kl=Re(Z+iW)^(2l) * sum_(s=0)^k
       (-1)^s (2k+2l-s)!/[s!(k+2l-s)!(k-s)!]
                            * (Z^2+W^2)^(k-s).

Their disk Gram inverse is diagonal with entry 2(k+l)+1 for l=0,
and 2[2(k+l)+1] for l>0. On the circle Q_kl=C_l, so the basis k+l<=d
has matrix size (d+1)(d+2)/2 but B rank d+1. Rectangular index sets
can retain B rank two while the matrix dimension grows. The circle
auxiliary Gram itself is singular on these full bases; the disk
operator is used for them.

Tests include 54 original T-companion matrices; 66 harmonic disk/circle
companion matrices through size 24; and 21 Zernike configurations through
size 45 and B degree nine, plus rectangular variants. All their primitive
values exceed one, apart from the two trivial X cases already counted.

One useful coefficient check is
g(C_k)=2/k * sum_(j=0)^(k-1) 1/(2j+1), k>=1.
It follows by restricting (2) to the holomorphic line (z,w)=(t,it):
K=1, delta=i rho, and the G germ becomes 2 atanh(t)^2.

7. Why coefficient-circle geometry does not settle the arithmetic
==================================================================
The identity a(TP)=0 might suggest that multiplication by T also removes
the highest denominator terms. Exact counterexamples disprove that
unqualified assertion. For each p=11,13,17,19,23,29, set

             P=Z^(p-1)W^(3p-1).

The saved rational coefficients satisfy

 a(TP)=0,   v_p(g(TP))=-2,   v_p(r(TP))=-4.              (14)

These are six exact examples, not a proved all-prime congruence. Here
E_W E_R P=6p^2 P, so the circle companion lowers the rational valuation
from -4 to -2. However, multiplication by D in (13) can restore the
fourth-power loss: D itself has second-power denominators.

This is visible in the size-24, offset-zero harmonic circle companion.
Its B matrix is A=diag(6,3,...,3). Let V=R-D H^(-1)U. The exact data are

 p    worst pole order of V    rank(p^4 V mod p)   v_p(primitive lead)
 11             4                      24                   96
 13             4                      24                   96
 17             4                      23                   95
 29             4                      18                   72

For p=29 the raw R only has pole order three; the projection introduces
fourth-order entries. For p=11,13 the rank statement proves the exponent
96 without relying on numerical determinant evaluation. Indeed, if
p^4 V is integral and invertible modulo p while A is a p-adic unit
matrix, det(V) has valuation -4n, all coefficients of det(xA+V) have
valuation at least -4n, and its leading coefficient is a unit. Clearing
and taking the primitive polynomial therefore leaves valuation 4n in
the leading coefficient. This argument is conditional in general and
fully instantiated by the saved matrices here.

There are 27 recorded matrix-prime checks. They do not establish a
uniform barrier for all numerator choices. They do show why the tested
cheap-inverse projection has not solved the arithmetic problem.

8. Checks, scope, and reproduction
==================================
The previous manifest's 411 recorded files matched their hashes before
old research records were edited. The baseline is saved in
results/two_loop_integrity_baseline.json. No source paper was modified,
and no new PDF was added in this pass.

Exact coefficient checks cover 153 coordinate transformations, 3,486
circle coefficients, 3,486 parity instances, 3,321 scaled-disk moments,
42 old hyperbolic edge moments, and 30 harmonic Catalan coefficients.
The separate symbolic script checks the Schwinger substitutions, support
Jacobian, and wave identity (8). Three direct polylog/Taylor comparisons
and five quadrature orders are explicitly numerical diagnostics.

The primitive determinants are formed over the rationals, cleared of
denominators, and divided by their integer coefficient gcd. Evaluations
use Arb balls at precision determined by coefficient size, and exclude
zero in every saved case. These are checks of finite instances, not
proofs of asymptotic decay, novelty, or a general nonvanishing theorem.

9. Consequence for the next attempt
===================================
The present formulas supply an analytic construction with exact lower-
period cancellation and uniform scalar nonvanishing. Improving its real
decay alone is not the most promising next step. The tested projection
can multiply two second-order denominator layers into a fourth-order
layer of full rank. An effective successor must prevent that multiplication
arithmetically, rather than just invert the auxiliary Gram more cheaply.

The concrete target is a numerator or coupled period construction whose
Catalan elimination preserves prescribed congruences at the bulk primes.
The circle and disk identities give exact functionals on which to impose
those congruences, but no uniform, low-height source construction with
the required properties has been obtained. Arbitrary prime-by-prime
interpolation would merely move the cost elsewhere. The existing coupled
holonomy and lattice approaches also retain their previously documented
gaps; nothing in this pass repairs them.

This is progress in constructing and diagnosing a different family,
not evidence that an irrationality proof is close.
