Beta(4): cyclotomic polynomial integrals and a prime-denominator obstruction
27 October 2026. Local research record; unpublished.

There is still no irrationality proof for beta(4).

This investigation constructs positive four-dimensional rational periods
whose only nonrational term is a rational multiple of beta(4). Their
coefficients are explicit, including a factorial formula for a broader
polynomial family. All 222 recorded finite evaluations of primitive integer
linear forms exceed one. More substantially, a two-parameter subfamily has
a proved obstruction: its primitive integer linear forms grow exponentially
as either parameter increases. This is a theorem about those linear forms,
not about all cyclotomic periods or determinants made from them.

The tractable constructions reduce to differentiated rational sums. They
therefore do not provide the change of arithmetic mechanism we sought.
Ordinary mathematical derivations and exact computations are supplied;
no independent audit, formal verification, or priority claim is made.


1. Source check and what was actually transferred

Tosi's thesis [T] supplies a framework for expressing periods of suitable
hyperplane arrangements in multiple polylogarithms at roots of unity.
Theorem 3.10 bounds the period space, not coefficient denominators. Its
worked beta-value example in Section 3.2.1 recovers Zudilin's existing
hypergeometric integrals. Tosi's separate paper [T2] treats ordinary
multiple zeta values; the four-dimensional member in its introduction is
3*pi^4/40, not a beta(4) approximation.

Eskandari--Murty--Nemoto [E], especially Section 8, illustrates how symmetry
and a constant-term computation isolate a Catalan period and determine
its coefficient. It does not supply a beta(4) version. The construction
below uses an elementary constant-term calculation of its own. No motivic
projection on numerical periods is assumed.

Selberg's integral, in the form recorded in [S, (1.1)], evaluates the
two-variable moments in Section 3. The factorial prefactor in that
evaluation is a common scalar; by itself it cannot improve primitive
integer linear forms.


2. An elementary period-isolation identity

For a polynomial P with rational coefficients define

  J(P) = integral_[0,1]^2
         P(u,v) log^2(uv) / (sqrt(uv)(1+uv)) du dv.              (1)

The integral is absolutely convergent. Put

  T_s(m) = sum_{0<=k<m} (-1)^k/(2k+1)^s.

For a monomial u^i v^j, expanding 1/(1+uv) gives the alternating
sum of second derivatives, at nonnegative integers t, of

                  1/((t+i+1/2)(t+j+1/2)).

Interchanging sum and integral is justified even with absolute values:
the logarithmic factor makes the corresponding sum O(sum (k+1)^-4).
If i=j the answer is

  J(u^i v^i) = 96 (-1)^i [beta(4)-T_4(i)].                    (2)

If i!=j it is

  16/(j-i) { (-1)^i [beta(3)-T_3(i)]
                   -(-1)^j [beta(3)-T_3(j)] }.                (3)

Indeed, the second derivative of 1/((t+x)(t+y)) is
2((t+x)^-3-(t+y)^-3)/(y-x), and on the diagonal it is
6(t+x)^-4. The factors 16 and 96 include the half-integer scaling.

Consequently, if P(-u,-v)=P(u,v), each monomial has i+j even and
the beta(3) term in (3) vanishes. Thus

  J(P) = A(P) beta(4) + B(P),
  A(P) = 96 sum_i (-1)^i [u^i v^i]P
       = 96 CT_z P(z,-1/z),                                  (4)

with B(P) rational and explicitly given by (2)-(3). Here CT denotes
the constant Laurent coefficient. This proves isolation of beta(4)
without cancelling a separately constructed Catalan coefficient.

If P is integral and its exponents in each variable are at most M, then

                  A(P) in Z,       L_(2M)^4 B(P) in Z,        (5)

where L_n=lcm(1,...,n), L_0=1. In (3), |j-i| divides L_(2M)
and the cubed odd denominators divide L_(2M)^3; (2) uses fourth
powers. This is a uniform upper bound, not an optimal denominator claim.

Positivity and nonvanishing of J(P) follow whenever P is nonnegative
and not identically zero on the square. For arbitrary invariant P,
A(P) can vanish: invariance alone does not guarantee a useful beta(4)
coefficient. The explicit families below have nonzero A.

2.1 A genuine rational fourfold integral, but a restricted pole geometry

The logarithms in (1) can be replaced by algebraic integrations. With
u=x^2, v=y^2, U=xy, V=y, and two auxiliary variables S,T, one obtains

  J(P) = 32 integral_D
       P(U^2/V^2,V^2) / ((1+U^2) V S T) dU dV dS dT,

  D = { 0<U<V<1, U<S<T<1 }.                                  (6)

For example, integrating S,T produces (log U)^2/2; the Jacobian
and the square substitutions give the factor 32. The domain is a
union of three ordered simplices, according to the position of V
relative to S,T. The poles are coordinate hyperplanes and U=+i,-i.
This locates the construction explicitly in a level-four cyclotomic
setting. It also exposes its limitation: there is only one nontrivial
pole variable. Integrating the other variables returns the rational
sums (2)-(3), not a new multivariable divisibility principle.


3. A Selberg family and its exact arithmetic

For nonnegative integers a,b,c put

  J_(a,b,c) = J((uv)^a (1-u)^b (1-v)^b (u-v)^(2c)).

This is positive. Let N=b+c+1, M=b+2c, and define

  R_(a,b,c)(t) = ((N-1)!)^2 /
         ((t+a+1/2)_N (t+a+c+1/2)_N),                         (7)

where (x)_N is the rising factorial. The n=2 Selberg formula gives

  I_(a,b,c) := (b+c)! c!/(b! (2c)!) J_(a,b,c)
             = sum_{k>=0} (-1)^k R_(a,b,c)''(k).              (8)

For clarity, the unnormalized moment before differentiating has
numerator b! (b+c)! (2c)!/c!, rather than ((b+c)!)^2. The ratio
in (8) accounts for this difference.

If b is even, reflection of the pole set cancels the order-three
period. Specifically, write

  R(t)=sum_{j=0}^M [r_(j,2)/(t+a+j+1/2)^2
                         +r_(j,1)/(t+a+j+1/2)].

The substitution t -> -(2a+1+M)-t fixes R, so r_(M-j,1)=-r_(j,1).
Since M is even, sum_j (-1)^j r_(j,1)=0. Differentiating twice
therefore gives

  I_(a,b,c)=A_(a,b,c) beta(4)+B_(a,b,c),
  A_(a,b,c)=96(-1)^(a+b/2) binom(b+c,b/2).                    (9)

One way to evaluate A is to note that, on the overlap c<=j<=b+c,

  r_(j,2)=(-1)^c binom(b+c,j) binom(b+c,j-c).

Its alternating sum is a coefficient of (1-z)^(b+c)(1+z)^(b+c).

The normalized partial fractions of (N-1)!/(x)_N have integer
numerators. Multiplying two such expressions proves

      r_(j,2) in Z,       L_M r_(j,1) in Z.

Equations (2)-(3) then give

                  L_(2(a+M))^4 B_(a,b,c) in Z.                (10)

The exact primitive forms in the experiments remove the full common
factor, rather than treating (10) as the actual denominator.


4. A proved obstruction for the entire b=0 subfamily

The statement concerns single linear forms. It does not rule out new
linear combinations or determinants constructed from this subfamily.

Theorem. Set b=0. For each a,c>=0, multiply I_(a,0,c) by the unique
positive rational number kappa_(a,c) for which its two integer
coefficients have greatest common divisor one. Then, uniformly as
a+c tends to infinity,

  log(kappa_(a,c) I_(a,0,c))
       >= (16/3-log 4) a + (8/3+2 log(3/8)) c + o(a+c).

The two displayed coefficients are approximately 3.9470389722 and
0.7050081606. In particular these positive primitive integer linear
forms tend to infinity exponentially. No choice of growing a,c in
this subfamily can give shrinking integer linear forms.

4.1 The surviving fourth powers

Write C=binom(2c,c), and B=B_(a,0,c). By (9), A=96(-1)^a.
The unique double pole is at j=c. The simple-pole coefficients are

  r_(j,1)=(-1)^(j+1) binom(2c,j)/((j-c) C)       (j!=c),
  r_(c,1)=0,             r_(c,2)=(-1)^c.

Thus

  B = -96(-1)^a T_4(a+c)
      +16(-1)^a/C sum_{0<=j<=2c, j!=c}
                    binom(2c,j) T_3(a+j)/(j-c).              (11)

Claim. For every prime p>=5 with

                2(a+2c)/3 < p < 2(a+c),                     (12)

one has v_p(B)=-4. In particular p^4 survives in the primitive
denominator. This is an exact assertion, not an average-prime heuristic.

Proof. The tail T_4(a+c) contains the denominator p exactly once;
3p>2(a+2c) excludes higher odd multiples of p in every tail in (11).
The first term in (11) consequently has valuation -4.

If p>2c, C and all j-c are p-units. The second term has valuation
at least -3 and cannot cancel that contribution.

Otherwise, (12) implies c<p<=2c and v_p(C)=1. The factors j-c
remain p-units. Put d=2c-p and h=(p+1)/2-a. The inequalities
in (12) imply d<h<=c. Only indices j>=h have a p term in T_3(a+j).
Modulo p, binom(2c,j) vanishes except when j<=d or j>=p. Since
h>d, the possible contribution of valuation -4 from the second
term is controlled by

  sum_{j=h,j!=c}^{2c} binom(2c,j)/(j-c)
       = sum_{l=0}^d binom(d,l)/(l+p-c)           (mod p).     (13)

The equality uses Lucas' congruence. Pair l with d-l. Their
binomial coefficients agree, and their denominators are negatives
modulo p because d=2c-p. The denominators are nonzero. Hence (13)
vanishes modulo p. After division by C, the p^-3 tail contribution
still has valuation at least -3; all other tail terms have valuation
at least -1. The first term's valuation -4 therefore survives.
This also covers the endpoints c=0 through the case p>2c. QED.

Because A=96(-1)^a is a p-unit for every p>=5, primitivity forces
v_p(kappa)=4 at the primes in (12). Negative valuations of kappa
can occur only at divisors of 96, and their total contribution is
bounded by 1/96. The prime number theorem now gives

  log kappa >= 4[theta(2(a+c))-theta(2(a+2c)/3)]-O(log(a+c+2))
            = (16/3)a+(8/3)c+o(a+c).                         (14)

Changing strict endpoints affects only O(log(a+c+2)). Both
endpoints are comparable to a+c, so the error is uniform in the
ratio of a and c.

4.2 The analytic decay is too slow

The factor 1/(1+uv) lies strictly between 1/2 and 1 on the
interior of the square. The positive Mellin representation gives

             R''(0)/2 < I_(a,0,c) < R''(0).                  (15)

Moreover,

  R(0)=(c!)^2 Gamma(a+1/2)/
                   ((a+c+1/2) Gamma(a+2c+3/2)).

The logarithm of R''(0)/R(0) is O(log(a+c+2)): the logarithmic
derivatives are positive sums of reciprocal first and second
powers of the pole distances, bounded above and below by
polynomial factors. Stirling's formula, including a=0 or c=0
by direct treatment of the fixed gamma factors, therefore yields

  log I = E(a,c)+O(log(a+c+2)),
  E(a,c)=2c log c+a log a-(a+2c) log(a+2c),                   (16)

with 0 log 0=0. For a=0 this is -c log 4+O(log(c+2)).

The elementary log-sum inequality, with weights 1/4 and 3/4,
implies

            E(a,c) >= -a log 4+2c log(3/8).                 (17)

Adding (14) and (17) proves the theorem. Notice that the proof
only uses one interval of primes. It does not need an exact
formula for the whole primitive denominator.


5. Broader polynomial weights: a closed coefficient, but no successful forms

To leave the single Selberg product, also consider

  P_(a,b,c,h)=(uv)^a(1-uv)^h(1-u^2)^b(1-v^2)^b(u-v)^(2c),
                                                       a,b,c,h>=0. (18)

These polynomials are nonnegative and invariant under (u,v)->(-u,-v).
By (4), J(P)=A beta(4)+B, and a constant-term evaluation gives

  A=96(-1)^a 2^h (2b)!(2c)!/(b!c!(b+c)!).                   (19)

For a direct proof put z=exp(i theta) in the constant-term integral.
After substituting (u,v)=(z,-1/z), the factors are

  (-1)^a 2^h 4^(b+c) sin^(2b)(theta) cos^(2c)(theta).

The elementary beta integral evaluates their average to (19).
The factorial ratio is also an integer, since it is a Laurent
coefficient of an integral polynomial. It is the familiar super
Catalan ratio; no new factorial identity is claimed. Formula (19)
in particular proves A!=0 for every member of (18).

This is a uniform elimination of unwanted periods, with no division
by a large Catalan coefficient. But it is not a proof of an extra
common factor in A and B. Exchanging b and c preserves (19), while
generally changing B; that coefficient symmetry does not establish
equality of the full integrals or a Rhin--Viola denominator gain.

For example, with a=h=0, the pairs (b,c)=(0,1) and (1,0) give
respectively

       192 beta(4)-4768/27,       192 beta(4)-992/9.

Their equal leading coefficients plainly do not make them the same form.


6. Exact tests and their interpretation

First family: 132 distinct parameter triples from
  a in {0,n,2n}, b in {0,2n,4n}, c in {0,n,2n},
  n in {1,2,4,8,16,32,64}, with b+c>0 and repeated triples removed.
Second family: 90 evaluations, comprising 15 specified parameter rays
at n=1,2,4,8,16,32. The exact rays are in the script and result file.
These are 222 evaluations across two families, not a claim of 222
inequivalent constructions. None has a primitive form below one.

Every case uses the exact rational coefficients, clears their least
common denominator, and removes their greatest common integer factor.
Arb then encloses the resulting primitive value. The precision is
increased until nonvanishing and at least 120 relative accuracy bits
are certified. All recorded intervals are positive and above one.

A representative first-family case is a=b=0,c=64. Its rational
approximation error is about 10^-40.265, but its primitive leading
coefficient has 373 digits and the positive integer form has size
about 10^331.883. Removing all actual common factors does not rescue it.

The broader case (a,b,c,h)=(32,32,32,32) has approximation error
about 10^-87.782, a 643-digit primitive leading coefficient, and
integer-form size about 10^554.733. The theorem of Section 4 is
not being asserted for this broader family; these are finite data.

Checks reproduced:
  * 23,664 partial-coefficient integrality checks;
  * 222 common-denominator and leading-coefficient checks;
  * 132 positive-integral enclosures (15);
  * 1,997 exact fourth-power prime-valuation checks for 0<=a<=16,
    0<=c<=24, and 437 instances of the paired congruence (13);
  * 36 coefficient triples compared between polynomial expansion
    and Selberg partial fractions;
  * a separate Fraction/Sympy implementation reproduced those
    36 triples, checked six symbolic Selberg identities and 21
    derivative identities, and checked 441 constant-term identities.

These computations support the derivations; finite prime checks are
not the proof of Section 4. The separate implementation is by the
same agent and is not independent mathematical verification.

7. Assessment

This pass solves a period-isolation problem, supplies exact arithmetic,
and excludes one whole two-parameter approximation family. It supplies
no favorable irrationality margin. The examples obtained from the
geometric viewpoint remain within differentiated rational-sum methods.

Consequently, neither the source framework nor these calculations justify
presenting cyclotomic geometry as a near-proof. More parameter tuning in
these families is a low priority. A genuinely different construction
would have to provide arithmetic information beyond these moment
reductions; merely expressing beta(4) as another rational period will
not do so. No such additional divisibility mechanism was found here.
