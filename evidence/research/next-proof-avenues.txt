Next proof avenues: build the period cancellation and arithmetic together
28 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

Research decision

The highest-priority next attempt is a parameter-dependent arcsine row
construction, with its Taylor contact and its prime-denominator layers
designed together. There is an explicit starting family below. The
missing object is a sequence of rows with a favorable combined real and
arithmetic estimate, not another way to write down beta(4).

This recommendation is a research judgment. The experiments and local
theorems below do not show that this construction exists, nor that a
proof is close. A second, more radical direction is to change the
integral geometry rather than continue differentiating the Catalan
construction. Neither direction is an irrationality theorem.

The companion orthogonal-cancellation.txt records the substantial work
behind this decision: positive variance forms, a dual Hahn determinant
with a uniform nonvanishing proof, and exact failures of their primitive
arithmetic. Here we explain a sharper denominator obstruction in the
older coupled family and an explicit parameter-level input for the
next attempt. All checks are by the same agent; no independent audit,
formal verification, or priority claim is made.

1. The criterion that must actually change

For a determinant strategy it would suffice to construct nonzero
P_N(beta(4)), with P_N in Z[X], degree O(N), and

                   log|P_N(beta(4))| <= -eta N^2         (1)

for some fixed eta>0 and all sufficiently large N (or an unbounded
subsequence). If beta(4)=a/b were rational, b^deg(P_N) P_N(a/b) would
be a nonzero integer, contradicting (1). A linear-form construction can
instead use nonzero integer A_N beta(4)-B_N tending to zero.

The latest families solve two real problems: they remove G=beta(2)
exactly, and some give uniform positivity. But their primitive values
are large. Improved bounds for a redundant common denominator do not
improve a primitive polynomial whose full content was already removed.

The present objective is to change the mechanism that pays for the
fourth powers of primes. The next section makes this much more precise
for one family; it is not a universal obstruction for beta(4).

2. A proved large-prime relation for the coupled family

Use the original EVEN source index n, with the conventions of
half-pole-arithmetic.txt:

  E_n(e)=a_n(e) T(e)/16+b_n(e),
  a_n(e)=a_n+c_n e^2+...,
  b_n(e)=b_n+d_n e^2+...,
  T(e)/16=sum_(r>=1 odd) chi(r)/(r^2-16e^2),
  chi(r)=(-1)^((r-1)/2).

Thus r_n=a_n G+b_n and s_n=16a_n beta(4)+c_n G+d_n.

Proposition. If p is an odd prime with n<p<2n, then, coefficientwise
as a Taylor series at e=0,

       b_n(e)+chi(p)a_n(e)/(p^2-16e^2) in Z_p[[e]].      (2)

In particular a_n,c_n are p-integral, and

  b_n+chi(p)a_n/p^2 in Z_p,
  d_n+chi(p)(16a_n/p^4+c_n/p^2) in Z_p.                 (3)

Proof. The terminating coefficient formula already proved in
half-pole-arithmetic.txt is

  a_n(e)=8 sum_(j=0)^n U_(n,j)
                 product_(k=1)^j(1-4e^2/k^2)^(-1),
  U_(n,j)=binom(n,j)binom(n-1/2,j)binom(n+j-1/2,j).

For p>n, every displayed coefficient is p-integral, and a_n(e) is a
rational function whose denominator is a unit at e=0 and at e=+/-p/4.

Now use the half-pole representation

  F_n(t,e)=n!(2t+n+1)
    product_(l=0)^(n-1)(t-l) product_(l=n+1)^(2n)(t+l)
    /product_(j=0)^n[(t+j+1/2)((t+j+1/2)^2-4e^2)].

Let D_(j,s)(e) be its residue at -j-1/2+2se, with s=-1,0,1.
The rational term is explicitly

  b_n(e)=-sum_(j=0)^n sum_(s=-1)^1 (-1)^j D_(j,s)(e)
                   *sum_(u=0)^(j-1) (-1)^u/(u+1/2-2se).

Before removable powers of e are cancelled, the residue denominators
have only powers of e and the factors h-j, h-j+/-2e, h-j+/-4e, with
0<|h-j|<=n. All nonzero constant terms of these differences are units
at p. A moving tail contributes only odd integers r+/-4e with
1<=r<=2n-1. The only nonunit pair is p+/-4e, each occurring simply.

There is one extra detail for the fixed tails (s=0): a factor 1/p is
possible. It occurs only when j>=(p+1)/2. At the fixed pole x=-j-1/2,
choose l=j+(p+1)/2. Then n+1<=l<=2n, since n<p<2n and j<=n, and

                              x+l=p/2.

This is one of the numerator factors of F_n. Its p divides the fixed
residue numerator and cancels the 1/p from the tail. Thus there is no
remaining constant p denominator from a fixed tail.

It follows that b_n(e) has a representation

                    b_n(e)=N(e)/[(p^2-16e^2)U(e)],
             N,U in Z_p[e], U(0) a p-adic unit.          (2a)

To justify removing the intermediate powers of e integrally, first
take a common denominator e^k(p^2-16e^2)U(e). Since b_n is regular at
e=0, its numerator is divisible by e^k as a polynomial over Q_p.
Division by this monomial preserves Z_p coefficients, proving (2a).

Next, E_n(e) is regular at e=+/-p/4: its quarter-pole sum has zero
terms before t=n/2, and its first possible positive parameter pole is
e=n/2+1/4>p/4. The remaining sum is locally uniformly convergent.
The only pole pair of T(e)/16 at those points is
chi(p)/(p^2-16e^2). Thus the rational function

                  b_n(e)+chi(p)a_n(e)/(p^2-16e^2)

is regular at both points. Write a_n=A/V with A,V in Z_p[e] and V(0)
a unit. In (2a) its combined numerator is NV+chi(p)AU. Regularity at
e=+/-p/4 says this polynomial is divisible by p^2-16e^2 over Q_p.
The divisor's leading coefficient -16 is a p-adic unit, so polynomial
division preserves Z_p coefficients. The remaining denominator UV
has unit constant term. This proves (2), including the integrality
that analytic pole cancellation by itself would not establish.

For every k>=0, if a_(n,l) and b_(n,k) denote the e^(2l) and e^(2k)
coefficients, respectively, the same proof gives

  b_(n,k)+chi(p)sum_(l=0)^k
               16^(k-l)a_(n,l)/p^(2(k-l+1)) in Z_p.     (2b)

2.1 Consequences of the local relation

The scalar elimination satisfies

  a_n d_n-b_n c_n+16chi(p)a_n^2/p^4 in Z_p.              (4)

If a_n is a p-adic unit, (4) implies the EXACT valuation
v_p(a_n d_n-b_n c_n)=-4. This is not a loose clearing bound.
The 19614 exact pairs in the current table satisfy (3) and (4);
19580 have a_n a unit and satisfy this sharp valuation.

More generally, suppose every source index in a block lies strictly
between p/2 and p, and source weights u_n,v_n are p-integral. If

  sum_n(u_n a_n+v_n c_n)=0,
  A=16sum_n v_n a_n,
  B=sum_n(u_n b_n+v_n d_n),

then (3) implies

                         B+chi(p)A/p^4 in Z_p.           (5)

In particular, if A and B are both p-integral, p^4 divides A in Z_p.
For a matrix of such forms M(X)=A X+B, if det(A) is a p-adic unit,
then for every p-integral rational X,

                        v_p(det M(X))=-4r,              (6)

where r is the matrix size. Multiply the matrix by p^4 and reduce
modulo p to see (6). The same conclusion applies to the coupled block
determinant when its Hankel matrix A is invertible modulo p: its Schur
complement has exactly this form, and det(A) itself is a unit.

We tested 141 coefficient Hankel matrices over the admissible prime
windows, at sizes 2,4,8,12,20 and several shifts. Exactly 140 were full
rank. The one deficiency was size 2, source indices 8--12, p=13.
This finite observation is not a uniform theorem on their ranks. It
does show that low rank cannot simply be assumed from the appearance
of the same a_n in two denominator layers.

This local result explains why ordinary coupled Hankel determinants
are a poor next target. It does not rule out
designed rank loss, source weights with controlled p-denominators,
different index geometry, or a different period representation.

3. A concrete parameter family for the next construction

Carreira's weighted arcsine construction [C, Section 6] has moment
weight q(theta)=theta^2-pi theta. Its two triangular auxiliary arrays
already remove the unwanted constants. Repeating that elimination is
not a new proposal. The proposed change is to retain a parameter while
choosing the rows, and derive its arithmetic layers before specializing.

For |a|<1 near zero, put

  dmu_a(t)=|t|/sqrt(1-t^2)
          *cos(a arccos|t|)/cos(pi a/2) dt, -1<t<1.

Its odd moments vanish and its even moments are exactly

  m_(2k)(a)=2(2k+1)!/
                 product_(j=0)^k((2j+1)^2-a^2).         (7)

Indeed, after t=cos(phi), integrate cos^(2k+1)(phi)cos(a phi).
Two integrations by parts give the ratio
(2k)(2k+1)/((2k+1)^2-a^2); the initial integral gives 2/(1-a^2).

For the Cauchy kernel, the starting period is

  integral_(-1)^1 integral_0^1 dmu_a(t) ds/(1-ts)
      =4 B(a),
  B(a)=sum_(r>=1 odd) chi(r)/(r^2-a^2)
      =G+beta(4)a^2+beta(6)a^4+... .                    (8)

For a derivation, the left side is
2/cos(pi a/2) integral_0^(pi/2) cos(a phi)log cot(phi/2)dphi.
Expand log cot(phi/2)=2sum_(r odd)cos(r phi)/r and integrate; the
result is (8). The identity may first be justified with Abel damping
and then by dominated convergence.

At a=0 the measure is the original arcsine-type measure. Its a^2
coefficient is -q(theta)/2 times that measure, with theta=arcsin|t|.
Thus (7)--(8) contain Carreira's weighted moment formula and boundary
value. They repackage known ingredients for the proposed construction;
no claim of a new special-function identity is made.

3.1 All local Taylor layers come from one product

For an odd z=2k+1 define

  J_z(a)=m_(z-1)(a)/m_(z-1)(0)
        =product_(1<=r<=z, r odd)(1-a^2/r^2)^(-1).

Take an odd prime p with p<=z<p^2, and put

  J_red(a)=product_(1<=v<=floor(z/p), v odd)
                                      (1-a^2/v^2)^(-1),
  H_2^*(z;p)=sum_(1<=r<=z, r odd, p not dividing r) 1/r^2.

Separating the multiples of p in the product gives, coefficientwise,

  J_z(pa) = J_red(a)
           product_(r odd<=z, p not dividing r)
                                      (1-p^2 a^2/r^2)^(-1),

  J_z(pa) == J_red(a)                         (mod p^2),
  J_z(pa) == J_red(a)(1+p^2 a^2 H_2^*(z;p))   (mod p^4). (9)

These ARE proved identities and congruences: all v in J_red are less
than p, and the omitted coefficients in each unit factor are divisible
by p^4. The congruences are valid at every Taylor order, not just at a^2.
The script checks coefficients through a^6 at 946 (z,p) pairs, giving
3784 checks for each modulus.

Equation (9) exposes a concrete extra harmonic kernel at the next
layer. The unresolved problem is to propagate these moment reductions
through the Cauchy boundary arrays and choose rows for which the
resulting pole layers have suitably small rank. A moment congruence
alone does not establish that matrix-rank claim.

4. The proposed construction and its first milestones

The source comparison is the OpenAI Catalan determinant [O]: its
Chebyshev rows have integral coefficients, their Taylor contact removes
an unwanted period, and column relations modulo primes save denominator
powers. The weighted beta(4) transfer needs all three effects together.

The proposed work is:

  (a) Derive the Cauchy arrays for mu_a and retain their Taylor data
      through a^2, with exact formal coefficients of every period.
      Use (9) to derive the leading and next local layers of these
      complete entries, including their boundary terms.

  (b) Construct polynomial rows approximating several of the resulting
      functions together (Hermite--Pade contact). Impose auxiliary-period
      cancellation and useful relations in the local layers as part
      of the row construction. Look for a closed Chebyshev, Jacobi,
      or terminating-hypergeometric formula that controls coefficient
      size and works uniformly in N.

  (c) Test exact primitive determinants at increasing sizes. Their
      coefficient matrix must retain sufficiently many beta(4)
      directions, while its bad denominator layers lose rank modulo
      a positive range of primes. A real estimate and nonvanishing
      proof must use these same rows.

The critical new lemma would bound the total local cost AFTER all layers
are accounted for. Saving the p^-4 layer but paying the same amount in
p^-3, p^-2, or p^-1 would not suffice. Likewise a construction that forces
congruences using exponentially larger coefficients may lose the saving
at the real place. The combined inequality (1) is the acceptance test.

This is more specific than asking for better approximations: (7)--(9)
specify the family, parameter, and initial congruences, and (a)--(c)
specify the missing construction. The actual uniformly controlled rows
have NOT yet been found. This remains the main research risk.

5. A more radical second direction: change the integral geometry

Most tested weight-four constructions add two derivatives or two
logarithms to a weight-two system. This naturally introduces the extra
prime powers. A more fundamental escape would make beta(4) the intrinsic
period of a rational four-variable construction whose extra variables
also supply stronger determinant decay.

The earlier product-variable Mellin families do not accomplish this.
Their positive projections often reduce to rank-one endpoint
approximation, and arithmetic-followup.txt proves polynomial error
barriers for the resulting Schur complements and denominator growth
for specified broader families. Simply tensoring that same kernel or
adding another squared polynomial would repeat those failures.

The proposed search is therefore in rational integrals with genuinely
overlapping variables, using the fourth-root-of-unity period geometry
as a guide. Before testing large sizes, a candidate must pass three
symbolic checks: its exact period reduction contains beta(4) alone
apart from rationals; its construction has a nonvanishing mechanism;
and its arithmetic exploits the coupled variables instead of paying
four unrelated least-common-multiple factors. One should not infer
the first condition from a numerical relation or merely from the
presence of i in a denominator.

There is no explicit successful integral of this kind in the present
work. This direction has less immediate structure than Section 4, but
could change the real/arithmetic balance more substantially. It is
worth a bounded conceptual search, not an unrestricted enumeration of
fourfold integrals.

6. What should not be the next large computation

Increasing the sizes of the failed dual Hahn or coupled Hankel families
has little support from the current evidence. Their failures are not
small numerical margins. The faster-decaying hyperbolic kernel in
orthogonal-cancellation.txt gives beta(4)+pi^2 G/24, rather than beta(4)
alone; its better real estimate cannot be imported without that cost.
The quotient metric's qualitative degeneration is already equivalent to
irrationality, so another finite small lattice vector would not supply
the missing theorem. These are reasons to change the construction,
not general impossibility statements about the corresponding methods.

6.1 Rational sampling of the parameter period was also tested

There is an initially attractive alternative to Taylor differentiation:
the period B(a) in (8) has rational values at every nonzero even integer,

  B(2j)=(-1)^j/(2j) sum_(k=0)^(j-1) (-1)^k/(2k+1), j>=1.

Partial fractions into 1/(r-2j) and 1/(r+2j), followed by shifts of the
alternating series, prove this identity. The two beta(1) terms cancel.

To test it, take n<m and

  A(z)=product_(k=1)^n(z-(2k-1)^2)
                      /product_(j=1)^m(z-4j^2),
  b(z)=-sum_(j=1)^m Res_(z=4j^2)A(z) *B(2j)/(z-4j^2).

Then E(z)=A(z)B(sqrt(z))+b(z) has no even-integer parameter poles, and

  E(z)=sum_(r>=1 odd) chi(r)A(r^2)/(r^2-z).

The first n terms vanish. Writing A_0=A(0), A_1=A'(0), b_0=b(0),
b_1=b'(0), the exact Catalan cancellation is

  A_0 E'(0)-A_1 E(0)=A_0^2 beta(4)+A_0 b_1-A_1 b_0.

The residues and all four coefficients are rational. The script tests
24 combinations, with m=4,8,16,32,64 and several n/m ratios, checking
A_0 and A_1 independently from the product formula. All primitive
absolute values exceed one. In most nontrivial zero configurations
even the rational approximation error becomes large. Thus access to
rational samples does not by itself supply favorable interpolation
geometry; this particular elementary sampling family is not promoted
as a third promising route.

The JSON results record 19614 exact source-index/prime pairs, 19580
sharp scalar valuations, 31 exact parameter specializations, 10
quadruples rebuilt from half-pole Laurent coefficients, 141 coefficient
matrix rank tests, and 7568 moment-layer congruence checks. A separate
Sympy implementation rebuilds the full rational parameter functions
for n=2,4,6 and verifies unit constant terms of the remaining denominator
at all five admissible primes; each check covers every Taylor order at
that selected (n,p). The sampling script saves its 24 exact primitive
forms. The uniform local-integrality proof is in Section 2; testing is
supplementary.
