Hyperbolic moments, ladder periods, and the next construction problem
28 October 2026. Local research record; unpublished.

Status
======
This investigation does not prove the irrationality of beta(4). It derives
several explicit families, proves a restricted positive-weight obstruction,
and tests alternatives suggested by that obstruction. None of the tested
nontrivial primitive integer forms or polynomials has absolute value below
one. No novelty, independent audit, or formal verification is claimed.

Write B=beta(4), G=beta(2), and chi(m)=(-1)^((m-1)/2) for positive odd m.
The objective remains to construct nonzero integer expressions tending to
zero, or integer polynomials small enough relative to their degrees to
contradict an arbitrary hypothetical rational denominator of B.

The main conclusions of this pass are:

* A hyperbolic moment family has rational coefficients of B and G, and
  permits exact cancellation of G using an integer inverse Gram matrix.
* In this family, cancellation of G by a nonnegative polynomial weight
  forces I(P)/c(P) >= pi^4/192. Thus this particular positive-weight route
  cannot even provide relative approximations to B approaching B.
* A separate auxiliary Gram matrix allows the B coefficient of a large
  determinant to have rank one. This algebraic improvement is real, but
  its tested primitive arithmetic still fails.
* Polynomial biorthogonality cancels G inside a common signed integral.
  Its tested forms also fail. The positive-weight obstruction does not
  apply to these signed constructions.
* Integer-power two-loop ladder formulas suggest a different rational
  tail family. Its periods lie in Q+Q B+Q G+Q log(2)+Q zeta(3). Doubling
  the pole spacing excludes the last two periods. Direct and internally
  weighted cancellations are explicit, but again fail the primitive test.

There are 210 nontrivial evaluated beta(4)-only cases across six experiment
families, all rigorously above one in absolute value. Additionally, there
are seven singular baseline Gram cases, the trivial polynomial X, and
110 period decompositions. These are finite results, not asymptotic
impossibility theorems. The six families count baseline hyperbolic
projection, its low-rank extension, its biorthogonal extension, ordinary
ladder elimination, parity-separated ladder elimination, and internal
ladder polynomial filters.

1. Sources and the actual change of direction
===========================================
The preceding arcsine-contact.txt isolated B but exposed third- and
fourth-power prime costs. Carreira's beta4_determinants.tex had already
examined closely related weight-four and hypergeometric obstructions.
This pass additionally read its main Sections 1-5 and 7-11, following the
earlier reading of the boundary identity and conclusion. This is source
study, not a fresh independent audit of every claim in that manuscript.

The alternative considered here is a coupled integral with a fixed
weight-four period, rather than another derivative of one rational sum.
Three primary sources were added:

[LS24] F. Loebbert and S. F. Stawinski, Conformal Four-Point Integrals:
       Recursive Structure, Toda Equations and Double Copy,
       arXiv:2408.15331v1. https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15331
       Definition (2.5), conventions (2.3), (2.7)-(2.9), Section 2.3,
       selected Fourier-Mellin and unit-propagator formulas in Section 2.6,
       and Sections 4.1 and 4.4 were consulted. Not the whole paper.

[P15]  E. Panzer, Feynman integrals and hyperlogarithms,
       arXiv:1506.07243v1. https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.07243
       Selected ladder recursion and evaluation formulas in Section 2.4.6,
       especially (2.4.39)-(2.4.40), printed pp.57-58. Not a review of the
       thesis or its general reduction algorithms.

[DIS25] S. E. Derkachov, A. P. Isaev and L. A. Shumilov, Conformal
        four-point ladder integrals in diverse dimensions and
        polylogarithms, arXiv:2510.04235v1.
        https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04235
        Introduction, initial graph definitions, and Sections 3.2-3.3,
        particularly (3.33), (3.43), (3.44), were consulted. The general
        operator formalism and analytic-continuation proof were not audited.

The evaluation of the standard two-loop ladder at z=i, zbar=-i is known:
in the convention of [LS24, (2.73)], it is 6B. This is not our discovery.
The positive four- or higher-dimensional integral is motivation; the
families tested below are defined directly by elementary integrals or
rational sums. No unverified normalization or continuation convention is
used to infer a Diophantine conclusion.

At u=z*zbar=1, v=(1-z)(1-zbar), the standard two-loop function has the
elementary representation

 F(v) = integral_0^1 (-log t)^3 / ((1-t)^2+v*t) dt,    F(2)=6B.

Expansion of the denominator or the classical polylogarithm series gives
this identity. Its derivatives at v=2 are

 (-1)^n F^(n)(2)/n! = integral_0^1 t^n(-log t)^3/(1+t^2)^(n+1) dt
                   = 2^(-n-1) integral_0^infinity u^3 sech(u)^(n+1) du.

Thus pure angular/dimension differentiation has already collapsed to one
variable. It is not, by itself, a genuinely interacting new geometry.
Also, the sign in [LS24, (2.32)] appears to require reconciliation with
the unit-propagator convention; no result here depends on resolving that
source-convention question. The displayed derivative identity is direct.

2. Exact hyperbolic moments
==========================
For m>0 and Re(s)>-m put

 F_m(s)=integral_0^infinity exp(-s*u) sech(u)^m du.

If f=sech(u)^m, then f''=m^2 f-m(m+1)sech(u)^(m+2), f(0)=1, f'(0)=0.
Two integrations by parts therefore give

 F_(m+2)(s) = ((m^2-s^2) F_m(s)+s)/(m(m+1)).                 (2.1)

For m=2k+1 this has the form

 F_(2k+1)(s)=P_k(s^2)F_1(s)+s Q_k(s^2),
 P_0=1, Q_0=0,
 P_(k+1)(v)=(m^2-v)P_k(v)/(m(m+1)),
 Q_(k+1)(v)=((m^2-v)Q_k(v)+1)/(m(m+1)),       m=2k+1.

The base expansion is

 F_1(s)=pi/2-2G*s+pi^3*s^2/16-2B*s^3+O(s^4).

It follows either by integrating the exponential series for sech(u), or
from the digamma formula. All derivatives used above are integrable near
s=0. Set

 c_k=P_k(0)=binomial(2k,k)/4^k,
 d_k=-P'_k(0)=c_k sum_(j=0)^(k-1) 1/(2j+1)^2,
 r_k=Q_k(0), e_k=Q'_k(0).

Then

 J_k=(1/2) integral_0^infinity u sech(u)^(2k+1) du
     =c_k G-r_k/2,

 I_k=(1/12) integral_0^infinity u^3 sech(u)^(2k+1) du
     =c_k B-d_k G-e_k/2.                                    (2.2)

All c_k,d_k,r_k,e_k are rational. Useful recurrences are

 c_(k+1)=m*c_k/(m+1),
 d_(k+1)=(m^2*d_k+c_k)/(m(m+1)),
 r_(k+1)=(m^2*r_k+1)/(m(m+1)),
 e_(k+1)=(m^2*e_k-r_k)/(m(m+1)).

This family is algebraically related to the old deformed arcsine family,
not an unrelated special-function miracle. If mu_(2k-2)(s) denotes that
family's moment, then, for k>=1,

 P_k(s^2) mu_(2k-2)(s)=1/k.

Indeed P_k(v)=product_(j=0)^(k-1)((2j+1)^2-v)/(2k)!, whereas
mu_(2k-2)(s)=2(2k-1)!/product_(j=0)^(k-1)((2j+1)^2-s^2).
Here the deformation has polynomial zeros instead of the reciprocal poles.

3. Cancelling G without an extra odd-prime matrix-inverse cost
============================================================
Extend the four rational sequences linearly to polynomial functionals
c(P),d(P),r(P),e(P). For polynomial rows P_i and columns Q_j define the
matrices C=(c(P_i Q_j)), D=(d(P_i Q_j)), R=(r(P_i Q_j)), E=(e(P_i Q_j)).
Then the corresponding integral matrices satisfy

 J=G C-R/2,     I=B C-G D-E/2.

When C is invertible,

 I+D C^(-1) J = B C-(E+D C^(-1)R)/2.                        (3.1)

For the unweighted monomial Gram matrix C_ij=c_(i+j), C^(-1) is integral.
Here is an exact proof. Under the probability measure

 dmu(x)=dx/(pi sqrt(x(1-x))),    0<x<1,

the shifted Chebyshev polynomials T_k(2x-1) have squared norms 1 for k=0
and 1/2 otherwise. Their coefficient matrix T is integral. Consequently

 T C T^t=diag(1,1/2,...,1/2),
 C^(-1)=T^t diag(1,2,...,2) T,

which is an integer matrix. This statement does not apply to arbitrary
weighted Gram matrices. It also does not remove the intrinsic rational
denominators of D,R,E, nor guarantee a favorable determinant bound.

The baseline tests used n up to 32 and several polynomial endpoint
weights. Of 45 configurations, seven had singular C; n=1 gives X; all
37 remaining primitive determinant evaluations have absolute value >1.

4. A proved obstruction for nonnegative cancellation
====================================================
The substitution x=sech(u)^2, with u(x)=atanh(sqrt(1-x)), gives

 J(P)=pi/4 integral P(x)u(x) dmu(x),
 I(P)=pi/24 integral P(x)u(x)^3 dmu(x).

The even part of (2.1), or the second derivative at zero, also gives

 d(P)=pi^2 c(P)/8-(1/2) integral P(x)u(x)^2 dmu(x).           (4.1)

Proposition. Let P be a nonzero rational polynomial nonnegative on [0,1].
If d(P)=0, so that I(P)=c(P)B-e(P)/2 contains no G, then

 I(P) >= (pi^4/192)c(P),     pi^4/192=0.5073390158... .       (4.2)

Proof. c(P)>0. Normalize P*dmu to a probability measure. Equation (4.1)
and d(P)=0 give expectation(u^2)=pi^2/4. The power-mean inequality gives
expectation(u^3)>=(expectation(u^2))^(3/2)=pi^3/8. Multiplication by
pi*c(P)/24 proves (4.2).

In particular, after any positive rational scaling that makes the B
coefficient A a positive integer,

 A B-C >= (pi^4/192) A >= pi^4/192.

Such forms cannot tend to zero. In fact their proposed rational
approximations C/A stay at least pi^4/192 below B. This is a uniform
obstruction to this stated family of nonnegative weights, not merely a
finite failed scan, and not an obstruction to arbitrary positive-integral
proofs of irrationality.

Matrix version. Let W(x) be a symmetric rational polynomial matrix that
is positive semidefinite at every x in (0,1). Suppose C=integral W dmu
is positive definite and d(W)=0 entrywise. Applying the scalar argument
to v^t W v for every real vector v gives

 I(W) >= (pi^4/192) C

as quadratic forms. Hence

 det I(W) >= (pi^4/192)^n det C.

If an overall rational multiplier makes det(X C-E/2) a primitive integer
polynomial p(X), then

 |p(B)| >= (pi^4/192)^n |leading coefficient of p|
        >= (pi^4/192)^n.                                    (4.3)

Its degree is n. Thus this positive matrix class cannot supply the
degree-normalized decay tending to minus infinity needed to deal with
an arbitrary fixed rational denominator by this determinant criterion.
It is not a theorem that B is rational or that every other criterion fails.

The projection (3.1) need not be an integral with nonnegative weight, so
(4.2)-(4.3) are not explanations of every finite failure in this note.

5. Low rank and cancellation inside a signed common integral
============================================================
The singular cases in Section 3 need not be discarded. Take

 P_i=T_(g+i)(2x-1), Q_j=T_j(2x-1), 0<=i,j<n, 0<g<n,

and use separate auxiliary rows U_i=Q_i for J. Write C0,R0 for their
Gram and rational matrices. Then

 I(P,Q)+D(P,Q) C0^(-1) J(U,Q)
     =B C(P,Q)-(E(P,Q)+D(P,Q)C0^(-1)R0)/2.                  (5.1)

Only C0 needs to be invertible. Chebyshev orthogonality gives

 rank C(P,Q)=n-g.

For g=n-1 a large n-by-n determinant is therefore a polynomial of degree
at most one in B. This is an exact low-rank construction, not an assertion
that every denominator layer has correspondingly low rank. All 35 tested
configurations through n=32 give primitive values >1, including rank-one
examples. The unshifted inverse being integral is insufficient.

A second construction removes G in the integrand itself. Choose columns
Q_j=T_j(2x-1). Take auxiliary rows T_0,...,T_(n+g-1) and source rows
T_(n+g),...,T_(2n+g-1). Subtract rational combinations of the auxiliary
rows so that every new row P_i satisfies

 d(P_i Q_j)=0 for all j<n,
 c(P_i Q_j)=0 for j<g.                                    (5.2)

This is a square rational linear system of n+g constraints. Whenever it
is invertible, the actual common integral matrix is

 I(P,Q)=B C(P,Q)-E(P,Q)/2,

and rank C<=n-g. This integral is signed, because the row and column
polynomials differ; the obstruction in Section 4 does not apply. It
would need a separate nonvanishing theorem. All 23 tested configurations
through n=24 have invertible constraints, full permitted B rank, and
primitive determinant values >1. These tests do not justify enlarging
this same canonical projection as the next research priority.

For reference, the differential operator behind (2.1) is

 T P=4x^2(1-x)P''+(8x-10x^2)P'+(1-2x)P.

It satisfies (sech(u)P(sech(u)^2))''=sech(u)(T P)(sech(u)^2),
and consequently

 J(TP)=P(1)/2,    I(TP)=J(P),
 c(TP)=0,        d(TP)=-c(P),
 r(TP)=-P(1),    e(TP)=r(P).

Thus integration by parts provides an exact period projection mechanism,
but its existence alone provides neither small integer coefficients nor
a suitable nonvanishing argument.

6. An explicit family suggested by coupled two-loop ladders
==========================================================
The parameter called beta in [DIS25] will be denoted b here to avoid
confusion with the Dirichlet beta function. For integer b>0 that paper's
holomorphic factor at two loops contains

 sum_(k>=0) z^k / product_(p=0)^(b-1)(k-p-epsilon)^3.

Its singular part is a finite sum over k=0,...,b-1. The product of this
part with the regular tail, followed by the second epsilon coefficient
and angular differentiation, motivates the following directly defined
rational functions. We do not need to identify their overall multiplier
with the paper's normalized Feynman integral.

For 0<=j<b, put

 C_j=(-1)^(b-1-j)/(j! (b-1-j)!)^3,
 H_j=sum_(p!=j)1/(j-p),   H2_j=sum_(p!=j)1/(j-p)^2,
 K_j=(9H_j^2+3H2_j)/2,
 B_j(m)=product_(p=0)^(b-1)(m+j-p)^(-3).

Define

 S_b(m)=sum_j C_j [K_j B_j(m)-3H_j B'_j(m)+B''_j(m)/2].    (6.1)

Equivalently it is the coefficient of epsilon^2 in

 sum_j product_(p!=j)(j-p-epsilon)^(-3)
       product_p(m+j-p-epsilon)^(-3).

For b<2h<3b, set

 P_h(m)=m product_(r=1)^(h-1)(m^2-(2r-1)^2),
 R_(b,h)(m)=P_h(m)S_b(m),
 L_(b,h)=sum_(m>=2h-1, m odd) chi(m) R_(b,h)(m).            (6.2)

The pole interval is [1-b,b-1], below the start of the sum. The rational
function decays at least quadratically in this range of parameters, so
the sum converges absolutely. The angular identity motivating P_h is

 2^(-(2h-1)) T_m^((2h-1))(0)
       =(-1)^(h-1) chi(m) P_h(m)/2^(2h-1)       (m odd).

This follows by taking the coefficient of x^(2h-1) in the Chebyshev
polynomial T_m(x); it includes its zeros for smaller positive odd m.

Exact period space. Reflection j -> b-1-j shows S_b(-m)=-S_b(m), hence
R_(b,h) is even. Its poles have order at most five. At odd poles P_h has
a zero, reducing the order to at most four. Summation on the odd grid
then gives

 L_(b,h) in Q + Q B + Q G + Q eta(1) + Q eta(3),            (6.3)

where eta(1)=log(2), eta(3)=3 zeta(3)/4. For even poles, reflection
cancels odd beta values; for odd poles it cancels even eta values.
The starting-tail corrections are rational. In particular, there is no
need for an unspecified list of higher polylogarithmic constants.

For a direct check of (6.1), the Laurent coefficients contributed at
m=p-j are C_j C_p times

 order 5: 6,
 order 4: 9(H_j-H_p),
 order 3: K_j+K_p-18H_j H_p,
 order 2: 3(H_j K_p-K_j H_p),
 order 1: K_j K_p.

Multiplying these principal parts by P_h gives the full partial fractions:
there is no polynomial part, by the decay at infinity. This provides a
fast exact evaluation without fitting numerical constants.

Examples:

 L_(1,1)=6B,
 L_(2,2)=-6+24G-12B-(9/2)eta(3),
 L_(3,3)=309/64-(1281/32)G+(579/16)B-(27/2)log(2)+6eta(3).

The latter two are approximately 0.05889759119636 and 0.00007740088336.
Their smallness before period elimination is not an irrationality result.

7. Three ways of removing the extra ladder periods
==================================================
First, four prescribed tails can be combined using the cofactors of
their G,eta(1),eta(3) coefficient matrix. This leaves a rational linear
form in B. We tested neighboring h at fixed b, and neighboring balanced
pairs (b,h)=(b,b). All 36 primitive forms, through b=67 in the latter
configuration, have absolute value >1.

Second, define a mathematical variant with spacing s=2: replace j-p by
2(j-p) in both products in the epsilon-coefficient definition of S.
Use h>=b and 2h<3b. All poles are then even, so (6.3) reduces to

 L_(b,h;2) in Q + Q B + Q G.

This is a parity-separated rational-sum construction, not a claimed
Feynman-graph identity. Two neighboring forms cancel G. All 49 primitive
forms tested have absolute value >1. The reduction in the period space
does not improve the arithmetic enough.

Third, keep spacing one but weight the internal j-sum by

 w_j=(2j-b+1)^(2ell),    ell=0,1,2,3.

These weights preserve reflection and the same period space. An integer
linear combination of these four weights can cancel G,log(2),eta(3)
before summing the tail, using a polynomial of degree at most six in j.
This tests a projection inside the coupling, rather than only a change
of the angular parameter h. All 30 resulting primitive forms, through
b=64, again have absolute value >1. The weights are constructed from
exact period coefficients, without fitting B or its continued fractions.

These failures do not rule out all two-loop numerator insertions, other
parameter directions, nonconsecutive pole sets, or determinant versions.
They do rule out treating the existence of a coupled integral, a small
uncleared integral, or an exact period projector as evidence of a working
irrationality proof.

9. What to pursue next, and what not to repeat
=============================================
The positive-weight proposition is the strongest mathematical conclusion
of this pass. The rest supplies explicit constructions and negative finite
evidence. I would not describe this as being close to an irrationality proof.

The priority should be a construction in which period projection and
arithmetic cancellation are designed together. Neither a cheap Gram inverse,
low B rank, positive integration, nor a low-dimensional period space suffices.

The most concrete untested geometric target is a polynomial numerator
insertion in the full two-loop ladder, depending on both internal variables.
The desired reduction is

 integral P_N(variables)*omega_N
       =a_N B+b_N G+c_N zeta(3)+d_N log(2)+e_N,

with b_N=c_N=d_N=0 enforced by a structured integration-by-parts identity.
The coefficients of that identity must have controlled arithmetic height,
and the retained signed integral needs a separate nonvanishing argument.
Changing all propagator powers together or adding the degree-six internal
filter tested above does not accomplish this. No such successful numerator
family has been constructed here. The Feynman connection is a design source,
not evidence that this target is attainable.

An equally important arithmetic target is simultaneous control of the
third- and fourth-power denominator layers, rather than just lowering the
rank of the B coefficient. Before a large analytic optimization, compute
the local denominator filtration of the proposed rational matrices at bulk
primes. A useful new family must show savings there that persist in the
primitive determinant. Arbitrary prime-by-prime interpolation is not a
solution: the cost of combining congruences can erase the savings. A
factorial or binomial identity supplying the congruences uniformly would
be a meaningful advance.

A longer-range alternative is an arithmetic-holonomy argument for the
coupled G/B system that keeps G until the final rank comparison. The
standard uniformizations already failed in coupled-holonomy.txt; they
should not be renamed as a new proposal. A different singularity geometry
or a sharper arithmetic filtration would be required before returning to
that route.

My practical choice for the next pass is the first target, with the local
arithmetic test used early. This is a research judgment under substantial
uncertainty, not a claim of favorable numerical evidence. If that target
again collapses to one of the already tested rational sums, it should be
identified as such and abandoned rather than enlarged mechanically.
