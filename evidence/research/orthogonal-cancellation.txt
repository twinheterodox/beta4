Orthogonal cancellation, positive forms, and determinant tests for beta(4)
28 October 2026. Local and unpublished.

Outcome and scope

This investigation constructs positive rational linear forms in beta(4),
and strictly positive determinants of growing degree in beta(4), by
discrete orthogonality. It removes Catalan's constant without dividing by
one of its large approximation coefficients. The relevant polynomials
are classical dual Hahn polynomials, with a symmetric Krawtchouk special
case. These names describe existing orthogonal-polynomial families; no
priority claim is made for the applications below.

The tested primitive integer forms and determinants still fail the size
test. All 35 initial scalar cases, all 384 cases in the expanded scalar
scan, and all 84 cross-parity determinants exceed one. These counts
include overlaps between scans. A separate matrix cancellation in the
older coupled family also fails in its 54 nontrivial tests. Thus neither
uniform positivity nor cheap removal of an auxiliary period is enough.

The proofs and checks here are work by the same agent, not independent
verification or formal verification. This is not an irrationality proof.
The recommended next construction, and a proved explanation of one of
the denominator failures, are in next-proof-avenues.txt.

1. A positive weight with rational polynomial moments

Put t=y^2 and f(y)=sech(pi y), and define

  C2(R) = integral_0^infinity R(y^2) [-y f'(y)] dy,
  C4(R) = integral_0^infinity R(y^2) [-y^3 f'''(y)/6] dy.

C4 is the signed functional used in the previous research notes. Write
w2=-y f', w4=-y^3 f'''/6. For x=pi y and u=x^2,

  w4/w2 = x^2(6 tanh^2(x)-5)/6.

Since sinh(x)>=x, tanh^2(x)>=u/(1+u). Consequently

  w4/w2+1/2 >= ((u-1)^2+2)/(6(1+u)) > 0.

The functional

                        W=C4+C2/2                         (1)

therefore has a strictly positive density on y>0. Unlike adding a multiple
of pi times another weight, (1) preserves rational polynomial moments.
If E_(2k) are the Euler numbers, integrations by parts give

  C2(t^k)=(2k+1)|E_(2k)|/2^(2k+1),
  C4(t^k)=binom(2k+3,3)|E_(2k)|/2^(2k+1).                 (2)

The base moment is integral y^(2k) sech(pi y)dy
=|E_(2k)|/2^(2k+1). All boundary terms vanish for polynomial or
negative-square-pole rational functions.

Let T_s(j)=sum_(0<=k<j)(-1)^k/(2k+1)^s and G=beta(2). For integer j>=1,

  C2(1/(t+j^2))=4(-1)^j(G-T_2(j)),
  C4(1/(t+j^2))=16j^2(-1)^j(beta(4)-T_4(j)).              (3)

For completeness, integral_0^infinity f(y)cos(vy)dy=1/(2cosh(v/2)).
After one integration by parts, the first resolvent becomes the integral
of f(y)(j^2-y^2)/(j^2+y^2)^2. Use

  (j^2-y^2)/(j^2+y^2)^2=integral_0^infinity v e^(-jv)cos(yv)dv.

Expanding 1/(2cosh(v/2)) gives the first sum in (3). For the second,
three integrations by parts and
y^3/(y^2+j^2)=y-j^2 y/(y^2+j^2) give j^2/6 times the same Laplace
integral with v^3. Its evaluation is the second sum in (3).

2. A variance construction using every integer pole

Fix h>=0 and N>=2 integers. Write

  Q(t)=product_(j=h+1)^(h+N)(t+j^2),
  w_j=1/|Q'(-j^2)|
     =2j(j+h)!/[(j-h-1)!(h+N-j)!(h+N+j)!].               (4)

All weights are positive, and sign Q'(-j^2)=(-1)^(j-h-1).
Choose a nonzero rational polynomial F with 2 deg(F)+1<N and put

  S_k=sum_j w_j j^(2k) F(-j^2)^2,
  mu=S_1/S_0,
  R(t)=(t+mu)F(t)^2/Q(t).                               (5)

Then mu>0, and R is nonnegative on t>=0 and not identically zero.
Its residue at -j^2 is (-1)^(j-h-1)w_j(mu-j^2)F(-j^2)^2.
By (3), the coefficient of G in C2(R) is

                    4(-1)^(h+1)(mu S_0-S_1)=0.

The beta(4) coefficient of C4(R) is

              A=16(-1)^h(S_2-S_1^2/S_0).                (6)

The bracket is a strictly positive weighted variance: a polynomial of
degree less than (N-1)/2 cannot vanish at all but one of the N nodes.
Hence

                 W(R)=A beta(4)+B>0,
                 A,B rational, A nonzero.              (7)

This gives a uniform nonvanishing proof and exact Catalan cancellation.
It does not bound the size of the primitive integer form obtained from
(7). For general F, even the rational number mu may be expensive.

3. Classical dual Hahn polynomials make (5) explicit

Define monic polynomials q_m by q_0=1, q_(-1)=0, and

  q_(m+1)(t)=(t+alpha_m)q_m(t)-beta_m q_(m-1)(t),

  alpha_m=((2h+3)N+2h(h+1)-1)/2+2m(N-m-1),
  beta_m=m(2m+2h+1)(N-m)(2N+2h+1-2m)/4.                 (8)

For 0<=m<N these are the dual Hahn polynomials on the squared nodes in
(4), after the change of variable x=j-h-1. The usual parameters are
gamma=delta=h+1/2 and M=N-1, with spectral variable
x(x+gamma+delta+1)=j^2-(h+1)^2. See [DLMF, 18.26.4 and Section 18.25]
for the classical family. The proof below also verifies the particular
normalization without relying on a table of weights.

Put gamma=h+1/2, M=N-1, and

  B_k(t)=product_(l=0)^(k-1)(t+(h+1+l)^2).

An explicit positive Newton expansion is

  q_m(t)=(gamma+1)_m/(M-m)!
       *sum_(k=0)^m binom(m,k)(M-k)!/(gamma+1)_k B_k(t).  (9)

Here (a)_k denotes a rising factorial. Comparing successive coefficients
in (9) gives (8); in particular 2^m q_m has integer coefficients.

One direct orthogonality proof uses the finite difference operator

  (Lf)(j)=b_j(f(j+1)-f(j))+d_j(f(j-1)-f(j)),
  b_j=(j+h+1)(h+N-j)/(4j),
  d_j=(j-h-1)(j+h+N)/(4j).                              (10)

At the two endpoints the outward rate is zero. The weights in (4)
satisfy w_j b_j=w_(j+1)d_(j+1), so L is self-adjoint for their inner
product. On the monic Newton polynomials
f_k(x)=product_(l<k)(x-(h+1+l)^2), regarded as functions of x=j^2,

  Lf_k=-k f_k+k(N-k)(gamma+k) f_(k-1).                  (11)

To see (11), the leading term of Lf_k is -k x^k, while Lf_k+k f_k
vanishes at the first k-1 squared nodes. Its remaining multiplier is
obtained at j=h+k. Substituting (9) now gives
L[q_m(-j^2)]=-m q_m(-j^2). Distinct eigenvalues and self-adjointness prove

  sum_j w_j q_m(-j^2)q_l(-j^2)=H_m [m=l],
  H_m=H_0 product_(r=1)^m beta_r,
  H_0=1/[(N-1)!(h+3/2)_(N-1)].                         (12)

The norm recursion follows by multiplying (8) in the two orders and
using orthogonality. Here is a finite-sum verification of H_0. Set
a=2h+2 and M=N-1. The first weight is a!/[M!(a+M)!], and its relative
weight sum is

  sum_(x=0)^M (1+2x/a)(a)_x(-M)_x(-1)^x
                           /[x!(a+M+1)_x]
                  =(a+1)_M/((a+1)/2)_M.

For M>=1, apply the beta-integral representation of
F(z)=2F1(a,-M;a+M+1;z) to F(-1)+(2/a)zF'(z)|_(z=-1).
Integration by parts replaces the first integral
integral t^(a-1)(1-t^2)^M dt by (2M/a)integral t^(a+1)(1-t^2)^(M-1)dt.
The remaining sum is (2M/a) times the same normalization factor times
integral t^a(1-t^2)^(M-1)dt. Its beta evaluation is the displayed ratio.
The case M=0 is immediate.

For F=q_m, (8)--(12) give

  S_0=H_m, mu=alpha_m,
  S_2-S_1^2/S_0=H_m(beta_m+beta_(m+1)).                 (13)

Thus the cancellation parameter is only a half-integer. At h=0 this is
also the odd sector of the symmetric Krawtchouk system: up to sign,
j q_m(-j^2) is its monic polynomial of degree 2m+1. The new application
here is to the positive functional (1), not a new orthogonal system.

4. Cross-parity determinants retain growing beta(4) degree

For N>=2r form the r by r matrix

  M_(i,j)=W(q_(2i)(t)q_(2j+1)(t)/Q(t)), 0<=i,j<r.       (14)

If a numerator has degree >=N, divide it by Q first. The polynomial
quotient contributes the rational moments (2); it is NOT discarded.
Orthogonality (12) makes the G coefficient of every entry zero.
Using (8) to multiply by t shows

  M=A beta(4)+B, with A,B rational matrices,

  A_(i,j)=16(-1)^h H_max(2i,2j+1) if i=j or i=j+1,
           0 otherwise.                                (15)

Thus A is lower bidiagonal with nonzero diagonal, and det(M) is a
polynomial of EXACT degree r in beta(4).

4.1 Strict positivity for every r

The Newton coefficient matrix in (9) is a positive row scaling and
column scaling of the Pascal matrix binom(m,k), hence totally
nonnegative. The coefficient matrix taking B_k to monomials is also
totally nonnegative. A short proof of the latter statement works for
any nonnegative sequence c_l in product_(l<k)(t+c_l). Separate the first
factor t+c_0: the finite coefficient matrix factors as blockdiag(1,T')
times the unit lower-bidiagonal matrix with subdiagonal c_0, where T'
is the coefficient matrix for c_1,c_2,... . Induction proves the claim.

Consequently the monomial coefficient matrix of q_m is totally
nonnegative, lower triangular, and has diagonal 1. For any increasing
degree indices m_1<...<m_r and 0<t_1<...<t_r, Cauchy--Binet and the
strict positivity of generalized monomial Vandermonde determinants give

                       det[q_(m_i)(t_j)]>0.

There is a strictly positive term from the selected leading degrees.
Apply this both to the even and to the odd degrees in (14). Andreief's
identity expresses det(M) as the integral of their product against r
copies of the positive measure W/Q, divided by r!. The ordered region
has positive measure, and the two determinants there are positive.
Hence

                         det(M)>0                      (16)

for all N>=2r and h>=0. Exponential decay still has to beat the exact
primitive coefficient size; (16) alone supplies no such inequality.

5. Exact arithmetic and size tests

The scalar tests clear the rational A,B and remove their full integer
gcd. The determinant tests interpolate the exact rational polynomial,
check its leading coefficient and an additional evaluation, then clear
all denominators and remove the full coefficient content. Arb evaluates
the resulting primitive polynomial at beta(4), with precision scaled to
coefficient size. Thus the failures below cannot be repaired by merely
proving a smaller generic clearing multiplier for those same examples.

  family                       scope                         result
  h=0 orthogonal scalar        35 cases, N through 128        all >1
  shifted scalar families      384 cases, N through 96        all >1
  cross-parity determinant      84 cases, r through 12        all >1

The shifted scan includes h=0,N/4,N/2,N,2N,4N, several polynomial
degrees, and five choices of F: q_m(t), q_m(-t), t^m, and products with
roots at the positive or negative integer squares. The test code uses
2deg(F)+1<=N-2, a slightly smaller range than the theorem permits.
Shifting poles sometimes improves the rational approximation error but
does not improve the tested primitive balance enough.

For illustration, the h=0,N=128,m=62 scalar example has
log(primitive form)/N about +6.689. The cross determinant with
r=12,N=24,h=0 has log(primitive value)/r^2 about +9.081.
These are finite measurements, not asymptotic impossibility theorems.

6. A separate coupled block determinant

For the previously constructed coefficient sequences, make Hankel
matrices A,B,C,D out of a_(2m),b_(2m),c_(2m),d_(2m). With G=beta(2),

  R=G A+B, S=16 beta(4) A+G C+D,

  det[[A,R],[C,S]]=det[[A,B],[C,D+16 beta(4) A]].         (17)

Subtracting G times the first block of columns proves the identity
without any division or assumption on independence of periods. If A is
invertible, the polynomial in (17) has degree r and leading coefficient
16^r(det A)^2. The Schur complement is used only for efficient exact
computation, not to define (17).

The 56 tests use r=1,2,3,4,6,8,12; block shifts 0,r,2r,4r; and both
original and moment-normalized sequences. The two r=1,shift=0 cases
give the uninformative primitive polynomial X. All other 54 primitive
absolute values exceed one. No uniform nonvanishing theorem for (17)
has been proved here. The large-prime explanation of this failure is
in next-proof-avenues.txt, Section 2.

7. Why a faster-decaying weight does not immediately solve the problem

There is an exact alternative cancellation:

  C4(R)-(pi^2/6)C2(tR)
    =-pi^2 integral_0^infinity t R(t) sech^2(pi y) w2(y)dy,
       t=y^2.                                         (18)

The density now decays like a polynomial times exp(-3pi y), rather than
exp(-pi y). This is an attractive real-place improvement. But if the
coefficient of beta(4) in C4(R) is A, then the coefficient of G in
C2(tR) is -A/4, by (3). Thus (18) carries

                   A(beta(4)+pi^2 G/24),               (19)

together with a rational number and pi^2 times a rational number.
The improved decay has changed the period being approximated. It does
not give a pure beta(4) form. This is an exact limitation of (18), not
a general theorem forbidding faster-decaying beta(4) constructions.
