1. A precise description of the content we need to save

For the fixed odd-k slow filter of dilations-and-positive-kernels.txt, put

 R_k(z)=r_k(sqrt(z))=sum_(j>=1) a_j z^j, a_0=0,
 b_i=a_i, H_ij=a_(i+j)-a_(i+j-1), 1<=i,j<=n,
 L_n(X)=det [[6X,b^T],[b,H]].

For a nonzero L_n let kappa_n>0 be the rational multiplier making
P_n=kappa_n L_n a primitive integer polynomial. Zero polynomials are
treated separately. In particular kappa_n need not be an integer.

If D is any positive integer clearing a_0,...,a_(2n), define

 T=DH, u=Db, d=det T, m=adj(T)u, Q=u^T adj(T)u,
 g=gcd(6D d,Q), Delta=gcd(d,m_1,...,m_n).

Then kappa_n=D^(n+1)/g, and the following exact bounds hold:

 Delta | g | 6D Delta^2,
 D^n/(6 Delta^2) <= kappa_n <= D^(n+1)/Delta.                 (1)

The key identity is m_i^2-Q adj(T)_(ii)=-d Q_i, a bordered
Desnanot--Jacobi identity. It gives
Delta | gcd(d,Q) | Delta^2, including the prime 2 and singular T.
Delta=0 is equivalent to the entire bordered pencil being zero.

Delta is also the gcd of the maximal minors of the rectangular matrix
F=(D a_(i+j))_(0<=i<=n,1<=j<=n). Consequently, away from primes dividing
6D, common numerator content is exactly loss of column rank of F modulo
the prime. Equivalently, there are polynomials q!=0 and p, both of degree
at most n-1, for which qR_k-p=O(z^(2n+1)) over that finite field.
This is an exact modular Pade condition, not a generic-rank heuristic.

A small counterexample matters. At k=3,n=6 the actual coefficient
denominator is D=4729725, but the minimally cleared endpoint pair has
content 2^14*23. Although 23 does not divide D,

 (1+2z+17z^2+13z^3+z^4+5z^5)R_3(z)=19z^5+O(z^13) mod 23.   (2)

Thus new common numerator primes really occur. This disproves support
restricted to the actual coefficient denominator; it says nothing about
the stronger proposed restriction to primes <=4n, since 23<24 here.

There is also a useful exact recursion. For a prime p>2n, let
s=(p+2k+3)/2, R=2n-s+1>0, m=n-R. Then

 p^R L_n(X)=(-1)^[R(R-1)/2](-32/3)^R L_m(X) mod p.           (3)

Both sides are coefficientwise p-integral. The expected exponent
v_p(kappa_n)=R holds exactly when the smaller pencil is nonzero modulo p.
For example, (2) explains why k=3,n=9,p=23 gives exponent 2 rather than 3.
The residue block alone would have missed this cancellation.

Finally, using the positions where successive prime-denominator layers
first appear, an exact two-layer matching lemma yields, for fixed odd k,

 limsup_(n,L_n!=0) log(kappa_n)/n^2 <= C_k,
 C_k=13/3+3/k+1/(k+2)+4/(3k+8)-1/(k+4).                    (4)

In particular C_3=5.6257703081... and C_5=5.1389924086.... These improve
the earlier sufficient rates 7.733333... and 7.085714.... They are upper
bounds, not actual asymptotics. They do not assume favorable content.
The elementary content and matching proofs are in arithmetic/findings.txt;
the asymptotic summation in (4) also uses the prime number theorem.

2. What a fixed-cut absolute contour estimate can and cannot do

The previous Kronecker argument already gives infinitely many nonzero
L_n(X) for every fixed X. Size, rather than all-size positivity, is the
remaining issue for this family.

The relevant generating functions are

 C_X(z)=(6X+R_k(z))/(1-z),
 G_X(w)=C_X(1/w)/w=(6X+R_k(1/w))/(w-1).

For k=3 and k=5, the analysis establishes the following component theorem:
in any compact filled admissible cut K for the chosen germ G_X, every
component containing a branch point outside the unit disk meets the
closed unit disk. Admissibility means single-valued continuation on the
unbounded complementary domain. No connectedness assumption on K is made.

The first draft had a substantive logarithm-sheet gap. Cross-review
caught it. The repair uses a single enclosing Jordan loop and weighted
pole counts N+4ell*S, where S=N mod 2. For 1<=N<=5 these cannot vanish
on any logarithm sheet. This is why the proved scope is specifically
k=3,5; the same argument has not been established for every odd k.

Exact branch locations and projection inequalities then imply

 k=3: cap(K)>6/5;
 k=5: cap(K)>7/20.                                         (5)

Sharper numerical enclosures are archived, but the rational bounds
already resolve the comparison with (4). The fixed-cut absolute
Cauchy--Vandermonde/Polya estimate has the form

 limsup log|L_n(theta)|/n^2 <= log cap(K).

For k=3 this cannot even supply a negative raw n^2 exponent. For k=5,
(5) limits the decay exponent obtainable this way to less than
log(20/7)<1.050, far short of the current multiplier bound 5.139.

This is a limitation of that estimate together with our arithmetic
bound. It is NOT a lower bound for actual determinant values, nor an
impossibility theorem for beta(4), weighted contours, size-dependent
contours, or other determinant constructions. Cancellation not seen by
an absolute contour bound could matter.

For completeness, explicitly containing ellipses give rigorous raw
upper exponents log(13/6) for k=3 and log(19/10) for k=5. Both are
positive. Eight paired examples at n=12,20,30,40 show growing raw and
primitive values; those are finite diagnostics only.

Separate fourth-order sign counterexamples also exclude global sign
regularity for the ordinary cubic Loewner kernel. They do not settle
special restrictions to particular integer frames. Full proofs and
the scope of the cited Polya theorem are in analysis/findings.txt.

3. A different approach through a classical polylogarithm ladder

The fresh worker first tried a Cayley substitution at sqrt(2)-1. An
exact splitting identity leaves a mixed integral, and a quadratic-field
trace reintroduces growth at the conjugate embedding. Neither operation
by itself produces the needed arithmetic saving.

It then derived the following consequence of Broadhurst's 1998 ladder
identities (source equations and the derivation are given in
fresh/findings.txt; https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9803067): define

 D4(z)=Im[Li4(z)-log|z| Li3(z)+(log|z|)^2 Li2(z)/3].

Then

 beta(4)=225/136 D4(i/2)-25/34 D4((1+i)/4)
                              +9/68 D4((1-i)/8).            (6)

All three arguments have modulus at most 1/2. This is an exact
consequence of classical identities, not a numerical recognition
claim or a claimed new functional equation. The logarithmic terms
remain important; (6) is not a pure Li4 digit-extraction formula.

For the natural rational Taylor germ H(t) obtained by replacing log 2
with ell(t)=-log(1-t/2) and each argument z by zt, H(1)=beta(4).
Writing H=sum h_N t^N and S_N=sum_(j<=N)h_j=p_N/q_N in lowest terms,
the worker proves the exact dyadic denominator law

 v_2(h_N)=-E_N,  v_2(q_N)=E_N,
 E_(4m)=10m+4, E_(4m+1)=10m+5,
 E_(4m+2)=10m+11, E_(4m+3)=10m+12.                         (7)

Here N>=1, and the first formula has m>=1. Also

 beta(4)-S_N ~ 2A(2) 2^(-N) log(N)/N,
 A(2)=(75/272)[pi log(2)/4-Im Li2((1+i)/4)]>15/272.         (8)

Together (7)--(8) prove that this scalar truncation family's primitive
forms tend to positive infinity, at least at the scale
2^(3N/2) log(N)/N. Unlike the contour comparison, this IS an actual
obstruction theorem for the specified family. It does not transfer
automatically to determinants or simultaneous approximants.

4. Matched polynomial multipliers: a small source error, not yet integers

A different construction uses

 P1(t)=t^2+4, P3(t)=t^2-4t+8, P5(t)=t^2-8t+32.

Their values at t=1 are 5,5,25. Thus the powers (2n,2n,n) have the
SAME value 25^n. Each polylogarithm block can be approximated separately
without multiplying three unrelated leading coefficients.

Multiply the respective blocks by these powers and truncate at degrees
(6n,6n,4n). Approximate ell and ell^2 using the common multiplier
(2-t)^(4n) and degree 8n. The resulting rational number R_n is defined
explicitly in fresh/simultaneous.txt. It satisfies

 Lambda_n=25^n beta(4)-R_n
          ~ (-1)^n K 27^(-n)/sqrt(n), K>0,                 (9)

where, with u=log(3)/2 and l=log(2),

 K=(225/884)sqrt(pi)[u^3/6+l u^2/2+l^2 u/3].

The proof uses an exact integral for each block's error. The dominant
positive integral has exponential factor x^2(1-x^2)^2, maximized at
x=1/sqrt(3). The other two blocks and the logarithm errors are
exponentially smaller. Thus (9) proves eventual nonvanishing and
exponential decay of these RATIONAL forms, not of integer forms.

The proved sufficient clearing factor is

 D_n=17*2^(9n+4)*lcm(1,...,6n)^2*lcm(1,...,8n)^2.            (10)

Its logarithmic rate 28+9log(2) exceeds the decay rate log(27) by a
large margin. All twelve tested primitive forms have absolute value
above one. The estimate (10) is not a lower bound for primitive
normalization, so these observations do not prove asymptotic failure
of this second family. No favorable primitive bound is presently known.

5. Assessment and next mathematical requirement

The round sharpens the diagnosis rather than establishing proximity to
a proof. The slow-filter family now has a concrete modular description
of its common content; the straightforward fixed-cut analytic estimate
is inadequate. The classical ladder gives an alternative whose source
error and nonvanishing can be proved, but the known arithmetic is poor.

There is no present reason to enlarge the same numerical scans or to
spend another round merely improving constants. A useful next step
must demonstrate structural common divisibility or low residue rank
inside a genuinely simultaneous construction, while retaining a
controlled real error. The content theorem (1) is a way to test such a
claim. The simultaneous family (9) is an explicit test case, not a
promised successful route. No such arithmetic mechanism was found here.
