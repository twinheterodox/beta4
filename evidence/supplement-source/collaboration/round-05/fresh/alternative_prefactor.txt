An antisymmetric Fricke prefactor: the next pair of poles does not cancel
======================================================================
29 October 2026. Local and unpublished. No irrationality proof.

This note develops the precise proposal recorded in
02_alternative_prefactor_initial.txt. It preserves the Euler--Boole
investigation in findings.txt as a separate branch.

The replacement prefactor DOES avoid the trace derivative's earlier
positive-real pole, has integral q coefficients in Z[sqrt(2)], and
cancels its nearest pole at the intended Fricke point. However, its
next pair of denominator zeros is explicit. Their beta(4) periods do
not cancel for all sufficiently large primes, and are rigorously
certified not to cancel for p=3,5,7,11,17,31,101. For each such fixed p,
the raw Taylor coefficient values in the chosen real embedding grow
exponentially in absolute value for EVERY sufficiently large index.

This is an obstruction to raw coefficient decay of this prefactor.
It is NOT a primitive-value theorem, and says nothing decisive about
splitting the two Q(sqrt(2)) components, their contents, selected linear
combinations, or more general determinants.

The coordinator independently proposed the shifted denominator zeros,
their derivative-limit noncancellation, and the residue-phase mechanism
for the all-index coefficient bound. I independently checked those
arguments and supplied the complete nearest-zero ordering and interval
certificates below. The analysis worker warned explicitly that repairing
the q-prefactor does not automatically repair the old trace inverse
branch. This note retains q throughout; no such repair is claimed.


1. Definitions and the new prefactor
-------------------------------------

Use the archived level-32 eta quotient

 t(tau)=eta(tau)^2 eta(4tau)eta(8tau)eta(32tau)^2 /
                         [eta(2tau)^3 eta(16tau)^3],
 q=exp(2pi i tau),
 J(tau)=theta3(2tau)^2+2sqrt(2)theta3(16tau)^2.

The relevant established inputs from modular-detour.txt, sections 3--4,
are: t has degree two on X0(32), is a degree-one coordinate on its
Fricke quotient, satisfies t(W32 tau)=t(tau), and

 J(W32 tau)=-i sqrt(32)tau J(tau), W32 tau=-1/(32tau).

Let p>=3 be an odd prime, chi=chi_4(p), L=32p^2, and

 T=t(tau)+t(p^2 tau),       D=t(tau)-t(p^2 tau),
 J_p=J(tau)+pJ(p^2 tau),
 Atilde_p=J_p^3 T/D.                                      (1)

For the Lambert series

 F(q)=sum_(d odd) chi_4(d)q^d/[d^4(1+q^d)],
 G(q)=sum_(d odd) q^d/[d^4(1+q^(2d))],
 U=16F(q)-10F(q^2)+F(q^4),
 V=(sqrt(2)/2)[4G(q)-5G(q^2)+G(q^4)], H32=U+V,

put C=(7/2)theta and use the balanced filter

 H_p=U+chi V-(p+chi/p^4)H32(p tau)
                     +p^(-3)[chi U(p^2 tau)+V(p^2 tau)],
 C_p=C(1-p)(1-chi/p^4).

The proposed remainder is

                    R_p=Atilde_p(H_p-C_p).               (2)

The new Fricke map W_L exchanges the two t terms, so T is invariant
and D is anti-invariant. Also

 J_p(W_L tau)=-i sqrt(L)tau J_p(tau),
 Atilde_p(W_L tau)=-i L^(3/2)tau^3 Atilde_p(tau).

The established balanced-filter identity transforms H_p-C_p by
i/(L^(3/2)tau^3), so R_p is Fricke invariant.

The apparent zero of D at q=0 causes no pole: s=t(q^(p^2))/t(q)
belongs to q^(p^2-1) Z[[q]], and

                     T/D=(1+s)/(1-s) in Z[[q]].          (3)

Thus Atilde_p belongs to Z[sqrt(2)][[q]]. This is exact coefficient
integrality, not a bound on primitive content of the final linear forms.


2. The positive-real pole is in the intended location
-----------------------------------------------------

Write q0=exp(-pi/(2sqrt(2))) and q_p=q0^(1/p). On 0<q<q0 both
t(q) and t(q^(p^2)) are increasing, and the former is larger. For
q0<q<q_p, old Fricke reflection sends q to q*=exp(-a^2/(-log q)),
where a=pi/(2sqrt(2)), with

                  q^(p^2)<q*<q0,  t(q)=t(q*).

Hence D(q)>0 for every 0<q<q_p. At q_p the terms agree. The derivative
formula in Section 4 shows this zero is simple. The balanced endpoint
identity H_p(q_p)=C_p cancels it. Therefore R_p is holomorphic there.
Invariance also makes that cancellation holomorphic in a local Fricke
quotient coordinate, though not necessarily in the global trace T.


3. Classification and ordering of ALL denominator zeros in the q disk
---------------------------------------------------------------------

The degree-two quotient property implies that t(tau)=t(p^2 tau) if and
only if, for some gamma in Gamma0(32), either

              p^2 tau=gamma tau,
       or     p^2 tau=gamma W32 tau.                    (4)

Write gamma=(a b;32c d), ad-32bc=1. These two alternatives give exact
quadratic equations. The formulas below use the root in the upper
half-plane and identify tau modulo integer translation for the q disk.

In the first case,

 32cp^2 tau^2+(dp^2-a)tau-b=0,
 discriminant=(a+dp^2)^2-4p^2.

There is no upper-half-plane solution with c=0. Consequently

                   Im tau <= 1/(32p).                  (5)

In the second case set r=p^2c-b. Then

 dp^2 tau^2-(p^2c+b)tau+a/32=0,
 Im tau = sqrt(p^2-8r^2)/(sqrt(32)|d|p^2),
                   |r|<p/sqrt(8).                     (6)

The determinant equation implies a and d are odd; in particular d is
nonzero. Formula (6) has maximum 1/(sqrt(32)p), attained only when
|d|=1,r=0. The corresponding real part is an integer, so the unique
smallest-modulus nonzero q zero is q_p itself.

The next maximum height is obtained at |d|=1,|r|=1. The resulting pair
is

 tau_+ = 1/(2p^2)+i sqrt(p^2-8)/(sqrt(32)p^2),
 tau_- =-1/(2p^2)+i sqrt(p^2-8)/(sqrt(32)p^2),
 q_+ = rho_p exp(pi i/p^2), q_-=conjugate(q_+),
 rho_p = exp[-pi sqrt(p^2-8)/(2sqrt(2)p^2)] <1.          (7)

These are actual zeros, not just bounds: for b=+/-1,

           p^2 tau_b=b-1/(32tau_b),                    (8)

and integer translation plus W32 invariance gives equality of the t's.

To verify strict ordering: for p>=5, sqrt(1-8/p^2)>1/3, while every
|d|>=3 in (6) has height at most one third of the maximum. The ordinary
case (5) has relative height at most 1/sqrt(32)<1/3. For p=3 the pair
in (7) has exactly one-third height, but |d|=3,r=0 is impossible:
then b=9c and ad=1+288c^2, which cannot be divisible by 3. Every other
case is strictly lower. The cases |d|=1,|r|>=2 are also strictly lower.

Thus q_p is the unique nearest zero, and q_+,q_- are the unique next
pair, for EVERY odd p>=3. Equations (4)--(6) classify further zeros,
but their individual H_p periods are not classified in this note.


4. Simplicity and numerator factors at the pair
----------------------------------------------

Differentiate old Fricke invariance at a point (8). Since t is periodic,

 (d/dtau)D(tau_b)
    =t'(tau_b)[1-32p^2 tau_b^2],
 t'(p^2 tau_b)=32tau_b^2 t'(tau_b).                     (9)

The bracket in (9) is nonzero because p tau_b is nonreal. At b=+/-1,
q(p^2 tau_b)=-exp[-a sqrt(p^2-8)], a=pi/(2sqrt(2)). For p>=7 its
modulus is below 1/100. The eta-product logarithmic derivative has
|Dlog t-1|<1/10 there, by the same absolute small-q bound used in the
trace review. Therefore t'(p^2 tau_b) and D'(tau_b) are nonzero.

Similarly J(p^2 tau_b) is nonzero: its constant term is 1+2sqrt(2),
and the elementary theta-series tail for |q|<=1/100 bounds its
deviation from that constant by less than 1/10. Moreover

 J_p(tau_b)=J(tau_b)[1-i sqrt(32)p tau_b],
 J(tau_b)=J(p^2 tau_b)/[-i sqrt(32)tau_b].              (10)

The real part of the bracket in (10) is
1+sqrt(1-8/p^2)>1, so J_p(tau_b) is nonzero. Finally T(tau_b)=
2t(p^2 tau_b) is nonzero because the eta quotient has no zero in the
upper half-plane. Hence noncancellation of H_p-C_p produces a genuine
simple pole of R_p at this pair for p>=7. Separate interval checks also
certify simplicity and nonzero factors for p=3 and p=5.


5. Exact shifted-period residual and its nonzero limit
------------------------------------------------------

The two base components obey separate identities

 U(W32 tau)=C+K(tau)V(tau),
 V(W32 tau)=K(tau)[U(tau)-C],
 K(tau)=i/[32^(3/2)tau^3].                              (11)

To derive the first, apply archived equation (1) of modular-detour.txt
to F at the three dilations. The moment sums 16-20+4=0 and
16-80+64=0 remove its pi^4 terms. The second follows by applying the
first twice; K(W32 tau)K(tau)=1. Thus no new period identity is being
assumed at a general CM point.

At the explicit point (8), put z=p tau_b, w=p^2 tau_b and
k=1/K(tau_b)=-i32^(3/2)tau_b^3. Since W32 tau_b=w-b and U,V are
periodic, (11) gives

             U(tau_b)=C+k V(w),
             V(tau_b)=k[U(w)-C].

Substitution into the balanced filter yields the exact identity

 H_p(tau_b)-C_p
   =-(p+chi/p^4)[H32(z)-C]
     +(k+p^(-3))[chi(U(w)-C)+V(w)].                    (12)

For fixed nonzero b and p tending to infinity through odd primes,

 z=tau0+b/(2p)+O(p^(-2)), tau0=i/sqrt(32),
 k+p^(-3)=O(p^(-4)),
 U(w),V(w)=O(exp(-a p)).

Since H32(tau0)=C, Taylor expansion of the analytic H32 function gives

 H_p(tau_b)-C_p --> -(b/2) H32'(tau0).                 (13)

The difference from the first term displayed in (12) is O(p^(-4));
the total convergence error in (13) is generally O(p^(-1)).

The derivative is rigorously nonzero. The interval program, using the
absolutely convergent differentiated F,G series with explicit tails,
certifies the imaginary-part enclosure below. Its exact imaginary
direction follows from the real q coefficients of H32 and
d/dtau=2pi i q*d/dq at the positive real point q0:

  H32'(tau0)=i*[7.64268690420189696909250738525019...],
                       7 < Im H32'(tau0) < 8.          (14)

Consequently the b=+/-1 periods do not cancel for ALL SUFFICIENTLY LARGE
primes. An explicit threshold is not claimed. The same program directly
certifies noncancellation for p=3,5,7,11,17,31,101. For p<=11 it also
checks (12) against a separate direct evaluation of H_p at tau_b and
checks the eta equality directly. In particular p=3 has a different
imaginary sign from the large-p limit; the argument does not assume
one uniform sign across all small primes.

For example at p=7,b=1, the actual residual is enclosed near

             -2.66960008578608 - 3.70852022190437 i,

which is far from zero. These finite certificates supplement (13);
they do not prove a claim for every untested prime below an unspecified
threshold. No assertion is made that all later denominator zeros fail
to cancel.


6. The exact raw radius and an eventual all-index lower bound
-------------------------------------------------------------

Fix a prime covered by the noncancellation and simplicity statements.
All functions in (2), apart from the denominator D, are holomorphic
for |q|<1. The q=0 apparent singularity and the q_p pole are removable.
The ordering in Section 3 and the two genuine poles in Section 5 show
that the Taylor series of R_p has radius EXACTLY rho_p from (7).

One can strengthen the usual limsup conclusion. Let c_+ and c_- be
the tau-variable residues of R_p at tau_+,tau_-. Fricke invariance and
reality of its q coefficients imply

 c_-=W_L'(tau_+)c_+=-conjugate(c_+).

Since |tau_+|^2=1/L and W_L'(tau_+)=conjugate(tau_+)/tau_+, this gives

                      c_+=i lambda tau_+, lambda real, lambda!=0.

The q-variable pole amplitude in a term A/(1-q/q_+) is
A=-2pi i c_+=2pi lambda tau_+. Therefore the pair contributes to
the n-th Taylor coefficient

       4pi lambda Re[tau_+ exp(-pi i n/p^2)] rho_p^(-n). (15)

The periodic real factor in (15) never vanishes for an integer n.
Indeed a zero, using (7), would imply

                    cos(2pi n/p^2)=1-16/p^2.           (16)

If a rational cosine of a rational multiple of pi is rational, twice
that cosine is a rational algebraic integer (a sum of roots of unity),
and therefore is one of -2,-1,0,1,2. None of the corresponding cosine
values equals 1-16/p^2 for an odd prime p. This proves the assertion.

For fixed p the periodic factor has only 2p^2 values, so its absolute
value has a strictly positive minimum. The next singularity is at
strictly larger modulus: D is analytic and its zeros are isolated, and
Section 3 shows no further zero on |q|=rho_p. Subtract the two simple
pole terms and use Cauchy's estimate on an intermediate circle. The
remainder coefficients are exponentially smaller than (15). Thus there
are constants c(p)>0 and n0(p) such that

       |[q^n]R_p| >= c(p) rho_p^(-n), n>=n0(p).        (17)

This is an ACTUAL all-index lower bound for the unnormalized real
coefficient values in this fixed embedding. It is not merely a failure
of an upper estimate. However (17) does NOT become a primitive bound:
splitting Q(sqrt(2)) components and removing integer content can change
the arithmetic scale, and (15) does not assert a separate bound for
both components. Those arithmetic questions remain open here.
The constants in (17) depend on the fixed prime p. No uniform statement
for a prime p growing with the coefficient index n is asserted.
